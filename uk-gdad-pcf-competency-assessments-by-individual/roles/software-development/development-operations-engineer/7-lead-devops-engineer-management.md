## Introduction

This is for you: a chance to rate your own current strengths against the
baseline the UK GDAD Profession Capability Framework sets for this role
level, using the framework's own words for what it covers.

Commonly referred to as ‘DevOps engineers’, development operations engineers support the development and operation of software through tools, environments and practices. A lead DevOps engineer leads one or a small number of related project teams and contributes to the development of the strategic direction.

Work through the competency matrix below, one skill at a time, then fill in

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating you
give yourself here must never be treated as equivalent to a formal
assessment for a hiring, promotion, pay or performance decision. Use it for
your own reflection, and talk to your manager, a mentor, or your
organisation's people development team about what you find.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you're rating
yourself against what this level actually expects rather than the level
above or below it. Then think back over recent, real examples of your work —
not how you'd like to have handled something, but what you actually did.

**Rate on evidence, not impression.** For every skill, try to recall a
specific moment that supports the rating you give yourself. If you can't
think of one, that's useful information too — it might mean the rating
should be lower, or just that you haven't had the chance to show this skill
yet, which is worth noting separately.

**Be consistent with yourself.** Try to apply the same standard across every
skill, rather than being generous where you feel confident and harsh where
you feel unsure. Coming back to this after a few days, with fresh examples in
mind, often sharpens a rating more than sitting with it in one go.

**Be kind, and be honest.** This only works if you can look at the result
without flinching. A low rating here is not a verdict on you — it's a
starting point. Everyone doing this role, at every level, has skills they are
still building.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

This is a **proficiency** scale, not a frequency scale: it rates how well someone does the thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it.

**Worked example — Development process optimisation.** At Intermediate (2), someone could describe analysing one process and spotting an improvement.
At Advanced (3) — this level's baseline — they lead and develop a team of
experts to deliver service improvements, and help set the policy and
standards a change is implemented against, rather than only proposing the
change. At Expert (4), above this level's baseline, they would be setting
process-optimisation strategy across more than one product line.

## About this role level

Development operations (DevOps) engineers — commonly called "DevOps
engineers" — support the development and operation of software through tools,
environments and practices. The role is responsible for underpinning good
development processes, including managing tools and testing environments,
central code control, maintaining development standards, and writing software
that automates systems.

This assessment covers the **Lead DevOps engineer** level, **management
track** — the sixth step of the role's ladder. The full ladder, in order, is:
Apprentice DevOps engineer, Junior DevOps engineer, DevOps engineer, Senior
DevOps engineer (technical and management), Lead DevOps engineer (a separate
technical-track document, and this level, management track), and Principal
DevOps engineer (technical and management).

A lead DevOps engineer leads one or a small number of related project teams
and contributes to the development of the strategic direction. At this
level, a person is expected to:

- act as a technical product owner, developing medium to long term strategies
  for product lines
- provide technical leadership and guidance through coaching and mentoring
- lead the sharing of knowledge and good practice

These accountabilities are shared with the technical track, and they are
already people- and strategy-focused — the management track builds directly
on them, with several skills below distinctly framed around leading a team of
experts, setting policy and resourcing rather than personal execution alone.
This level names ten skills: Availability and capacity management,
Development process optimisation, Information security, Modern development
standards, Programming and build (software engineering), Prototyping, Service
support, Systems design, Systems integration, and User focus.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Availability and capacity management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead DevOps engineer on the management track is expected to ensure the
correct implementation of availability and capacity management standards and
procedures, to identify capacity issues and implement the required changes,
and to initiate remedial action.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. "Ensure the correct implementation of... standards
and procedures" and "initiate remedial action" describe governing the
practice across a product line, which needs a strong, independent grasp of
the underlying work to do credibly.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot describe what standards or procedures govern availability
  and capacity management for their product line.
- 1 — Beginner: knows the standards exist but has not checked they are
  followed.
- 2 — Intermediate: checks standards are followed in one part of the
  product, with guidance.
- 3 — Advanced: ensures correct implementation across the product line they
  lead, identifies capacity issues before they bite, and initiates remedial
  action.
- 4 — Expert: sets or revises the standards themselves across more than one
  product line.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe a time you identified a capacity issue
  across your product line and initiated remedial action.
- Ask how you check that standards and procedures are actually followed by
  the teams you lead.

### Skill: Development process optimisation

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead DevOps engineer on the management track is expected to analyse
current processes, identify and implement opportunities to optimise them,
lead and develop a team of experts to deliver service improvements, and help
evaluate and establish requirements for the implementation of changes by
setting policy and standards.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. "Lead and develop a team of experts" and "setting
policy and standards" are clear people-leadership responsibilities, well
beyond the technical track's guided-contribution wording for this same skill
at this level.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not led a team through a process improvement.
- 1 — Beginner: contributes to a process improvement led by someone else.

  experts to deliver improvements, and helps set the policy or standards a
  change is implemented against.
- 4 — Expert: sets development process strategy across more than one product
  line.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe a service improvement you led a team of
  experts to deliver, and how you developed the team along the way.
- Ask about a policy or standard you helped establish for implementing
  process changes.

### Skill: Information security

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead DevOps engineer on the management track is expected to design
solutions and services with security controls included, specifically
engineered to mitigate security threats.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. The wording is shared with the technical track, and a
strong, independent grasp continues to fit this level's leadership over a
product line, alongside oversight of the teams they lead.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: designs a solution without considering security threats, and
  does not check whether their teams do either.

  guidance on complex cases.
- 3 — Advanced: consistently designs solutions with appropriate security
  controls, and sets the expectation that the teams they lead do the same.
- 4 — Expert: is accountable for security posture across more than one
  product line.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe how you ensure the teams you lead build
  security controls into your designs.
- Ask about a time you caught a security gap in a design produced by
  someone on a team you led.

### Skill: Modern development standards

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead DevOps engineer on the management track is expected to identify and
apply modern development standards to support complex projects and
programmes, and to lead others in applying modern development standards.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Identifying which standards a whole programme needs and
leading their adoption beyond a single team matches full mastery, and fits
naturally with this level's technical leadership accountability.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: does not apply or lead on development standards.
- 1 — Beginner: applies standards to their own work with reminders.
- 2 — Intermediate: applies standards consistently to their own work.
- 3 — Advanced: identifies which standards a complex project needs and leads
  a team through adopting them, with some support at programme scale.
- 4 — Expert: identifies and applies the right standards for complex projects
  and programmes, and leads others — beyond their own immediate team — in
  adopting them.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask you about a time you led adoption of a development standard
  across more than one team or a whole programme.
- Ask how you handled resistance to a standards change you were leading.

### Skill: Programming and build (software engineering)

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead DevOps engineer on the management track is expected to collaborate
with others when necessary to review specifications, and to use the agreed
specifications to design, code, test and document programs or scripts of
medium-to-high complexity, using the right standards and tools.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. This wording matches the technical track's, and a
manager at this level still needs a strong, independent grasp of the craft to
make credible resourcing and quality decisions for their team.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot independently build from an agreed specification.
- 1 — Beginner: needs significant support even on medium-complexity work.
- 2 — Intermediate: builds medium-complexity programs with occasional
  guidance.
- 3 — Advanced: designs, codes, tests, corrects and documents programs or
  scripts of medium-to-high complexity independently, and uses that
  credibility in resourcing and quality decisions for their team.
- 4 — Expert: is the technical benchmark their teams are measured against.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask to see a recent piece of hands-on technical work, to confirm you remains a credible technical authority.
- Ask about a resourcing or quality decision you made using your own
  technical judgement.

### Skill: Prototyping

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead DevOps engineer on the management track is expected to approach
prototyping as a team activity, actively soliciting prototypes and testing
with others, to establish design patterns and iterate them, and to use a
variety of prototyping methods and choose the most appropriate.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. This wording matches the technical track's, and the
management angle shows in how someone resources and organises the team's
prototyping activity rather than only doing it themselves.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not organised or led a team prototyping activity.
- 1 — Beginner: takes part in prototyping activities others organise.
- 2 — Intermediate: organises prototyping activities with occasional
  guidance.
- 3 — Advanced: consistently chooses appropriate prototyping methods,
  establishes reusable design patterns, and organises the team's time around
  prototyping activity that matters.
- 4 — Expert: not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask you how you decide, as a lead, when to allocate team time to
  prototyping versus building the real thing.
- Ask about a reusable design pattern that came from a prototyping exercise
  you organised.

### Skill: Service support

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead DevOps engineer on the management track is expected to identify,
locate and fix service faults.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. Worth noting plainly, not smoothing over: this
level's own wording for this skill is simpler than the technical track's
("complex service faults; advise others..."), so the baseline sits at
Intermediate — solid personal practice — while this person's broader
leadership is better evidenced through how they organise their team's

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot locate the source of a typical service fault.

- 2 — Intermediate: identifies, locates and fixes a service fault

- 3 — Advanced: does this for less obvious faults and organises how their

- 4 — Expert: not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd walk through a service fault you diagnosed and fixed
  independently.
- Ask how you organise your team's response to an incident affecting more

### Skill: Systems design

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead DevOps engineer on the management track is expected to design systems
characterised by medium levels of risk, impact, and business or technical
complexity, to select appropriate design standards, methods and tools and
ensure they are applied effectively, and to review the systems designs of
others to ensure the selection of appropriate technology, efficient use of
resources and integration of multiple systems and technology.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Ensuring standards are applied effectively and
reviewing others' designs across the teams they lead are management
responsibilities layered on top of independent design ability.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot design a system of medium risk or complexity
  independently.
- 1 — Beginner: designs simple systems with heavy support.
- 2 — Intermediate: designs medium-complexity systems with some guidance, and
  does not yet review others' work.
- 3 — Advanced: independently designs systems of medium risk, impact and
  complexity, ensures design standards are applied effectively across the
  teams they lead, and reviews others' designs.
- 4 — Expert: sets design standards across more than one product line.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask you how you ensure design standards are applied effectively
  across the teams you lead.
- Ask about a time reviewing a colleague's design led to a better choice of
  technology or resource use.

### Skill: User focus

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead DevOps engineer on the management track is expected to collaborate
with user researchers and represent users internally, to explain the
difference between user needs and the desires of the user, to champion user
research to focus on all users, to prioritise and define approaches to
understand the user story while guiding others in doing so, and to offer
recommendations on the best tools and methods to use.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Championing research and guiding others is naturally
a leadership responsibility, and matches this level's coaching and knowledge-
sharing accountability.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: does not represent user needs in their team's work or decisions.
- 1 — Beginner: uses user evidence when given it, but does not champion
  research.
- 2 — Intermediate: engages with user researchers and uses evidence, with
  some guidance on prioritising approaches.
- 3 — Advanced: represents users internally, champions research across the
  teams they lead, and guides others on the right approach to understanding
  a user story.
- 4 — Expert: not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask you about a time you championed user research that shaped a
  strategic decision on your product line.
- Ask how you guide others on distinguishing user needs from stated user
  desires.
