# Senior Test Engineer - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This is for you: a chance to rate your own current strengths against the
baseline the UK GDAD Profession Capability Framework sets for this role
level, using the framework's own words for what it covers.

A test engineer undertakes test planning activity including discovery capture, test definition and the estimation of test effort, as part of a broader risk-based approach. A senior test engineer has similar responsibilities to a test engineer but at a more complex level and on a greater scale.

Work through the competency matrix below, one skill at a time, then fill in
the overall determination at the end. Treat this as a mirror, not a test —
there's no assessor watching, so being honest with yourself only costs you a
slightly less flattering picture today in exchange for a far more useful one.
Read `## Advisory` before you begin.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating you
give yourself here must never be treated as equivalent to a formal
assessment for a hiring, promotion, pay or performance decision. Use it for
your own reflection, and talk to your manager, a mentor, or your
organisation's people development team about what you find.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you're rating
yourself against what this level actually expects rather than the level
above or below it. Then think back over recent, real examples of your work —
not how you'd like to have handled something, but what you actually did.

**Rate on evidence, not impression.** For every skill, try to recall a
specific moment that supports the rating you give yourself. If you can't
think of one, that's useful information too — it might mean the rating
should be lower, or just that you haven't had the chance to show this skill
yet, which is worth noting separately.

**Be consistent with yourself.** Try to apply the same standard across every
skill, rather than being generous where you feel confident and harsh where
you feel unsure. Coming back to this after a few days, with fresh examples in
mind, often sharpens a rating more than sitting with it in one go.

**Be kind, and be honest.** This only works if you can look at the result
without flinching. A low rating here is not a verdict on you — it's a
starting point. Everyone doing this role, at every level, has skills they are
still building.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point scale.
This is a proficiency scale: it rates how well someone does the thing, on
the evidence available, not how often they do it or how they feel about it.

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

**Worked example: Test analysis.** At 2, someone can analyse a single
story or design on their own and identify a plausible risk, but works within
one team and would not usually be asked to settle a disagreement about
whether an approach needs to change. At 3 — the baseline for this level — someone leads test analysis work with stakeholders across more than one
team, decides whether an approach needs to change based on effort and risk,
uses more than one technique to analyse complex information, and coaches at
least one colleague through analysing something difficult. At 4, the same person would be setting the standards and principles other teams use for
test analysis across the whole organisation, not just leading analysis
within their own area. Look for the difference between "I lead analysis work
that crosses my team's boundary" (3) and "other teams' approach to analysis
follows a standard I set" (4).

## About this role level

**The role: test engineer.** A test engineer undertakes test planning
activity — capturing what has been discovered, defining tests, and estimating
the effort testing will take — as part of a broader, risk-based approach to
quality.

**This level: senior test engineer.** A senior test engineer has similar
responsibilities to a test engineer, but at a more complex level and on a
greater scale. At this level, the framework expects someone to:

- identify and raise awareness of risks arising from the automation test
  results
- identify new and implement existing test frameworks to improve confidence
  in testing
- be proficient in a range of coding languages, though they may be an expert
  in only one
- mentor and advise other team members in testing practices

**Where this level sits.** The test engineer role has four levels, and this
is the third:

1. Associate test engineer
2. Test engineer
3. **Senior test engineer** — this level
4. Lead test engineer

Below this level, a test engineer writes, debugs and refactors test code
within their own team, with some support on the harder parts. Above it, a
lead test engineer sets standards and influences decisions across the whole
organisation, writes new automation test frameworks from project
requirements, and has a presence in external test communities.

**The skills this assessment covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Communicating between the technical and non-technical
2. Designing and executing tests
3. Managing, reporting and resolving defects
4. Test analysis
5. Test and quality planning
6. Test engineering

The framework's wording at this level is dominated by leading, guiding and
coaching others, and by scope that crosses team boundaries — "across teams",
"lead others", "coach others" — rather than by any remaining reference to
support someone themselves receives. Every skill here carries a baseline
of Advanced: a strong, independent grasp that is already starting to extend
to other people, but that has not yet reached the organisation-wide
standard-setting the framework reserves for lead test engineer.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior test engineer is expected to listen to and interpret the needs of
technical and non-technical stakeholders and manage their expectations, to
manage both active and reactive communication, and to support or host
difficult discussions within the team or with diverse senior stakeholders.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. The scope has grown to include senior stakeholders
and reactive as well as active communication, with no support qualifier
anywhere in the wording — a strong, independent grasp rather than a
supervised or partially supported one.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Avoids communication with senior or diverse stakeholders and
  leaves it to others.
- 1 — Beginner: Communicates with familiar stakeholders, but needs close
  support for anything involving a senior or diverse audience.
- 2 — Intermediate: Communicates with a range of stakeholders with some
  guidance, but has not yet hosted a genuinely difficult discussion with
  senior stakeholders unsupported.
- 3 — Advanced: Interprets stakeholder needs and manages their expectations
  unsupported, and has supported or hosted a difficult discussion with
  senior stakeholders.
- 4 — Expert: Is someone the organisation turns to for the hardest
  stakeholder conversations, and develops others' ability to handle them.

**Evidence to gather**

- Structured interview question: "Tell me about a difficult discussion you
  hosted or supported with a senior stakeholder. What made it difficult, and
  how did it end?"
- Referee question: "How does you manage a stakeholder whose
  expectations do not match what testing can realistically deliver?"
- Observed scenario: sit in on a meeting you leads involving
  more than one type of stakeholder, and note how you manage the room.

### Skill: Designing and executing tests

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior test engineer is expected to set up suitable environments, to
influence and guide the use of appropriate test types and techniques to
mitigate risk early, to lead others in designing, building, maintaining and
executing tests, to contribute to developing and implementing standards for
designing and executing tests, and to improve test types and techniques
through a structured process.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Every accountability is unsupported and several
involve leading or guiding others, but standard-setting is only
"contribute to" rather than owned outright — a strong, independent
practitioner who is starting to shape how others work, not yet the sole
authority on it.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Follows existing test types and techniques without questioning
  or improving them.
- 1 — Beginner: Selects test types and techniques for their own work, but
  has not guided anyone else's choices.
- 2 — Intermediate: Sets up environments and designs tests unsupported for
  familiar work, but has not yet led others through the same activity.
- 3 — Advanced: Guides the choice of test types and techniques to mitigate
  risk early, leads others in designing and executing tests, and has
  contributed to a written standard for how the team does this.
- 4 — Expert: Owns the organisation's standards for test types, techniques,
  design and execution, and makes strategic decisions about which to adopt.

**Evidence to gather**

- Work sample: ask you to review a colleague's test design and
  identify a risk it misses.
- Structured interview question: "Describe a time you improved a test
  type or technique your team used. What was wrong with the old one, and how
  did you know the new one was better?"
- Referee question: "Has you led others through designing tests
  for a piece of work? How did that go?"

### Skill: Managing, reporting and resolving defects

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior test engineer is expected to contribute to developing standards for
defect management processes, to manage and escalate dependencies, defects
and risks across teams, to contribute to mitigation and contingency plans
across teams, to use defect patterns and trends to make recommendations on
testing and quality approaches with support, and to manage stakeholder
expectations and communications during defect resolution.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Most of this skill is independent and cross-team in
scope, though the framework still names "with support" for using defect
patterns and trends to make recommendations — one part of this skill that someone strong here may still be developing even at Advanced overall.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Manages defects within their own work only, without considering
  cross-team dependencies.
- 1 — Beginner: Escalates a defect when told to, but needs close support to
  judge cross-team risk or dependencies.
- 2 — Intermediate: Manages defects and escalates dependencies with some
  guidance, mostly within a single team's scope.
- 3 — Advanced: Manages and escalates dependencies, defects and risks across
  more than one team unsupported, contributes to mitigation plans that span
  teams, and is starting to use defect patterns and trends to inform
  recommendations, checking the harder judgement calls with someone else.
- 4 — Expert: Leads and coaches others in improving defect management
  processes, and makes confident, evidence-based recommendations from
  defect patterns without needing to check them.

**Evidence to gather**

- Structured interview question: "Describe a defect whose impact crossed
  team boundaries. How did you manage that?"
- Work sample: ask you to look at a set of recent defects and
  identify a pattern or trend, then say what you would recommend.
- Referee question: "How does you manage stakeholder expectations
  when a defect resolution is taking longer than hoped?"

### Skill: Test analysis

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior test engineer is expected to lead work with stakeholders across
teams to determine which functional and non-functional quality
characteristics add value, to determine if an approach needs to change based
on effort and risk, to ensure test needs are implemented early, to use
multiple techniques to analyse complex information to identify risks, and to
coach others in test analysis.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. This is fully independent, cross-team work that
includes coaching others, but the scope named is "across teams" rather than
"across the organisation" — the wider, standard-setting scope the framework
reserves for lead test engineer.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Analyses only what is put directly in front of them, without
  considering whether the approach itself is right.
- 1 — Beginner: Analyses a single story or design with support, and has not
  led analysis work that spans more than one team.
- 2 — Intermediate: Analyses requirements and designs unsupported within
  their own team, but has not yet led cross-team analysis or coached anyone
  else in it.
- 3 — Advanced: Leads analysis work with stakeholders across more than one
  team, uses more than one technique to unpick complex information, judges
  when an approach needs to change, and has coached at least one colleague
  through a difficult piece of analysis.
- 4 — Expert: Sets the standards and principles other teams use for test
  analysis across the organisation.

**Evidence to gather**

- Structured interview question: "Tell me about a time you judged that the
  planned testing approach needed to change. What told you that, and what
  did you do?"
- Work sample: give you a complex, multi-part requirement and try to analyse it using more than one technique.
- Referee question: "Has you coached anyone through test
  analysis? What did that involve?"

### Skill: Test and quality planning

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior test engineer is expected to work with teams to develop and
implement appropriate quality testing approaches, plans and strategies, to
contribute to organisational quality testing strategies, to implement ways
to capture data to drive continuous improvement, and to advocate for full
team ownership of quality testing activities, encouraging early engagement.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. The scope is now organisational, but the framework
says "contribute to" organisational strategy rather than own or lead it,
placing this just below the level-setting scope of lead test engineer.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Follows a quality testing plan without contributing to how it is
  shaped.
- 1 — Beginner: Follows and adapts a plan for their own team with support.
- 2 — Intermediate: Develops and implements a quality testing approach for
  their own team with some guidance.
- 3 — Advanced: Works with other teams to develop and implement quality
  testing approaches, contributes to organisational strategy, implements a
  way to capture data that drives improvement, and actively advocates for
  early team engagement with quality.
- 4 — Expert: Creates and manages multiple quality testing plans, approaches
  and strategies across the organisation, and leads other teams in adopting
  them.

**Evidence to gather**

- Structured interview question: "Describe your contribution to an
  organisational, not just a team, quality testing strategy."
- Work sample: ask you to describe a way you capture data about
  testing effectiveness and what you have changed as a result.
- Referee question: "How does you encourage a team to engage with
  quality early, rather than testing being an afterthought?"

### Skill: Test engineering

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior test engineer is expected to develop, standardise and extend
reusable frameworks and tools to support a range of testing activities, to
guide and coach others in creating and maintaining comprehensive and reliable
tests that meet standards, to research and prepare for future testing needs
including tools, methodologies and techniques, and to maintain and adapt
continuous integration and deployment pipelines.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. This is independent, standard-setting work within
the team's tooling, and the level's own accountability text explicitly notes
someone "may be an expert in only one" coding language — a strong,
independent grasp across the board, not full mastery of everything.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Uses existing frameworks and tools as given, without extending
  or standardising them.
- 1 — Beginner: Maintains automated tests and pipelines with support, and
  has not built a reusable framework or tool.
- 2 — Intermediate: Maintains automated tests and pipelines unsupported for
  routine work, but has not yet developed a reusable framework others use.
- 3 — Advanced: Develops or extends a reusable framework or tool that more
  than one team uses, guides and coaches colleagues in writing tests that
  meet the team's standards, and actively researches future testing needs.
- 4 — Expert: Establishes and leads the organisation's test engineering
  practices, standards and tool choices.

**Evidence to gather**

- Work sample: ask you to describe a reusable test framework or
  tool you built or extended, and who else uses it.
- Structured interview question: "How have you prepared your team for a
  testing need you expected to arrive before it did?"
- Referee question: "How does you coach others in writing tests
  that meet the team's standards, rather than just writing you personally?"
