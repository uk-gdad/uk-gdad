## Introduction

This is for you: a chance to rate your own current strengths against the

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating you
give yourself here must never be treated as equivalent to a formal
assessment for a hiring, promotion, pay or performance decision. Use it for
your own reflection, and talk to your manager, a mentor, or your
organisation's people development team about what you find.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you're rating
yourself against what this level actually expects rather than the level
above or below it. Then think back over recent, real examples of your work —
not how you'd like to have handled something, but what you actually did.

**Rate on evidence, not impression.** For every skill, try to recall a
specific moment that supports the rating you give yourself. If you can't
think of one, that's useful information too — it might mean the rating
should be lower, or just that you haven't had the chance to show this skill
yet, which is worth noting separately.

**Be consistent with yourself.** Try to apply the same standard across every
skill, rather than being generous where you feel confident and harsh where
you feel unsure. Coming back to this after a few days, with fresh examples in
mind, often sharpens a rating more than sitting with it in one go.

**Be kind, and be honest.** This only works if you can look at the result
without flinching. A low rating here is not a verdict on you — it's a
starting point. Everyone doing this role, at every level, has skills they are
still building.

## Rating scale

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

This is a proficiency scale, not a frequency scale — it rates how well someone does the thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it.

**Worked example — Service management framework knowledge.** Someone who holds only a Level 3 qualification, without the expert-level certificate the framework names at this level, sits at 2 or 3. Someone who holds the expert-level certificate and applies it consistently sits at 3. Someone who holds the expert certificate and is regularly asked to settle disputes about how the framework should be applied, or trains others in it, sits at 4 — full mastery, demonstrated by teaching.

## About this role level

Command and control centre managers proactively monitor live services and performance trends to identify potential problems or areas for improvement that can then be investigated. The role runs across four levels: operations analyst, senior operations analyst, operational control manager, and head of command and control.

The head of command and control is the top of the ladder. The framework describes this level as responsible for the strategy roadmap, integration and building the capability of the team. At this level, you ensure all tools are fit for purpose and meet business requirements, and you may still be required to address issues across IT operations personally.

The framework names ten skills at this level: asset and configuration management (managing configuration items and related information, and managing service compliance and risk), availability and capacity management (ensuring the correct implementation of availability and capacity management standards and procedures, identifying capacity issues and implementing required changes, and initiating remedial action), change management (managing high impact, complex change requests, and ensuring that release policies, procedures and processes are applied), community collaboration (working collaboratively in a group and actively networking with others, adapting feedback to ensure it is effective and lasting, using their topic to identify and rectify problems in the team dynamic, and identifying issues through Agile "health checks" with the team), continuity management (managing the runbooks and the processes around service continuity, and testing the runbooks to ensure that service availability is maintained), incident management (leading the investigation and resolution of incidents), ownership and initiative (taking accountability for issues that occur and being proactive in searching for potential problems, and achieving excellent user outcomes), problem management (ensuring the right actions are taken to investigate, resolve and anticipate problems, and co-ordinating the team to investigate problems, implement solutions and take preventive measures), service focus (seeing the bigger picture by taking groups of services and investigating how to get the best of underlying services), service management framework knowledge (holding an expert certificate in the service management framework qualification), and user focus (giving direction on which tools or methods to use, demonstrating experience in meeting the needs of users across a variety of channels, bringing insight and expertise in how user needs have changed over time to ensure they're met by the business, and applying strategic thinking to provide the best service for the end user).

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Asset and configuration management

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a head of command and control to manage configuration items and related information, and to manage service compliance and risk.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "Manage service compliance and risk" is organisation-level accountability, not day-to-day maintenance, matching full mastery at the top of the role's ladder.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe what service compliance or risk means for configuration management.
- 1 — Beginner: Manages configuration items for a single area with support.
- 2 — Intermediate: Manages configuration items independently but has not yet owned compliance or risk at an estate level.
- 3 — Advanced: Manages configuration compliance and risk across more than one area, catching issues before they become material.
- 4 — Expert: Owns configuration compliance and risk policy for the whole operations centre, and other managers escalate their hardest configuration risk decisions to this person.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe how you identify and manage configuration-related risk across the whole operations estate.
- Ask a referee whether you have owned a compliance issue that reached senior management attention.
