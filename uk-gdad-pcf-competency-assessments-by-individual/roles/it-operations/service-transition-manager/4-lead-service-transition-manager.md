# Lead Service Transition Manager - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This is for you: a chance to rate your own current strengths against the
baseline the UK GDAD Profession Capability Framework sets for this role
level, using the framework's own words for what it covers.

IT service transition managers provide overall planning for service transitions and co-ordinate the required resources. In this role, you will control the product life cycle of all changes, enabling beneficial changes to be made with minimum disruption to IT services. A lead service transition manager is responsible for the planning and co-ordination of resources to ensure that services are effectively transitioned into service operation.

Work through the competency matrix below, one skill at a time, then fill in
the overall determination at the end. Treat this as a mirror, not a test —

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating you
give yourself here must never be treated as equivalent to a formal

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you're rating
yourself against what this level actually expects rather than the level
above or below it. Then think back over recent, real examples of your work —
not how you'd like to have handled something, but what you actually did.

**Rate on evidence, not impression.** For every skill, try to recall a
specific moment that supports the rating you give yourself. If you can't
think of one, that's useful information too — it might mean the rating
should be lower, or just that you haven't had the chance to show this skill
yet, which is worth noting separately.

**Be consistent with yourself.** Try to apply the same standard across every
skill, rather than being generous where you feel confident and harsh where
you feel unsure. Coming back to this after a few days, with fresh examples in
mind, often sharpens a rating more than sitting with it in one go.

**Be kind, and be honest.** This only works if you can look at the result
without flinching. A low rating here is not a verdict on you — it's a
starting point. Everyone doing this role, at every level, has skills they are
still building.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

This is a **proficiency** scale, not a frequency scale: it rates how well someone does the thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it.

**Worked example — Service focus.** At 3 — Advanced, someone might
describe independently investigating a group of related services to improve
a go-live decision. At 4 — Expert, someone for lead service transition
manager should be able to describe maintaining focus on the entire life
cycle of a service across a whole transition programme, ensuring it stayed at
the forefront of readiness activities, and actively championing that
approach within their team until it became how the team worked by default —
that scale of sustained, embedded leadership, not a single successful
investigation, is what "Expert" evidence requires at this level.

## About this role level

The IT operations profession's **service transition manager** role covers
people who provide overall planning for service transitions and co-ordinate
the required resources, controlling the product life cycle of all changes so
that beneficial changes can be made with minimum disruption to IT services.
It has four levels:

1. Service acceptance analyst
2. Service readiness analyst
3. Service transition manager
4. **Lead service transition manager** — this level

The **lead service transition manager** is the most senior level of this
role. They are responsible for the planning and co-ordination of resources to
ensure that services are effectively transitioned into service operation.

At this level, you will be solely responsible for the co-ordination
activities across projects, suppliers and service teams.

This role level names eleven skills: Asset and configuration management,
Availability and capacity management, Change management, Community
collaboration, Continuity management, Incident management, Ownership and
initiative, Problem management, Service focus, Service management framework
knowledge, and User focus.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Asset and configuration management

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a lead service transition manager to maintain secure
configuration and accurate information, control IT assets in one or more
areas, and verify the location and state of IT assets.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. The wording is unchanged from the service
transition manager level below — this level's added weight comes from being
solely accountable for co-ordination across projects, suppliers and service
teams, but the framework's own description of this specific skill does not
escalate, so the baseline is unchanged too.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Relies entirely on suppliers or project teams to keep
  configuration data accurate.
- 1 — Beginner: Checks configuration accuracy occasionally, when prompted.
- 2 — Intermediate: Owns the accuracy of configuration data across the
  projects and suppliers they co-ordinate, and can verify asset location and
  state on request.
- 3 — Advanced: Proactively audits configuration data across multiple
  suppliers before a transition decision, and closes gaps others would miss.
- 4 — Expert: Sets configuration standards adopted across the wider
  transition programme.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask how you assures configuration accuracy when co-ordinating
  across multiple suppliers.
- Ask for an example of an issue caused, or nearly caused, by inaccurate
  configuration data across a multi-supplier transition.

### Skill: Availability and capacity management

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a lead service transition manager to ensure the
correct implementation of availability and capacity management standards and
procedures, identify capacity issues and implement the required changes, and
initiate remedial action.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Moving from "manage service components to ensure
they meet ... KPIs" at the level below to "ensure the correct implementation
of ... standards" and "initiate remedial action" is a step into leading and
correcting practice across others' work, not just checking it.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Reacts to a capacity or availability failure without ensuring
  standards were correctly implemented in the first place.
- 1 — Beginner: Identifies capacity issues with support, and needs help
  initiating remedial action.
- 2 — Intermediate: Identifies and remedies straightforward capacity issues
  independently.
- 3 — Advanced: Ensures availability and capacity standards are correctly
  implemented across projects and suppliers, and independently initiates
  remedial action when they are not.
- 4 — Expert: Has changed how availability and capacity standards are applied
  across the transition programme, preventing repeat issues.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of identifying that a supplier or project had not
  correctly implemented availability or capacity standards, and what you did.
- Ask about a time you initiated remedial action for a capacity issue.

### Skill: Change management

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a lead service transition manager to manage
high-impact, complex change requests, and ensure that release policies,
procedures and processes are applied.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. "High impact, complex" change requests, and ensuring
release policy is applied across the board, is a step up from the "analyse
and assess" wording at the level below, matching this level's sole
accountability for co-ordination across projects and suppliers.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Handles only routine, low-impact change requests.
- 1 — Beginner: Manages a complex change request with close support.
- 2 — Intermediate: Manages moderately complex change requests
  independently.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently manages high-impact, complex change requests
  and ensures release policy is applied consistently across suppliers.
- 4 — Expert: Is trusted with the organisation's highest-risk changes, and
  their approach to release policy is adopted by other leads.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of managing a high-impact, complex change request
  spanning more than one supplier or project.
- Ask how you ensure release policy is applied consistently when several
  suppliers are involved.

### Skill: Community collaboration

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a lead service transition manager to work
collaboratively in a group, actively networking with others, adapt feedback
to ensure it is effective and lasting, use their topic to identify problems
or issues in the team dynamic and rectify them, and identify issues through
Agile "health checks" with the team, helping to stimulate the right
responses.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. This fuller, actively facilitative wording — adapting
feedback, running health checks, rectifying team dynamics — is a step up from
the level below, matching this level's sole responsibility for co-ordination
across projects, suppliers and service teams.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Works alongside others without engaging with team dynamics
  across projects.
- 1 — Beginner: Notices team dynamic issues but needs support addressing
  them.
- 2 — Intermediate: Actively networks and addresses straightforward team
  dynamic issues.
- 3 — Advanced: Runs or contributes meaningfully to structured checks across
  multiple teams and suppliers, and acts on what they reveal.
- 4 — Expert: Is known across the transition programme for improving how
  groups of teams and suppliers work together.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of identifying and rectifying a team dynamic issue that
  spanned more than one supplier or project team.
- Ask how you have used structured feedback (a retrospective, a
  health check) to improve cross-team working.

### Skill: Ownership and initiative

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a lead service transition manager to take
accountability for issues that occur and be proactive in searching for
potential problems, and to achieve excellent user outcomes.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. The shift from "own an issue" at the level below to

**Behavioural indicators**

  across projects, suppliers and service teams, and takes accountability for
  outcomes.
- 4 — Expert: Has a track record of catching serious cross-supplier problems
  before they escalated, and models this for other leads.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of proactively identifying a problem spanning more than
  one supplier before it was formally raised.
- Ask a referee about your reputation for proactive risk-spotting
  across a complex, multi-party programme.

### Skill: Service focus

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a lead service transition manager to maintain focus on
the entire life cycle of the service and ensure it is at the forefront of
readiness activities, understand and communicate service benefits to end
users, and ensure that service focus is championed within their team and
actively recommend its adoption.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. This is the fullest, most senior description of this
skill anywhere in the role — maintaining whole-life-cycle focus, championing
it within a team, and actively recommending its adoption — matching an
expert-level baseline.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Focuses on transition milestones without reference to the
  service's whole life cycle.
- 1 — Beginner: Understands the whole-life-cycle view but does not yet keep
  it at the forefront of readiness activities.
- 2 — Intermediate: Keeps a whole-life-cycle view for a defined part of a
  transition, with support.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently maintains whole-life-cycle focus and
  communicates service benefits to end users.
- 4 — Expert: Has championed whole-life-cycle service focus within their team
  until it became standard practice, and actively recommends its adoption
  beyond their own team.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of keeping a whole-life-cycle view at the forefront of
  a transition, even under pressure to focus only on the immediate
  milestone.
- Ask how you have championed service focus within a team, and
  what changed as a result.

### Skill: Service management framework knowledge

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a lead service transition manager to show they have a

knowledge of the life cycle or capability elements of ITIL (Information
Technology Infrastructure Library).

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. The wording is unchanged from the service
transition manager level below, so the baseline stays the same even though
this level carries wider co-ordination responsibility, because the
framework's own description of this specific skill does not escalate.

- 3 — Advanced: Uses ITIL concepts to diagnose why a cross-supplier

### Skill: User focus

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a lead service transition manager to collaborate with
user researchers and represent users internally, explain the difference
between user needs and the desires of the user, champion user research to
focus on all users, prioritise and define approaches to understand the user
story while guiding others, and offer recommendations on the best tools and
methods to use.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. This is a substantially fuller description than the
levels below — championing research, guiding others, recommending methods —
a clear step up from the basic user needs wording used earlier in the role's
ladder, matching a leading role in how user focus is practised across the
transition programme.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Treats user needs and user requests as the same thing.
- 1 — Beginner: Understands the difference between needs and desires in
  theory, but does not apply it consistently.
- 2 — Intermediate: Applies the distinction and represents users internally
  with support.
- 3 — Advanced: Champions user research across the transition programme,
  guides others in approach, and recommends tools and methods.
- 4 — Expert: Their advocacy has changed how the wider organisation treats
  user research as part of service transition.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of explaining, to a resistant stakeholder or supplier,
  why a user's stated request differed from your real need.
- Ask how you have guided others in choosing a research approach
  during a transition.
