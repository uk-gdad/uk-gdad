# Lead Business Relationship Manager - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This is for you: a chance to rate your own current strengths against the
baseline the UK GDAD Profession Capability Framework sets for this role
level, using the framework's own words for what it covers.

A business relationship manager, sometimes known as ‘business partner’, establishes and maintains meaningful relationships with colleagues to deliver positive impact across the organisation. A lead business relationship manager leads engagement with internal and external stakeholders to ensure complex needs and challenges are understood, prioritised and communicated to relevant audiences. They may lead a team of business relationship managers.

Work through the competency matrix below, one skill at a time, then fill in
the overall determination at the end. Treat this as a mirror, not a test —
there's no assessor watching, so being honest with yourself only costs you a
slightly less flattering picture today in exchange for a far more useful one.
Read `## Advisory` before you begin.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating you
give yourself here must never be treated as equivalent to a formal
assessment for a hiring, promotion, pay or performance decision. Use it for
your own reflection, and talk to your manager, a mentor, or your
organisation's people development team about what you find.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you're rating
yourself against what this level actually expects rather than the level
above or below it. Then think back over recent, real examples of your work —
not how you'd like to have handled something, but what you actually did.

**Rate on evidence, not impression.** For every skill, try to recall a
specific moment that supports the rating you give yourself. If you can't
think of one, that's useful information too — it might mean the rating
should be lower, or just that you haven't had the chance to show this skill
yet, which is worth noting separately.

**Be consistent with yourself.** Try to apply the same standard across every
skill, rather than being generous where you feel confident and harsh where
you feel unsure. Coming back to this after a few days, with fresh examples in
mind, often sharpens a rating more than sitting with it in one go.

**Be kind, and be honest.** This only works if you can look at the result
without flinching. A low rating here is not a verdict on you — it's a
starting point. Everyone doing this role, at every level, has skills they are
still building.

## Rating scale

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

This is a proficiency scale, not a frequency scale — it rates how well someone does the thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it.

**Worked example — Stakeholder relationship management (IT operations).** Someone who manages long-term strategic relationships independently, but has not yet been asked to step in when someone else's relationship broke down, sits at 3. Someone who directs the strategic approach for stakeholder relationships, establishes stakeholder objectives, acts as the point of escalation when relationships become difficult for more junior colleagues, and arbitrates when a blocker reaches senior stakeholders, sits at 4 — the framework names exactly that escalation and arbitration role at this level.

## About this role level

The business relationship manager establishes and maintains meaningful relationships with colleagues to deliver positive impact across the organisation. Across the whole role, a business relationship manager brings together different functions to ensure value across the organisation, works strategically across teams to ensure digital and data investment adds value, explores the perspectives, challenges and ambitions of teams to shape a pipeline of opportunities, acts as a point of focus for colleagues who influence strategic direction, and sets the conditions for a culture of creating meaningful connections. The role runs across three levels: business relationship manager, senior business relationship manager, and lead business relationship manager.

The lead business relationship manager level is the most senior of the three. The framework describes it as leading engagement with internal and external stakeholders to ensure complex needs and challenges are understood, prioritised and communicated to relevant audiences, and notes that a lead business relationship manager may lead a team of business relationship managers. At this level, you ensure strategic demands — such as spending reviews and services strategy commitments — are met and appropriately communicated; promote ways of working that support meaningful connections and outcomes, aligned to the organisation's changing needs; set conditions that enable a culture of positive impact through increased value, improved user experience and enhanced efficiency; and enable others to implement activities that improve relationship maturity.

The framework names six skills at this level: business analysis (IT operations) (directing business analysis, including the collection of information and evidence and the identification of problems and opportunities, ensuring recommendations fit with strategic business objectives, communicating recommendations persuasively to important stakeholders, and identifying the simplest of a variety of approaches), ownership and initiative (taking accountability for issues that occur and being proactive in searching for potential problems, and achieving excellent user outcomes), service focus (seeing the bigger picture by taking groups of services and investigating how to get the best of underlying services), service management framework knowledge (holding a Level 3 service management framework qualification and demonstrating knowledge of the life cycle or capability elements of ITIL), stakeholder relationship management (IT operations) (managing long-term strategic relationships with stakeholders, identifying where new connections need to be made and existing ones nurtured, directing the strategic approach for stakeholder relationships and establishing stakeholder objectives, acting as a point of escalation if relationships break down or become challenging for more junior team members, influencing important senior stakeholders and arbitrating when blockers are escalated, and facilitating discussions across high risk and complex areas or projects under constrained timelines), and strategic thinking (defining strategies and policies while providing guidance to others on working in the strategic context, and evaluating current strategies to ensure business requirements are being met and exceeded where possible), and user focus (giving direction on which tools or methods to use, demonstrating experience in meeting the needs of users across a variety of channels, bringing insight and expertise in how user needs have changed over time to ensure they're met by the business, and applying strategic thinking to provide the best service for the end user).

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Business analysis (IT operations)

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a lead business relationship manager to direct business analysis, including the collection of information and evidence and the identification of problems and opportunities, to ensure that recommendations fit with strategic business objectives, to communicate recommendations persuasively to important stakeholders, and to identify the simplest of a variety of approaches.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "Direct" business analysis — setting the approach others follow, and persuading important stakeholders — is authority over the discipline itself, which matches full mastery at the top of this role's ladder.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe how to direct a business analysis effort involving other people.
- 1 — Beginner: Contributes analysis to an effort someone else directs.
- 2 — Intermediate: Directs a straightforward analysis effort with support ensuring it fits strategic objectives.
- 3 — Advanced: Directs business analysis independently, and communicates recommendations persuasively to important stakeholders most of the time.
- 4 — Expert: Consistently directs complex, multi-stakeholder analysis efforts, identifies the simplest of several viable approaches, and is trusted to make the persuasive case to the most important stakeholders in the organisation.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe a business analysis effort you directed, including how you chose between several viable approaches and why you picked the simplest one.
- Ask for an example of a recommendation you had to persuade a difficult or sceptical senior stakeholder to accept.
- Ask a referee whether your recommendations tend to hold up under scrutiny from senior leadership.

### Skill: Ownership and initiative

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a lead business relationship manager to take accountability for issues that occur, to be proactive in searching for potential problems, and to achieve excellent user outcomes.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. The wording is unchanged from the level below, so the baseline stays consistent at Advanced rather than rising on wording alone.

**Evidence to gather**

### Skill: Service focus

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a lead business relationship manager to see the bigger picture by taking groups of services and investigating how to get the best of underlying services.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "See the bigger picture" across groups of services is the broadest reading of this skill in the role's ladder, matching full mastery.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Only considers a single relationship or engagement in isolation.
- 1 — Beginner: Considers how one other service or relationship connects to their own, with help.
- 2 — Intermediate: Considers how a small group of related services or relationships interact.
- 3 — Advanced: Investigates how to get the best from groups of underlying services independently.
- 4 — Expert: Is someone others rely on to see how the whole set of business relationships and underlying services should best work together.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe a time you improved outcomes by looking across a group of relationships or services rather than one in isolation.
- Ask you what trade-off you would make between two business areas competing for the same limited investment.

### Skill: Service management framework knowledge

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a lead business relationship manager to show they have a Level 3 service management framework qualification, and to demonstrate knowledge of the life cycle or capability elements of ITIL (Information Technology Infrastructure Library).

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. The wording is unchanged from the level below, so the baseline stays at Advanced rather than rising on wording alone.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Holds no service management qualification and cannot describe ITIL's life cycle.
- 1 — Beginner: Holds a Level 3 qualification but cannot describe how ITIL life cycle elements connect to their own work.
- 2 — Intermediate: Holds Level 3 and can describe ITIL life cycle elements in general terms.
- 3 — Advanced: Holds Level 3 and applies ITIL life cycle or capability knowledge directly to how they manage strategic relationships.
- 4 — Expert: Holds expert-level certification and teaches ITIL concepts to others.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd explain how an ITIL life cycle stage applies to a strategic relationship or programme you oversee.

### Skill: Stakeholder relationship management (IT operations)

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a lead business relationship manager to manage long-term strategic relationships with stakeholders, identifying where new connections need to be made and existing ones nurtured, to direct the strategic approach for stakeholder relationships and establish stakeholder objectives, to act as a point of escalation if relationships break down or become challenging for more junior team members, to influence important senior stakeholders and arbitrate when blockers are escalated, and to facilitate discussions across high risk and complex areas or projects under constrained timelines.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. This is the core skill of the role, and the level's own wording names escalation, arbitration and directing the strategic approach for others — full mastery, demonstrated by being someone others rely on when a relationship is genuinely difficult.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has never negotiated with a senior stakeholder or been asked to resolve a strained relationship.
- 1 — Beginner: Manages a straightforward strategic relationship with support.
- 2 — Intermediate: Manages strategic relationships independently, with occasional support on the most difficult ones.
- 3 — Advanced: Directs the strategic approach for a set of stakeholder relationships and establishes clear objectives for them, largely without support.
- 4 — Expert: Is the organisation's point of escalation when a stakeholder relationship breaks down, influences the most senior stakeholders directly, and facilitates discussion across high risk, complex, time-constrained situations that others could not resolve alone.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe a relationship that had broken down and how you, specifically, repaired it or arbitrated the underlying blocker.
- Ask for an example of facilitating a high risk or complex discussion under a tight deadline.
- Ask a referee, ideally a senior stakeholder you have worked with, how you experienced being managed by you.

### Skill: User focus

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a lead business relationship manager to give direction on which tools or methods to use, to demonstrate experience in meeting the needs of users across a variety of channels, to bring insight and expertise in how user needs have changed over time to ensure they are met by the business, and to apply strategic thinking to provide the best service for the end user.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "Give direction on which tools or methods to use" and bringing insight into how user needs change over time is teaching and setting direction for others, which matches full mastery.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Does not engage with user needs or channel differences in their work.
- 1 — Beginner: Considers user needs for a single channel, with support.
- 2 — Intermediate: Meets user needs across more than one channel independently.
- 3 — Advanced: Applies strategic thinking to how the business serves the end user, adjusting as needs change.
- 4 — Expert: Gives direction to others on which tools or methods to use, and brings recognised insight into how user needs have shifted over time to keep the business aligned to them.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe how user needs for a service you support have changed over time, and how you made sure the business kept up.
- Ask a referee whether other business relationship managers come to you for direction on which tools or methods to use.
