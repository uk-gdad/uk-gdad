## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating you
give yourself here must never be treated as equivalent to a formal
assessment for a hiring, promotion, pay or performance decision. Use it for
your own reflection, and talk to your manager, a mentor, or your

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you're rating
yourself against what this level actually expects rather than the level
above or below it. Then think back over recent, real examples of your work —
not how you'd like to have handled something, but what you actually did.

**Rate on evidence, not impression.** For every skill, try to recall a
specific moment that supports the rating you give yourself. If you can't
think of one, that's useful information too — it might mean the rating
should be lower, or just that you haven't had the chance to show this skill
yet, which is worth noting separately.

**Be consistent with yourself.** Try to apply the same standard across every
skill, rather than being generous where you feel confident and harsh where
you feel unsure. Coming back to this after a few days, with fresh examples in
mind, often sharpens a rating more than sitting with it in one go.

**Be kind, and be honest.** This only works if you can look at the result
without flinching. A low rating here is not a verdict on you — it's a
starting point. Everyone doing this role, at every level, has skills they are
still building.

## Rating scale

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

This is a proficiency scale, not a frequency scale — it rates how well someone does the thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it.

**Worked example — Change management.** Someone who has only ever implemented change requests under supervision sits at 2 or below. Someone who analyses and assesses the impact of change, documents change requests, and actions changes from those requests sits at 3 — this level's own baseline, since the framework's own wording moves from implementing under supervision to independent analysis and action. Someone who additionally sets the release plan for a programme of upcoming changes and liaises directly with DevOps and service design teams to shape it is showing evidence closer to the level above.

## About this role level

Change and release managers lead the change advisory board, ensuring adequate risk assessment and scheduling of technical changes and releases. They own the configuration management database and the access, security and configuration of change activities and release procedures. The role runs across three levels: configuration analyst, change and release analyst, and change and release manager.

The change and release analyst level sits second of three, above configuration analyst and below change and release manager. The framework describes it as planning and rolling out software, and designing and carrying out procedures for the distribution and installation of changes to systems and services. At this level, you undertake analysis after a release and set the plan for upcoming changes, and you liaise with DevOps and service designers.

The framework names eleven skills at this level: asset and configuration management (maintaining secure configuration and accurate information, controlling IT assets in one or more areas, verifying the location and state of IT assets), availability and capacity management (explaining availability and capacity management processes), change management (analysing and assessing the impact of change, documenting change requests, and actioning changes from change requests), community collaboration (contributing to the work of others, motivating and empowering teams, creating the right environment for teams to work in and identifying the best team makeup, and recognising and dealing with issues), continuity management (explaining the basic principles of IT standard continuity management processes and procedures), incident management (identifying and registering incidents, gathering the required information and allocating it to the appropriate channel), ownership and initiative (showing an awareness of problem resolution processes and passing problems on to your team), problem management (investigating problems in systems, processes and services with an understanding of their level — strategic, tactical or operational — and contributing to remedies and preventative measures), service focus (showing an awareness of different products and services), service management framework knowledge (holding a Level 3 service management framework qualification), and user focus (showing an awareness or understanding of user experience analysis and its principles, and explaining the purpose of user stories and the focus on user needs).

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Asset and configuration management

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a change and release analyst to maintain secure configuration and accurate information, to control IT assets in one or more areas, and to verify the location and state of IT assets.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. The wording is unchanged from the level below, so the baseline stays at Intermediate rather than rising on wording alone.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe what a configuration record is for.
- 1 — Beginner: Updates a configuration record correctly only with someone checking.
- 2 — Intermediate: Maintains accurate configuration and asset records for their own area unprompted, and periodically verifies location and state.
- 3 — Advanced: Proactively catches and corrects configuration discrepancies before a release is affected.
- 4 — Expert: Owns configuration accuracy for the organisation.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe how you check configuration accuracy before rolling out a change.
- Give a short scenario where a configuration record disagrees with reality ahead of a planned release, and ask what you would do.
