# Trainee Business Architect - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This is for you: a chance to rate your own current strengths against the
baseline the UK GDAD Profession Capability Framework sets for this role
level, using the framework's own words for what it covers.

A business architect develops an integrated view of an enterprise, such as a business area or organisation. They use this view to help their organisation put its strategy in place. A trainee business architect supports the more senior business architect roles. They also receive training on the tasks they support so they can work on their own.

Work through the competency matrix below, one skill at a time, then fill in
the overall determination at the end. Treat this as a mirror, not a test —
there's no assessor watching, so being honest with yourself only costs you a
slightly less flattering picture today in exchange for a far more useful one.
Read `## Advisory` before you begin.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating you
give yourself here must never be treated as equivalent to a formal
assessment for a hiring, promotion, pay or performance decision. Use it for
your own reflection, and talk to your manager, a mentor, or your
organisation's people development team about what you find.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you're rating
yourself against what this level actually expects rather than the level
above or below it. Then think back over recent, real examples of your work —
not how you'd like to have handled something, but what you actually did.

**Rate on evidence, not impression.** For every skill, try to recall a
specific moment that supports the rating you give yourself. If you can't
think of one, that's useful information too — it might mean the rating
should be lower, or just that you haven't had the chance to show this skill
yet, which is worth noting separately.

**Be consistent with yourself.** Try to apply the same standard across every
skill, rather than being generous where you feel confident and harsh where
you feel unsure. Coming back to this after a few days, with fresh examples in
mind, often sharpens a rating more than sitting with it in one go.

**Be kind, and be honest.** This only works if you can look at the result
without flinching. A low rating here is not a verdict on you — it's a
starting point. Everyone doing this role, at every level, has skills they are
still building.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

This is a **proficiency** scale, not a frequency scale: it rates how well someone does the thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it.

**Worked example — Business architecture.** Someone rated 0 would show no
awareness of what a business architecture framework is for. Someone rated
1 (the baseline expected at this level) can point to a business architecture
principle or technique they have read about or seen used, and — with
guidance — can describe how it might apply to a change their organisation is
making, even if they could not yet apply it unsupported. Someone rated 2
would already be applying a framework to a live piece of work with only
occasional guidance, which is more than this level expects, and would be
worth noting as a strength.

## About this role level

**Architecture role: Business architect.** A business architect develops an
integrated view of an enterprise, such as a business area or organisation,
and uses that view to help their organisation put its strategy in place.

In this role, a business architect will:

- understand and apply business architectural principles, techniques, and
  tools to business challenges
- ensure design work from projects and programmes aligns with strategy
- ensure business change is implemented effectively to meet strategy
- aggregate and disaggregate complex information from across the organisation
- communicate complex information about the organisation to stakeholders

**Role level: Trainee business architect.** This is the first of four levels
in the business architect role, in order: trainee business architect,
associate business architect, business architect, and lead business
architect. A trainee business architect supports the more senior business
architect roles, and receives training on the tasks they support so they can
work on their own.

At this role level, a trainee business architect will:

- develop their knowledge of the frameworks, practice, and principles of
  business architecture
- work with other business architects to support the production of clear
  business architecture outputs
- support during workshops and meetings with stakeholders in the organisation

The framework names six skills for this role level: Business architecture,
Communicating information, Consultancy, Making and guiding decisions, Methods
and tools, and Strategic thinking.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Business architecture

**What the framework expects at this level**

A trainee business architect shows an awareness of business architecture
frameworks, principles and techniques, and supports the application of
frameworks to business change in the organisation, under guidance.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. This is the level's core skill, but the framework's
own wording — "show an awareness", "support... under guidance" — describes
someone learning the discipline, not yet applying it unsupported.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot describe what a business architecture framework is or why
  an organisation uses one.
- 1 — Beginner: can name a business architecture framework, principle or
  technique, and, with guidance, can describe how it might apply to a piece
  of business change.
- 2 — Intermediate: applies a business architecture technique to a small,
  defined piece of work with occasional guidance, and can explain what it
  produced.
- 3 — Advanced: applies business architecture frameworks to business change
  independently, and adapts the technique to the situation.
- 4 — Expert: develops or extends business architecture practice for the
  organisation and coaches others in applying it.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd look at a simple business capability map or process
  diagram and explain, in your own words, what it shows and why it might be
  useful.
- A structured interview question: "Tell us about a business architecture
  framework or technique you have read about or seen used. What is it for?"
- If already in post, review a piece of business architecture work the person
  contributed to under supervision, and try to talk through your part
  in it.

### Skill: Communicating information

**What the framework expects at this level**

A trainee business architect listens to the needs of design and business
stakeholders and interprets information, and takes part in discussions within
a multidisciplinary team.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. Listening to stakeholders and taking part in team
discussions is expected from day one in this role, even though someone is
new to the discipline — it is a foundation the rest of the role is built on,
so the bar sits a point higher than the more specialist skills below.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: struggles to follow or contribute to a discussion involving
  business or design stakeholders.
- 1 — Beginner: listens attentively but needs prompting to interpret what a
  stakeholder has said or to contribute to a discussion.
- 2 — Intermediate: listens to stakeholders, interprets what they need with
  some accuracy, and takes part in multidisciplinary team discussions without
  prompting.
- 3 — Advanced: interprets stakeholder needs reliably, including where they
  are unclear or conflicting, and contributes useful points to discussions
  unprompted.
- 4 — Expert: shapes how a team communicates with stakeholders and coaches
  others in interpreting stakeholder needs.

**Evidence to gather**

- Reflect on a recent time you in a short discussion or role-play about a business
  problem, and note whether you listen, ask clarifying questions, and
  contribute.
- A structured interview question: "Describe a time you had to work out what
  someone actually needed, when what you said was not fully clear."
- If already in post, ask a colleague or manager who has worked with you in
  meetings for a brief, evidence-based comment on how you listen and
  contribute.

### Skill: Consultancy

**What the framework expects at this level**

A trainee business architect shows an understanding of the need for
consulting in the role and its purpose, works under guidance to identify what
advice, guidance and recommendations might be appropriate — usually by
referring to previous work by others — and shows an understanding of how to
frame problems that need to be solved.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. Every part of the framework's own wording is guided:
"show an understanding", "work under guidance". This is someone learning what
consultancy means in a business architecture context, not yet giving advice
unsupported.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot explain why a business architect gives advice or
  recommendations, or what that advice might be for.
- 1 — Beginner: explains, in general terms, why consultancy matters in the
  role, and, with guidance and reference to previous work, can suggest what
  advice might apply to a simple problem.
- 2 — Intermediate: identifies relevant precedent unprompted and drafts a
  first attempt at advice for a colleague to check.
- 3 — Advanced: gives sound, well-framed advice on business problems with
  minimal guidance.
- 4 — Expert: is sought out for advice on complex or ambiguous business
  problems and mentors others in framing them.

**Evidence to gather**

- Give yourself a short, simple business scenario and try to
  describe how you would go about working out what advice to give, including
  where you would look for precedent.
- A structured interview question: "What do you think a business architect
  is being asked to do when someone says you need consultancy advice?"
- If already in post, review a note or recommendation the person has drafted
  under supervision.

### Skill: Making and guiding decisions

**What the framework expects at this level**

A trainee business architect recommends decisions and describes the reasoning
behind them, and identifies and articulates technical disputes between direct
peers and local stakeholders.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. The framework has this level recommending decisions
rather than making them, which fits someone still building the judgement a
more independent decision requires.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot explain the reasoning behind a recommendation they have
  made, or gives no reasoning at all.
- 1 — Beginner: makes a simple recommendation and explains the reasoning
  behind it when asked, and can point out when two colleagues disagree on a
  straightforward point.
- 2 — Intermediate: makes recommendations with clear, unprompted reasoning,
  and articulates the substance of a dispute between peers accurately.
- 3 — Advanced: makes sound recommendations on moderately complex questions
  without support, and helps peers see each other's position in a dispute.
- 4 — Expert: is trusted to make decisions directly on complex questions and
  helps resolve disputes between others.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd talk through a small decision you made recently — at
  work, in study, or in a comparable setting — and explain your reasoning.
- A structured interview question: "Tell us about a time two people you
  worked with disagreed about something. How did you notice, and what did you
  do?"
- If already in post, look for a documented recommendation, with reasoning,
  the person has written.

### Skill: Methods and tools

**What the framework expects at this level**

A trainee business architect follows advice to apply the most appropriate
tools and techniques to support the planning, analysis, development, testing,
implementation and improvement of systems and services.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. The framework's wording — "follow advice to apply" —
is explicitly guided: someone using a method or tool because a more senior
colleague has told them to, not yet choosing it themselves.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: does not use, or cannot name, any business architecture method or
  tool relevant to the role.
- 1 — Beginner: uses a method or tool correctly once shown how, and follows
  advice on which to apply to a given piece of work.
- 2 — Intermediate: selects a familiar method or tool for a routine task
  without being told, checking with a colleague when unsure.
- 3 — Advanced: selects and applies the right method or tool for most
  situations independently.
- 4 — Expert: builds the organisation's capability in methods and tools, and
  advises others on which to use and why.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe a method, template or tool you have used —
  in this role or a comparable one — and what it was for.
- A structured interview question: "Someone asks you to help map a business
  process. What would you use, and how would you decide?"
- If already in post, look at an output the person has produced using a
  method or tool a more senior colleague pointed you to.

### Skill: Strategic thinking

**What the framework expects at this level**

A trainee business architect explains the strategic context of their work and
why it is important, and supports strategic planning in an administrative
capacity.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. The framework limits this level to explaining
strategic context and supporting strategic planning administratively, not
shaping strategy — appropriate for someone still learning what "strategic"
means in this organisation's terms.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot connect their day-to-day work to the organisation's wider
  strategy or goals.
- 1 — Beginner: explains, when asked, how a piece of work they are doing
  relates to the organisation's strategy, and helps with administrative tasks
  supporting strategic planning.
- 2 — Intermediate: makes the strategic connection unprompted in most cases,
  and takes on a defined piece of strategic planning support without close
  oversight.
- 3 — Advanced: consistently frames their own work, and that of others, in
  terms of strategic goals.
- 4 — Expert: shapes strategic thinking across the organisation and develops
  others' ability to do the same.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe, for a piece of work you know well, how it
  connects to a wider organisational goal.
- A structured interview question: "What do you understand this
  organisation's current strategy to be, and how might your work support
  it?"
- If already in post, review any note, agenda or summary the person has
  produced in support of a strategic planning activity.
