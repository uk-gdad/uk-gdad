# Head of Content Design - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This is for you: a chance to rate your own current strengths against the
baseline the UK GDAD Profession Capability Framework sets for this role
level, using the framework's own words for what it covers.

Content designers make things easier for people to understand and use. This can involve working on a single piece of content or on the end-to-end journey of a service to help users complete their goal and government deliver a policy intent. In this role your work may involve the creation of, or change to, a transaction, product or single piece of content that stretches across digital and offline channels. A head of content design is an expert practitioner with broad industry experience who can define and assure best practice while influencing, leading and mentoring others.

Work through the competency matrix below, one skill at a time, then fill in
the overall determination at the end. Treat this as a mirror, not a test —
there's no assessor watching, so being honest with yourself only costs you a
slightly less flattering picture today in exchange for a far more useful one.
Read `## Advisory` before you begin.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating you
give yourself here must never be treated as equivalent to a formal
assessment for a hiring, promotion, pay or performance decision. Use it for
your own reflection, and talk to your manager, a mentor, or your
organisation's people development team about what you find.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you're rating
yourself against what this level actually expects rather than the level
above or below it. Then think back over recent, real examples of your work —
not how you'd like to have handled something, but what you actually did.

**Rate on evidence, not impression.** For every skill, try to recall a
specific moment that supports the rating you give yourself. If you can't
think of one, that's useful information too — it might mean the rating
should be lower, or just that you haven't had the chance to show this skill
yet, which is worth noting separately.

**Be consistent with yourself.** Try to apply the same standard across every
skill, rather than being generous where you feel confident and harsh where
you feel unsure. Coming back to this after a few days, with fresh examples in
mind, often sharpens a rating more than sitting with it in one go.

**Be kind, and be honest.** This only works if you can look at the result
without flinching. A low rating here is not a verdict on you — it's a
starting point. Everyone doing this role, at every level, has skills they are
still building.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point
proficiency scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

**Worked example**, using User-centred content design, a skill this level
names:

- Someone who takes responsibility for one team's content quality sits
  at **3 — Advanced**, below this assessment's baseline.
- Someone who directs content life cycle management across multiple
  teams, is accountable for the quality of what they produce, and identifies
  capability gaps to grow those teams sits at **4 — Expert** — the baseline
  this assessment sets for this skill.

## About this role level

**The role: content designer.** Content designers make things easier for
people to understand and use. Their work can involve a single piece of
content or the end-to-end journey of a service, helping users complete their
goal and government deliver a policy intent. The role makes sure appropriate
content is shown to a user in the right place and the best format, starting
from discovery and working closely with user researchers, service designers
and interaction designers.

**This level: head of content design.** The framework describes this level
as an expert practitioner with broad industry experience who can define and
assure best practice while influencing, leading and mentoring others. At
this level, the framework expects someone to:

- develop a strategy for content that meets the organisation's objectives
- lead a team capable of executing that strategy
- work with senior stakeholders to influence organisational strategy
- prioritise and collaborate with counterpart colleagues across government
- champion good content design practice within government and industry

**Where this level sits.** Content designer has six levels:

1. Associate content designer
2. Junior content designer
3. Content designer
4. Senior content designer
5. Lead content designer
6. **Head of content design** — assessed here

**A note on skill wording.** Five of this level's six skills — Agile working
(content design), Stakeholder relationship management (content design),
Strategic thinking (content design), User focus (content design) and
User-centred content design — carry published text identical to lead content
designer's, which this assessment treats as a deliberate, honest signal that
the framework considers lead content designer's description of those skills
to already describe the top of the ladder, and it keeps the same baselines
rather than inventing a difference the text does not state.

Content concepts and prototyping is different, and more striking: its
published text at head of content design reverts to the entry-level wording
used at associate and junior content designer — "show an awareness of
prototyping and explain why and when to use it" — rather than building on
content designer, senior content designer or lead content designer's more
advanced descriptions. This assessment sets that skill's baseline from the
framework's actual published text rather than assuming this level's overall
seniority, and says so here rather than smoothing the anomaly away. In
practice, expect a genuine head of content design to substantially exceed
this particular floor.

**The six skills this assessment covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Agile working (content design)
2. Content concepts and prototyping
3. Stakeholder relationship management (content design)
4. Strategic thinking (content design)
5. User focus (content design)
6. User-centred content design

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Agile working (content design)

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects someone to coach and lead teams in Agile and Lean
practices, determining the right approach for the team or the project;
evaluate and review the approach through the life of a project and iterate
and pivot accordingly; think of new and innovative ways of working to
achieve the right outcomes; act as a recognised expert and advocate the
approaches proposed, continuously reflecting on the work of the team and
constructively challenging them to improve processes and delivery; and
regularly assess and review capability within teams and ensure individuals
have the skills needed to deliver.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. This text is identical to lead content designer's, and
coaching, leading and assessing capability across teams matches this
assessment's Expert point directly.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has never coached anyone in Agile or Lean practice.
- 1 — Beginner: Suggests an Agile improvement when asked.
- 2 — Intermediate: Applies an Agile mindset well individually but has not
  led a team's approach.
- 3 — Advanced: Determines the right approach for one project and reviews it
  through its life — one step below this level's baseline.
- 4 — Expert: Coaches and leads more than one team, or a whole discipline,
  in Agile and Lean practice, regularly assessing and closing capability
  gaps.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe how you set the Agile or Lean approach
  across more than one team or the whole content design discipline.
- Think about how you'd describe a capability gap you identified across teams and how
  you closed it.
- Ask a referee, ideally someone senior, what changed organisation-wide
  because of your approach.

### Skill: Content concepts and prototyping

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects someone to show an awareness of prototyping and
explain why and when to use it, and understand how to work in an open and
collaborative environment, for example by pair working.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. As explained in `## About this role level`, this
skill's published text at head of content design matches associate and
junior content designer, not the more advanced text published for every
level in between. This assessment sets the baseline to what the framework
actually states for this skill at this level, while expecting you to
use judgement, and the evidence log, where a real person's practical
prototyping grounding needs to be weighed more heavily than this floor
suggests.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot explain what a content prototype is or why a team might
  make one.
- 1 — Beginner: Explains what prototyping is and gives a plausible reason to
  use it, and has taken part in pair working — this level's published
  baseline.
- 2 — Intermediate: Has helped build a simple prototype as part of a team.
- 3 — Advanced: Builds prototypes across a range of techniques
  independently, establishes content patterns from them, and coaches others
  to do the same — the description published for content designer through
  lead content designer, which many real head of content design people
  will also meet even though this skill's text at this level does not
  require it.
- 4 — Expert: Sets prototyping standards for the whole discipline.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd explain, in your own words, why a team might
  prototype a piece of content before finalising it.
- Ask about your practical experience building or directing prototypes
  across your career, since the published text alone does not establish
  this.
- Look back at a prototype, or a case where you directed a team's
  prototyping approach, if available.

### Skill: Stakeholder relationship management (content design)

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects someone to manage long-term strategic relationships
with stakeholders, identifying where new connections need to be made and
existing ones nurtured; direct the strategic approach for stakeholder
relationships, establishing stakeholder objectives; act as a point of
escalation if stakeholder relationships break down or become challenging for
more junior members of the team; influence important senior stakeholders and
arbitrate when blockers are escalated; and facilitate discussions across
high risk and complex areas or projects under constrained timelines.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. This text is identical to lead content designer's, and
this assessment keeps the same baseline, matching this level's own
accountability to work with senior stakeholders to influence organisational
strategy.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has never been the escalation point for a broken stakeholder
  relationship.
- 1 — Beginner: Manages their own strategic stakeholder relationships
  competently.
- 2 — Intermediate: Occasionally supports a colleague with a difficult
  stakeholder, with guidance.
- 3 — Advanced: Manages long-term strategic relationships independently —
  one step below this level's baseline.
- 4 — Expert: Acts as the organisation's escalation point for stakeholder
  relationships, arbitrates escalated blockers, and directs the strategic
  approach for how the whole discipline manages stakeholders.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe a time you influenced organisational
  strategy through a senior stakeholder relationship.
- Think about how you'd describe the strategic approach you set for how your
  discipline manages stakeholder relationships.
- Ask a referee, ideally a senior stakeholder, how you are regarded
  under pressure.

### Skill: Strategic thinking (content design)

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects someone to help lead the design and implementation
of strategies, evaluating their impact and progress to ensure business
objectives and the needs of users are being met, and to lead and direct
strategic content improvement projects, focusing effort in the areas of
greatest priority and ensuring goals and objectives are met.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. This text is identical to senior and lead content
designer's, and this assessment keeps the same baseline for the skill's own
text rather than inventing a higher one, while noting that this level's own
accountability to develop content strategy for the whole organisation is
the broadest in the role's ladder — worth probing directly in the evidence
log even where this specific skill's baseline has not moved further.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Contributes to strategy only when told exactly what to write.
- 1 — Beginner: Contributes meaningfully to a strategy someone else leads.
- 2 — Intermediate: Leads a small, contained strategic improvement with
  support.
- 3 — Advanced: Helps lead the design and implementation of a strategy that
  spans the organisation, evaluates its impact against business objectives
  and user needs, and directs strategic content improvement projects by
  prioritising where effort matters most.
- 4 — Expert: Owns content strategy for the whole organisation outright and
  is measured directly against its outcomes.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe a strategy for content that you developed
  for an organisation, and how it met the organisation's objectives.
- Think about how you'd describe how you prioritised across competing strategic
  priorities.
- Look back at a strategy document you authored for an organisation, if
  available.

### Skill: User focus (content design)

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects someone to give direction on which tools or
methods are best for teams to use; demonstrate extensive experience in
meeting the needs of users across a variety of channels; understand complex
user journeys and direct solutions to meet different needs within them;
bring insight and expertise in how user needs have changed over time to
ensure these are still relevant and met; and apply strategic thinking to
providing the best service for the user while considering business needs
and the wider cross-government context.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. This text is identical to lead content designer's, and
directing tools, methods and solutions across teams matches this
assessment's Expert point.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe how user needs have changed over time for a
  service they have worked on.
- 1 — Beginner: Meets user needs for one channel with support.
- 2 — Intermediate: Meets user needs across more than one channel, with some
  support directing a solution for a complex journey.
- 3 — Advanced: Meets user needs across a variety of channels with extensive
  experience, but has not yet directed a whole discipline's approach — one
  step below this level's baseline.
- 4 — Expert: Directs the whole discipline's approach to complex user
  journeys, gives direction on tools and methods across teams, and applies
  strategic thinking that balances user needs against organisational and
  cross-government context.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe how you directed an organisation-wide
  approach to meeting complex user needs.
- Think about how you'd describe how you balanced user needs against significant
  organisational or cross-government pressure.
- Ask a referee about your judgement when user needs and business
  needs pulled in different directions.

### Skill: User-centred content design

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects someone to direct the approach to content life
cycle management to ensure content is regularly reviewed and evaluated by
teams; oversee teams' work to ensure the right content is being produced to
meet the needs of users; demonstrate extensive experience in creating,
iterating, managing and overseeing content across multiple channels;
encourage teams to review and evaluate the effectiveness of processes and
systems and support them to iterate for improvements; be accountable for the
production of high quality, user-focused content; and identify gaps in
content design skills and capability, helping teams to grow and develop.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. This text is identical to lead content designer's, and
this assessment keeps the same baseline, matching this level's own
accountability to lead a team capable of executing the organisation's
content strategy.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has never overseen another content designer's output.
- 1 — Beginner: Reviews a colleague's content when asked.
- 2 — Intermediate: Oversees a small piece of a team's work with support.
- 3 — Advanced: Takes responsibility for one team's content quality and
  coaches individuals directly — one step below this level's baseline.
- 4 — Expert: Directs content life cycle management across the
  organisation's teams, is accountable for the quality of what they
  collectively produce, and closes capability gaps at organisational scale.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe how you directed content life cycle
  management across the whole organisation, not just one team.
- Think about how you'd describe a capability gap at organisational scale and what
  you did to close it.
- Ask a referee how content quality changed across the organisation under
  your direction.
