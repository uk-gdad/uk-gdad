# Head of Graphic Design - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This is for you: a chance to rate your own current strengths against the
baseline the UK GDAD Profession Capability Framework sets for this role
level, using the framework's own words for what it covers.

A graphic designer creates graphic elements that underpin interaction and service design. You will use layout, spacing, colour, type and iconography to ensure that content is legible and readable and that users see and understand interactions. A head of graphic design is an expert practitioner with broad industry experience, who can define and assure best practice while influencing, leading and mentoring others.

Work through the competency matrix below, one skill at a time, then fill in
the overall determination at the end. Treat this as a mirror, not a test —
there's no assessor watching, so being honest with yourself only costs you a
slightly less flattering picture today in exchange for a far more useful one.
Read `## Advisory` before you begin.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating you
give yourself here must never be treated as equivalent to a formal
assessment for a hiring, promotion, pay or performance decision. Use it for
your own reflection, and talk to your manager, a mentor, or your
organisation's people development team about what you find.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you're rating
yourself against what this level actually expects rather than the level
above or below it. Then think back over recent, real examples of your work —
not how you'd like to have handled something, but what you actually did.

**Rate on evidence, not impression.** For every skill, try to recall a
specific moment that supports the rating you give yourself. If you can't
think of one, that's useful information too — it might mean the rating
should be lower, or just that you haven't had the chance to show this skill
yet, which is worth noting separately.

**Be consistent with yourself.** Try to apply the same standard across every
skill, rather than being generous where you feel confident and harsh where
you feel unsure. Coming back to this after a few days, with fresh examples in
mind, often sharpens a rating more than sitting with it in one go.

**Be kind, and be honest.** This only works if you can look at the result
without flinching. A low rating here is not a verdict on you — it's a
starting point. Everyone doing this role, at every level, has skills they are
still building.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix below is rated on the same five-point
scale. This is a **proficiency** scale — it rates how well someone does the
thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it or how they feel
about it.

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

**Worked example — Leading design.** The framework expects a head of graphic
design to "set a vision for and lead design across multiple teams or a whole
organisation," "work effectively with senior leaders across your
organisation or wider public sector," and "iterate and measurably improve
design maturity within your organisation and its partners." Someone who
leads and mentors well within a single team, as a lead graphic designer
does, but has not set a vision spanning multiple teams or measurably moved
design maturity, is showing at most **3 — Advanced**. Someone who can
show a design vision they set for a whole organisation, and evidence that
design maturity measurably improved as a result, is showing **4 — Expert**,
the baseline this level expects.

## About this role level

The User-centred design profession's Graphic designer role creates graphic
elements that underpin interaction and service design. Across the role, a
graphic designer uses layout, spacing, colour, type and iconography to ensure
that content is legible and readable, and that users see and understand
interactions.

The role has six levels, in order: Associate graphic designer, Junior graphic
designer, Graphic designer, Senior graphic designer, Lead graphic designer, and
Head of graphic design. This assessment is for the last of them.

A **head of graphic design** is an expert practitioner with broad industry
experience, who can define and assure best practice while influencing,
leading and mentoring others. At this role level, they will influence both
design and organisational strategy and priorities, collaborate with
counterpart colleagues across government, and focus on ensuring the right
conditions and environment for designers to work effectively.

The framework names seven skills for this role level: Design communication,
Designing for everyone, Designing strategically, Designing together,
Evidence-based design, Iterative design, and Leading design.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Design communication

**What the framework expects at this level**

A head of graphic design is expected to gain support for design decisions
and design strategy from senior leaders, communicate design strategy across
organisational boundaries, coach others in how to effectively communicate
design, and develop or introduce more effective methods of communicating
design and working in the open.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. The framework repeats this skill's wording unchanged
from the lead graphic designer level below, so the baseline stays at Expert —
this is already the top of what the framework describes for this skill.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot explain a design decision beyond their immediate team.
- 1 — Beginner: Explains a design decision clearly within their own team.
- 2 — Intermediate: Explains complex design problems to stakeholders outside
  design.
- 3 — Advanced: Builds consensus on a design approach without needing
  support.
- 4 — Expert: Gains support for design strategy from senior leaders,
  communicates it across organisational boundaries, and coaches others in
  effective design communication.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask about a design strategy you got support for from senior
  leaders, and how you made and sustained the case.
- Ask about a communication method or way of working in the open you
  introduced that spread beyond your own organisation.
- Think about how you'd describe how you have coached senior designers in effective
  communication.

### Skill: Designing for everyone

**What the framework expects at this level**

A head of graphic design is expected to measurably improve the practice of
inclusive, accessible and environmentally sustainable design in multiple
teams or across their organisation, and help others understand and mitigate
the influence of privilege and power on the design and delivery of content
and services.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. The framework repeats this skill's wording unchanged
from the lead graphic designer level below, so the baseline stays at Expert.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe how privilege or power might influence a design
  decision.
- 1 — Beginner: Applies accessible design practice to their own work.
- 2 — Intermediate: Advises a single team on inclusive or accessible design.
- 3 — Advanced: Advises multiple teams on inclusive, accessible and
  sustainable design.
- 4 — Expert: Measurably improves inclusive, accessible and sustainable
  design practice across multiple teams or the organisation, and helps
  others understand and mitigate the influence of privilege and power.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for a concrete, measured improvement in inclusive or accessible design
  practice at organisational scale, and how it was measured.
- Ask about a time you helped a leader recognise how privilege or
  power was shaping a decision about content or services.
- Look back at any organisation-wide standard or policy you authored or
  drove.

### Skill: Designing strategically

**What the framework expects at this level**

A head of graphic design is expected to work with others to ensure design
becomes part of the development and implementation of their organisation's
strategy, work with leaders to ensure design is included in policy,
technology and delivery processes early enough to inform effective decision
making, and enable use of patterns and components across the organisation.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. This is a genuinely new, higher tier of wording — full
ownership of design's place in organisational strategy and in policy and
delivery processes, and enabling patterns and components at organisational
scale, going beyond even the lead graphic designer's creation of new
patterns for a team.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot explain how design fits into organisational strategy.
- 1 — Beginner: Aligns their own work to organisational goals, with
  guidance.
- 2 — Intermediate: Helps a single team align to organisational vision.
- 3 — Advanced: Helps multiple teams align to organisational vision and
  creates new design patterns and components.
- 4 — Expert: Ensures design is part of organisational strategy
  development, works with leaders to bring design into policy, technology
  and delivery processes early, and enables the use of patterns and
  components across the organisation.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of design being included in a policy, technology or
  delivery decision early enough to change its direction, and your part in that.
- Ask how you have enabled design patterns and components to be
  used consistently across an organisation, not just one team.
- Ask about a time you contributed directly to organisational strategy.

### Skill: Designing together

**What the framework expects at this level**

A head of graphic design is expected to effectively plan and run design
sessions that include senior leaders or stakeholders, help teams connect and
work across organisational boundaries, coach other designers in how to
involve stakeholders of any level in the design process, and develop or
introduce more effective ways to design together.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Running sessions that include senior leaders, coaching
others on involving stakeholders "of any level," and introducing new ways of
working together is full mastery exercised for the benefit of the whole
organisation, a genuinely new tier of wording above the lead graphic
designer's advisory role.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe how to plan a design session.
- 1 — Beginner: Contributes to a design session someone else runs.
- 2 — Intermediate: Plans and runs their own design sessions.
- 3 — Advanced: Advises other designers on planning and running sessions and
  works across team or profession boundaries.
- 4 — Expert: Plans and runs sessions that include senior leaders, helps
  teams connect and work across organisational boundaries, coaches other
  designers on involving stakeholders of any level, and introduces more
  effective ways to design together.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask about a design session you ran that included senior leaders,
  and how you made it work.
- Ask for an example of helping two teams that did not normally work
  together connect through a design process.
- Ask about a new way of designing together you introduced, and
  how it was adopted.

### Skill: Evidence-based design

**What the framework expects at this level**

A head of graphic design is expected to embed a practice of
hypothesis-driven design in a team, guide the organisation in how to use
evidence to improve services, analyse, synthesise and use evidence to improve
the way the organisation works, and ensure user insights are shared with and
used by the wider organisation and across government.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. The framework repeats this skill's wording unchanged
from the lead graphic designer level below, so the baseline stays at Expert —
already the top of what the framework describes for this skill.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot explain what a piece of research or data evidence shows.
- 1 — Beginner: Explains simple evidence with support.
- 2 — Intermediate: Independently analyses and explains evidence relevant to
  a design.
- 3 — Advanced: Analyses and explains complex evidence and helps a team frame
  and use design hypotheses effectively.
- 4 — Expert: Embeds a hypothesis-driven design practice in a team, guides
  the organisation on using evidence to improve services, and ensures
  insights are shared across the organisation and government.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask about how you have guided a whole organisation, not just a
  team, in using evidence to improve services.
- Ask for an example of a user insight that was shared and used beyond your own organisation, across government.
- Ask how you have used evidence to change the way the organisation itself
  works, not just a single service.

### Skill: Iterative design

**What the framework expects at this level**

A head of graphic design is expected to embed the practice of iterative
design and agile working in teams or the wider organisation, lead on
prototyping complex ideas at any fidelity, help teams adapt quickly to
changes in requirements, priorities or user needs, and iterate delivery
processes or team structures to help teams work effectively.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. The framework repeats this skill's wording unchanged
from the lead graphic designer level below, so the baseline stays at Expert.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe how a design changed across iterations.
- 1 — Beginner: Iterates a simple design with guidance on what to change.
- 2 — Intermediate: Independently iterates straightforward designs across
  rounds of research.
- 3 — Advanced: Helps other designers apply iterative principles and
  iterates complex designs.
- 4 — Expert: Embeds iterative design and agile practice across teams or the
  organisation, leads prototyping of complex ideas at any fidelity, and
  iterates delivery processes or team structures.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask about a delivery process or team structure change you led
  that improved how multiple teams iterate.
- Think about how you'd describe leading the prototyping of a complex, organisation-
  wide idea at a fidelity you judged was right.
- Ask about a time you helped several teams, not just one, adapt quickly
  when requirements changed.

### Skill: Leading design

**What the framework expects at this level**

A head of graphic design is expected to set a vision for and lead design
across multiple teams or a whole organisation, work effectively with senior
leaders across their organisation or the wider public sector, support a
culture of learning and experimentation in their organisation, and iterate
and measurably improve design maturity within their organisation and its
partners.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Setting an organisation-wide vision, working with
senior leaders across the wider public sector, and measurably improving
design maturity is the top rung of this skill in the framework — a genuinely
higher tier of wording than even the lead graphic designer's team-level
leadership.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe a vision for design beyond their own work.
- 1 — Beginner: Leads a piece of design work in a team, with support.
- 2 — Intermediate: Leads design work in a team independently.
- 3 — Advanced: Leads design across a team and mentors other designers,
  advocating for user-centred design with leaders outside digital and data.
- 4 — Expert: Sets a vision for and leads design across multiple teams or a
  whole organisation, works effectively with senior leaders across the wider
  public sector, supports a culture of learning and experimentation, and
  measurably improves design maturity within the organisation and its
  partners.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe a vision you set for design across an
  organisation, and how you got it adopted.
- Ask for evidence that design maturity measurably improved under your
  leadership, and how it was measured.
- Ask about a time you supported a culture of learning and experimentation,
  including how you responded when an experiment failed.
