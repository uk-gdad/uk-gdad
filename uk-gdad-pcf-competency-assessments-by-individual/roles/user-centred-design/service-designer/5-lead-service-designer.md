# Lead Service Designer - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This is for you: a chance to rate your own current strengths against the
baseline the UK GDAD Profession Capability Framework sets for this role
level, using the framework's own words for what it covers.

Service designers design the end-to-end journey of a service. This helps a user complete their goal and government deliver a policy intent. In this role, your work may involve the creation of, or change to, transactions, products and content across both digital and offline channels provided by different parts of government. A lead service designer is an expert practitioner who influences and mentors others.

Work through the competency matrix below, one skill at a time, then fill in
the overall determination at the end. Treat this as a mirror, not a test —
there's no assessor watching, so being honest with yourself only costs you a
slightly less flattering picture today in exchange for a far more useful one.
Read `## Advisory` before you begin.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating you
give yourself here must never be treated as equivalent to a formal
assessment for a hiring, promotion, pay or performance decision. Use it for
your own reflection, and talk to your manager, a mentor, or your
organisation's people development team about what you find.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you're rating
yourself against what this level actually expects rather than the level
above or below it. Then think back over recent, real examples of your work —
not how you'd like to have handled something, but what you actually did.

**Rate on evidence, not impression.** For every skill, try to recall a
specific moment that supports the rating you give yourself. If you can't
think of one, that's useful information too — it might mean the rating
should be lower, or just that you haven't had the chance to show this skill
yet, which is worth noting separately.

**Be consistent with yourself.** Try to apply the same standard across every
skill, rather than being generous where you feel confident and harsh where
you feel unsure. Coming back to this after a few days, with fresh examples in
mind, often sharpens a rating more than sitting with it in one go.

**Be kind, and be honest.** This only works if you can look at the result
without flinching. A low rating here is not a verdict on you — it's a
starting point. Everyone doing this role, at every level, has skills they are
still building.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point proficiency scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

This rates how well someone does the thing, on the evidence available — not how often
they do it. A worked example, using this level's skill of Leading design:

- **3 — Advanced**: they led and coordinated design work in their own team without
  needing support, and mentored one other designer informally.
- **4 — Expert**: they created an inclusive, productive working environment across a
  team of designers, formally mentored and coached several colleagues, advocated for
  user-centred design directly to leaders outside digital and data, and measurably
  improved the team's design process as a result.

A lead service designer's baseline for this skill, below, is 4.

## About this role level

**The role: service designer.** A service designer designs the end-to-end journey of a
service. This helps a user complete their goal and government deliver a policy intent.
The work may involve creating, or changing, transactions, products and content across
both digital and offline channels provided by different parts of government.

**The level: lead service designer.** The framework describes this level as an expert
practitioner who influences and mentors others. It is the fifth of six levels the
framework publishes for this role, sitting above senior service designer and below head
of service design at the top of the ladder — the full ladder, in order, is associate
service designer, junior service designer, service designer, senior service designer,
lead service designer, and head of service design. At this level, you work with service
managers and programme directors to develop design concepts; set direction and assure
the quality of design delivery across teams; and lead multiple or highly complex
services.

**The skills this level names**, in the summary's order: Design communication;
Designing for everyone; Designing strategically; Designing together; Evidence-based
design; Iterative design; and Leading design. One of these, Designing strategically,
carries text identical to the senior service designer level below; the other six
skills' text is distinct to this level, and Leading design in particular drops the
"with support" qualifier that limited it at senior service designer.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Design communication

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a lead service designer to gain support for design decisions and
design strategy from senior leaders; communicate design strategy across organisational
boundaries; coach others in how to effectively communicate design; and develop or
introduce more effective methods of communicating design and working in the open.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Coaching others in how to communicate design, and developing new
methods of communicating rather than only applying existing ones, is
teach-and-guide-others, mastery-level work.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not gained support for a design decision from a senior leader.
- 1 — Beginner: has presented a design decision to a senior leader with someone else
  leading the conversation.
- 2 — Intermediate: has gained a senior leader's support for one design decision, with
  guidance preparing the case.
- 3 — Advanced: independently gains senior leader support for design decisions and
  communicates design strategy across an organisational boundary.
- 4 — Expert: coaches other designers in how to communicate design effectively, and has
  introduced a new method of communicating design or working in the open that others
  adopted.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Tell me about coaching another designer in how to
  communicate a design decision more effectively."
- Ask for an example of gaining a senior leader's support for a design strategy, and
  what made the case land.
- Ask for a new method of communicating design, or working in the open, that you
  introduced and that others went on to use.

### Skill: Designing for everyone

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a lead service designer to measurably improve the practice of
inclusive, accessible and environmentally sustainable design in multiple teams or
across the organisation; and help others understand and mitigate the influence of
privilege and power on the design and delivery of content and services.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Measurably improving practice across multiple teams or the whole
organisation is organisation-facing, mastery-level ownership, well beyond advising one
team.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not improved inclusive design practice beyond their own work.
- 1 — Beginner: has raised an accessibility issue in their own team.
- 2 — Intermediate: has helped one team improve one aspect of inclusive design
  practice, with support.
- 3 — Advanced: independently advises multiple teams on inclusive, accessible and
  sustainable design.
- 4 — Expert: has measurably improved inclusive, accessible or sustainable design
  practice across multiple teams or the organisation, and has helped others understand
  and mitigate how privilege and power shape design and delivery.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Tell me about a measurable improvement you made to
  inclusive or accessible design practice across more than one team."
- Ask for an example of helping a colleague understand how privilege or power was
  shaping a design decision, and what changed.
- Ask a referee about your influence on accessibility or inclusion practice
  beyond your immediate team.

### Skill: Designing strategically

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a lead service designer to help a team understand how user-centred
design helps it meet its goals; help teams align their work to the goals and vision of
the organisation; use risks, opportunities and constraints in technology, systems and
policy to shape design; and identify and create new design patterns and components.
This wording is identical to the senior service designer level below.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Because the framework repeats the senior level's text word for
word here, the honest baseline for this specific skill stays the same as the level
below — this level's real step up shows in the other six skills and in this level's own
accountability to set direction and assure design delivery across teams, not in a claim
this text does not make.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not connected a design decision to an organisational goal.
- 1 — Beginner: has followed a strategic direction someone else set.
- 2 — Intermediate: has helped align one piece of design work to a team's goals, with
  guidance.
- 3 — Advanced: independently helps teams understand how their design work supports
  organisational goals, weighs technology, systems and policy constraints to shape
  design, and creates a new design pattern or component when an existing one does not
  fit.
- 4 — Expert: shapes design strategy across multiple teams or the whole organisation.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Tell me about helping more than one team see how
  your design work connects to organisational goals."
- Ask for a design pattern or component you created that other teams went on to
  reuse.
- Look back at a design strategy document you contributed to, if available.

### Skill: Designing together

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a lead service designer to effectively plan and run design sessions
that include senior leaders or stakeholders; help teams connect and work across
organisational boundaries; coach other designers in how to involve stakeholders of any
level in the design process; and develop or introduce more effective ways to design
together.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Running sessions that include senior leaders, and coaching other
designers in stakeholder involvement, is teach-and-guide-others, mastery-level
facilitation.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not run a design session that included a senior leader.
- 1 — Beginner: has attended a design session with senior leaders present, without
  running any part of it.
- 2 — Intermediate: has co-run a design session with senior leaders present, with
  support.
- 3 — Advanced: independently plans and runs design sessions that include senior
  leaders or stakeholders, and helps teams connect across organisational boundaries.
- 4 — Expert: coaches other designers in how to involve stakeholders of any level in
  the design process, and has introduced a new way of designing together that other
  teams adopted.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Tell me about planning and running a design
  session that included senior leaders. What made it work?"
- Ask for an example of coaching another designer on involving a difficult
  stakeholder group.
- Ask for a new way of designing together that you introduced and that spread beyond
  your own team.

### Skill: Evidence-based design

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a lead service designer to embed a practice of hypothesis-driven
design in a team; guide the organisation in how to use evidence to improve services;
analyse, synthesise and use evidence to improve the way the organisation works; and
ensure user insights are shared with and used by the wider organisation and across
government.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Guiding the organisation, and ensuring insights are shared and
used across government, is organisation-facing, mastery-level ownership of an
evidence practice.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not embedded a hypothesis-driven design practice in a team.
- 1 — Beginner: has used a design hypothesis in their own work.
- 2 — Intermediate: has helped one team adopt hypothesis-driven design, with support.
- 3 — Advanced: independently embeds hypothesis-driven design in a team, and uses
  evidence to improve how a team works.
- 4 — Expert: guides the wider organisation in how to use evidence to improve services,
  and ensures user insights are shared with and used by the wider organisation and
  across government.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Tell me about embedding a hypothesis-driven way of
  working in a team that did not have one before."
- Ask for an example of user insight you ensured was shared beyond the team that
  gathered it.
- Ask a referee whether you have guided the wider organisation on how to use
  evidence.

### Skill: Iterative design

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a lead service designer to embed the practice of iterative design
and agile working in teams or the wider organisation; lead on prototyping complex ideas
at any fidelity; help teams adapt quickly to changes in requirements, priorities or user
needs; and iterate delivery processes or team structures to help teams work
effectively.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Embedding a practice across teams or the organisation, and
iterating team structures and not just designs, is organisation-facing, mastery-level
ownership.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not embedded an iterative design practice beyond their own work.
- 1 — Beginner: has followed an iterative design practice someone else set up.
- 2 — Intermediate: has helped one team adopt iterative practice, with support.
- 3 — Advanced: independently leads on prototyping complex ideas at any fidelity, and
  helps teams adapt quickly to changing requirements.
- 4 — Expert: embeds iterative design and agile working across teams or the wider
  organisation, and has iterated a delivery process or team structure to make teams
  work more effectively.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Tell me about embedding an iterative or agile
  practice across more than one team."
- Ask for an example of changing a delivery process or team structure to improve how
  teams worked, and what improved as a result.
- Ask for an example of leading on a prototype at an unusually high or low fidelity,
  and why you chose it.

### Skill: Leading design

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a lead service designer to lead and coordinate design work in a
team; advocate for user-centred design with leaders outside of digital and data;
create an inclusive, productive environment for designers to work in; mentor and coach
other designers; and iterate and improve design processes within their team.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Unlike the senior service designer level below, where leading
design work is done "with support", this level leads and coordinates design work
unsupported, mentors and coaches other designers formally, and advocates for
user-centred design directly to leaders outside digital and data — a clear step up in
this specific skill.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not coordinated design work across a team without support.
- 1 — Beginner: has coordinated design work with close support from a more senior
  colleague.
- 2 — Intermediate: has led design work in a team with occasional support, and
  informally supported one other designer.
- 3 — Advanced: independently leads and coordinates design work in a team, and
  mentors other designers.
- 4 — Expert: creates an inclusive, productive environment for designers, formally
  mentors and coaches several colleagues, advocates for user-centred design directly
  to leaders outside digital and data, and has measurably improved the team's design
  process.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Tell me about advocating for user-centred design
  to a leader outside digital and data. What was the outcome?"
- Ask for an example of mentoring or coaching another designer, and what changed for
  you.
- Ask a referee about the working environment you created for the
  designers around you.
