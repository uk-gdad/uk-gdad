# Principal Data Scientist - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This is for you: a chance to rate your own current strengths against the
baseline the UK GDAD Profession Capability Framework sets for this role
level, using the framework's own words for what it covers.

Data science is a broad and fast-moving field spanning maths, statistics, software engineering and communications. Data scientists will often work as part of a multidisciplinary team, using data and analytics to inform and achieve organisational goals. A principal data scientist is a leader of data science, quite often with responsibility for managing and developing teams.

Work through the competency matrix below, one skill at a time, then fill in
the overall determination at the end. Treat this as a mirror, not a test —
there's no assessor watching, so being honest with yourself only costs you a
slightly less flattering picture today in exchange for a far more useful one.
Read `## Advisory` before you begin.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating you
give yourself here must never be treated as equivalent to a formal
assessment for a hiring, promotion, pay or performance decision. Use it for
your own reflection, and talk to your manager, a mentor, or your
organisation's people development team about what you find.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you're rating
yourself against what this level actually expects rather than the level
above or below it. Then think back over recent, real examples of your work —
not how you'd like to have handled something, but what you actually did.

**Rate on evidence, not impression.** For every skill, try to recall a
specific moment that supports the rating you give yourself. If you can't
think of one, that's useful information too — it might mean the rating
should be lower, or just that you haven't had the chance to show this skill
yet, which is worth noting separately.

**Be consistent with yourself.** Try to apply the same standard across every
skill, rather than being generous where you feel confident and harsh where
you feel unsure. Coming back to this after a few days, with fresh examples in
mind, often sharpens a rating more than sitting with it in one go.

**Be kind, and be honest.** This only works if you can look at the result
without flinching. A low rating here is not a verdict on you — it's a
starting point. Everyone doing this role, at every level, has skills they are
still building.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix below is rated on the same five-point
scale. This is a **proficiency** scale — it rates how well someone does the
thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it or how they feel
about it.

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

**Worked example — Data science innovation.** The framework expects a
principal data scientist to "be a leader in the data science space" and to
"act as a coach, inspiring curiosity and creativity in others". Someone
who can describe current data science techniques accurately, but has never
applied one to solve a novel organisational problem, is showing evidence
towards **2 — Intermediate**, below this level's baseline. Someone who has
led the creative application of a technique to solve a genuinely new problem,
independently and without a template to follow, is showing **3 — Advanced** —
close to this level's baseline. Someone who, beyond that, has actively
coached other data scientists to build their own creative problem-solving, and
is sought out across the organisation for a view on where the field is
heading, is showing **4 — Expert**, the baseline this skill sets at this
level.

## About this role level

The Data profession's Data scientist role explores and applies statistical
tools and techniques — including machine learning and predictive analytics —
to existing and new data, finding patterns and turning them into
organisational insight. Across the role, a data scientist is inquisitive;
explores and visualises data; makes recommendations that address complex
problems and inform strategic and operational decision-making; uses data
ethically and appropriately; is innovative and adaptable; and works, often as
part of a multidisciplinary team, to use data and analytics in support of
organisational goals.

The role has six levels, in order: Trainee data scientist, Associate data
scientist, Data scientist, Principal data scientist, Lead data scientist, and
Head of data science. This assessment is for the fourth of them.

A **principal data scientist** is a leader of data science, quite often with
responsibility for managing and developing teams. At this role level, they
will have a broad knowledge of data science techniques, use cases and
potential impact, as well as the tools and technologies involved; have
extensive experience in scoping, designing and delivering data science outputs
and products; work collaboratively with a range of experts in support of
organisational objectives; communicate effectively and challenge delivery
plans and priorities; appreciate and understand data ethics, data preparation
and manipulation; and appreciate and understand delivery methods, and how to
deliver supported solutions at scale.

The framework names eight skills for this role level: Applied maths,
statistics and scientific practices; Data engineering; Data ethics and
privacy; Data science innovation; Delivering business impact; Developing data
science capability; Programming and build (data science); and Understanding
product delivery.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Applied maths, statistics and scientific practices

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal data scientist is expected to identify opportunities to develop
statistical insight, reports and models that support organisational
objectives while collaborating effectively across the organisation; to
critique statistical analyses; to use a variety of data analytics techniques,
such as data mining and prescriptive and predictive analytics, for complex
analysis through the whole data life cycle; to use model outputs to produce
evidence and help design services and policies; and to understand a broad
range of statistical tools, particularly those deployed within the
organisation, well enough to use them appropriately and help others use them.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Critiquing statistical analyses others have produced,
and using model outputs to shape services and policies, is independent,
judgement-driven work; "help others to use them" adds a coaching element
without yet making the whole skill about teaching, so Advanced fits better
than Expert.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot use a statistical technique to analyse a real data set
  without step-by-step direction.
- 1 — Beginner: Applies a statistical technique to a well-specified problem
  with close supervision, and needs help interpreting the output.
- 2 — Intermediate: Runs standard statistical analyses independently, but
  needs support to critique someone else's analysis or to choose between
  competing techniques.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently critiques a colleague's statistical analysis,
  selects an appropriate technique from a broad toolkit for a complex,
  whole-life-cycle problem, and uses the output to shape a service or policy
  recommendation.
- 4 — Expert: Sets the organisation's standards for statistical practice and
  is someone others bring their hardest analytical judgement calls to.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd critique a piece of statistical analysis (a report or
  model output) that is not your own, and explain what you would change.
- Think about how you'd describe a time you chose between two or more analytical
  techniques for the same problem, and why.
- Ask a referee about a time your statistical judgement changed the
  direction of a service or policy decision.

### Skill: Data engineering

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal data scientist is expected to help identify the data engineering
requirements for a data science product, working with data engineers and
other data scientists to design and deliver those products effectively; to
understand the need to cleanse and prepare data before it enters a data
science product, and to put reusable processes and checks in place; and to
understand a broad range of architectures, including cloud and on-premise, and
the data manipulation and transformation tools deployed within the
organisation, well enough to use them appropriately and help others do so.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Putting reusable processes and checks in place, and
working across cloud and on-premise architectures, is independent technical
leadership on the data engineering side of a product, even though the role
itself is not a data engineering specialism.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe what data preparation a data science product would
  need before it could reliably be built.
- 1 — Beginner: Identifies an obvious data quality issue when it is pointed
  out, but needs help specifying what engineering work is required.
- 2 — Intermediate: Works with a data engineer to specify requirements for a
  straightforward product, with review support.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently identifies the data engineering requirements for
  a complex product, sets up reusable cleansing and quality checks, and works
  confidently across more than one architecture the organisation uses.
- 4 — Expert: Is the reference point across the organisation for how data
  engineering and data science work should fit together.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe the data engineering requirements you
  identified for a specific data science product, and how you worked with
  data engineers to deliver you.
- Ask about a reusable data quality check or process you put in place, and
  what problem it prevented.
- Ask which architectures — cloud, on-premise, or both — you have worked
  across, and for an example of choosing between you.

### Skill: Data ethics and privacy

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal data scientist is expected to work with stakeholders to identify
and address ethical and privacy concerns; to demonstrate and communicate how
data ethics issues fit into the wider organisational context; to research
developments in data ethics and privacy to improve compliance and processes;
and to assess and constructively challenge proposed data ethics policies.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. "Assess and constructively challenge proposed data
ethics policies" is independent, critical engagement with organisational
policy, not just following it — a clear step beyond applying existing
guidance, without yet being someone who sets that policy.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot identify an ethical or privacy concern in a data science
  product without it being pointed out.
- 1 — Beginner: Recognises a flagged ethical or privacy concern and follows
  existing guidance to address it, with support.
- 2 — Intermediate: Identifies ethical and privacy concerns in their own work
  and raises them with stakeholders appropriately.
- 3 — Advanced: Constructively challenges a proposed data ethics policy with a
  reasoned, evidence-based case, and explains how an ethics issue connects to
  the organisation's wider context to stakeholders who are not specialists.
- 4 — Expert: Shapes the organisation's data ethics policy and is sought out
  to resolve its hardest ethical judgement calls.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe a time you raised or addressed an ethical or
  privacy concern with stakeholders, and how the conversation went.
- Think about how you'd critique a short, given data ethics policy statement and say
  what you would challenge or change, and why.
- Ask a referee about a time your ethical judgement changed the
  direction of a project.

### Skill: Data science innovation

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal data scientist is expected to be a leader in the data science
space; to demonstrate in-depth knowledge of data science tools and
techniques, using them to solve problems creatively and to create
opportunities for their team; to act as a coach, inspiring curiosity and
creativity in others; to demonstrate in-depth knowledge of the data science
profession and keep up to date with industry change; and to challenge the
status quo, always looking for ways to improve data science.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. The framework's own wording names coaching explicitly —
"act as a coach, inspiring curiosity and creativity in others" — which matches
this scale's definition of Expert directly: full mastery combined with
teaching and guiding others, not just applying the skill independently.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Applies a data science technique only in the way it was originally
  taught, with no evidence of adapting it creatively.
- 1 — Beginner: Recognises when an established technique might be adapted for
  a new problem, with support in working out how.
- 2 — Intermediate: Adapts a known technique to a moderately novel problem
  independently, but has not yet led others through the same kind of thinking.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently and creatively solves a genuinely new problem
  with no established template, and keeps demonstrably current with
  developments in the field.
- 4 — Expert: Actively coaches other data scientists to think more creatively
  and curiously, and is recognised, inside or outside the team, as someone who
  challenges and improves how the organisation does data science.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe a genuinely novel problem you solved
  creatively, with no established technique to follow, and how you arrived
  at your approach.
- Think about how you'd describe a time you coached or inspired curiosity in a
  colleague — what you did, and what changed for that colleague afterwards.
- Ask a referee whether you are someone others go to when a data
  science approach needs rethinking, and for an example.

### Skill: Delivering business impact

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal data scientist is expected to lead and support their
organisational area by using data science to create change; to identify
opportunities to develop data science products that support organisational
objectives, while collaborating across the organisation to fulfil goals; to
show an understanding of the role of user research, and to design and manage
processes for gathering and establishing user needs; to communicate relevant
and compelling stories effectively, presenting analysis and data
visualisations clearly to convey complex messages; and to work with
colleagues to implement scalable data science products and understand their
maintenance requirements.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Leading organisational change through data science,
and designing and managing the process for establishing user needs rather
than simply contributing to one, is independent, accountable delivery
leadership — a strong grasp working independently, without yet being the
level's coaching-defined skills such as Data science innovation.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot connect a data science product to a specific organisational
  objective it is meant to support.
- 1 — Beginner: Contributes to a data science product with a clear business
  purpose, under someone else's direction.
- 2 — Intermediate: Identifies an opportunity to apply data science to an
  organisational objective and delivers a bounded piece of it, with review
  support.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently leads a data science initiative from identifying
  the opportunity through to a scalable, maintained product, designing the
  process for establishing user needs and presenting the resulting story
  clearly to non-specialist stakeholders.
- 4 — Expert: Is someone others in the organisation come to when they need
  data science to change how an entire organisational area operates.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd walk through a data science product you led from
  identifying the opportunity to delivery, including how you established
  user needs.
- Look back at a data visualisation or presentation you produced for a
  non-specialist audience, if one is available, or ask you to talk through
  one.
- Ask a referee about the organisational impact of a product you
  led, and how it was maintained afterwards.

### Skill: Developing data science capability

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal data scientist is expected to manage their own continuous
professional development and link their learning to objectives and
organisational goals; to support data science capability building across the
team and the wider organisation; to talk confidently about the benefits of
data science approaches to existing and potential customers; and to
demonstrate a good understanding of a range of data science techniques, such
as machine learning and natural language processing, using them to build
solutions including reports, models and dashboards.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. "Support... capability building across the team and
wider organisation" is real contribution to others' development, but the
framework's own wording at this level stops short of setting the direction for
that development the way the level above does — so Advanced, working
independently and supporting others, rather than Expert.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has no active plan for their own development and has not helped
  anyone else develop their data science skills.
- 1 — Beginner: Follows a development plan set by someone else, and has
  occasionally helped a colleague informally.
- 2 — Intermediate: Manages their own CPD, linking it to team or
  organisational goals, and helps a colleague develop a specific skill when
  asked.
- 3 — Advanced: Actively supports capability building across the team and the
  wider organisation, and confidently and credibly explains the benefits of
  data science approaches to people outside the team, including potential
  customers.
- 4 — Expert: Sets the direction for data science capability building across
  the organisation, not just supporting it.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe how you have supported another data
  scientist's development or your team's wider capability, with a specific
  example.
- Think about how you'd explain, as if to a potential customer, why a data science
  approach would help with a described problem.
- Ask about a specific technique, such as machine learning or natural language
  processing, you have used to build a solution — a report, model, or
  dashboard — and what it achieved.

### Skill: Programming and build (data science)

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal data scientist is expected to write complex programs and scripts;
to seek to make code open source where appropriate; to supervise junior
analysts and set coding standards for their team; to understand software
architecture and how to write efficient, optimised code; and to perform user
testing on products prior to launch.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "Supervise junior analysts and set coding standards for
your team" is, in this scale's own terms, teaching and guiding others directly
— combined with writing complex, optimised programs independently, this is
full mastery applied to setting the standard others follow, not just
independent competence.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot write a program of any complexity without significant
  support.
- 1 — Beginner: Writes and tests simple scripts under direction, and follows
  coding standards someone else has set.
- 2 — Intermediate: Writes moderately complex programs independently, but
  needs support to judge software architecture trade-offs or to review
  others' code.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently writes complex, efficient, optimised programs
  and reviews a colleague's code against existing standards.
- 4 — Expert: Sets the coding standards their team works to, supervises junior
  analysts' code and practice, and makes considered software architecture
  decisions that others rely on.

**Evidence to gather**

- Look back at a code sample you wrote or led, and try to explain a
  software architecture or efficiency decision you made in it.
- Ask about the coding standards you have set or maintained for a team, and
  how you introduced you.
- Ask a referee, or a junior colleague if appropriate, about your
  experience supervising and reviewing others' code.
- Ask how you have approached user testing a product before launch.

### Skill: Understanding product delivery

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal data scientist is expected to understand the differences between
delivery methods, such as Agile and waterfall, and to choose the most
appropriate method to deliver each product; to define the minimum viable
product (MVP) and support decisions about priorities; and to work with
specialists in multidisciplinary teams to smoothly deliver data science
products into the organisation.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Choosing a delivery method for a product, and defining
its MVP, is independent product judgement exercised across a multidisciplinary
team — a strong, working-independently grasp of delivery, without the
framework naming any coaching or standard-setting element for this specific
skill at this level.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot explain why one delivery method might suit a product better
  than another.
- 1 — Beginner: Follows a delivery method chosen by someone else, with
  guidance on how it applies to their work.
- 2 — Intermediate: Contributes to defining an MVP and delivery priorities for
  a product, with review support.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently chooses the most appropriate delivery method for
  a given product, defines its MVP, and works effectively with specialists
  across a multidisciplinary team to deliver it smoothly.
- 4 — Expert: Sets how the organisation chooses delivery methods for data
  science products more broadly, beyond any one product.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe a product where you chose between delivery
  methods, such as Agile and waterfall, and why.
- Think about how you'd describe how you defined an MVP for a data science product and
  the priority decisions that followed from it.
- Ask a referee, ideally from a different specialism, about your
  effectiveness working within a multidisciplinary delivery team.
