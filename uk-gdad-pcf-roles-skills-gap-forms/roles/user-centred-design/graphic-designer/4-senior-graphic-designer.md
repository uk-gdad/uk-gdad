# Senior graphic designer - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a senior graphic designer in the UK Government Digital and
Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out what you do
now, where the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this level as a designer who works with minimal support and can influence
and mentor others. It expects you to work with service managers and programme directors to
develop design concepts; potentially have responsibility across complex services; help set
direction and embed good practice within teams; and make important decisions based on research,
understanding how that research impacts others.

*Understand how this research impacts others* is a quiet but important phrase. Research findings
rarely affect only your design — they often mean a policy assumption was wrong, or that another
team's work needs to change. Carrying that well is part of being senior.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Seven skills, each with an explanation, a rating and
some questions. Expect about 30 minutes.

You can fill this form in on screen: the boxes below take typing, the tick
boxes tick, and each skill has a rating to choose. Your answers are saved in
this browser, on this device, so you can close the page and come back to them.
Nothing is sent anywhere.

When you have finished, use Export TSV or Export JSON at the top of the
page to take your answers with you, or print the page, or copy them into
whatever your organisation uses for development conversations. Every question
is numbered, so answers travel back on their own. Clear answers empties the
form and deletes the saved copy, which is worth doing on a computer other
people use.

Worth knowing:

- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a finding.
- **"The team does this" is not an answer to "what do you do".** Name your part.
- **"I haven't had the chance" is a real answer**, and more useful than a generous reading of a
  thin example.
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers, what they are used for, or
  how long they are kept, ask before you fill it in.

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not the remit on your job description.
- **A 4 is a claim about other people** — that someone else's practice changed because of you and

## About this role level

**The role: graphic designer.** A graphic designer creates the graphic elements that underpin
interaction and service design. You use layout, spacing, colour, type and iconography to ensure
that content is legible and readable and that users see and understand interactions.

**Your level: senior graphic designer.** You work with minimal support and can influence and
mentor others. The framework expects that you:

- work with service managers and programme directors to develop design concepts
- potentially have responsibility across complex services
- help set direction and embed good practice within teams
- make important decisions based on research and understand how this research impacts others

**Where this level sits.** This role has six levels:

1. Associate graphic designer
2. Junior graphic designer
3. Graphic designer
4. **Senior graphic designer** — you are here
5. Lead graphic designer
6. Head of graphic design

Your next step is lead graphic designer: an expert practitioner who influences and mentors
others, sets direction and assures quality of design delivery across teams, and leads multiple or
highly complex services.

**One thing worth knowing about this role.** **Your published wording for *Leading design* is
identical to the graphic designer's below you** — including "lead and co-ordinate design work in
your team, **with support**". Everything else about your level describes working with minimal
support, influencing and mentoring. That is how the framework publishes it, and it means leading
is the one skill where your level does not show progression at all.

Two other skills — designing strategically and designing together — are worded identically at
lead graphic designer, so they will not distinguish you at the next step either.

**The seven skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Design communication
2. Designing for everyone
3. Designing strategically
4. Designing together
5. Evidence-based design
6. Iterative design
7. Leading design

## Job skills

Seven skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the level expects, what it looks
like in practice, and what changes at lead graphic designer.

### Skill: Design communication

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: clearly explain complex problems and
ideas to teams or stakeholders outside of design; **clearly document and communicate design
decisions, related risks and any unresolved issues**; and build consensus around a design
approach, for example by asking difficult questions and challenging assumptions.

**What that looks like here.** Documenting unresolved issues is the item people skip. A design
that shipped with a known problem, recorded honestly, is far better than one where the compromise
was silently forgotten and rediscovered a year later.

**The next step.** At lead you gain support for design decisions and strategy from senior
leaders, communicate design strategy across organisational boundaries, coach others in
communicating design, and introduce more effective methods of communicating and working in the
open.

### Skill: Designing for everyone

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: advise teams on how to design
inclusive, accessible and environmentally sustainable content or services; design and deliver
ethical content or services that consider the personal and social context of users; and ensure a
design meets appropriate standards, for example accessibility regulations.

**What that looks like here.** The *with support* qualifier has gone — meeting accessibility
standards is now yours to ensure. And *personal and social context* asks for something beyond
compliance: a form that demands documents someone may not have, or a tone that assumes a
situation they are not in, meets every guideline and still fails them.

**The next step.** At lead you measurably improve inclusive, accessible and sustainable design
practice across multiple teams or your organisation, and help others understand and mitigate the
influence of privilege and power on design.

### Skill: Designing strategically

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: help a team understand how
user-centred design helps it meet its goals; help teams align their work to the goals and vision
of their organisation; use risks, opportunities and constraints in technology, systems and policy
to shape design; and **identify and create new design patterns and components**.

**Same wording at lead graphic designer.**

**What that looks like here.** Creating a new pattern is a commitment — somebody has to maintain
it, and every team that adopts it inherits your decisions. The discipline is creating few and
maintaining them properly.

### Skill: Designing together

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: advise others how to effectively plan
and run design sessions with a team, users or stakeholders; adapt a design session to ensure you
achieve a useful outcome; effectively involve the right people throughout the design process; and
**work across team or profession boundaries, for example with policy teams**.

**Same wording at lead graphic designer.**

**What that looks like here.** Working with policy teams is the demanding item. Policy colleagues
often arrive with a decision already made and a request to present it well; turning that into a
conversation about what people can actually do is where design earns its place.

### Skill: Evidence-based design

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: analyse, synthesise and clearly
explain complex evidence relevant to users or a service, for example large data sets; help your
team use design hypotheses effectively; and use complex research and data to develop and test
design ideas.

**What that looks like here.** Helping the team use hypotheses means the whole team frames
questions testably, not just you. That changes disagreements about design into questions with
answers.

**The next step.** At lead you embed hypothesis-driven design in a team, guide the organisation
in using evidence to improve services, use evidence to improve how the organisation works, and
ensure user insights are shared across government.

### Skill: Iterative design

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: help other designers apply iterative
design principles and agile methodologies to their work; iterate and improve complex designs
based on successive rounds of research; **independently prototype complex ideas at an appropriate
fidelity**; and adapt designs quickly to changes in requirements, priorities or user needs.

**What that looks like here.** *Independently* and *complex* are both new. Prototyping something
complicated without help — and choosing the fidelity that answers the actual question — is the
technical core of this level.

**The next step.** At lead you embed iterative design and agile working in teams or the wider
organisation, lead on prototyping complex ideas at any fidelity, help teams adapt to change, and
iterate delivery processes or team structures.

### Skill: Leading design

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: lead and co-ordinate design work in
your team, **with support**; communicate the value of user-centred design to your team; support
other designers; and work with digital and data leaders in your organisation, when needed.

**Identical wording to graphic designer**, the level below — including the *with support*
qualifier, and despite your level description saying you work with minimal support and mentor
others. That is how the framework publishes it.

**What that looks like here.** In practice most senior graphic designers lead without support and
mentor deliberately. If you do, you are beyond this wording, and it is worth saying so.

**The next step.** At lead you lead and co-ordinate design work in a team without the qualifier,
advocate for user-centred design with leaders outside digital and data, create an inclusive and
productive environment for designers, mentor and coach others, and iterate design processes.
