# Junior user researcher - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a junior user researcher in the UK Government Digital and Data
profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out what you do now,
where the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this level as embedded in a multidisciplinary team to carry out user research
activities. It expects you to have some practical experience but need regular guidance and training to
produce your best work and develop your skills, and to work in combination with a more senior user
researcher.

**Regular guidance is still written into your level**, which is worth holding your organisation to.
Junior researchers are often the only researcher on a team, which is a difficult position: the team
treats you as the expert while the framework says you should still be paired with someone more
experienced.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Seven skills, each with an explanation, a rating and some
questions. Expect about 25 minutes.

## Advisory

This skills gap form is created by AI and is work in progress for educational purposes. If you intend
to use it in any way, please consult with your organization's appropriate teammates, such as with
human resources staff, or people and organizational development staff, or legal compliance staff.

## How to fill in this form

You can fill this form in on screen: the boxes below take typing, the tick
boxes tick, and each skill has a rating to choose. Your answers are saved in
this browser, on this device, so you can close the page and come back to them.
Nothing is sent anywhere.

When you have finished, use Export TSV or Export as JSON at the top of the
page to take your answers with you, or print the page, or copy them into
whatever your organisation uses for development conversations. Every question
is numbered, so answers travel back on their own. Clear answers empties the
form and deletes the saved copy, which is worth doing on a computer other
people use.

Worth knowing:

- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a finding.
- **Answer about the job you do**, not the one on the job description.
- **"I haven't had the chance" is a real answer**, and more useful than a generous reading of a thin
  example.
- **Never include anything about a research participant** — no names, details or identifying quotes.
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers, what they are used for, or how
  long they are kept, ask before you fill it in.

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not what you studied.
- **1s and 2s are the shape of this level.** Your published wording for research methods says *with

## About this role level

**The role: user researcher.** User researchers plan, design and carry out research activities with
users that help teams get a deep understanding of the people who use government services. This
research informs policy, proposition, service, content and interaction design, so that services work
well for users and achieve policy intent.

**Your level: junior user researcher.** You are embedded in a multidisciplinary team to carry out user
research activities. The framework expects that you:

- have some practical experience but need regular guidance and training to produce your best work and
  develop your skills
- work in combination with a more senior user researcher

**Where this level sits.** This role has six levels:

1. Associate user researcher
2. **Junior user researcher** — you are here
3. User researcher
4. Senior user researcher
5. Lead user researcher
6. Head of user research

Your next step is user researcher: usually embedded in a multidisciplinary team, responsible for
planning and carrying out research activities, and expected to **work independently on a team**.

**One thing worth knowing about this role.** The two skills that changed when you were promoted —
*Analysis and synthesis* and *User research methods* — are worded **identically at user researcher**,
the level above. The five that stayed the same at your level are the ones that change next.

So the step up is not about running better sessions. It is about agile practice, inclusive research,
research management, stakeholder relationships and advocacy — the things around the research rather
than the research itself.

**The seven skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Agile research practices
2. Analysis and synthesis
3. Inclusive research
4. Research management, leadership and assurance
5. Stakeholder relationship management
6. User research methods
7. User-centred practice and advocacy

## Job skills

Seven skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the level expects, what it looks like in
practice, and what changes at user researcher.

### Skill: Agile research practices

**What the framework expects at this level.** One thing: describe agile working and the roles in a
multidisciplinary digital team.

**Same wording as associate user researcher.**

**What that looks like here.** In practice you are probably doing more than describing — fitting
rounds of research into sprints, and getting findings to the team fast enough to affect the next
piece of work. That is beyond the published wording.

**The next step.** At user researcher you understand and explain the benefits and limitations of agile
working, design and conduct research so findings can be embedded into an agile workflow, and work with
the different roles to plan and do research.

### Skill: Analysis and synthesis

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: understand how to apply basic techniques
for the analysis of research data and synthesis of findings; effectively involve your team in analysis
and synthesis; and present clear findings that colleagues can understand and use.

**Same wording at user researcher.** This skill does not change between your level and the next.

**What that looks like here.** Involving the team is the item that changes how findings land. A team
that watched the sessions and helped sort the notes does not need persuading of the conclusions.

### Skill: Inclusive research

**What the framework expects at this level.** One thing: demonstrate some awareness of the diversity of
users of government services and the need to make government services usable and accessible for
everyone.

**Same wording as associate user researcher.**

**What that looks like here.** In practice inclusion is decided by recruitment, and recruitment is
usually the hardest and least supported part of the job. Research that only reaches people who are
easy to recruit tells you about people who are easy to recruit.

**The next step.** At user researcher you understand the diversity of users and work with colleagues to
include many kinds of users in appropriate research activities.

### Skill: Stakeholder relationship management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: describe who your stakeholders are and the
importance of managing relationships with them; and explain what your stakeholders find important and
why.

**Same wording as associate user researcher.**

**What that looks like here.** Getting stakeholders to observe sessions is the most effective
stakeholder management available to a researcher. Ten minutes watching someone struggle does more than
any report.

**The next step.** At user researcher you identify important stakeholders and communicate clearly and
regularly, tailor communication to their needs, build and reach consensus, and use evidence to explain
decisions.

### Skill: User research methods

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: plan and conduct a user research project,
**with support**; understand when to use basic user research methods and how to apply them correctly;
and effectively involve your team in research activities.

**Same wording at user researcher.** This skill does not change between your level and the next —
including the *with support* qualifier, which sits oddly against a level description that says user
researchers work independently.

**What that looks like here.** Choosing the right method is the judgement. Usability testing a
prototype answers "can they do it"; it does not answer "should we build this at all".
