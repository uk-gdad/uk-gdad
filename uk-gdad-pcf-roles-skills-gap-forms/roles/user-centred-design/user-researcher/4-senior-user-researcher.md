# Senior user researcher - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a senior user researcher in the UK Government Digital and Data
profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out what you do now,
where the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this level as an experienced practitioner who can plan and lead user research
activities in larger teams and on more complex services. It expects you to build user-centred
practices in new teams; align research activities with wider plans to inform a service proposition;
and **supervise and develop other user researchers to assure and improve research practice**.

Assuring other people's research is the change at this level. It means having a view on whether a
colleague's method was right, whether their findings are supported, and whether their participants
were treated well — and being able to say so in a way that improves their practice rather than
deflating it.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Seven skills, each with an explanation, a rating and some
questions. Expect about 30 minutes.

## Advisory

This skills gap form is created by AI and is work in progress for educational purposes. If you intend
to use it in any way, please consult with your organization's appropriate teammates, such as with
human resources staff, or people and organizational development staff, or legal compliance staff.

## How to fill in this form

This is a static web page. There is nothing to type into and nothing to send from here — your answers
never touch this site.

Print it, copy the questions into a document, or paste them into whatever your organisation uses for
development conversations. Every question is numbered, so answers travel back on their own.

Worth knowing:

- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a finding.
- **"The team does this" is not an answer to "what do you do".** Name your part.
- **"I haven't had the chance" is a real answer**, and more useful than a generous reading of a thin
  example.
- **Never include anything about a research participant** in your answers.
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers, what they are used for, or how
  long they are kept, ask before you fill it in.

## Rating scale

Where a question asks for a rating, use this scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | You have not worked with this |
| 1 | Awareness | You know what it is and why it matters |
| 2 | Working | You do it with support |
| 3 | Practitioner | You do it unsupported, and handle the usual exceptions |
| 4 | Expert | You set the approach and develop others in it |

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not the remit on your job description.
- **A 4 is a claim about other people** — that someone else's practice changed because of you and
  would stay changed if you left. Supervising and developing researchers is written into your level,
  so one or two are reasonable.

A filled-in answer looks like this:

> **Research management, leadership and assurance**
>
> - [x] 2 — Working: I do it with support
>
> *Evidence:* I review colleagues' plans and give feedback that they act on. Evaluating research
> against professional standards is something I do by instinct rather than against anything written
> down.

Named practice, and a clear line about where it stops.

## About this role level

**The role: user researcher.** User researchers plan, design and carry out research activities with
users that help teams get a deep understanding of the people who use government services. This
research informs policy, proposition, service, content and interaction design, so that services work
well for users and achieve policy intent.

**Your level: senior user researcher.** You are an experienced practitioner who can plan and lead
research activities in larger teams and on more complex services. The framework expects that you:

- build user-centred practices in new teams
- align user research activities with wider plans to inform a service proposition
- supervise and develop other user researchers to assure and improve research practice

**Where this level sits.** This role has six levels:

1. Associate user researcher
2. Junior user researcher
3. User researcher
4. **Senior user researcher** — you are here
5. Lead user researcher
6. Head of user research

Your next step is lead user researcher: leading and aligning research across several teams, ensuring
teams take a user-centred evidence-based approach, and developing and assuring good research practice.

**One thing worth knowing about this role.** Every one of your seven skills changed when you reached
this level — unusual in this framework, and a sign that senior user researcher is a genuine step
rather than a title change. Three of them — agile research practices, inclusive research, and
user-centred practice and advocacy — are then worded **identically at lead**, so they will not
distinguish you at the next step.

**The seven skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Agile research practices
2. Analysis and synthesis
3. Inclusive research
4. Research management, leadership and assurance
5. Stakeholder relationship management
6. User research methods
7. User-centred practice and advocacy

## Job skills

Seven skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the level expects, what it looks like in

### Skill: Agile research practices

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: advocate for agile working; adapt how you
design and conduct research to respond to the complexity of the product environment; understand the
strategic decisions the team needs to make so you can design appropriate research; and influence
decisions about priorities and agile processes in the team.

**What that looks like here.** *Understand the strategic decisions the team needs to make* is the key
item — designing research backwards from the decision it has to inform, rather than forwards from what
would be interesting to know.

**The next step.** Identical wording at lead user researcher. It changes at head, where you advocate
for agile working at organisational level, help organisations adopt agile research practices, and set
standards for how insights are delivered.

### Skill: Analysis and synthesis

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: understand and help teams to apply a range
of methods to analyse research data and synthesise findings; **effectively engage sceptical colleagues
in analysis and synthesis**; and advise on the choice and application of techniques, and critique
colleagues' findings to assure best practice.

**What that looks like here.** Getting a sceptic into the analysis is the most reliable way to change
their mind — much better than presenting conclusions to them afterwards. Critiquing colleagues'
findings is the assurance half, and it needs care: the aim is better practice, not a corrected
report.

**The next step.** At lead this becomes helping an organisation adopt a wide range of techniques and
continually assure, improve and innovate its practice.

### Skill: Inclusive research

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: help teams understand the diversity of
users of government services; **effectively include all kinds of users in appropriate research
activities**; and advocate inclusive practices and help teams design and deliver accessible services
that work for all users.

**What that looks like here.** "All kinds of users" is a large claim and is where the budget argument
lives. Reaching people who are digitally excluded, in crisis, or without stable housing costs money
and time that has to be argued for before the round starts.

**The next step.** Identical wording at lead user researcher.

### Skill: Research management, leadership and assurance

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: evaluate the quality of research against
accepted professional standards for user research; explain what good user research practice involves;
and **give constructive feedback to other user researchers to ensure work meets good practice
standards**.

**What that looks like here.** This is a real assurance role. Evaluating against *accepted
professional standards* implies those standards exist somewhere and you know them — worth checking
whether your organisation has written any down.

**The next step.** At lead you establish and align research programmes to organisational goals,
synthesise findings into strategic insight, set and assure standards including ethics and safeguarding,
and assure quality across the organisation.

### Skill: Stakeholder relationship management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: work with the team to develop and maintain
an understanding of stakeholders; work with the team to develop and implement stakeholder
communications strategies; identify and resolve issues, influence stakeholders and manage relationships
effectively; and build long-term strategic relationships, communicating clearly and regularly.

**What that looks like here.** Stakeholder understanding is now shared with the team rather than held
by you. That matters because research findings land better when several people carry them.

**The next step.** At lead you direct the stakeholder relationship strategy for your teams, ensure
stakeholders' objectives are set, and negotiate with senior stakeholders to unblock progress.

### Skill: User research methods

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: use a wide range of user research methods,
and help teams to adopt them; **plan user research for services with challenging user needs and complex
user journeys**; and advise colleagues on the choice and application of methods to assure best
practice.

**What that looks like here.** The *with support* qualifier that persisted through the three levels
below has finally gone. Challenging user needs means research with people who may be distressed,
suspicious of government, or unable to take part in a standard session — which changes the method, the
setting and the safeguarding.

**The next step.** At lead you help an organisation adopt a wide range of methods and lead a community
of practice to continually assure, improve and innovate research.

### Skill: User-centred practice and advocacy

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: evaluate and choose approaches for building
your team's understanding of the user; **advocate for user research with sceptical colleagues and
stakeholders**; help inexperienced teams to adopt user-centred practices; identify the most important
challenges and opportunities for your team's service development; and align research plans to address
team priorities.

**What that looks like here.** Scepticism is named explicitly, which is honest. The most effective
response is rarely argument — it is arranging for the sceptic to watch two sessions.

**The next step.** Identical wording at lead user researcher.
