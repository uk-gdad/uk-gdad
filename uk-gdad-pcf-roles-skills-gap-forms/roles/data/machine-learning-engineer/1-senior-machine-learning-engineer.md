# Senior machine learning engineer - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a senior machine learning engineer in the UK
Government Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it
so that you can set out what you do now, where the gaps are, and what would close
them.

The framework describes a machine learning engineer as someone who develops, assures
and maintains machine learning models so they can be used in products and services.
At this level you develop the models. It expects you to decide which model is most
suitable; customise, optimise, re-train and maintain existing models; deploy models
into production, testing and assuring them against performance requirements; work
with others to integrate models with existing systems; and **check that models used
in live products and services stay safe, secure and continue to work effectively**.

That last one is the part that separates machine learning engineering from model
building. A model that was accurate at launch and has not been checked since is a
liability, not an asset.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Six skills — the narrowest set in the data
profession — each with an explanation, a rating and some questions. Expect about 30
minutes.

You can fill this form in on screen: the boxes below take typing, the tick

When you have finished, use Export TSV or Export as JSON at the top of the
page to take your answers with you, or print the page, or copy them into

is numbered, so answers travel back on their own. Clear answers empties the

Worth knowing:

- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a finding.
- **Answer about the job you do**, not the one on the job description.
- **"I haven't had the chance" is a real answer**, and more useful than a
  generous reading of a thin example.
- **Do not paste real data, model weights or credentials.** Describe the work, not
  the contents. If a model informs decisions about people, describe it at a level
  you would be comfortable saying in a corridor.
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers, what they are
  used for, or how long they are kept, ask before you fill it in.

## About this role level

**The role: machine learning engineer.** A machine learning engineer develops,
assures and maintains machine learning models so they can be used in products and
services. In this role you will:

- be responsible for the software development and technical infrastructure needed to
  design, train, deploy and scale machine learning models
- provide and maintain effective, secure and sustainable models for use in products
  and services
- support all stages of the machine learning life cycle
- help product teams evaluate and choose appropriate machine learning solutions

**Your level: senior machine learning engineer.** You develop machine learning models
so they can be used in products and services. The framework expects that you:

- decide what model is most suitable for use in products and services
- customise, optimise, re-train and maintain existing models
- deploy models into production, testing and assuring them to ensure they meet
  performance requirements
- work with others to integrate models with existing systems
- check that models used in live products and services stay safe, secure and
  continue to work effectively

**Where this level sits.** This role has two levels only:

1. **Senior machine learning engineer** — you are here
2. Lead machine learning engineer

**The framework publishes no junior level for this role.** It starts at senior. That
has a practical consequence worth naming: there is no defined route *into* this role,
so people arrive from software engineering, data science or research, each with a
different half of the job already covered and a different half missing. Whichever
half you arrived without is a legitimate development need, not a shortfall.

Your next step is lead machine learning engineer: leading the most complex technical
work, co-ordinating models from research and development into production, defining
ways of working across the life cycle, identifying training needs, and defining
software standards for ethics, risk, and security.

**One thing worth knowing about this role.** Two of your six skills — applied maths,
statistics and scientific practices, and data ethics and privacy — are worded
**identically** at lead. A third of the published skills do not change between the
two levels of this role.

**The six skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Applied maths, statistics and scientific practices
2. Communicating between the technical and non-technical
3. Data ethics and privacy
4. Data science innovation
5. Programming and build (software engineering)
6. Systems integration

## Job skills

Six skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the level expects, what

### Skill: Applied maths, statistics and scientific practices

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: apply designated
quantitative techniques such as time series analysis, optimisation and simulation to
create and embed appropriate models for analysis and prediction; provide guidance on
matching data sources with relevant applied mathematics and statistical techniques to
meet analysis goals; apply appropriate statistical techniques to available data to
discover new relations and offer insight into research problems, helping improve
organisational processes and support decision making; and access and use the
statistical tools available within the organisation.

**What that looks like here.** Choosing the technique the problem actually calls for
rather than the one you know best. Also knowing when a simpler method would perform
as well — a well-specified regression that people can explain often beats a model
nobody can interrogate.

**The next step.** Identical wording at lead machine learning engineer. This is the
top published expectation for the skill in this role.

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: listen to and interpret
the needs of technical and non-technical stakeholders, and manage their expectations;
manage active and reactive communication; and support or host difficult discussions
within the team or with diverse senior stakeholders.

**What that looks like here.** Managing expectations about machine learning is a
larger job than in most engineering roles, because what people believe it can do is
shaped by things they read rather than by what your data supports.

**The next step.** At lead you mediate at all levels, moderate difficult discussions
about high-risk topics under time pressure, and represent the community to large
audiences inside and outside the organisation.

### Skill: Programming and build (software engineering)

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: collaborate with others
when necessary to review specifications; and use the agreed specifications to design,
code, test and document programs or scripts of medium-to-high complexity, using the
right standards and tools.

**What that looks like here.** This is a **software engineering** skill, not a data
one — the framework is explicit that machine learning engineering is engineering.
Version control, tests, code review, deployment pipelines, and code someone else can
maintain.

### Skill: Systems integration

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: define the integration
build; co-ordinate build activities across systems; and understand how to undertake
and support integration testing activities.

**What that looks like here.** A model that works in a notebook and a model serving a
live service under load are different things. Integration is where latency, failure
modes and fallback behaviour get decided — including what the service does when the
model is unavailable.

**The next step.** At lead you establish standards and procedures across the service
and development life cycles and ensure practitioners adhere to them, and manage
resources so the systems integration function works effectively.

lives, which brings duties around data protection, automated decision-making,
equality and transparency. These questions ask about awareness, training and support,
