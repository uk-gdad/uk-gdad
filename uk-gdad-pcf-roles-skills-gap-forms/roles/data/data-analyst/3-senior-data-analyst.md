# Senior data analyst - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a senior data analyst in the UK Government
Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that
you can set out what you do now, where the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this level as leading data analysis in a team or across an
area, ensuring data is appropriately prepared, managed and used, and that analysis
outcomes align with the organisation's goals. It expects you to guide others in
appropriate practices for preparing, managing and using data; lead the application
and improvement of processes and ways of working with data; and lead communication
of data and data projects with stakeholders, adapting to their needs.

Every one of those three begins with *guide* or *lead*. This is the level where
the job stops being about your analysis and starts being about everyone's.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Nine skills, each with an explanation, a
rating and some questions. Expect about 35 minutes.

## Advisory

This skills gap form is created by AI and is work in progress for educational

Worth knowing:

- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a finding.
- **"My team does this" is not an answer to "what do you do".** Name your part.
- **"I haven't had the chance" is a real answer**, and more useful than a
  generous reading of a thin example.
- **Be careful with data.** Describe the work, not the contents. No records, no
  identifiers, nothing that would identify a person.
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers, what they
  are used for, or how long they are kept, ask before you fill it in.

## Rating scale

Where a question asks for a rating, use this scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | You have not worked with this |
| 1 | Awareness | You know what it is and why it matters |
| 2 | Working | You do it with support |
| 3 | Practitioner | You do it unsupported, and handle the usual exceptions |
| 4 | Expert | You set the approach and develop others in it |

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not the remit on your job
  description.
- **A 4 is a claim about other people** — that someone else's practice changed
  because of you and would stay changed if you left. At senior, one or two are
  reasonable.

A filled-in answer looks like this:

> **Data preparation and linkage**

> team follows it. Getting the wider organisation to care about quality at source
> is something I have argued for without much movement.

Named practice, and a clear line about where it stops.

## About this role level

**The role: data analyst.** A data analyst collects, manages, explores and shares
data to support organisational objectives and deliver business impact. In this
role you will:

- identify, collect and move data between a range of systems
- apply data analysis and data visualisation tools and techniques
- prepare, manage and model data, following appropriate standards and practices
- communicate data, insights and conclusions in appropriate formats

**Your level: senior data analyst.** You lead data analysis in a team or across an
area, and ensure data is appropriately prepared, managed and used and that
outcomes align with organisational goals. The framework expects that you:

- guide others in appropriate practices for preparing, managing and using data
- lead the application and improvement of processes and ways of working with data
- lead communication of data and data projects with stakeholders, adapting to
  their needs and requirements

**Where this level sits.** This role has four levels:

1. Associate data analyst
2. Data analyst
3. **Senior data analyst** — you are here
4. Principal data analyst

Your next step is principal data analyst: leading teams or significant areas of
work, determining standards and structures for the whole organisation, influencing
organisational decision making, and representing the data analyst community inside
and outside the organisation.

**One thing worth knowing about this role.** *Data ethics and privacy* is worded
**identically** at your level and at principal. The framework does not raise that
expectation at all between the top two levels of this role. If you are already
challenging proposed data ethics policies and researching developments in the
field, you are meeting the top published expectation for that skill — which is
worth recording, because it will not appear as growth in a comparison of the two
levels.

**The nine skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Applying statistical and analytical tools and techniques
2. Communicating between the technical and non-technical
3. Data ethics and privacy
4. Data management
5. Data preparation and linkage
6. Data visualisation
7. Delivering business impact through data
8. Developing code for analysis
9. Managing a data project

## Job skills

Nine skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the level expects,
what it looks like in practice, and what changes at principal data analyst.

### Skill: Applying statistical and analytical tools and techniques

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: lead the development of
valuable statistical insights and reports; identify and apply a range of
statistical and analytical techniques and tools, and support others in applying
them; quality assure statistical techniques and analytical outputs across a team;
and engage with stakeholders and share statistical and analytical techniques to
increase understanding and trust.

**What that looks like here.** You are the person who catches a method that will
not survive scrutiny before it is published. Sharing techniques to build trust is
the subtle item — stakeholders who half-understand a method tend to either
over-trust or dismiss it.

**The next step.** At principal you identify opportunities for insights that
support organisational objectives, develop and guide others in a range of
techniques, oversee quality assurance while improving practice, and use outputs to
influence stakeholders across the organisation and beyond.

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: listen to and
interpret the needs of technical and non-technical stakeholders, and manage their
expectations; manage active and reactive communication; and support or host
difficult discussions within the team or with diverse senior stakeholders.

**What that looks like here.** *Reactive* communication is the part nobody plans
for — the urgent question from a minister's office, the press query, the number
that appeared somewhere and is wrong. Handling those well without letting them
consume the team is most of this skill.

**The next step.** At principal you mediate between people and strengthen
relationships at all levels, moderate difficult discussions about high-risk and
complex topics within constrained timescales, and represent the community to large
audiences inside and outside the organisation.

### Skill: Data ethics and privacy

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: work with stakeholders
to identify and address ethical and privacy concerns; demonstrate and communicate
how data ethical issues fit into the wider organisational context; research
developments in data ethics and privacy to improve compliance and processes; and
assess and constructively challenge proposed data ethics policies.

**What that looks like here.** *Constructively challenge* is the demanding
phrase. It means engaging with a proposed policy on its merits and saying where
it will not work — not simply complying, and not simply objecting.

**The next step.** Identical wording at principal data analyst. This is the top
published expectation for the skill in this role.

### Skill: Data management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: advocate for effective
data governance and data management policies and guidelines within your team and
across your organisation; monitor and improve data management practices in your
team; and help define, develop and support the use of data management tools,
procedures and methods in compliance with governance policies.

**What that looks like here.** Advocacy across the organisation means arguing for
governance in rooms where it is heard as bureaucracy. Monitoring your own team's
practice means actually looking, not assuming.

**The next step.** At principal you champion and lead governance across the
organisation, define and lead implementation of data management responsibilities,
define tools and training requirements, and enable continuous improvement.

### Skill: Data preparation and linkage

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: plan the framework of
an analytical data model based on appropriate data sources; support the
implementation of data models using appropriate tools and systems; and ensure your
team understands the importance of data quality and modelling.

**What that looks like here.** You design the shape the data will take, which
determines what questions become easy and which stay hard for years. Making the
team care about quality is a culture job more than a technical one.

**The next step.** At principal you develop models from a deep understanding of
sources, ensure they align with modelling standards, set data quality standards
across the organisation or beyond, and mentor others.

### Skill: Data visualisation

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: use a range of data
visualisations to simplify a complex story; adapt a story to the needs of
multidisciplinary stakeholders; advise and support others in developing inclusive,
accessible data visualisations that recognise different user needs; and suggest
improvements to data visualisation processes and standards to increase efficiency
and quality.

**What that looks like here.** You are the person who reviews other people's
charts before they go out, and the one who can say why a chart is misleading even
when every number in it is correct.

**The next step.** At principal you translate business requirements into
visualisation goals, set and advocate for new accessibility standards, identify
capability gaps and implement training, and promote adoption across the
organisation.

### Skill: Delivering business impact through data

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: lead and support others
in using data to deliver business impact; define and communicate the scope of the
business impact you deliver so the value is clear; identify opportunities to
develop data products that deliver business impact; and develop and deliver
scalable data products that align to organisational priorities and enable
decision making.

**What that looks like here.** *Scalable* is the operative word — something that
does not need you to run it every Monday. Defining the scope of impact so the
value is clear is really about being able to justify your team's existence in
terms a director recognises.

**The next step.** At principal you champion the role of data across the
organisation, communicate impact strategically, lead others in delivering scalable
products, and partner with other professions to decide when to pivot.

### Skill: Developing code for analysis

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: design and implement
the analytical approach, including code, using appropriate standards and tests;
lead collaboration to review and improve analytical approaches, including shared
code; and guide others in producing analytical approach documentation, identifying
improvements.

**What that looks like here.** Code review as a genuine team practice rather than
a rubber stamp. Reproducible analytical pipelines live or die at this level —
whether they exist usually depends on whether a senior analyst insisted.

**The next step.** At principal you oversee and promote coding standards on
existing and new outputs, contribute to standards across the organisation, and
ensure reproducibility of code and documentation organisation-wide.

### Skill: Managing a data project

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: identify and define a
problem statement; apply and share project management methodologies, including
tools and techniques; plan and implement different stages of the data project life
cycle; communicate progress of data projects with stakeholders; and adapt teams'
ways of working to deliver different project outcomes and requirements.

**What that looks like here.** Defining the problem statement is now yours, which
means you are also the person who has to say no when the question as asked cannot
be answered with the data available.

**The next step.** At principal you advise on problem statement development and
ensure standards are met, oversee planning and implementation across varied
projects, share good practice in adapting ways of working, and lead stakeholder
relationships for your team's projects.
