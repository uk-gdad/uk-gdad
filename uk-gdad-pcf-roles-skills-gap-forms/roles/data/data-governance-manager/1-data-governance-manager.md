# Data governance manager - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a data governance manager in the UK Government
Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you
can set out what you do now, where the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes a data governance manager as someone who plans, monitors and
enforces the management of data assets. At this level you define, implement and
manage data governance frameworks, policies and standards. It expects you to work on
the design and implementation of practical data governance solutions; identify
opportunities to improve the quality and trustworthiness of data across the
organisation; establish wider data management competencies such as data quality,
metadata management, master data management, data modelling and data standards;
apply governance to optimise the safety and value of data throughout its life cycle;
and work to minimise data risks and increase data literacy.

**Practical** is the word doing the work in that list. Data governance fails when it
becomes a set of documents nobody reads, and succeeds when it becomes something
people barely notice they are following.

This role has thirteen skills — the widest of any role in the data profession. That
is a lot to hold, and nobody is strong across all of them.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Expect about 40 minutes.

## About this role level

**The role: data governance manager.** You plan, monitor and enforce the management
of data assets. In this role you will:

- catalogue, monitor and identify data assets essential to the organisation's
  operations or services
- assign ownership of data, ensuring there is a clear responsible, accountable,
  consulted and informed matrix in the organisation
- incorporate governance of data into routine processes
- support or manage risk processes around data
- advocate for better use and management of data within the organisation
- encourage better data literacy within the organisation

**Your level: data governance manager.** You define, implement and manage data
governance frameworks, policies and standards. The framework expects that you:

- work on the design and implementation of practical data governance solutions to
  support organisational needs
- identify opportunities and make recommendations to improve the quality and
  trustworthiness of data throughout the organisation
- implement and establish wider data management competencies within the
  organisation — data quality, metadata management, master data management, data
  modelling, and development of data standards and data strategy
- apply data governance to optimise the safety and value of data throughout the
  data life cycle
- work within the organisation to minimise data risks and increase data literacy

**Where this level sits.** This role has three levels:

1. **Data governance manager** — you are here
2. Lead data governance manager
3. Head of data governance

Your next step is lead data governance manager: leading the formation and management
of frameworks, policies and standards, and being responsible for understanding and
managing organisational data risks, co-ordinating with data owners to accept or
resolve them.

**Two things worth knowing about this role.**

First, your published wording for *Data governance* includes developing an
end-to-end framework, while the level above is worded as taking responsibility for
specific parts of one. Read literally, yours sounds broader. That is how the
framework publishes it; the real distinction between the levels is scale and
authority rather than scope of the words.

Second, *Data ethics and privacy* is worded at your level as applying fundamental
principles **under supervision** — the same wording used for a data analyst at the
second level of that role. For someone accountable for data governance that reads
low, and it rises steeply at the level above.

**The thirteen skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Communicating analysis and insight

### Skill: Communicating analysis and insight

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: choose the appropriate
medium to communicate findings; shape and share communications relevant to the
audience; present analysis and visualisations in clear ways to specialist and
non-specialist audiences, with support; and give tactical recommendations based on
your analysis and insight to inform decision making.

**What that looks like here.** Governance findings are usually unwelcome — a
dataset with no owner, a standard nobody follows. Presenting them so they read as
useful rather than accusatory is most of whether anything changes.

**The next step.** At lead you turn complex data into compelling, actionable
stories, share communication skills across government, communicate negative as well
as positive information, and give strategic as well as tactical recommendations.

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: communicate effectively
with technical and non-technical stakeholders; support and host discussions within a
multidisciplinary team, with potentially difficult dynamics; be an advocate for the
team externally; and manage differing stakeholder perspectives.

without picking a side is the skill.

### Skill: Data governance

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: demonstrate up-to-date
knowledge of data governance theory, standard methodology and practical
considerations; develop an end-to-end data governance framework — the processes,
operational policies and standards needed to support data management; and work
effectively with technical teams and other stakeholders to implement a framework and
make recommendations to ensure compliance.

**What that looks like here.** A framework is easy to write and hard to land. The
difference is whether it changes what people do on a Tuesday, or only what they say
at an audit.

**The next step.** At lead you implement and monitor governance using standard
methodology throughout the life cycle in a large organisation, meet the requirement
for relevant accreditation, and take responsibility for specific parts of the
framework.

### Skill: Data life cycle

**What the framework expects at this level.** One thing: understand and apply data
governance over a data life cycle, ensuring best practice at each phase.

**What that looks like here.** Creation, storage, use, sharing, archiving,
deletion. Deletion is the phase almost everyone skips, and the one that turns into a
problem — data kept indefinitely because nobody decided otherwise.

**The next step.** At lead you understand structures and principles across the life
cycle, demonstrate knowledge of industry standards, design continuous improvements,
and build organisational capacity in governance.

### Skill: Data literacy improvement

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: raise awareness of the
need for data literacy within the organisation; and support data literacy training
and development by enabling data consumers to understand what they can and cannot do
with data.

**What that looks like here.** *What they can and cannot do* is the practical half.
Most misuse of data is not malicious; it is someone who did not know a dataset had
conditions attached.

**The next step.** At lead you recommend data literacy as a valuable skill across
all levels, and identify training courses and other opportunities to improve the
organisation's literacy and data-driven culture.

### Skill: Data standards

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: use data policies,
processes and standards effectively; work with subject matter experts to develop
standards, policies and guidance to protect data; monitor compliance with policies
and standards in a team and take action if needed; and analyse the impact if a
standard is breached.

**What that looks like here.** *Take action if needed* is where governance becomes
real or does not. A monitored standard with no consequence for breaching it teaches
everyone that the standard is optional.

**The next step.** At lead you create standards for different subjects and ensure
senior leaders understand them, introduce best practice across the organisation,
monitor compliance organisation-wide, and recommend how breaches should be resolved.

### Skill: Enabling and informing risk-based decisions

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: work with risk owners to
advise and give feedback; advise on risk impact and whether it is within risk
tolerance; and describe different risk methodologies and how they are applied, as
well as the proportionality of risk.

**What that looks like here.** *Within risk tolerance* assumes someone has set a
tolerance. Often nobody has, and the useful contribution is noticing that out loud.

**The next step.** At lead you work with higher impact or more complex risks,
advising on impact and tolerance, and apply different methodologies in proportion.
