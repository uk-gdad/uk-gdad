# Performance analyst - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a performance analyst in the UK Government Digital
and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out
what you do now, where the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this level as having a strong understanding of a wide range of
sources and analytical tools for the project you are on. It expects you to be responsible
for performance analysis in your area; help the team measure and assess outcomes; help
plan and ensure delivery; share information with the team, community and others; work
independently while knowing when to seek guidance; be adaptable in applying techniques to
new contexts, **especially when you are the sole performance analyst on a project**;
create a performance measurement framework with the wider team, including reporting
requirements and advising on procurement, implementation and validation of tools; be
responsible for the accuracy and quality of data and analysis; and actively contribute to
the community.

That "sole representative" clause is the reality of this job in much of government. You
are often the only person in the room who knows what the numbers can and cannot support.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Seven skills, each with an explanation, a
rating and some questions. Expect about 30 minutes.

## About this role level

**The role: performance analyst.** A performance analyst conducts analysis. In this role
you develop performance measurement frameworks — key performance indicators, goals, user
needs and benefits — and analyse the performance of a service or product against them,
adapting your approach and framework as things change.

**Your level: performance analyst.** You have a strong understanding of a wide range of
sources and analytical tools for the project you are working on. The framework expects
that you:

- be responsible for the performance analysis in your given area
- help the project team measure and assess outcomes — what good looks like and how it
  will be measured
- help to plan and ensure project delivery
- find opportunities to share information with the project team, community colleagues
  and others
- work independently, but know to seek guidance when necessary
- be adaptable in applying analysis techniques to new contexts, especially when acting
  as the sole representative for performance analysts on a project
- create a performance measurement framework for a project with the wider team,
  including identifying reporting requirements and advising on procurement,
  implementation and validation of tools
- be responsible for the accuracy and quality of data and analysis, and how they are
  used
- be engaged with, and actively contribute to, the performance analysis community

**Where this level sits.** This role has five levels:

1. Associate performance analyst
2. **Performance analyst** — you are here
3. Senior performance analyst
4. Lead performance analyst
5. Head of performance analysis

Your next step is senior performance analyst: delegating, automating, or upskilling others
where possible, providing leadership in the team, possibly managing one or more analysts,
and carrying out increasingly complex analysis.

**Two things worth knowing about this role.**

First, *Communicating analysis and insight* is worded **identically at your level and at
associate**. The published expectation for communication does not rise when you are
promoted into this level.

Second, *Analysis and insight* is worded **identically at your level, at senior, and at
head of performance analysis** — three of the five levels, including the top one. That
skill will never show progression for you above senior, so the case rests elsewhere.

**The seven skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Analysis and insight
2. Communicating analysis and insight
3. Performance measurement
4. Quality assurance of data and analysis
5. Technical understanding (performance analyst)
6. Understanding analysis across the product life cycle
7. User-centred analysis

## Job skills

Seven skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the level expects, what it
looks like in practice, and what changes at senior performance analyst.

### Skill: Analysis and insight

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: understand and help teams to
apply a range of techniques to analyse data and provide insight; be proactive and present
compelling findings that inform wider decisions; and apply innovative approaches to
resolve problems.

**What that looks like here.** *Proactive* is the change from associate. Nobody asks for
the finding that matters most — you notice it, and you bring it to people who were not
looking for it.

**The next step.** Identical wording at senior performance analyst, **and at head of
performance analysis**. This skill does not rise again except at lead, where you teach and
supervise a wide range of techniques and help organisations learn from strategic
insights.

### Skill: Communicating analysis and insight

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: choose the appropriate medium
to communicate findings; shape and share communications relevant to the audience; present
analysis and visualisations in clear ways to both specialist and non-specialist audiences,
with support; and give tactical recommendations based on your analysis and insight to
inform decision making.

**Same wording as associate performance analyst.** The published expectation does not rise
between those two levels.

**What that looks like here.** As the sole analyst on a project you are usually presenting
to people who will act on what you say, and the tactical recommendation is what they
actually want. "The numbers show X, so I suggest Y" is a more useful sentence than a
dashboard.

**The next step.** At senior you turn complex data into compelling, actionable stories,
share communication skills across government, communicate **negative** as well as positive
information, and give strategic as well as tactical recommendations.

### Skill: Performance measurement

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: participate in discussions
determining which performance measurements are appropriate, with guidance from more
experienced colleagues; and demonstrate experience in turning business needs and goals into
performance measures.

**What that looks like here.** Turning a goal into a measure is where most of the honesty
in this job lives. "Improve the user experience" has to become something countable, and
the countable version is always a partial account of the goal. Saying which part it misses
is as important as choosing it.

**The next step.** At senior you lead the development of measurement frameworks including
key performance indicators, and demonstrate a deep understanding of business goals.

### Skill: Quality assurance of data and analysis

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: identify appropriate ways to
collect, collate and prepare data; decide if data and analysis is accurate and fit for
purpose; perform data preparation and cleansing with limited guidance; undertake quality
assurance processes; and **peer-review colleagues' outputs from data analysis to ensure
quality**.

**What that looks like here.** As the sole analyst on a project, peer review often has to
come from the community rather than the team — which is one concrete reason the framework
asks you to be engaged with it.

**The next step.** At senior you set up a system to get data ready for use, specify how
data should be cleansed and prepared, bring data together from different sources, and
communicate the limitations of data.

### Skill: Technical understanding (performance analyst)

**What the framework expects at this level.** One thing: show an understanding of the
core technical concepts related to the role, and apply them.

**What that looks like here.** Knowing how tracking actually works — what a tag is, what
breaks when a page changes, why two tools give different numbers for the same thing, and
what the data does not capture at all.

**The next step.** At senior you show a deep understanding of technical concepts and how
they fit the wider technical landscape, and understand the limitations of digital
technology.

### Skill: Understanding analysis across the product life cycle

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: understand how the needs of the
team and product vary across the product life cycle; ensure the team is measuring
performance as appropriate for the relevant service standard phase; manage the delivery of
services or products at different phases; and plan and look at data requirements in future
phases.

**What that looks like here.** *Data requirements in future phases* is the item that saves
projects. Deciding in beta what you will need in live means the data is collected from the
start, because you usually cannot reconstruct it afterwards.

**The next step.** At senior you apply experience in multiple parts of the life cycle,
**recognise when to move forward and when to stop**, engage the right people for the right
deliverables, work with other Agile disciplines, and identify which tools and techniques
suit each stage.

### Skill: User-centred analysis

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: use quantitative and
qualitative data about users to turn user focus into outcomes; deliver analysis that
addresses stakeholder needs and provides recommendations; effectively collaborate with user
researchers and other user-focused professions; and guide others on approaches to
understand user stories.

**What that looks like here.** Analytics tells you what happened; research tells you why.
Putting them together — a drop-off in the data, and a recording of someone getting stuck at
exactly that step — is more persuasive than either alone.

**The next step.** At senior you understand the difference between user needs and user
desires, meet needs across a variety of channels, integrate digital analytics with
qualitative data to develop hypotheses for testing, and recommend tools and methods.
