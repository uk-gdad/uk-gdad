# Senior performance analyst - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a senior performance analyst in the UK Government
Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can
set out what you do now, where the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this level as looking to **delegate, automate or upskill others**
in analysis where possible and appropriate, providing leadership within your team, and
proactively using analysis to improve services. It expects you to potentially manage one
or more performance analysts; be proficient in a wide range of tools; ensure the team has
the skills required by the strategy set out by the head of performance analysis; be
skilled at the day-to-day work including setting up measurement frameworks; carry out
increasingly complex analysis; and contribute to the community while forging links with
other analytical disciplines.

The framework also notes that as a **technical specialist senior performance analyst**
you would develop tools and techniques to aid others in analysis, and be responsible for
the accuracy and quality of data and analysis and how they are used. That is a genuine
alternative shape for this level, and it is worth knowing it exists if managing people is
not what you want.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Seven skills, each with an explanation, a
rating and some questions. Expect about 30 minutes.

## About this role level

**The role: performance analyst.** A performance analyst conducts analysis. In this role
you develop performance measurement frameworks — key performance indicators, goals, user
needs and benefits — and analyse the performance of a service or product against them,
adapting your approach and framework as things change.

**Your level: senior performance analyst.** You look to delegate, automate or upskill
others in analysis where possible and appropriate, provide leadership within your team,
and proactively use analysis to improve services. The framework expects that you:

- potentially manage one or more performance analysts
- be proficient in a wide range of tools
- ensure the team is equipped with the required skills, according to the strategy set out
  by the head of performance analysis
- be skilled and experienced at the day-to-day activities of a performance analyst,
  including setting up performance measurement frameworks
- carry out increasingly complex analysis, working with other teams where necessary
- actively contribute to the performance analysis community in your organisation, while
  forging links with other analytical disciplines

**A technical specialist route.** The framework says that as a technical specialist senior
performance analyst you would develop tools and techniques to aid others in analysis, and
be responsible for the accuracy and quality of data and analysis and how they are used.
That is an explicit alternative to the management shape of this level.

**Where this level sits.** This role has five levels:

1. Associate performance analyst
2. Performance analyst
3. **Senior performance analyst** — you are here
4. Lead performance analyst
5. Head of performance analysis

Your next step is lead performance analyst: managing analysts at any level, co-ordinating
teams or groups of analysts, influencing the strategy set by the head of performance
analysis, acting as an escalation point, and being an ambassador for analytics across
government.

**One thing worth knowing about this role.** *Analysis and insight* is worded
**identically at performance analyst, at your level, and at head of performance
analysis** — three of the five levels including the top one. It rises only at lead, where
it becomes teaching and supervising a wide range of techniques and helping organisations
learn from strategic insights. So the analysis skill itself will not distinguish you here;
what you delegate, automate and teach will.

**The seven skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Analysis and insight
2. Communicating analysis and insight
3. Performance measurement
4. Quality assurance of data and analysis
5. Technical understanding (performance analyst)
6. Understanding analysis across the product life cycle
7. User-centred analysis

## Job skills

### Skill: Analysis and insight

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: understand and help teams to
apply a range of techniques to analyse data and provide insight; be proactive and present
compelling findings that inform wider decisions; and apply innovative approaches to resolve
problems.

**Same wording as performance analyst — and as head of performance analysis.**

**What that looks like here.** At senior the difference is that you are increasingly
helping others produce the analysis rather than producing it, which is the delegate,
automate, upskill instruction in practice.

**The next step.** At lead you understand, **teach and supervise** a wide range of
techniques, help organisations create and learn from strategic insights, and apply
innovative approaches.

### Skill: Communicating analysis and insight

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: turn complex data into
compelling, clear and actionable stories; share data communication skills with the team and
across government; present analysis and visualisations to communicate complex messages;
**communicate negative and positive information to stakeholders**; and give tactical and
strategic recommendations.

**What that looks like here.** This is a real step up from the two levels below, which
shared the same lighter wording. Communicating negative information is written in
explicitly — telling a team the thing they built is not working is the hardest and most
valuable output of performance analysis.

**The next step.** At lead this shortens to communicating relevant, compelling stories
using the most appropriate medium, and making complex recommendations at tactical and
strategic level.

### Skill: Performance measurement

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: lead the development of
performance measurement frameworks including key performance indicators; and demonstrate a
deep understanding of business goals and turn these into tangible performance measures.

**What that looks like here.** Leading a framework means arbitrating between people who
want different things measured, usually because they disagree about what the service is
for. That disagreement is worth surfacing rather than averaging away into a long list of
indicators.

**The next step.** At lead you oversee the development and consistency of frameworks
across multiple projects, and ensure they are aligned to business needs and strategy.

### Skill: Quality assurance of data and analysis

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: set up a system to get data
ready for use; specify how data should be cleansed and prepared; bring data together from
different sources; and **communicate the limitations of data**.

**What that looks like here.** Bringing sources together is where most performance analysis
goes wrong — two systems counting the same thing differently, and a joined number that is
neither. Being explicit about that in the output is what keeps it trustworthy.

**The next step.** At lead you set up tools and systems for assuring data across multiple
teams, verify and validate data and analysis, influence senior stakeholders over
approaches, and coach and mentor others in assurance.

### Skill: Technical understanding (performance analyst)

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: show a deep understanding of the
technical concepts required for the role and understand how they fit into the wider
technical landscape; and **understand the limitations of digital technology**.

**What that looks like here.** Knowing what analytics genuinely cannot see: people who
never reached the service, people who used a different channel, people using privacy
settings that block measurement. The users most likely to be invisible are often the ones
who most need the service to work.

**The next step.** At lead you identify opportunities to use new digital technologies to
enhance analytical capability in alignment with business goals.

### Skill: Understanding analysis across the product life cycle

**What the framework expects at this level.** Six things: apply experience in multiple
parts of the product life cycle; **recognise when to move forward and when to stop**;
recognise the appropriate deliverables and engage the right people to meet them; work with
other Agile delivery disciplines throughout the life cycle; plan and proactively engage
with stakeholders at the appropriate stage; and identify which tools and techniques should
be used at each stage.

**What that looks like here.** *Recognise when to stop* is the most consequential item.
Performance analysts are often the first to see that something is not working, and saying
so early — while it is still cheap — is the value of the role.

**The next step.** At lead you develop sustainable support models, identify and deal with
risks across all stages, coach others, contribute to assessing other teams, and lead and
champion best practice.

### Skill: User-centred analysis

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: understand the difference
between user needs and desires of the user; demonstrate experience in meeting user needs
across a variety of channels; integrate digital analytics with qualitative data such as
user surveys and research to develop hypotheses for testing; and offer recommendations on
the best tools and methods to use.

**What that looks like here.** Needs versus desires is a genuinely useful distinction and
easily misused — it can become a way of dismissing what people asked for. The honest
version treats a stated desire as evidence about an underlying need, not as noise.

**The next step.** At lead you give direction on tools and methods, bring insight into how
user needs have changed over time, apply strategic thinking to providing the best service,
and consider the political strategy and implications of analyses and insights.
