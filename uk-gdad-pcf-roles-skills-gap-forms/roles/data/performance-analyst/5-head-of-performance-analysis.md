# Head of performance analysis - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you are a head of performance analysis in a UK government
organisation. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out what you
do now, where the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this role as leading the performance analysis community and acting
as the point of contact between the community and the business. It expects you to remove
blockers and act as an escalation point for important issues; set the performance analysis
strategy with input from lead and senior performance analysts; be responsible for defining
a framework for quality assurance within performance analysis; champion performance
analysis and performance analysts, working with colleagues across government and other
Government Digital and Data communities and analytical professions; and manage the
organisation's performance analysis capability through business planning, development and
recruitment.

**Point of contact between the community and the business** is the phrase that describes
the job. You are the translation layer in both directions: what the organisation needs
measured, and what the analysts can honestly tell it.

Head of performance analysis is the top of this role. Seven skills. Expect about 30
minutes.

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not the remit on your job description.
- **A 4 is a claim about other people** — that someone else's practice changed because of
  you and would stay changed if you left. The honest test here is whether performance
  analysis practice would survive your departure.

## About this role level

**The role: performance analyst.** A performance analyst conducts analysis. In this role
you develop performance measurement frameworks — key performance indicators, goals, user
needs and benefits — and analyse the performance of a service or product against them,
adapting your approach and framework as things change.

**Your level: head of performance analysis.** You lead the performance analysis community
and act as the point of contact between the community and the business. The framework
expects that you:

- remove blockers and act as an escalation point for important issues
- set the performance analysis strategy with input from lead and senior performance
  analysts
- be responsible for defining a framework for quality assurance within performance
  analysis
- champion performance analysis and performance analysts, working with colleagues across
  government and other Government Digital and Data communities and analytical professions
- manage the organisation's performance analysis capability through business planning,
  development and recruitment

**There is no level above this one.** This role has five levels and this is the highest.
Where people go next is a question about other roles — analytical or data leadership, or
leadership outside the profession — and the framework does not define it as a next step, so
this form does not invent one.

**Where this level sits:**

1. Associate performance analyst
2. Performance analyst
3. Senior performance analyst
4. Lead performance analyst
5. **Head of performance analysis** — you are here

**One thing worth knowing about this role.** **Six of your seven skills are worded
identically at lead performance analyst.** The seventh — *Analysis and insight* — is worded
**lower** at your level than at lead: yours is the performance analyst wording, while lead
is teaching and supervising a wide range of techniques and helping organisations learn from
strategic insights.

That is how the framework publishes it. It means the published skills do not describe what
makes this job different, and everything distinctive about it — community leadership,
setting strategy, the assurance framework, business planning and recruitment — appears only
in the accountabilities. Worth knowing if the skills are ever used to assess you.

**The seven skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Analysis and insight
2. Communicating analysis and insight
3. Performance measurement
4. Quality assurance of data and analysis
5. Technical understanding (performance analyst)
6. Understanding analysis across the product life cycle
7. User-centred analysis

### Skill: Analysis and insight

**What the framework expects.** Three things: understand and help teams to apply a range of
techniques to analyse data and provide insight; be proactive and present compelling findings
that inform wider decisions; and apply innovative approaches to resolve problems.

**A note on the wording.** This is the **performance analyst** wording, three levels below,
and less than lead's — which is teaching and supervising techniques and helping
organisations learn from strategic insights. That is how the framework publishes it.

**What that looks like here.** In practice the question at your level is whether the
organisation acts on analysis at all, or commissions it and proceeds as intended.

### Skill: Communicating analysis and insight

**What the framework expects.** Two things: communicate relevant, compelling stories using

more optimistic and better resourced, and choosing when to press and when to let something

### Skill: Performance measurement

**What the framework expects.** Two things: oversee the development and consistency of

which means persuading teams to give up their own. The reward is an aggregate picture that

### Skill: Quality assurance of data and analysis

**What the framework expects.** Four things: set up tools and systems for effectively
assuring data and analysis across multiple teams; use appropriate approaches for verifying
and validating data and analysis; influence senior stakeholders over data and analysis
approaches; and coach and mentor others in assuring data and analysis.

**Same wording as lead performance analyst** — although *defining a framework for quality
assurance* is separately in your accountabilities, which this skill does not mention.

**What that looks like here.** A framework that depends on your attention is not a
framework. The test is whether a weak analysis would be caught by the system in a team you
never look at.

### Skill: User-centred analysis

**What the framework expects.** Four things: give direction on which tools or methods to
use; bring insight and expertise to how user needs have changed over time and ensure they
are met; apply strategic thinking to how to provide the best service for the end user; and
consider the political strategy and implications of any analyses and insights.

**Same wording as lead performance analyst.**

**What that looks like here.** Considering political implications while keeping findings
honest is a line you hold on behalf of your whole community. Analysts below you will take
their cue from how you handle the awkward ones.
