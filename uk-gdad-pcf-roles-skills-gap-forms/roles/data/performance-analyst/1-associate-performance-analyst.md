# Associate performance analyst - skills gap form

## Introduction

Hello, and welcome. This form is for you if you work as an associate performance analyst
in the UK Government Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has
sent it so that you can say what you do now, what you would like to get better at, and
what would help.

The framework describes this as an entry-level role requiring numeracy and some
experience in analysis or data handling. It expects you to have the aptitude to learn
analytical tools and produce standardised reports and charts; start on projects with
limited scope under guidance, replicating existing analysis and techniques; progress to
greater independence where appropriate; have the opportunity to develop an understanding
of the wider analytics context; be responsible for the accuracy and quality of data and
analysis and how they are used; and engage with the performance analysis community.

**Responsibility for accuracy is in there from day one.** That is unusual for an entry
level, and it is deliberate — a wrong number in a report is wrong whoever produced it,
and checking your own work is a habit worth building now.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. There are no right answers. Seven skills, each
with a plain explanation, a rating and a couple of questions. Take about 25 minutes.

## About this role level

**The role: performance analyst.** A performance analyst conducts analysis. In this role
you develop performance measurement frameworks — key performance indicators, goals, user
needs and benefits — and analyse the performance of a service or product against them,
adapting your approach and framework as things change.

**Your level: associate performance analyst.** This is an entry-level role requiring
numeracy and some experience in analysis or data handling. The framework expects that
you:

- have the aptitude to learn to use analytical tools and produce standardised reports
  and charts
- start by working on projects with limited scope under the guidance of performance
  analysts and more senior colleagues, replicating existing analysis and data techniques
- progress to greater independence where appropriate
- have the opportunity to develop an understanding of the wider analytics context, tools
  and techniques
- be responsible for the accuracy and quality of data and analysis, and how they are
  used
- engage with the performance analysis community

**Where this level sits.** This role has five levels:

1. **Associate performance analyst** — you are here
2. Performance analyst
3. Senior performance analyst
4. Lead performance analyst
5. Head of performance analysis

Your next step is performance analyst: being responsible for the analysis in a given
area, working independently, and creating a performance measurement framework with the
wider team.

**One thing worth knowing about this role.** *Communicating analysis and insight* is
worded **identically at your level and at performance analyst**, the level above. It is
already quite demanding — choosing the medium, tailoring to the audience, and giving
tactical recommendations. If that reads as more than an entry level would do, that is the
framework being ambitious rather than you falling short.

**The seven skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Analysis and insight
2. Communicating analysis and insight
3. Performance measurement
4. Quality assurance of data and analysis
5. Technical understanding (performance analyst)
6. Understanding analysis across the product life cycle
7. User-centred analysis

### Skill: Analysis and insight

**What the framework expects at your level.** Two things: understand and work with
colleagues to apply core techniques for analysis and insight; and present clear insights
that colleagues can understand and use.

**What that means day to day.** *Insight* is the important word — a number is not an
insight. "Completion dropped 12% in March" is a number; "completion dropped when we added
the extra question, and people are abandoning at that step" is an insight, because
someone can act on it.

**What changes at performance analyst.** You would help teams apply a range of
techniques, be proactive and present compelling findings that inform wider decisions, and
apply innovative approaches to problems.

### Skill: Communicating analysis and insight

**What the framework expects at your level.** Four things: choose the appropriate medium
to communicate findings; shape and share communications relevant to the audience; present
analysis and visualisations in clear ways to both specialist and non-specialist
audiences, with support; and give tactical recommendations based on your analysis and
insight to inform decision making.

**What that means day to day.** This is demanding wording for an entry level — and it is
**identical at the level above**, so it is not a step you are expected to climb yet. In
practice, start with charts that answer the question and do not need explaining, and with
saying what you think should happen rather than only what the numbers say.

**What changes at performance analyst.** Nothing — the wording is the same. It first
changes at senior, where you turn complex data into compelling stories and communicate
negative as well as positive information.

### Skill: Performance measurement

**What the framework expects at your level.** One thing: explain the importance of
performance measurement and business objectives.

**What that means day to day.** Understanding what your service is trying to achieve, and
how the things being counted relate to that. Plenty of services measure what is easy to
count rather than what matters, and noticing the gap is the beginning of this skill.

**What changes at performance analyst.** You would participate in discussions determining
which measurements are appropriate, with guidance, and turn business needs and goals into
performance measures.

### Skill: Quality assurance of data and analysis

**What the framework expects at your level.** Four things: describe some basic data
issues and how to check that data and analysis look right; explain the concept of data
being fit for purpose and the context of the data; describe some methods for preparing
data for analysis; and describe quality assurance processes.

**What that means day to day.** You are responsible for the accuracy of what you produce
from day one. Useful habits: check totals against a known figure, look at the extremes,
ask whether a sudden change is real or a data problem, and have someone else look before
it goes out.

**What changes at performance analyst.** You would identify how to collect and prepare
data, decide whether data and analysis are accurate and fit for purpose, cleanse data with
limited guidance, undertake quality assurance, and peer-review colleagues' work.

### Skill: Technical understanding (performance analyst)

**What the framework expects at your level.** Two things: show an awareness of analytical
tools and technologies and use some of them; and show an awareness of digital technology,
with an understanding of what it involves and its impact.

**What that means day to day.** Knowing roughly how the data reaches you — that a page
view is recorded by something on the website, that a tag can break, and that a number
being zero might mean nothing happened or might mean the measurement stopped.

**What changes at performance analyst.** You would show an understanding of the core
technical concepts related to the role, and apply them.

### Skill: Understanding analysis across the product life cycle

**What the framework expects at your level.** One thing: describe the importance of
performance measures, their relation to business objectives, and how they vary at
different stages of the product life cycle.

**What that means day to day.** Digital services move through discovery, alpha, beta and
live. Early on you are trying to learn quickly with rough measures; later you are
monitoring something real and precision matters more. Knowing which phase your team is in
tells you what kind of analysis is useful.

**What changes at performance analyst.** You would understand how the needs of the team
and product vary across the life cycle, ensure the team measures appropriately for its
service standard phase, manage delivery at different phases, and plan for future data
requirements.

### Skill: User-centred analysis

**What the framework expects at your level.** One thing: show an awareness that
everything we do is focused on the user of the product or service.

**What that means day to day.** This is the shortest expectation in the whole role, and
it is a reminder rather than a technique. Behind every drop-off in a funnel is a person
who could not do the thing they came to do — often someone who needed the service most.

**What changes at performance analyst.** You would use quantitative and qualitative data
about users to turn user focus into outcomes, deliver analysis addressing stakeholder
needs, collaborate with user researchers, and guide others on understanding user stories.
