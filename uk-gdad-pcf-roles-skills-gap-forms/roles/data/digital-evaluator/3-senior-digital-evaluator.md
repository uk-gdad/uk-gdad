# Senior digital evaluator - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a senior digital evaluator in the UK Government
Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can
set out what you do now, where the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this level as managing the evaluations of products or services
and ensuring they follow government guidance and meet standards. It expects you to work
in a multidisciplinary team; develop theories of change and evaluation plans with
stakeholders; manage relationships with stakeholders, user researchers and performance
analysts; collect and analyse quantitative and qualitative data through research; assure
the work of other evaluators, including evaluation plans, data collection and analysis;
and identify the resources needed for evaluation activities.

This is the level where the support qualifiers disappear. At the two levels below, the
published wording for delivering an evaluation said "with support" three times. Yours
does not.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Seven skills, each with an explanation, a
rating and some questions. Expect about 30 minutes.

## How to fill in this form

This is a static web page. There is nothing to type into and nothing to send from here —
your answers never touch this site.

Print it, copy the questions into a document, or paste them into whatever your
organisation uses for development conversations. Every question is numbered, so answers
travel back on their own.

Worth knowing:

- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a finding.
- **"The team does this" is not an answer to "what do you do".** Name your part.
- **"I haven't had the chance" is a real answer**, and more useful than a generous
  reading of a thin example.
- **Do not paste real data or anything about identifiable people.**
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers, what they are
  used for, or how long they are kept, ask before you fill it in.

## Rating scale

Where a question asks for a rating, use this scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | You have not worked with this |
| 1 | Awareness | You know what it is and why it matters |
| 2 | Working | You do it with support |
| 3 | Practitioner | You do it unsupported, and handle the usual exceptions |
| 4 | Expert | You set the approach and develop others in it |

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not the remit on your job description.
- **A 4 is a claim about other people** — that someone else's practice changed because
  of you and would stay changed if you left. Assuring others' work is written into your
  level, so one or two are reasonable.

## About this role level

**The role: digital evaluator.** A digital evaluator assesses the design, implementation
and outcomes of a digital product or service, and whether it delivers value for the
public and society. Some are also members of a government analytical profession where
evaluation is a core skill. In this role you will:

- work closely with delivery and policy teams
- develop theories of change for products and services, explaining the links between
  activities, outputs, intended outcomes and unintended effects
- follow established monitoring and evaluation methods and government guidance
- produce evaluation plans
- design quantitative and qualitative research
- collect and analyse data
- communicate findings, including by publishing reports

**Your level: senior digital evaluator.** You manage the evaluations of products or
services, and ensure they follow government guidance and meet standards. The framework
expects that you:

- work in a multidisciplinary team
- develop theories of change and evaluation plans with stakeholders
- manage relationships with stakeholders, user researchers and performance analysts
- collect and analyse quantitative and qualitative data through research
- assure the work of other evaluators, including evaluation plans, data collection and
  analysis
- identify the resources needed for evaluation activities

**Where this level sits.** This role has five levels:

1. Junior digital evaluator
2. Digital evaluator
3. **Senior digital evaluator** — you are here
4. Lead digital evaluator
5. Head of digital evaluation

Your next step is lead digital evaluator: planning and leading evaluation in larger
teams and for more complex services, usually managing multiple evaluations and possibly
leading a team.

**One thing worth knowing about this role.** Two of your seven skills are worded
**identically** at lead digital evaluator — evaluation delivery, and evaluation planning
and strategy. Those two do not distinguish your level from the one above. What does
change at lead: communication becomes strategic recommendations; data ethics becomes
overseeing organisational compliance; life cycle work becomes recommending when to stop;
monitoring becomes defining best practice; and quality assurance becomes systems across
multiple teams and coaching others.

**The seven skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Communicating analysis and insight
2. Data ethics and privacy
3. Evaluation delivery
4. Evaluation planning and strategy
5. Monitoring and evaluation across the product life cycle
6. Product and service monitoring
7. Quality assurance of data and analysis

## Job skills

Seven skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the level expects, what it
looks like in practice, and what changes at lead digital evaluator.

### Skill: Communicating analysis and insight

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: turn complex data into
compelling, clear and actionable stories; share data communication skills with the team
and across government; present analysis and visualisations to communicate complex
messages; **communicate negative and positive information to stakeholders**; and give
tactical and strategic recommendations.

**What that looks like here.** Communicating negative information is explicit in your
level, and it is most of what evaluation produces — services rarely work as well as
hoped. Delivering that so it is heard rather than resisted is the skill.

**The next step.** At lead this shortens to communicating relevant, compelling stories
using the most appropriate medium, and making complex recommendations at tactical and
strategic level.

### Skill: Data ethics and privacy

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: work with stakeholders to
identify and address ethical and privacy concerns; demonstrate and communicate how data
ethical issues fit into the wider organisational context; research developments in data
ethics and privacy to improve compliance and processes; and assess and constructively
challenge proposed data ethics policies.

**What that looks like here.** Evaluation reaches people who could not complete a
service or were refused something, and their willingness to speak depends on trust you
have to earn and protect. Challenging a policy that would compromise that is part of
your level.

**The next step.** At lead you oversee compliance with ethics standards and legislation
across the organisation, develop and improve an ethical framework, develop an ethics
culture, and ensure ethics is applied in formulating and evaluating policies.

### Skill: Evaluation delivery

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: evaluate digital services
using some appropriate techniques including process evaluation, impact evaluation, theory
of change, experimentation and economic evaluation; collect quantitative and qualitative
data; analyse and interpret data you have collected and data from other sources; and use
a **wide range** of analytical approaches, including descriptive and inferential
analysis.

**What that looks like here.** The support qualifiers are gone, which is the real change
from the level below. Impact evaluation in digital services is the hardest of these —
establishing what would have happened otherwise is rarely straightforward, and being
honest about that limitation is more valuable than a confident number.

**The next step.** Identical wording at lead digital evaluator.

### Skill: Evaluation planning and strategy

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: influence stakeholders to
secure resources for an evaluation project; identify evaluation and evidence needs;
ensure plans for monitoring and evaluation are appropriate and **proportionate**;
consider wider policy and practical constraints that affect evaluation strategy; and
agree the evaluation approach with diverse stakeholders.

**What that looks like here.** *Proportionate* is the operative word. A rigorous
evaluation of a small change can cost more than the change, and knowing when a lighter
approach is right is a mark of experience rather than a compromise.

**The next step.** Identical wording at lead digital evaluator.

### Skill: Monitoring and evaluation across the product life cycle

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: ensure the team is measuring
performance as appropriate for the relevant service standard phase; manage the delivery
of evaluation of services or products at different phases; and plan and look at data
requirements in future phases.

**Same wording as digital evaluator.** This skill does not change between those two
levels.

**What that looks like here.** Across several evaluations at different phases, the useful
discipline is making sure the data needed later is being collected now — which usually
means asking a delivery team to add something they do not currently need.

**The next step.** At lead you apply monitoring and evaluation techniques in multiple
parts of the life cycle, **recommend when to move forward and when to stop**, work with
other Agile disciplines throughout, and engage stakeholders proactively at the right
stage.

### Skill: Product and service monitoring

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: develop a theory of change
for a product or service through discussion with stakeholders; turn theory of change
indicators into measurement frameworks for a digital service; and work with performance
analysts to develop a performance dashboard reporting service analytics and other
identified indicators.

**What that looks like here.** Turning a theory of change into a measurement framework
is where good intentions meet available data. Some indicators simply cannot be measured
affordably, and saying so early prevents a framework that quietly fails.

**The next step.** At lead you get senior support for the need to develop theories of
change, provide guidance on how to develop them, and define best practice for measuring
indicators including economic outcomes and communicating limitations.

### Skill: Quality assurance of data and analysis

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: set up a system to get data
ready for use; specify how data should be cleansed and prepared; bring data together from
different sources; and **communicate the limitations of data**.

**What that looks like here.** Communicating limitations is the item that separates
trustworthy evaluation from persuasive evaluation. A caveat buried in an annex is not a
communicated limitation.

**The next step.** At lead you set up tools and systems for assuring data across multiple
teams, verify and validate data and analysis, influence senior stakeholders over
approaches, and coach and mentor others in assurance.
