# Digital evaluator - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a digital evaluator in the UK Government Digital and
Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out
what you do now, where the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this level as supporting the evaluation of products and
services, with a good understanding of the role and some practical experience. It
expects you to work with a more senior digital evaluator to plan and deliver
evaluations; manage the day-to-day development of monitoring, evaluation and analytical
tools; support the design of quantitative and qualitative research; help with research
activities and other data collection; report on the performance of products and
services; communicate findings, for example through written reports; and receive
guidance and training to develop your skills.

Training and guidance are still written into your level, which is unusual for a second
rung and worth holding your organisation to.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Seven skills, each with an explanation, a
rating and some questions. Expect about 30 minutes.

## How to fill in this form

This is a static web page. There is nothing to type into and nothing to send from here —
your answers never touch this site.

Print it, copy the questions into a document, or paste them into whatever your
organisation uses for development conversations. Every question is numbered, so answers
travel back on their own.

Worth knowing:

- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a finding.
- **Answer about the job you do**, not the one on the job description.
- **"I haven't had the chance" is a real answer**, and more useful than a generous
  reading of a thin example.
- **Do not paste real data or anything about identifiable people.** Describe the work.
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers, what they are
  used for, or how long they are kept, ask before you fill it in.

| 3 | Practitioner | You do it unsupported, and handle the usual exceptions |
| 4 | Expert | You set the approach and develop others in it |

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not the remit on your job description.
- **2s are the honest shape of this level.** The framework's own wording says *with
  support* repeatedly, and your accountabilities say you work *with a more senior
  digital evaluator*. A 2 is the level, not a shortfall.

## About this role level

**The role: digital evaluator.** A digital evaluator assesses the design, implementation
and outcomes of a digital product or service, and whether it delivers value for the
public and society. Some are also members of a government analytical profession where
evaluation is a core skill. In this role you will:

- work closely with delivery and policy teams
- develop theories of change for products and services, explaining the links between
  activities, outputs, intended outcomes and unintended effects
- follow established monitoring and evaluation methods and government guidance
- produce evaluation plans
- design quantitative and qualitative research
- collect and analyse data
- communicate findings, including by publishing reports

**Your level: digital evaluator.** You support the evaluation of products and services,
with a good understanding of the role and some practical experience. The framework
expects that you:

- work with a more senior digital evaluator to plan and deliver evaluations
- manage the day-to-day development of monitoring, evaluation and analytical tools
- support the design of quantitative and qualitative research
- help with research activities and other data collection
- report on the performance of products and services
- communicate findings of evaluations, for example through written reports
- receive guidance and training to support your work and develop your skills

**Where this level sits.** This role has five levels:

1. Junior digital evaluator
2. **Digital evaluator** — you are here
3. Senior digital evaluator
4. Lead digital evaluator
5. Head of digital evaluation

Your next step is senior digital evaluator: managing evaluations, developing theories of
change and evaluation plans with stakeholders, managing relationships with user
researchers and performance analysts, assuring other evaluators' work, and identifying
the resources evaluation needs.

**One thing worth knowing about this role.** *Evaluation delivery* — the skill most
obviously at the centre of the job — is worded **identically at your level and at junior
digital evaluator**, "with support" included. The published expectation for actually
delivering an evaluation does not change between the first two levels. What does change
is planning and strategy, communication, life cycle work, monitoring and quality
assurance. If you are delivering evaluation with materially less support than a junior
would, that is real and worth recording, because the wording will not show it.

**The seven skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Communicating analysis and insight
2. Data ethics and privacy
3. Evaluation delivery
4. Evaluation planning and strategy
5. Monitoring and evaluation across the product life cycle
6. Product and service monitoring
7. Quality assurance of data and analysis

## Job skills

Seven skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the level expects, what it
looks like in practice, and what changes at senior digital evaluator.

### Skill: Communicating analysis and insight

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: choose the appropriate medium
to communicate findings; shape and share communications relevant to the audience;
present analysis and visualisations in clear ways to both specialist and non-specialist
audiences, with support; and give tactical recommendations based on your analysis and
insight to inform decision making.

**What that looks like here.** Evaluation reports are frequently long and rarely read.
Choosing the medium sometimes means a one-page summary and a conversation instead of a
sixty-page document nobody opens.

**The next step.** At senior you turn complex data into compelling, actionable stories,
share communication skills across government, communicate **negative** as well as
positive information, and give strategic as well as tactical recommendations.

### Skill: Data ethics and privacy

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: apply fundamental principles
of data ethics and privacy in your work under supervision; and share data ethics and
privacy risks through appropriate channels.

**What that looks like here.** Evaluation often reaches people at difficult moments —
those who could not complete a service, or who were refused something. How you approach
them, what you promise, and what you do with what they tell you are the substance of
this skill, not a compliance step.

**The next step.** At senior you work with stakeholders to identify and address ethical
and privacy concerns, place them in organisational context, research developments, and
constructively challenge proposed policies.

### Skill: Evaluation delivery

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: evaluate interventions with
support, using techniques including process evaluation, impact evaluation, theory of
change, experimentation and economic evaluation; collect quantitative and qualitative
data with support; analyse and interpret data you have collected and data from other
sources with support; and use some analytical approaches, including descriptive and
inferential analysis.

**Same wording as junior digital evaluator.** The published expectation does not change
between the first two levels of this role.

**What that looks like here.** In practice most digital evaluators at your level are
running parts of an evaluation with a senior reviewing rather than directing. If that is
you, you are ahead of the wording.

**The next step.** At senior the support qualifiers drop away: you evaluate digital
services using appropriate techniques, collect and analyse data, and use a **wide range**
of analytical approaches.

### Skill: Evaluation planning and strategy

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: work with stakeholders to
understand their needs; translate stakeholder needs into potential research questions
and evaluation approaches; present options for monitoring and evaluation of products and
services; and influence decisions on evaluation plans with support.

**What that looks like here.** This is a real step up from junior, where all of it was
*describe*. Translating a stakeholder's hope into an answerable question is the craft —
and sometimes the honest translation is that the question cannot be answered with the
time and data available.

**The next step.** At senior you influence stakeholders to secure resources, identify
evaluation and evidence needs, ensure plans are appropriate and proportionate, consider
wider policy and practical constraints, and agree the approach with diverse stakeholders.

### Skill: Monitoring and evaluation across the product life cycle

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: ensure the team is measuring
performance as appropriate for the relevant service standard phase; manage the delivery
of evaluation of services or products at different phases; and plan and look at data
requirements in future phases.

**What that looks like here.** *Data requirements in future phases* is the item that
saves projects later. Deciding in beta what you will need to measure in live means the
data gets collected from the start rather than reconstructed afterwards, which is often
impossible.

**The next step.** Identical wording at senior digital evaluator.

### Skill: Product and service monitoring

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: develop a theory of change
for a product or service through discussion with stakeholders, with support; identify and
present options for measuring indicators to stakeholders; turn some identified theory of
change indicators into measurement frameworks for a digital service; and support the
development of a performance dashboard.

**What that looks like here.** Building a theory of change with stakeholders usually
surfaces that they disagree about the intended outcome. That is a finding in itself, and
often the most valuable part of the exercise.

**The next step.** At senior you develop theories of change without the support
qualifier, turn indicators into measurement frameworks, and work with performance
analysts to develop a dashboard reporting service analytics and other indicators.

### Skill: Quality assurance of data and analysis

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: identify appropriate ways to
collect, collate and prepare data; decide if data and analysis is accurate and fit for
purpose; perform data preparation and cleansing with limited guidance; undertake quality
assurance processes; and **peer-review colleagues' outputs from data analysis to ensure
quality**.

**What that looks like here.** Peer review is the addition at your level, and it is a
genuine responsibility — you are being asked to catch someone else's mistakes, which
requires both the skill to see them and the confidence to say so.

**The next step.** At senior you set up a system to get data ready for use, specify how
data should be cleansed and prepared, bring data together from different sources, and
communicate the limitations of data.
