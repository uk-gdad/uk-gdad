# Lead data scientist - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a lead data scientist in the UK Government Digital
and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set
out what you do now, where the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes a lead data scientist as a leader, coach and champion of data
science, with responsibility for managing and developing teams, building organisational
capability, and setting and communicating direction. It expects you to have experience
identifying opportunities to deploy extensive knowledge of data science techniques and
challenging the status quo; design and implement scalable outputs and products, driving
collaboration in support of organisational objectives; select the most appropriate tools
and technologies; **navigate ethical and data challenges**; communicate and present
effectively; and challenge delivery plans and priorities.

*Navigate* is a well-chosen verb for the ethics part. At this level the questions
rarely have clean answers, and the job is finding a defensible path rather than a rule.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Eight skills, each with an explanation, a
rating and some questions. Expect about 30 minutes.

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not the remit on your job
  description.
- **A 4 is a claim about other people** — that someone else's practice changed

## About this role level

**The role: data scientist.** Data science is a broad and fast-moving field spanning
maths, statistics, software engineering and communications. Data scientists often work
as part of a multidisciplinary team, using data and analytics to inform and achieve
organisational goals. In this role you will:

- be inquisitive
- explore and visualise data
- make recommendations to address complex problems and inform strategic and
  operational decision making
- use data ethically and appropriately
- be innovative and adaptable
- explore existing and new data using a range of statistical tools and techniques,
  such as machine learning and predictive analytics
- find patterns in data and transform them into organisational insight

**Your level: lead data scientist.** You are a leader, coach and champion of data
science, responsible for managing and developing teams, building organisational
capability, and setting and communicating direction. The framework expects that you:

- have experience in identifying opportunities to deploy extensive knowledge of data
  science techniques, and in challenging the status quo
- design and implement scalable data science outputs and products, driving
  collaboration with others in support of organisational objectives
- select the most appropriate tools and technologies
- navigate ethical and data challenges
- communicate and present effectively
- challenge delivery plans and priorities

**Where this level sits.** This role has six levels, published in this order:

1. Trainee data scientist
2. Associate data scientist
3. Data scientist
4. Principal data scientist
5. **Lead data scientist** — you are here
6. Head of data science

**Two things worth knowing about this role.**

First, **lead sits above principal here**, which is the reverse of most roles in this
framework. Worth knowing when comparing grades or adverts across professions.

Second, and more substantially: **four of your eight skills are worded more demandingly
than at head of data science.** Programming and build at head returns to the wording
used for an *associate*; data science innovation returns to the data scientist wording;
data engineering returns to a single line; and understanding product delivery returns
to the data scientist wording. On those four, your published expectation is the highest
in the role. That is how the framework publishes it, and it is worth knowing before any
conversation that assumes the ladder rises evenly.

**The eight skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Applied maths, statistics and scientific practices
2. Data engineering
3. Data ethics and privacy
4. Data science innovation
5. Delivering business impact
6. Developing data science capability
7. Programming and build (data science)
8. Understanding product delivery

## Job skills

Eight skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the level expects, what it
looks like in practice, and how it compares with head of data science.

### Skill: Applied maths, statistics and scientific practices

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: identify opportunities to
develop statistical insight, reports and models to support organisational objectives,
collaborating across the organisation; critique statistical analyses; use a variety of
analytics techniques such as data mining and prescriptive and predictive analytics for
complex analysis across the whole data life cycle; use model outputs to produce
evidence and help design services and policies; and understand a broad range of
statistical tools, particularly those deployed in the organisation, and help others use
them.

**Same wording at principal and at head of data science.** The top three levels share
this expectation.

**What that looks like here.** Critiquing analyses is what protects the organisation
from confident, wrong conclusions. At lead you also have to do it in a way that teaches
rather than deters.

### Skill: Data engineering

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: help identify data
engineering requirements for any data science product, working with data engineers and
data scientists to design and deliver those products effectively; understand the need
to cleanse and prepare data and put reusable processes and checks in place; and
understand a broad range of architectures, including cloud and on-premise, and the
manipulation and transformation tools deployed in the organisation, using them
appropriately and helping others use them.

**Same wording as principal — and more than at head of data science**, where this
returns to one line about working with data engineers.

**What that looks like here.** Knowing enough to specify what engineering support a
product needs before it is half-built, and enough to tell when your team is quietly
building infrastructure it should not own.

### Skill: Data ethics and privacy

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: oversee compliance with data
ethics standards and legislation across the organisation; develop, manage and
continuously improve an ethical framework within your area; develop a data ethics
culture, influencing others to act on it; and ensure data ethics is applied
appropriately when formulating, implementing and evaluating organisational policies and
programmes.

**What that looks like here.** This is a large jump from principal, where the wording
was working with stakeholders on concerns. *Culture* is the operative word — a framework
reaches the projects you see, a culture reaches the ones you do not.

### Skill: Data science innovation

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: be a leader in the data
science space; demonstrate in-depth knowledge of tools and techniques, used to solve
problems creatively and create opportunities for your team; act as a coach, inspiring
curiosity and creativity in others; demonstrate in-depth knowledge of your profession

**Same wording as principal — and more than at head of data science**, where the
wording returns to demonstrating practical knowledge.

genuinely hard, and it is a decision about how you spend time rather than something that

### Skill: Delivering business impact

### Skill: Developing data science capability

**What the framework expects at this level.** Six things: act as a leader or technical

### Skill: Programming and build (data science)

### Skill: Understanding product delivery

**What the framework expects at this level.** Six things: understand the differences
between delivery methods, such as Agile and waterfall, and set out how your team should
use and adapt them; lead a team through the different phases of the product delivery
life cycle; collaborate with the product manager to influence the direction of work;
identify and involve relevant teams to smoothly deliver products into the organisation,
ensuring they inform decision making; ensure products are monitored, maintained and
continually improved; and have oversight of any data science features implemented
within products or services.

**This is the most demanding delivery wording in the role — more than at head of data
science**, where it returns to the data scientist wording.

**What that looks like here.** *Oversight of any data science features implemented
within products or services* is a wide remit: models built by other teams inside their
products are still your concern.
