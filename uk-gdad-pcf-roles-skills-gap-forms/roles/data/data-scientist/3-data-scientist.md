# Data scientist - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a data scientist in the UK Government Digital and
Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out
what you do now, where the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this level as working **independently**, with a good
understanding of a range of topics including data science techniques, delivery methods
and stages such as minimum viable products, tools and technologies. It expects you to
develop complex solutions using a range of techniques while understanding the ethical
considerations; understand the role and benefits of data science in the organisation;
support capability building; collaborate on solutions; prepare and manipulate data and
perform complex analytics; and present and communicate effectively.

*Independently* is the word that marks this level. At associate the work was checked;
here it is yours, including the judgement about when something is good enough to act
on.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Eight skills, each with an explanation, a
rating and some questions. Expect about 30 minutes.

## How to fill in this form

You can fill this form in on screen: the boxes below take typing, the tick
boxes tick, and each skill has a rating to choose. Your answers are saved in
this browser, on this device, so you can close the page and come back to them.
Nothing is sent anywhere.

When you have finished, use Export TSV or Export JSON at the top of the
page to take your answers with you, or print the page, or copy them into
whatever your organisation uses for development conversations. Every question
is numbered, so answers travel back on their own. Clear answers empties the
form and deletes the saved copy, which is worth doing on a computer other
people use.

Worth knowing:

- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a finding.
- **Answer about the job you do**, not the one on the job description.
- **"I haven't had the chance" is a real answer**, and more useful than a generous
  reading of a thin example.
- **Do not paste real data.** Describe the work, not the contents.
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers, what they are
  used for, or how long they are kept, ask before you fill it in.

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not the remit on your job
  description.
- **3s are the shape of this level.** The framework's own word for you is
  *independently*. A 4 is a claim about other people — that someone else's practice
  changed because of you.

## About this role level

**The role: data scientist.** Data science is a broad and fast-moving field spanning
maths, statistics, software engineering and communications. Data scientists often work
as part of a multidisciplinary team, using data and analytics to inform and achieve
organisational goals. In this role you will:

- be inquisitive
- explore and visualise data
- make recommendations to address complex problems and inform strategic and
  operational decision making
- use data ethically and appropriately
- be innovative and adaptable
- explore existing and new data using a range of statistical tools and techniques,
  such as machine learning and predictive analytics
- find patterns in data and transform them into organisational insight

**Your level: data scientist.** You work independently and have a good understanding
of a range of topics, including data science techniques, delivery methods and stages,
tools and technologies. The framework expects that you:

- develop complex solutions using a range of data science techniques, while
  understanding any ethical considerations
- understand the role and benefits of data science within the organisation
- support capability building within the organisation
- collaborate with others to develop data science solutions and outputs
- prepare and manipulate data, and perform complex analytics
- present and communicate effectively

**Where this level sits.** This role has six levels:

1. Trainee data scientist
2. Associate data scientist
3. **Data scientist** — you are here
4. Senior data scientist
5. Lead data scientist
6. Principal data scientist

**Two things worth knowing about this role.**

First, *Delivering business impact* is worded **identically** at your level and at
associate. The published expectation for connecting your work to organisational value
does not rise when you become a data scientist — what rises is the complexity of what
you build and the independence with which you build it.

Second, *Data engineering* shrinks at your level. At associate it was four detailed
items about cleansing, architectures, and transformation tools; at yours it is one
line — work with data engineers and data scientists to design and provide products.
That is not a reduction in what you do, but it is what the framework publishes.

**The eight skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Applied maths, statistics and scientific practices
2. Data engineering
3. Data ethics and privacy
4. Data science innovation
5. Delivering business impact
6. Developing data science capability
7. Programming and build (data science)
8. Understanding product delivery

## Job skills

Eight skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the level expects, what
it looks like in practice, and what changes at senior data scientist.

### Skill: Applied maths, statistics and scientific practices

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: apply designated
quantitative techniques such as time series analysis, optimisation and simulation to
create and embed appropriate models for analysis and prediction; provide guidance on
matching data sources with relevant applied mathematics and statistical techniques to
meet analysis goals; apply appropriate statistical techniques to available data to
discover new relations and offer insight into research problems, helping improve
organisational processes and support decision making; and access and use the
statistical tools available within the organisation.

**What that looks like here.** *Embed* is the interesting verb — a model that lives in
a service rather than a notebook. And guidance to others on matching sources to
techniques means you are now the person asked whether an approach will work.

**The next step.** See your senior colleague's page — but be aware that this wording
is shared with the machine learning engineer role at senior level, which tells you
something about where the technical bar sits.

### Skill: Data engineering

**What the framework expects at this level.** One thing: work with data engineers and
data scientists to design and provide products for the organisation.

**A note on the wording.** This is shorter than the associate level's wording, which
covers cleansing processes, architectures and transformation tools in detail. Read
literally your published expectation is narrower. In practice most data scientists at
your level still do all of it, and if you do, that is worth recording.

**What that looks like here.** Knowing enough engineering to have a useful conversation
with an engineer — and enough to know when you should stop building infrastructure
yourself and ask.

### Skill: Data ethics and privacy

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: work with stakeholders to
identify and address ethical and privacy concerns; demonstrate and communicate how data
ethical issues fit into the wider organisational context; research developments in
data ethics and privacy to improve compliance and processes; and assess and
constructively challenge proposed data ethics policies.

**What that looks like here.** This is a steep rise from associate, where the wording
was applying principles under supervision. You now challenge policy, which means
having a defensible view rather than a concern.

**The next step.** Data ethics is one of the skills where this role expects real depth
from the middle levels upward — worth noticing, because engineering-heavy data
scientists often treat it as someone else's specialism.

### Skill: Data science innovation

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: demonstrate practical
knowledge of data science tools and techniques; develop data science solutions that
maximise insight; and identify opportunities for how data science can improve data
practices.

**What that looks like here.** *Practical* knowledge — used, not read about. And
spotting where data science could improve how the organisation handles data generally,
not only where it could answer a question.

**The next step.** At principal data scientist you are expected to be a leader in the data science
space, coach others into curiosity and creativity, keep in-depth knowledge of the profession current,
and challenge the status quo in the search for better ways of working.

### Skill: Developing data science capability

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: manage your continuing
professional development and link learning to objectives and organisational goals;
**support data science capability building across the team and wider organisation**;
confidently talk about the benefits of data science approaches to existing and
potential customers; and demonstrate a good understanding of a range of techniques such
as machine learning and natural language processing, using them to build solutions
including reports, models and dashboards.

**What that looks like here.** Supporting capability beyond your own team is the
addition at your level. Teaching a colleague to do something themselves is slower than
doing it for them and is the whole point.

**Identical wording at principal data scientist**, so this is a skill where you can already be working

### Skill: Programming and build (data science)

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: write moderate-to-complex
programs and scripts; and show a good understanding of testing methodologies and how to
deploy code.

**What that looks like here.** *How to deploy code* is the addition. A data scientist
who can get their own work into production, or at least talk sensibly with whoever
does, is a different proposition from one who hands over a notebook.

**The next step.** At principal you write complex programs and scripts, seek to make code open source
where appropriate, supervise junior analysts and set coding standards for your team, understand
software architecture and how to write efficient, optimised code, and perform user testing before
launch.

### Skill: Understanding product delivery

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: understand the differences
between delivery methods, such as Agile and waterfall, and choose the most appropriate
method to deliver each product; define the minimum viable product and support
decisions about priorities; and work with specialists in multidisciplinary teams to
smoothly deliver data science products into the organisation.

**What that looks like here.** Defining a minimum viable product for data science is
harder than for software, because you often do not know whether the thing is possible
until you have tried. Being honest about that uncertainty while still committing to
something is the skill.

**Identical wording at principal data scientist.**
