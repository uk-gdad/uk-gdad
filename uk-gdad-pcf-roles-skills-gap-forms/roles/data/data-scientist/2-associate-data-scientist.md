# Associate data scientist - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as an associate data scientist in the UK Government
Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you
can set out what you do now, where the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes an associate data scientist as inquisitive, curious about
data, and keen to learn and develop — the same words it uses for a trainee. What
changes is the accountability list: you now understand the basics of software
development and analytical methods and can develop basic models; understand and
implement a range of data science techniques including machine learning; collaborate
with others on solutions; prepare and manipulate data and perform analytics; present
and communicate effectively; be aware of project delivery methods; be aware of ethical
considerations; and have knowledge of the technologies used.

This is the level where you start producing rather than assisting. The models are
basic, but they are yours.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Eight skills, each with an explanation, a
rating and some questions. Expect about 30 minutes.

## About this role level

**The role: data scientist.** Data science is a broad and fast-moving field spanning
maths, statistics, software engineering and communications. Data scientists often work
as part of a multidisciplinary team, using data and analytics to inform and achieve
organisational goals. In this role you will:

- be inquisitive
- explore and visualise data
- make recommendations to address complex problems and inform strategic and
  operational decision making
- use data ethically and appropriately
- be innovative and adaptable
- explore existing and new data using a range of statistical tools and techniques,
  such as machine learning and predictive analytics
- find patterns in data and transform them into organisational insight

**Your level: associate data scientist.** You are inquisitive, curious about data and
keen to learn and develop. The framework expects that you:

- understand the basics of software development and analytical methods, and can
  develop basic models
- understand and can implement a range of data science techniques, including machine
  learning
- collaborate with others to develop data science solutions and outputs supporting
  the organisation
- prepare and manipulate data, and perform analytics
- present and communicate effectively
- be aware of project delivery methods
- be aware of ethical considerations
- have knowledge of the technologies used

**Where this level sits.** This role has six levels — the longest ladder in the data
profession:

1. Trainee data scientist
2. **Associate data scientist** — you are here
3. Data scientist
4. Senior data scientist
5. Lead data scientist
6. Principal data scientist

Your next step is data scientist: working independently, developing complex solutions,
and supporting capability building in the organisation.

**One thing worth knowing about this role.** *Delivering business impact* is worded
**identically** at your level and at data scientist. The published expectation for
connecting your work to organisational value does not change when you become a data
scientist, which means what changes is the complexity of what you build and how
independently you build it — not how you justify it.

**The eight skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Applied maths, statistics and scientific practices
2. Data engineering
3. Data ethics and privacy
4. Data science innovation
5. Delivering business impact
6. Developing data science capability
7. Programming and build (data science)
8. Understanding product delivery

## Job skills

Eight skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the level expects, what
it looks like in practice, and what changes at data scientist.

### Skill: Applied maths, statistics and scientific practices

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: apply analytical methods
including exploratory data analysis and statistical testing to a specific data set, to
reach accurate and reliable conclusions; understand and use different performance and
accuracy metrics for model validation, hypothesis testing and information retrieval;
compare selected applied mathematics and statistical methods and identify their
differences; and access and use the statistical and scientific tools available within
the organisation.

**What that looks like here.** Choosing a metric that reflects what the model is
actually for. Accuracy is a poor guide when the thing you are predicting is rare, and
picking the wrong metric is how a useless model looks impressive.

**The next step.** At data scientist you apply designated quantitative techniques such
as time series analysis, optimisation and simulation, guide others on matching data
sources to techniques, and use statistical techniques to discover new relations.

### Skill: Data engineering

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: identify the appropriate
engineering support to design and provide products for the organisation; recognise the
processes and tools for cleansing and preparing data, and put reusable processes and
checks in place; use different architectures, including cloud and on-premise; and use
data manipulation and transformation tools.

**What that looks like here.** *Reusable processes and checks* is the item worth
taking seriously. Cleaning done once by hand has to be done again; cleaning written as
a repeatable step is a small piece of infrastructure.

**The next step.** At data scientist this shrinks to one line — working with data
engineers and data scientists to design and provide products for the organisation. The
detailed expectation is at your level.

### Skill: Data ethics and privacy

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: apply fundamental principles
of data ethics and privacy in your work under supervision; and share data ethics and
privacy risks through appropriate channels.

**What that looks like here.** Noticing when a model would treat groups differently,
when a linkage makes people identifiable, or when data collected for one purpose is
being used for another — and raising it rather than assuming someone senior already
thought about it.

**The next step.** At data scientist you work with stakeholders to address ethical and
privacy concerns, place them in organisational context, research developments, and
constructively challenge proposed policies. That is a large jump.

### Skill: Data science innovation

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: adopt an inquisitive and
curious approach to data; seek out and research new data science techniques to support
learning; ask questions to improve your knowledge and learn about data science norms;
and see possibilities for improvements and innovation.

**What that looks like here.** *Data science norms* is the interesting phrase — the
conventions about how work is done, reviewed and reported, which are learned by asking
rather than reading.

**The next step.** At data scientist you demonstrate practical knowledge of tools and
techniques, develop solutions that maximise insight, and identify opportunities for
data science to improve data practices.

### Skill: Delivering business impact

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: show an understanding of
the organisation and the benefits of data science; collaborate to help identify user
needs and develop and deliver data science products; and communicate effectively and
present analysis and visualisations tailored to your audience.

**Identical wording at data scientist.** This skill does not change between your level
and the next.

**What that looks like here.** *Tailored to your audience* is the practical half —
the same finding needs different framing for an analyst, a policy colleague and a
director, without changing what is true.

### Skill: Developing data science capability

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: manage your continuing
professional development and link your learning to objectives and organisational
goals; confidently talk about the benefits of data science approaches to existing and
potential customers; and demonstrate a good understanding of key techniques such as
machine learning, using them to build solutions including reports, models and
dashboards.

**What that looks like here.** Your development plan is now yours to manage rather
than something your manager helps you create. Linking it to organisational goals is
what turns "I would like to learn this" into a fundable request.

**The next step.** At data scientist you also support capability building across the
team and wider organisation, and understand a broader range of techniques including

### Skill: Programming and build (data science)

### Skill: Understanding product delivery

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: show an awareness of the
differences between delivery methods, such as Agile and waterfall, and propose the most
appropriate method to deliver each product; and manage your contribution to tasks to
fit in with the work of your wider team.

**What that looks like here.** Knowing enough about how work gets organised to say
when an approach does not suit a data science problem — research with an uncertain
outcome does not fit neatly into fixed two-week commitments, and saying so early helps.

**The next step.** At data scientist you understand the differences and *choose* the
method, define the minimum viable product and support prioritisation, and work with
specialists to deliver products into the organisation.
