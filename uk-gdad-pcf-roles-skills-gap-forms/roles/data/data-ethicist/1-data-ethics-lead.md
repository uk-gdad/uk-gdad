# Data ethics lead - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a data ethics lead in the UK Government
Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that
you can set out what you do now, where the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes a data ethicist as someone who assesses the societal
effect of technology and data and produces recommendations for other data
professionals — thinking about fairness, accountability, the law, moral dilemmas
and risks in the creation of technology and data products and policies. At your
level you support the head of data ethics to enable others across the organisation
to understand data ethics and implement best practice in their work.

This is one of the few roles in the framework whose subject matter is other
people's judgement. You rarely build anything, and your influence depends almost
entirely on whether people bring you a question early or tell you about it
afterwards.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Ten skills, each with an explanation, a
rating and some questions. Expect about 35 minutes.

When you have finished, use Export TSV or Export JSON at the top of the
page to take your answers with you, or print the page, or copy them into

is numbered, so answers travel back on their own. Clear answers empties the

people use.

Worth knowing:

- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a finding.
- **Answer about the job you do**, not the one on the job description.
- **"I haven't had the chance" is a real answer**, and more useful than a
  generous reading of a thin example.
- **Be careful how you describe live cases.** Some of your work concerns decisions
  that are contested, unresolved, or about identifiable people. Describe the
  work at a level you would be comfortable saying in a corridor, and do not name
  anyone.
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers, what they
  are used for, or how long they are kept, ask before you fill it in.

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not the remit on your job
  description.
- **A 4 is a claim about other people** — that someone else's practice changed
  because of you and would stay changed if you left. In a role that exists to
  change other people's practice, that is a fair test.

## About this role level

**The role: data ethicist.** A data ethicist assesses the societal effect of
technology and data, and produces recommendations for other data professionals.
This involves thinking about fairness, accountability, the law, moral dilemmas and
risks in the creation of technology and data products and policies. In this role
you will:

- provide research and expertise on data ethics
- enable others to implement data ethics best practice in their work — through
  training, advising data science teams, or demonstrating how to apply ethical
  principles through examples and case studies
- communicate effectively to explain and raise awareness of data ethics issues,
  and to listen to, convene, advise and mediate between parts of the organisation
- help people to ask questions, express concerns and discuss ethical dilemmas

**Your level: data ethics lead.** You support the head of data ethics to enable
others across the organisation to understand data ethics and implement best
practice in their work.

**Where this level sits.** This role has two levels only:

1. **Data ethics lead** — you are here
2. Head of data ethics

Your next step is head of data ethics: leading the development and implementation
of organisational data ethics policies.

**Two things worth knowing about this role.**

First, unlike almost every other role in the framework, **neither level publishes a
list of accountabilities**. There is a role description, a one-sentence level
description, and then the skills. Everything about what you actually do day to day
is local to your organisation — which makes this form's first section unusually
important, because nobody else has written that down.

Second, four of your ten skills are worded **identically** at head of data
ethics — communication (data ethics), empathy and inclusivity, managing decisions
and risks, and problem management. Those four do not distinguish the two levels at
all.

**The ten skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Analysis and synthesis (data ethics)
2. Applied social sciences
3. Communicating between the technical and non-technical
4. Communication (data ethics)
5. Data ethics and privacy
6. Empathy and inclusivity
7. Managing decisions and risks
8. Problem management
9. Product ownership (data ethics)

## Job skills

Ten skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the level expects, what

### Skill: Analysis and synthesis (data ethics)

**What the framework expects at this level.** Six things: draw together, analyse
and evaluate qualitative and quantitative data and information; quickly read and
interpret complex documents from a range of sources and distil what is relevant;
turn research data into clear findings that inform decisions; effectively involve
colleagues in analysis and synthesis to increase consensus and challenge
assumptions; advise on the choice and application of techniques and critique
colleagues' findings to assure best practice; and help teams define their project
outcomes and ethical considerations, and integrate ethical diagnostics and
assessment.

**What that looks like here.** Reading a model specification, an equality impact
assessment and a policy document in an afternoon and saying what matters in them.
*Involve colleagues to increase consensus* is the part that makes findings stick —
a conclusion people helped reach is harder to set aside.

**The next step.** At head you do this expertly, produce findings for the entire
organisation, build capacity in others, and help the organisation continually assure
and improve its practices.

### Skill: Applied social sciences

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: explain social
sciences — such as anthropology, economics, sociology, philosophy, psychology or
race theory — and apply social science theories to inform data projects, products
and policies; evaluate and challenge assumptions made in data science projects; and
work with academics and external researchers, describing emerging theories and
concepts.

**What that looks like here.** Being able to say *why* a proxy variable is a poor
stand-in for what a model is really trying to measure, in terms a data scientist
recognises as substantive rather than philosophical.

**The next step.** At head you explain complex concepts across the social sciences,
apply theories to strategic oversight, identify and use best practice, and work
with academics to publish research on applied digital ethics.

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: listen to and interpret
the needs of technical and non-technical stakeholders, and manage their
expectations; manage active and reactive communication; and support or host
difficult discussions within the team or with diverse senior stakeholders.

**What that looks like here.** *Reactive* communication in data ethics usually
means something has gone public, or is about to. Being ready with a position that
is honest rather than defensive is the difference between being consulted next time
and not.

**The next step.** At head you mediate at all levels, moderate difficult
discussions about high-risk topics under time pressure, and represent the community
to large audiences inside and outside the organisation.

### Skill: Communication (data ethics)

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: expertly translate
technical concepts to non-technical audiences so they are understood by all;
demonstrate a good understanding of how technology and data products and services
are built; understand technical jargon and have enough knowledge to hold meaningful
conversations with data science experts — on issues such as minimising bias in data,
or gathering, collecting, cleansing, triangulating and reusing data; and effectively
support data scientists and engineers in implementing data ethics.

**What that looks like here.** Credibility in both directions. A data ethicist who
cannot follow a conversation about how a model was trained gets politely managed;
one who can gets consulted.

**The next step.** Identical wording at head of data ethics. This is the top
published expectation for the skill in this role.

### Skill: Data ethics and privacy

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: work with stakeholders
to identify and address ethical and privacy concerns; demonstrate and communicate
how data ethical issues fit into the wider organisational context; research
developments in data ethics and privacy to improve compliance and processes; and
assess and constructively challenge proposed data ethics policies.

**What that looks like here.** *Constructively challenge* means engaging with a
proposed policy on its merits and saying where it will not work — which is harder
than either agreeing or objecting, and is what makes you worth having.

**The next step.** At head you oversee compliance with data ethics standards and
legislation across the organisation, develop and continuously improve an ethical
framework, develop a data ethics culture, and ensure ethics is applied when
formulating and evaluating policies and programmes.

### Skill: Empathy and inclusivity

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: incorporate a wide
variety of views from underrepresented groups into product and policy work, using
in-depth consulting and outreach strategies; be involved in the wider organisational
diversity and inclusion plan; draw on your multidisciplinary background and personal
experience to understand the consequences of data systems on a diverse range of
stakeholders; and demonstrate a thorough understanding of social issues, types of
bias and discrimination different groups can face, and use this to inform your data
ethics work.

**What that looks like here.** Outreach is the item that needs time and budget.
Consulting people who are affected by a system, rather than reasoning about them, is
the difference between ethics work and a well-argued document.

**The next step.** Identical wording at head of data ethics.

### Skill: Problem management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: ensure that the right
actions are taken to investigate, resolve and anticipate problems; and co-ordinate
the team to investigate problems, implement solutions and take preventive measures.

**What that looks like here.** In an ethics context a problem is usually a system
behaving in a way nobody intended for a group nobody considered. Anticipating means
asking who a system will work badly for before it is built.

### Skill: Product ownership (data ethics)

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: develop data ethics tools
and translate theoretical principles into practice; use a range of product
management principles and approaches; consider new ways of working and adapt to
change; capture and translate user needs into deliverables; and demonstrate
familiarity with feedback gathering, evaluation mechanisms and product promotion.

**What that looks like here.** Ethics guidance is a product with users — data
scientists, product managers, policy colleagues. Guidance nobody uses has failed,
however sound its reasoning, and finding out why it is not used is product work.

**The next step.** At head you define and create organisation-wide tools, show
expertise in product management principles, set new ways of working, and show
expertise in feedback gathering, evaluation and promotion.

### Skill: Stakeholder relationship management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: work with the team to
develop and maintain an understanding of stakeholders; work with the team to develop
and implement stakeholder communications strategies; identify and resolve issues,
influence stakeholders and manage relationships effectively; and build long-term
strategic relationships, communicating clearly and regularly.

**What that looks like here.** In this role relationships *are* the mechanism. There
is no process that forces anyone to consult you, so being someone people want to
talk to early is the job's infrastructure.

**The next step.** At head you direct the stakeholder relationship strategy for your
teams, ensure stakeholders' objectives are set, and negotiate with senior
stakeholders to unblock progress.
