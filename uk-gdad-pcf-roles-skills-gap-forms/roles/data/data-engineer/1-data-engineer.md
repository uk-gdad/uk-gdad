# Data engineer - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a data engineer in the UK Government Digital
and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can
set out what you do now, where the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes a data engineer as someone who develops and constructs
data products and services, and integrates them into systems and business
processes. At this level you deliver the designs set by more senior members of the
data engineering community. It expects you to implement data flows connecting
operational systems, analytics and business intelligence systems; document
source-to-target mappings; re-engineer manual data flows so they scale and repeat;
support the build of data streaming systems; write extract, transform and load
scripts that perform well; develop reusable business intelligence reports; and
build accessible data for analysis.

That is the most concrete accountability list in the whole data profession. This
is a building job, and this level is where the pipelines actually get written.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Ten skills, each with an explanation, a
rating and some questions. Expect about 35 minutes.

## About this role level

**The role: data engineer.** A data engineer develops and constructs data products
and services, and integrates them into systems and business processes. Unlike most
roles in this framework, there is no separate list of things the role does in
general — the accountabilities are all at the level.

**Your level: data engineer.** You deliver the designs set by more senior members
of the data engineering community. The framework expects that you:

- implement data flows to connect operational systems, data for analytics and
  business intelligence systems
- document source-to-target mappings
- re-engineer manual data flows to enable scaling and repeatable use
- support the build of data streaming systems
- write extract, transform and load scripts and code so the process performs
  optimally
- develop business intelligence reports that can be reused
- build accessible data for analysis

**Where this level sits.** This role has four levels:

1. **Data engineer** — you are here
2. Senior data engineer
3. Lead data engineer
4. Head of data engineering

Your next step is senior data engineer: designing the flows rather than delivering
someone else's design, leading the build of streaming systems, optimising code, and
leading work on database management.

**Two things worth knowing about this role.**

First, *Testing* is worded **identically at all four levels** of this role. A head
of data engineering has the same published testing expectation as you do. If your
work involves designing a testing approach rather than defining conditions for one,
you are already beyond the published wording — worth recording, because it will
never show as progression.

Second, one item in your accountabilities is easy to skim past: *build accessible
data for analysis*. Accessible here means findable and usable by the people who
need it, not locked behind knowledge only you have. It is the difference between a
pipeline and a data product.

**The ten skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Communicating between the technical and non-technical
2. Data analysis and synthesis
3. Data development process
4. Data innovation
5. Data integration design
6. Data modelling
7. Metadata management
8. Problem management
9. Programming and build (data and analytics engineering)
10. Testing

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: explain why it is
important to communicate technical concepts in non-technical language; and explain
the types of communication that can be used with internal and external
stakeholders, and their impact.

**What that looks like here.** This is the lowest published wording of the ten
skills at your level, which is worth noticing: the framework does not expect a data
engineer at this level to be the one negotiating with stakeholders. In practice
many are, because the pipeline breaking is immediately everyone's problem.

**The next step.** At senior you communicate effectively with both groups, host
discussions in a multidisciplinary team with difficult dynamics, advocate for the
team externally, and manage differing stakeholder perspectives.

### Skill: Data analysis and synthesis

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: undertake data profiling
and source system analysis; and present clear insights to colleagues to support the
end use of the data.

**What that looks like here.** Profiling before you build, so you find out that the
key is not unique before the pipeline does. Source system analysis means
understanding how the operational system actually behaves — including the batch job
at 2am that rewrites yesterday's rows.

**The next step.** Identical wording at senior data engineer.

### Skill: Data development process

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: design, build and test
data products based on feeds from multiple systems, using a range of different
storage technologies, access methods or both; and create repeatable and reusable
products.

**What that looks like here.** *Repeatable and reusable* is the difference between
engineering and scripting. A pipeline that runs from a schedule, recovers from
failure and can be pointed at a new source is a product; one that you rerun by hand
is a chore.

**The next step.** At senior you design, build and test data products that are
complex or large scale, and build teams to complete data integration services.

### Skill: Data integration design

**What the framework expects at this level.** One thing: deliver data solutions in
accordance with agreed organisational standards that ensure services are resilient,
scalable and future-proof.

**What that looks like here.** You build to the standard rather than setting it.
Resilient means it survives an upstream outage; scalable means ten times the volume
does not break it; future-proof mostly means it is not tied to one vendor's quirk
or one person's memory.

**The next step.** At senior you select and implement the appropriate technologies
to deliver resilient, scalable and future-proofed solutions — the choice becomes
yours.

### Skill: Data modelling

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: explain the concepts
and principles of data modelling; produce, maintain and update relevant data models
for an organisation's specific needs; and reverse-engineer data models from a live
system.

**What that looks like here.** Reverse-engineering is a daily reality — working out
how a source is structured from the thing itself, because the documentation is
missing or wrong.

**A note on the wording.** This exact text also appears at **head of data
engineering**, the top level of this role, while senior and lead have more
demanding wording. That is how the framework publishes it. It means this skill does
not describe a progression in the usual way, and the useful comparison for you is
with senior rather than with the top.

**The next step.** At senior you produce models across multiple subject areas,
explain which model suits which purpose, understand industry-recognised patterns and
standards and when to apply them, and compare and align different models.

### Skill: Metadata management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: use metadata repositories
to complete complex tasks such as data and systems integration impact analysis; and
maintain a metadata repository to ensure information remains accurate and up to
date.

**What that looks like here.** Impact analysis is the practical use: if this column
changes, what downstream breaks? Without real metadata that question is answered by
searching code and hoping.

**The next step.** At senior you design an appropriate repository, suggest

### Skill: Problem management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: investigate problems in
systems, processes and services, with an understanding of the level of a problem —
strategic, tactical or operational; and contribute to the implementation of
remedies and preventative measures.

**What that looks like here.** A failed run at 3am is operational. The same
pipeline failing every month is tactical. Failing because the source has no reliable
change indicator is strategic, and no amount of retry logic will fix it.

**The next step.** At senior you initiate and monitor investigation of patterns and
trends, consult specialists, determine the resolution and assist with
implementation, and determine preventative measures.

### Skill: Testing

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: review requirements and
specifications, and define test conditions; identify issues and risks associated
with work; and analyse and report test activities and results.

**Identical wording at all four levels of this role.** Yours is the same published
expectation as a head of data engineering — unusually demanding for an entry level,
and worth reading as the framework being ambitious rather than you falling short.

**What that looks like here.** Deciding what "correct" means for a pipeline: row
counts within tolerance, keys unique, no future dates, totals reconciling to source.
Untested pipelines fail silently, which is worse than failing loudly.

## Legal compliance

access in the organisation and can create combinations of data that did not
previously exist. These questions ask about awareness, training and support, not
about whether you can state the law correctly.
