# Head of data engineering - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you are a head of data engineering in the UK Government
Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you
can set out what you do now, where the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this role as leading multi-functional delivery teams to
deliver robust data services for your department, other government departments and
private sector partners. It expects you to inspire best practice for data products
and services within your teams; build data engineering capability by providing
technical leadership and career development for the community; and work with other
senior team members to identify, plan, develop and deliver data services.

*Other government departments and private sector partners* is the phrase that
enlarges this job. Your services have customers outside your own organisation, which
means expectations you did not set and dependencies you cannot escalate internally.

Head of data engineering is the top of this role. Nothing here is scored, marked or
ranked. Ten skills, each with an explanation, a rating and some questions. Expect
about 35 minutes.

## How to fill in this form

This is a static web page. There is nothing to type into and nothing to send from
here — your answers never touch this site.

Print it, copy the questions into a document, or paste them into whatever your
organisation uses for development conversations. Every question is numbered, so
answers travel back on their own.

Worth knowing:

- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a finding.
- **"The community does this" is not an answer to "what do you do".** Name your
  part.
- **"I haven't had the chance" is a real answer** at this level too.
- **Do not paste real data, credentials or connection strings.**
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers, what they are
  used for, or how long they are kept, ask before you fill it in.

## Rating scale

Where a question asks for a rating, use this scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | You have not worked with this |
| 1 | Awareness | You know what it is and why it matters |
| 2 | Working | You do it with support |
| 3 | Practitioner | You do it unsupported, and handle the usual exceptions |
| 4 | Expert | You set the approach and develop others in it |

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not the remit on your job
  description.
- **A 4 is a claim about other people** — that someone else's practice changed
  because of you and would stay changed if you left. The honest test here is
  whether the practice you have inspired would survive your departure.

A filled-in answer looks like this:

> **Problem management**
>
> - [x] 3 — Practitioner: I do it unsupported, and handle the usual exceptions
>
> *Evidence:* I run our quarterly review of systemic causes and two classes of
> failure have gone away as a result. Building problem-solving capability in others
> is something I do by example rather than deliberately.

Named practice, and a clear line about where it stops.

## About this role level

**The role: data engineer.** A data engineer develops and constructs data products
and services, and integrates them into systems and business processes.

**Your level: head of data engineering.** You lead multi-functional delivery teams
to deliver robust data services for your department, other government departments
and private sector partners. The framework expects that you:

- inspire best practice for data products and services within your teams
- build data engineering capability by providing technical leadership and career
  development for the community
- work with other senior team members to identify, plan, develop and deliver data
  services

**There is no level above this one.** This role has four levels and this is the
highest. Where people go next is a question about other roles — data leadership, a
chief data officer role, or leadership outside the profession — and the framework
does not define it as a next step, so this form does not invent one.

**Where this level sits:**

1. Data engineer
2. Senior data engineer
3. Lead data engineer
4. **Head of data engineering** — you are here

**Two things worth knowing about this role.**

First, four of your ten skills do not distinguish your level from lead: data
analysis and synthesis, data development process and data integration design are
worded identically, and *Testing* is worded identically at **every** level of this
role, including the entry level.

Second, and more striking: **your published wording for *Data modelling* is the same
as the entry level's**, and less demanding than senior's or lead's. That is how the
framework publishes it. If you are setting modelling direction for the organisation
— and at this level you almost certainly are — you are working well beyond the
published expectation, and that is worth recording rather than assuming it is
understood.

**The ten skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Communicating between the technical and non-technical
2. Data analysis and synthesis
3. Data development process
4. Data innovation
5. Data integration design
6. Data modelling
7. Metadata management
8. Problem management
9. Programming and build (data and analytics engineering)
10. Testing

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What the framework expects.** Three things: mediate between people and strengthen
relationships, adopting the appropriate communication method with stakeholders at
all levels; manage stakeholder expectations and moderate difficult discussions about
high-risk and complex topics, even within constrained timescales; and speak on
behalf of, and represent the community to, large audiences inside and outside the
organisation.

**What that looks like here.** When a shared service fails, the difficult
conversation is with another department that has no reason to be patient. Doing that
without either over-promising or hiding behind process is most of the skill.

### Skill: Metadata management

**What the framework expects.** Three things: identify how metadata repositories can
support different areas of the organisation; communicate the value of metadata
repositories; and set up robust governance processes to keep repositories up to
date.

**What that looks like here.** Most organisations have tried a catalogue and most
have one that is out of date. The governance question — who owns each entry, and
what happens when it goes stale — is what separates attempts that last from those
that become an embarrassment.
