# Lead analytics engineer - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a lead analytics engineer in the UK Government
Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that
you can set out what you do now, where the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this level as leading the design and deployment of data
models for analysis. It expects you to lead and support a team of analytics
engineers to design, build and maintain models; work with stakeholders and teams
across the organisation to understand the relationships between data and
organisational processes, and use that to define data requirements; promote, build
awareness and support understanding of analytics engineering; review the work of
other analytics engineers; create standards for communication, data models and
documentation; and define and improve ways of working in the team.

*Promote, build awareness and support understanding of analytics engineering* is
the one people skip past. Analytics engineering is a newer role than the ones
around it, and a good deal of this job is explaining what it is for.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Eight skills, each with an explanation, a
rating and some questions. Expect about 30 minutes.

## About this role level

**The role: analytics engineer.** An analytics engineer transforms data relevant
to the organisation into structures that enable analysis and decision making. In
this role you will:

- work with subject matter experts to understand organisational processes and
  translate these into data structures optimised for analysis
- work with data users to design and build data models they can use, using
  modelling techniques such as Kimball or Inmon
- support data quality improvement
- develop standards for data transformation
- create and maintain data documentation
- refine requirements in response to feedback and changes in the organisation
- provide ongoing support to users

**Your level: lead analytics engineer.** You lead the design and deployment of data
models for analysis. The framework expects that you:

- lead and support a team of analytics engineers to design, build and maintain
  data models
- work with stakeholders and teams across the organisation to understand
  relationships between data and organisational processes, and use this to define
  data requirements
- promote, build awareness and support understanding of analytics engineering
- review the work of other analytics engineers
- create standards for communication, data models and documentation
- define and improve ways of working in the team

**Where this level sits.** This role has five levels:

1. Trainee analytics engineer
2. Analytics engineer
3. Senior analytics engineer
4. **Lead analytics engineer** — you are here
5. Head of analytics engineering

Your next step is head of analytics engineering: building relationships with senior
stakeholders to determine what the organisation needs, aligning the team to
strategic priorities, advocating for the role to senior leadership and other
organisations, building capability and careers for the community, and ensuring the
right technology is available.

**One thing worth knowing about this role.** Four of your eight skills are worded
**identically** at senior analytics engineer — data modelling, metadata management,
programming and build, and testing. Those four do not distinguish your level from
the one below at all. What is yours rather than senior's: communicating (managing
expectations and hosting difficult discussions with senior stakeholders), data
analysis and synthesis (conformed models across multiple sources), data innovation
(judging timing for adoption), and problem management (ensuring and co-ordinating
rather than doing).

**The eight skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Communicating between the technical and non-technical
2. Data analysis and synthesis
3. Data innovation
4. Data modelling, cleansing and enrichment
5. Metadata management
6. Problem management
7. Programming and build (data and analytics engineering)
8. Testing

## Job skills

Eight skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the level expects,
what it looks like in practice, and what changes at head of analytics engineering.

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: listen to and interpret
the needs of technical and non-technical stakeholders, and manage their
expectations; manage active and reactive communication; and support or host
difficult discussions within the team or with diverse senior stakeholders.

**What that looks like here.** *Interpret* is the operative word — turning "we need
better reporting" into something buildable, without either taking it literally or
substituting your own idea of what they meant.

**The next step.** At head you mediate between people and strengthen relationships
at all levels, moderate difficult discussions about high-risk topics within
constrained timescales, and represent the community to large audiences inside and
outside the organisation.

### Skill: Data analysis and synthesis

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: understand and help
teams to apply a range of techniques for data profiling; source system analysis
from a complex single source; and bring multiple data sources together in a
conformed model for analysis.

**What that looks like here.** A conformed model means shared dimensions and agreed
definitions across sources, so that "customer" or "case" means the same thing
everywhere. Getting there is usually a negotiation between teams rather than a
technical exercise.

**The next step.** Identical wording at head of analytics engineering.

### Skill: Data innovation

**What the framework expects at this level.** One thing: identify areas of
innovation in data tools and techniques, and recognise appropriate timing for
adoption.

**What that looks like here.** *Timing* is the whole skill. Adopting too early
means being the organisation that debugged someone else's product; too late means a
migration under pressure. Being able to say "not yet, and here is what would change
my mind" is the mark of doing this well.

**The next step.** At head you investigate emerging trends, perform horizon
scanning for the organisation, and introduce innovative ways of working.

### Skill: Data modelling, cleansing and enrichment

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: build and review complex
data models, ensuring adherence to standards; use data integration tools and
languages to integrate and store data, and advise teams on best practice; ensure
data for analysis meets quality standards and is interoperable, enabling reuse; and
work with other data professionals to improve modelling and integration patterns
and standards.

**Same wording as senior analytics engineer.**

**What that looks like here.** At lead the difference is that *ensuring* quality and
interoperability is now a matter of standards and review across a team rather than
your own care.

**The next step.** At head you understand different ways to model data to maximise
value, ensure modelling standards exist and are complied with, ensure teams have
support and training for integration methods, and build relationships with senior
data professionals across fields to improve practice.

### Skill: Metadata management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: design an appropriate
metadata repository; suggest changes to improve current repositories; understand a
range of tools for storing and working with metadata; and advise less experienced
members of the team about metadata management.

**Same wording as senior analytics engineer.**

**What that looks like here.** At lead the practical question is whether metadata is
generated as a by-product of the work or maintained as a separate chore. The first
survives; the second decays.

**The next step.** At head you identify how repositories can support different areas
of the organisation, communicate their value, and set up robust governance to keep
them up to date.

### Skill: Problem management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: ensure that the right
actions are taken to investigate, resolve and anticipate problems; and co-ordinate
the team to investigate problems, implement solutions and take preventive measures.

**What that looks like here.** You are now accountable for problems being handled
rather than for handling them, which means resisting the pull to fix things
yourself when you can see the answer.

**The next step.** At head you anticipate problems and defend against them at the
right time, understand how a problem fits the larger picture, help others describe
problems, and build problem-solving capability in others.

### Skill: Programming and build (data and analytics engineering)

scripts from agreed specifications and subsequent iterations; and collaborate with
others to review specifications where appropriate.

**Same wording as senior analytics engineer.**

**What that looks like here.** Most leads write less code than they used to and
review more. Whether that is the right balance depends on your team's size and
whether your hands-on knowledge is still current enough to review well.

**The next step.** At head you set local or team-based standards for tools and
techniques, select development methods, advise on standards and ensure compliance,
