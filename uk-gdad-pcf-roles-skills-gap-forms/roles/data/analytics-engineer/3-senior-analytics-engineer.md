# Senior analytics engineer - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a senior analytics engineer in the UK
Government Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent
it so that you can set out what you do now, where the gaps are, and what would
close them.

The framework describes this level as overseeing the development and testing of
data models, including directing the work of other analytics engineers. It expects
you to oversee tasks and support less senior colleagues; build relationships with
stakeholders within a defined area; coach and mentor less senior analytics
engineers; ensure documentation of data meets standards; ensure issues and risks
are resolved; oversee training and support for users of data sets; and explore and
develop new ways of working with data.

*Ensure* appears twice and *oversee* three times. This is the level where you stop
being measured by what you built and start being measured by what your area
produces.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Eight skills, each with an explanation, a
rating and some questions. Expect about 30 minutes.

## Rating scale

Where a question asks for a rating, use this scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | You have not worked with this |
| 1 | Awareness | You know what it is and why it matters |
| 2 | Working | You do it with support |
| 3 | Practitioner | You do it unsupported, and handle the usual exceptions |
| 4 | Expert | You set the approach and develop others in it |

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not the remit on your job
  description.
- **A 4 is a claim about other people** — that someone else's practice changed
  because of you and would stay changed if you left. Coaching is written into your
  level, so one or two are reasonable.

## About this role level

**The role: analytics engineer.** An analytics engineer transforms data relevant
to the organisation into structures that enable analysis and decision making. In
this role you will:

- work with subject matter experts to understand organisational processes and
  translate these into data structures optimised for analysis
- work with data users to design and build data models they can use, using
  modelling techniques such as Kimball or Inmon
- support data quality improvement
- develop standards for data transformation
- create and maintain data documentation
- refine requirements in response to feedback and changes in the organisation
- provide ongoing support to users

**Your level: senior analytics engineer.** You oversee the development and testing
of data models, including directing the work of other analytics engineers. The
framework expects that you:

- oversee tasks and support less senior analytics engineers
- build relationships with stakeholders within a defined area
- coach and mentor less senior analytics engineers
- ensure documentation of data meets standards
- ensure issues and risks are resolved
- oversee training and support for users of data sets
- explore and develop new ways of working with data

**Where this level sits.** This role has five levels:

1. Trainee analytics engineer
2. Analytics engineer
3. **Senior analytics engineer** — you are here
4. Lead analytics engineer
5. Head of analytics engineering

Your next step is lead analytics engineer: leading a team, working across the
organisation to define data requirements, creating standards for communication,
models and documentation, and defining ways of working.

**One thing worth knowing about this role.** Four of your eight skills are worded
**identically** at lead analytics engineer — data modelling, metadata management,
programming and build, and testing. Half the published skills do not change between
your level and the one above. What does change is communicating between the
technical and non-technical, data analysis and synthesis, data innovation, and
problem management. If you want the lead role, those four are the list, and the
rest of the case rests on the accountabilities.

**The eight skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Communicating between the technical and non-technical
2. Data analysis and synthesis
3. Data innovation
4. Data modelling, cleansing and enrichment
5. Metadata management
6. Problem management
7. Programming and build (data and analytics engineering)
8. Testing

Eight skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the level expects,

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: communicate effectively
with technical and non-technical stakeholders; support and host discussions within
a multidisciplinary team, with potentially difficult dynamics; be an advocate for
the team externally; and manage differing stakeholder perspectives.

**Same wording as analytics engineer.** This skill does not change between those
two levels.

**What that looks like here.** Advocating for the team externally usually means
explaining why the model everyone wants tomorrow will take three weeks, in a way
that does not sound defensive.

**The next step.** At lead you listen to and interpret stakeholder needs and manage
expectations, handle active and reactive communication, and host difficult
discussions with diverse senior stakeholders.

### Skill: Data analysis and synthesis

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: undertake data profiling
and source system analysis; and present clear insights to colleagues to support the
end use of the data.

**Same wording as analytics engineer.**

**What that looks like here.** At senior the difference is usually breadth — you
have profiled enough sources to recognise the failure patterns before you look.

**The next step.** At lead you understand and help teams apply a range of profiling
techniques, do source system analysis from a complex single source, and bring
multiple sources together in a conformed model.

### Skill: Data innovation

**What the framework expects at this level.** One thing: understand the impact on
the organisation of emerging trends in data tools, analysis techniques and data
usage.

**What that looks like here.** Not just knowing that a tool exists, but being able
to say what adopting it would mean here — the migration, the retraining, the things
that would break. This is the first level where the wording rises above awareness.

**The next step.** At lead you identify areas of innovation in tools and
techniques, and recognise the appropriate timing for adoption.

### Skill: Data modelling, cleansing and enrichment

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: build and review complex
data models, ensuring adherence to standards; use data integration tools and
languages to integrate and store data, and advise teams on best practice; ensure
data for analysis meets quality standards and is interoperable with other data
sets, enabling reuse; and work with other data professionals to improve modelling
and integration patterns and standards.

**What that looks like here.** Reviewing is now as important as building. And
*interoperable, enabling reuse* is the item that separates a model serving one team
from one serving the organisation — usually a matter of shared keys and agreed
definitions rather than technology.

**The next step.** Identical wording at lead analytics engineer.

### Skill: Metadata management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: design an appropriate

capture, because a repository that asks for too much is filled in badly and one
that asks for too little is useless.

### Skill: Problem management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: initiate and monitor
actions to investigate patterns and trends to resolve problems; effectively consult
specialists where required; determine the appropriate resolution and assist with
its implementation; and determine preventative measures.

**What that looks like here.** *Initiate* is the change from the level below — you
start the investigation rather than contribute to it, and you decide when the fix
is good enough. Determining preventative measures means someone has to argue for
the time, and that someone is you.

**The next step.** At lead you ensure the right actions are taken to investigate,
resolve and anticipate problems, and co-ordinate the team to do it.

### Skill: Programming and build (data and analytics engineering)

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: use agreed standards and
tools to design, code, test, correct and document moderate-to-complex programs and
scripts from agreed specifications and subsequent iterations; and collaborate with
others to review specifications where appropriate.

**What that looks like here.** Reviewing the specification, not only implementing
it, is the shift. A specification that cannot be built cleanly is worth challenging
before rather than after.

**The next step.** Identical wording at lead analytics engineer. It changes at
head, where you set standards for tools and techniques and take technical
responsibility for all stages of a project.

### Skill: Testing

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: review requirements and
specifications, and define test conditions; identify issues and risks associated
with work; and analyse and report test activities and results.

**What that looks like here.** This is a real step up from the two levels below,
where testing was executing scripts under supervision. You now decide what
"correct" means for a model, which is a design decision as much as a testing one.

**The next step.** Identical wording at lead **and** at head of analytics
engineering. This is the top published expectation for testing in this role.
