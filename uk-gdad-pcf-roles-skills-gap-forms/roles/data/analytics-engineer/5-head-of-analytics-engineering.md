# Head of analytics engineering - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you are a head of analytics engineering in the UK
Government Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it
so that you can set out what you do now, where the gaps are, and what would close
them.

The framework describes this role as overseeing the development of a range of data
models that meet analysis needs across the organisation. It expects you to build
relationships with senior stakeholders to determine what the organisation needs;
ensure the activities of the analytics engineering team align with strategic
priorities; ensure the team works to standards for communication, data models and
documentation; advocate for the analytics engineering role to senior leadership and
other organisations; build analytics engineering capability by providing technical
leadership and career development for the community; and ensure appropriate
technology is available for the community.

**Advocating for the role itself is written into the job.** Analytics engineering
is newer than the professions around it, and in many organisations you are the
reason it exists as a distinct thing at all.

Head of analytics engineering is the top of this role. Nothing here is scored,
marked or ranked. Eight skills, each with an explanation, a rating and some
questions. Expect about 30 minutes.

## Rating scale

Where a question asks for a rating, use this scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | You have not worked with this |
| 1 | Awareness | You know what it is and why it matters |
| 2 | Working | You do it with support |
| 3 | Practitioner | You do it unsupported, and handle the usual exceptions |
| 4 | Expert | You set the approach and develop others in it |

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not the remit on your job
  description.
- **A 4 is a claim about other people** — that someone else's practice changed
  because of you and would stay changed if you left. The honest test at this level
  is whether the standards you set would survive your departure.

## About this role level

**The role: analytics engineer.** An analytics engineer transforms data relevant
to the organisation into structures that enable analysis and decision making. In
this role you will:

- work with subject matter experts to understand organisational processes and
  translate these into data structures optimised for analysis
- work with data users to design and build data models they can use, using
  modelling techniques such as Kimball or Inmon
- support data quality improvement
- develop standards for data transformation
- create and maintain data documentation
- refine requirements in response to feedback and changes in the organisation
- provide ongoing support to users

**Your level: head of analytics engineering.** You oversee the development of a
range of data models that meet analysis needs across the organisation. The
framework expects that you:

- build relationships with senior stakeholders across the organisation to
  determine what the organisation needs
- ensure the activities of the analytics engineering team align with strategic
  priorities
- ensure the team works to standards for communication, data models and
  documentation
- advocate for the analytics engineering role to senior leadership and other
  organisations
- build analytics engineering capability by providing technical leadership and
  career development for the community
- ensure appropriate technology is available for the community

**There is no level above this one.** This role has five levels and this is the
highest. Where people go next is a question about other roles — data leadership, a
chief data officer role, or leadership outside the profession — and the framework
does not define it as a next step, so this form does not invent one.

**Where this level sits:**

1. Trainee analytics engineer
2. Analytics engineer
3. Senior analytics engineer
4. Lead analytics engineer
5. **Head of analytics engineering** — you are here

**One thing worth knowing about this role.** *Testing* is worded identically at
senior, lead and your level. It is the only one of the eight that does not rise
across the top three levels. And *data analysis and synthesis* is identical to
lead. If your job requires you to own the organisation's testing standards for
data — and at head level it usually does — you are working beyond the published
expectation.

**The eight skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Communicating between the technical and non-technical
2. Data analysis and synthesis
3. Data innovation
4. Data modelling, cleansing and enrichment
5. Metadata management
6. Problem management
7. Programming and build (data and analytics engineering)
8. Testing

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What the framework expects.** Three things: mediate between people and strengthen
relationships, adopting the appropriate communication method with stakeholders at
all levels; manage stakeholder expectations and moderate difficult discussions
about high-risk and complex topics, even within constrained timescales; and speak
on behalf of, and represent the community to, large audiences inside and outside
the organisation.

**What that looks like here.** Much of the difficulty is explaining why a request
that sounds simple is not, to someone who will hear any explanation as an excuse.
Representing the community to large audiences means public speaking is part of the
job.

### Skill: Data modelling, cleansing and enrichment

**What the framework expects.** Four things: understand different ways to model
data to maximise its use and value; ensure data is modelled appropriately, and that
modelling standards exist and are complied with; understand a number of data
integration tools and patterns, and ensure your teams have the support and training
needed to use the most appropriate methods; and build relationships with other
senior data professionals — in fields such as data architecture, data engineering
and data science — to share best practice and continually improve modelling and
integration processes and standards.

**What that looks like here.** *Exist and are complied with* is two separate
problems, and most organisations solve only the first. The relationships with data
architecture, engineering and science are where boundary disputes get settled
before they become turf wars.

### Skill: Metadata management

**What the framework expects.** Three things: identify how metadata repositories can
support different areas of the organisation; communicate the value of metadata
repositories; and set up robust governance processes to keep repositories up to
date.

**What that looks like here.** Every organisation has tried a catalogue; most have
one that is out of date. The governance question — who is responsible for keeping
each entry current, and what happens when they do not — is what separates the
attempts that last.

### Skill: Problem management

**What the framework expects.** Four things: anticipate problems and defend against

identify and describe problems, and help others to describe them; and build
problem-solving capabilities in others.

**What that looks like here.** *Help others describe them* is the underrated item.
A badly described problem gets solved wrongly by competent people, and teaching
people to describe accurately is worth more than solving things for them.
