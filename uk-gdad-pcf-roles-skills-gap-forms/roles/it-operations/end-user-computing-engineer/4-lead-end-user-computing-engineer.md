# Lead end user computing engineer - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a lead end user computing engineer in the UK Government Digital and Data
profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out what you do now, where the
gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this level as having expert technical understanding and being **accountable for
vendor and supplier management**. At this level you work with relevant IT operations and other functions,
such as technical architecture, to understand lower level productivity, development and feasibility.

Supplier accountability is the sharp end here. Most of an end user computing estate is bought rather than
built — devices, licences, support contracts, management tooling — and what your team can promise is largely
determined by contracts signed elsewhere.

Eleven skills. Expect about 35 minutes.

This skills gap form is created by AI and is work in progress for educational purposes. If you intend to use

## How to fill in this form

This is a static web page. There is nothing to type into and nothing to send from here — your answers never
touch this site.

Print it, copy the questions into a document, or paste them into whatever your organisation uses for
development conversations. Every question is numbered, so answers travel back on their own.

Worth knowing:

- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a finding.
- **"My team does this" is not an answer to "what do you do".** Name your part.
- **"I haven't had the chance" is a real answer** at this level too.
- **Do not include anything sensitive about contracts or systems.**
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers, what they are used for, or how long
  they are kept, ask before you fill it in.

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not the remit on your job description.
- **A 4 is a claim about other people** — that someone else's practice changed because of you and would stay

## About this role level

**The role: end user computing engineer.** You manage the product life cycle of all service-raised incidents
and all service requests, using knowledge management, inform customers of progress, advise on workarounds,
and support and maintain end user services.

**Your level: lead end user computing engineer.** You have expert technical understanding and are accountable
for vendor and supplier management. You work with relevant IT operations and other functions, such as
technical architecture, to understand lower level productivity, development and feasibility.

**Where this level sits.** This role has five levels:

1. Associate end user computing engineer
2. End user computing engineer
3. Senior end user computing engineer
4. **Lead end user computing engineer** — you are here
5. Principal end user computing engineer

Your next step is principal end user computing engineer: owning the strategy roadmap, leading on resourcing,
learning and development for the team, and looking at the bigger picture and trends in the business.

**Two things worth knowing about this role.**

First, *Service management framework knowledge* at your level requires a **Level 3 qualification plus
knowledge of the life cycle or capability elements of ITIL** — and then **disappears entirely at principal**,
which has only ten skills. Yours is the highest published requirement for it in this role.

Second, four of your eleven skills are worded **identically at principal** — change management, incident
management, ownership and initiative, and problem management is close.

**The eleven skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Asset and configuration management
2. Change management
3. IT service reporting
4. Incident management
5. Ownership and initiative
6. Problem management
7. Service focus

### Skill: Asset and configuration management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: maintain secure configuration and accurate
information; control IT assets in one or more areas; and verify the location and state of IT assets.

**Same wording as the two levels below.**

**What that looks like here.** At lead this is a licensing and cost question as well as an operational one.
Devices and licences that nobody can account for are money spent on nothing, and they usually only surface at
renewal.

**The next step.** At principal you manage configuration items and related information, and manage service
compliance and risk.

### Skill: Testing

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: **manage the planning of system and acceptance
tests, co-ordinating both functional and non-functional specifications**; provide authoritative advice and
guidance on test planning; and identify process improvements and contribute to the definition of best
practice.

**What that looks like here.** Acceptance testing matters most when a supplier delivers. Non-functional
requirements — performance on the oldest hardware, behaviour on a poor connection — are what usually get
skipped and later become tickets.

**The next step.** At principal this becomes managing testing activities within development or integration,
managing risks, and managing customer relations.

### Skill: User focus

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: collaborate with user researchers and represent
users internally; explain the difference between user needs and the desires of the user; champion user
research to focus on all users; prioritise and define approaches to understand the user story, guiding others
in doing so; and offer recommendations on the best tools and methods to use.

**What that looks like here.** *All users* includes colleagues with access needs, who are usually served
worst by standard device builds and standard software choices.

**The next step.** At principal you give direction on tools and methods, demonstrate experience across
channels, bring insight into how needs have changed, and apply strategic thinking to providing the best
service.
