# Head of command and control - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you are a head of command and control in a UK government organisation. Human
resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out what you do now, where the gaps are, and
what would close them.

The framework describes this role as responsible for the **strategy roadmap, integration and building the
capability of the team**. It expects you to ensure all tools are fit for purpose and meet business
requirements, and potentially to be required to address issues across IT operations.

Tooling is named explicitly, which is unusual and sensible. An operations centre is only as good as what
it can see, and monitoring that grew organically over a decade usually watches what was easy to
instrument rather than what matters.

Head of command and control is the top of this role. Eleven skills. Expect about 35 minutes.

- **A 4 is a claim about other people** — that someone else's practice changed because of you and would

## About this role level

**The role: command and control centre manager.** Command and control centre managers proactively monitor
live services and performance trends to identify potential problems or areas for improvement.

**Your level: head of command and control.** You are responsible for the strategy roadmap, integration and
building the capability of the team. The framework expects that you:

- ensure all tools are fit for purpose and meet business requirements
- potentially be required to address issues across IT operations

**There is no level above this one.** This role has four levels and this is the highest. Where people go
next is a question about other roles — IT service leadership, infrastructure leadership, or wider
operations management — and the framework does not define it as a next step, so this form does not invent
one.

**Where this level sits:**

1. Operations analyst
2. Senior operations analyst
3. Operational control manager
4. **Head of command and control** — you are here

**One thing worth knowing about this role.** Six of your eleven skills are worded **identically at
operational control manager** below you. Five are yours alone: asset and configuration management (managing
configuration items and service compliance and risk), availability and capacity (initiating remedial
action), problem management (ensuring the right actions are taken and co-ordinating the team), service
focus (the bigger picture across groups of services), and user focus (collaborating with researchers and
championing research).

**The eleven skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Asset and configuration management
2. Availability and capacity management
3. Change management
4. Community collaboration
5. Continuity management
6. Incident management
7. Ownership and initiative
8. Problem management
9. Service focus
10. Service management framework knowledge
11. User focus

### Skill: Service management framework knowledge

**What the framework expects.** One thing: show you have an **expert certificate in the service
management framework qualification**.

**The top of a three-step ladder.** Operations analyst and senior operations analyst need a Level 3
qualification; operational control manager needs that **plus** knowledge of the life cycle or
capability elements of ITIL; yours is an expert certificate. Each step costs money and study time.

**Worth being plain about.** This is a document, not a practice. If you do not hold it, the ask is
funding and study time — a concrete, costed request rather than a development aspiration.

### Skill: User focus

**What the framework expects.** Four things: **give direction on which tools or methods to use**;
demonstrate experience in meeting the needs of users across a variety of channels; **bring insight and
expertise in how user needs have changed over time** to ensure they are met by the business; and **apply
strategic thinking to provide the best service for the end user**.

**A step up from operational control manager**, where it was collaborating with researchers and championing
research.

**What that looks like here.** Insight over time is the distinctive one, and the practical version:
your monitoring should reflect what users experience, not only what servers report. A service that is
technically up and unusable is showing green on your wall.
