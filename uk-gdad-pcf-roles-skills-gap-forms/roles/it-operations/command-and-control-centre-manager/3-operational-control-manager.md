# Operational control manager - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as an operational control manager in the UK Government Digital and Data
profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out what you do now, where
the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this level as **accountable for day-to-day IT operations**, acting as the
escalation point for incidents and problems. It expects you to be responsible for effective change
management processes that maintain the availability of services, using proactive and preventative
measures; to be **on call 24/7 or according to business requirements**; and to be allowed to address
issues across IT operations.

"On call 24/7" is written plainly in the framework, and it is worth treating as a working-conditions
question rather than a badge. Sustainable arrangements have cover, compensation and recovery time; the
alternative is a person who becomes a single point of failure.

Eleven skills. Expect about 35 minutes.

## About this role level

**The role: command and control centre manager.** Command and control centre managers proactively monitor
live services and performance trends to identify potential problems or areas for improvement.

**Your level: operational control manager.** You are accountable for day-to-day IT operations and act as
the escalation point for incidents and problems. The framework expects that you:

- be responsible for effective change management processes that maintain the availability of services,
  using proactive and preventative measures
- be on call 24/7 or according to business requirements
- be allowed to address issues across IT operations

**Where this level sits.** This role has four levels:

1. Operations analyst
2. Senior operations analyst
3. **Operational control manager** — you are here
4. Head of command and control

Your next step is head of command and control: responsible for the strategy roadmap, integration and
building the capability of the team, ensuring all tools are fit for purpose and meet business
requirements.

**One thing worth knowing about this role.** Six of your eleven skills are worded **identically at head of
command and control** — change management, community collaboration, continuity, incident management,
ownership and initiative, and service management framework knowledge. What changes at head: asset and
configuration management, availability and capacity, problem management, service focus and user focus.
Your accountabilities carry the rest.

**The eleven skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Asset and configuration management

3. Change management
4. Community collaboration

### Skill: Asset and configuration management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: maintain secure configuration and accurate
information; control IT assets in one or more areas; and verify the location and state of IT assets.

are unreliable, monitoring coverage is unknowable — you cannot tell what you are not watching.

**The next step.** At head you manage configuration items and related information, and manage service
compliance and risk.

### Skill: User focus

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: **collaborate with user researchers and
represent users internally**; **explain the difference between user needs and the desires of the user**;
**champion user research to focus on all users**; prioritise and define approaches to understand the user
story, guiding others in doing so; and offer recommendations on the best tools and methods to use.

**A large step up from the two levels below**, where it was identifying and engaging with users and using
data about them.

**What that looks like here.** Everything the centre prioritises is a judgement about who matters most.
Making that explicit — which services, at which times, for whom — turns instinct into policy, and
representing users internally is how that judgement gets challenged before an incident tests it.

**The next step.** At head of command and control you give direction on which tools or methods to use,
demonstrate experience across a variety of channels, bring insight into how user needs have changed over
time, and apply strategic thinking to providing the best service.
