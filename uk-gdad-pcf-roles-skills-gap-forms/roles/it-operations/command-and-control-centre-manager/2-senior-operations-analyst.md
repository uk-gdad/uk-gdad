# Senior operations analyst - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a senior operations analyst in the UK Government Digital and Data
profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out what you do now, where
the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this level as prioritising incoming messages for the team and ensuring correct
information flow, record keeping and data capture. It expects you to detect notifications and conduct
monitor checks to ensure systems are running correctly; **in emergencies, be on call outside your working
hours** to check events logged as not functioning; and in some organisations to oversee the virtual
environment.

Prioritising incoming messages is the job in one phrase. An operations centre receives far more signal
than it can act on, and deciding what matters — quickly, repeatedly, often at night — is the skill that
everything else rests on.

Eleven skills. Expect about 35 minutes.

- **Answer about the job you do**, not the one on the job description.
- **"I haven't had the chance" is a real answer.** With eleven skills, several blanks are expected.
- **Do not include anything sensitive about your systems.**
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers, what they are used for, or how long
  they are kept, ask before you fill it in.

## About this role level

**The role: command and control centre manager.** Command and control centre managers proactively monitor
live services and performance trends to identify potential problems or areas for improvement.

**Your level: senior operations analyst.** You prioritise incoming messages for the team and ensure
correct information flow, record keeping and data capture. The framework expects that you:

- detect notifications and conduct monitor checks to ensure that systems are running correctly
- in emergencies, be on call outside your working hours to check events that have been logged as not
  functioning
- oversee the virtual environment, in some organisations

**Where this level sits.** This role has four levels:

1. Operations analyst
2. **Senior operations analyst** — you are here
3. Operational control manager
4. Head of command and control

Your next step is operational control manager: accountable for day-to-day IT operations, acting as the
escalation point for incidents and problems, responsible for effective change management processes that
maintain availability, on call 24/7 or according to business requirements, and allowed to address issues
across IT operations.

**One thing worth knowing about this role.** Seven of your eleven skills are worded **identically at
operations analyst** below you — availability and capacity, community collaboration, continuity, ownership
and initiative, service focus, service management framework knowledge, and user focus. Four rose: asset
and configuration management, change management, incident management and problem management. Your
accountabilities carry the rest of the difference, particularly the on-call expectation.

**The eleven skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Asset and configuration management

3. Change management
4. Community collaboration
5. Continuity management

### Skill: Asset and configuration management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: maintain secure configuration and accurate
information; control IT assets in one or more areas; and verify the location and state of IT assets.

**What that looks like here.** A step up from tracking and logging. Verification matters most in a
monitoring role — if what you are watching does not match what is deployed, you are monitoring a fiction.

**The next step.** Identical wording at operational control manager.

### Skill: Change management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: **analyse and assess the impact of change**;
document change requests; and action changes from change requests.

**What that looks like here.** A real step up. From the operations centre you can often see impacts a
change requester cannot — that two changes are scheduled in the same window, or that one touches a system
already under strain.

**The next step.** At operational control manager you manage high impact, complex change requests and
ensure release policies, procedures and processes are applied.

### Skill: Community collaboration

### Skill: Incident management

**What the framework expects at this level.** One thing: **diagnose and prioritise incidents, investigate
their causes and find resolutions.**

**What that looks like here.** The biggest change from the level below. You are no longer routing — you
are working out what is wrong. Under time pressure, the discipline is checking the boring explanations
first: what changed, what is full, what is unreachable.

**The next step.** At operational control manager you lead the investigation and resolution of incidents.

### Skill: Problem management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: **initiate and monitor actions to investigate

appropriate resolution and assist with its implementation; and determine preventative measures.

**What that looks like here.** A large step up. You see every alert, so you see every pattern. Initiating
an investigation rather than waiting for someone else to notice is what makes an operations centre
preventative rather than reactive.
