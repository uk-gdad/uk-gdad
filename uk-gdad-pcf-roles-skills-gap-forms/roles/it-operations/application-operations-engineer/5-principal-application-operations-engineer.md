# Principal application operations engineer - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a principal application operations engineer in the UK Government
Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out what
you do now, where the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this level as **owning the application strategy** and leading on resourcing,
learning and development for your team. It expects you to have expert technical understanding, to be
**accountable for vendor and supplier management**, and possibly to manage and maintain certain
aspects of an application depending on its complexity, size and business impact.

Supplier accountability is the sharpest item. Much of what an operations team can promise depends on
what a vendor will deliver, and the contract was often signed before anyone asked an engineer what
would be needed.

Principal is the top of this role. Eleven skills. Expect about 35 minutes.

## About this role level

**The role: application operations engineer.** You support, manage and maintain a single application
or a suite of applications.

**Your level: principal application operations engineer.** You own the application strategy and lead on
resourcing, learning and development for your team. The framework expects that you:

- have expert technical understanding
- be accountable for vendor and supplier management
- potentially manage and maintain certain aspects of an application, depending on complexity, size
  and likely business impact

**There is no level above this one.** This role has five levels and this is the highest. Where people
go next is a question about other roles — IT service management, technical architecture, or wider
operations leadership — and the framework does not define it as a next step, so this form does not
invent one.

**Where this level sits:**

1. Associate application operations engineer
2. Application operations engineer
3. Senior application operations engineer
4. Lead application operations engineer
5. **Principal application operations engineer** — you are here

**Two things worth knowing about this role.**

First, *Service management framework knowledge* at your level requires an **expert certificate in the
service management framework qualification**. That is the highest qualification requirement anywhere in
this framework, and it is concrete: either you hold it or you do not.

Second, *Testing* at your level is worded differently rather than obviously more demanding than lead's:
yours is managing testing activities within development or integration, managing risks and managing
customer relations, while lead's covers planning system and acceptance tests and giving authoritative
advice on test planning. Worth knowing if that skill is used to compare the two levels.

**The eleven skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Asset and configuration management

3. Change management
4. Incident management
5. Ownership and initiative

### Skill: Asset and configuration management

**What the framework expects.** Two things: **manage configuration items and related information**; and
**manage service compliance and risk**.

**What that looks like here.** Compliance and risk arrive at your level as a named responsibility.
Knowing which of your applications are out of support, unpatched or undocumented — and having that
recorded rather than remembered — is what makes an audit survivable.

### Skill: Technical specialism

**What the framework expects.** Two things: **understand the direction of future technologies**; and

cleanly — new platforms run alongside old ones for years, and designing an operating model that copes
with both is the actual work.

### Skill: Testing

**What the framework expects.** Three things: manage testing activities within development or

management and customer relations instead. That is how the framework publishes it.

### Skill: User focus

**What the framework expects.** Five things: collaborate with user researchers and represent users
internally; explain the difference between user needs and the desires of the user; champion user
research to focus on all users; prioritise and define approaches to understand the user story, guiding
others in doing so; and offer recommendations on the best tools and methods to use.

**Same wording as lead application operations engineer.**

**What that looks like here.** At principal the useful version is making sure resilience and capacity
investment is argued in terms of people rather than uptime percentages. A number nobody feels does not
win funding.
