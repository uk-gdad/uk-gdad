# Senior application operations engineer - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a senior application operations engineer in the UK Government
Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out what
you do now, where the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this level as dealing with more complex issues, being responsible for code
deployment and co-ordinating change-based activities such as **taking services live**. Depending on
the complexity and size of a project and its likely impact on the business, you might also manage and
maintain certain aspects of an application.

That is the whole level description — there is no separate accountability list. Taking a service live
is the sharpest responsibility named here: the moment when something that worked in a controlled
environment meets real people and real load.

Eleven skills. Nobody is strong across all of them. Expect about 35 minutes.

Worth knowing:

- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a finding.
- **"The team does this" is not an answer to "what do you do".** Name your part.
- **"I haven't had the chance" is a real answer.** With eleven skills, several blanks are expected.
- **Do not include anything sensitive about your systems** — no hostnames, credentials, or details of
  an unresolved weakness.
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers, what they are used for, or how
  long they are kept, ask before you fill it in.

## About this role level

**The role: application operations engineer.** You support, manage and maintain a single application
or a suite of applications.

**Your level: senior application operations engineer.** You deal with more complex issues and are
responsible for code deployment and co-ordinating change-based activities, such as taking services
live. You might manage and maintain certain aspects of an application, depending on its complexity,
size and likely business impact.

**Where this level sits.** This role has five levels:

1. Associate application operations engineer
2. Application operations engineer
3. **Senior application operations engineer** — you are here
4. Lead application operations engineer
5. Principal application operations engineer

**Two things worth knowing about this role.**

First, six of your eleven skills are worded **identically at application operations engineer** below
you — asset and configuration management, availability and capacity management, ownership and
initiative, service focus, service management framework knowledge, and user focus. What actually rose
at your level was change management, incident management, problem management, technical specialism
and testing. That is a clear list of where the step up sits.

Second, *Service management framework knowledge* is still worded as a **Level 3 service management
framework qualification** — the same as the level below. It rises to Level 3 plus knowledge of the
life cycle or capability elements of ITIL at lead, and to an expert certificate at principal.

**The eleven skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Asset and configuration management
2. Availability and capacity management
3. Change management
4. Incident management
5. Ownership and initiative
6. Problem management
7. Service focus
8. Service management framework knowledge
9. Technical specialism
10. Testing
11. User focus

### Skill: Asset and configuration management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: maintain secure configuration and accurate
information; control IT assets in one or more areas; and verify the location and state of IT assets.

**Same wording as application operations engineer.** It changes at principal, where you manage
configuration items and related information, and manage service compliance and risk.

**What that looks like here.** Configuration that is described accurately enough for someone else to
rebuild it. If your environments differ in ways nobody has recorded, deployments will keep surprising
you.

### Skill: Change management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: **analyse and assess the impact of
change**; document change requests; and action changes from change requests.

**What that looks like here.** Assessing impact is the new item and the valuable one — knowing what
else depends on the thing you are about to change, including the things that depend on it in ways
nobody documented.

**The next step.** At lead you manage high-impact, complex change requests and ensure release policies,
procedures and processes are applied.

### Skill: Incident management

who is affected rather than which system is loudest.

### Skill: Problem management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: initiate and monitor actions to investigate

as it is recorded, not forgotten.

### Skill: Technical specialism

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: use management system software and tools;
use logical schemata to investigate problems, collect performance statistics and create reports; carry
out the routine configuration, installation and reconfiguration of database and related products; and
**optimise performance and forecast resource needs**.

**What that looks like here.** This is a substantial jump from assisting with routine tasks, and it is
database-flavoured. Forecasting resource needs requires data over time — if nobody has been keeping
it, that is the first thing to fix.

**The next step.** At lead you draft and maintain procedures and documentation, and set standards for
the definition, security and integrity of objects.

### Skill: Testing

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: review requirements and specifications, and

means before the night itself — including the checks you will run at 2am when everyone is tired.

**The next step.** At lead you manage the planning of system and acceptance tests, co-ordinate
functional and non-functional specifications, and provide authoritative advice on test planning.

### Skill: User focus

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: identify and engage with users or
stakeholders to collate user needs evidence; understand and define research that fits user needs; and
use quantitative and qualitative data about users to turn user focus into outcomes.

**Same wording as application operations engineer.**

**What that looks like here.** For an operations role this is research-flavoured wording. The
practical version is knowing what your service failing actually does to people, and using that to
argue for resilience work.

**The next step.** At lead you collaborate with user researchers and represent users internally,
explain the difference between user needs and desires, champion research covering all users, and guide
others on approaches.
