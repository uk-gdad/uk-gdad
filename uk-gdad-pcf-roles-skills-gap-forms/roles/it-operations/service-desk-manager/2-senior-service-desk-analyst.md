# Senior service desk analyst - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a senior service desk analyst in the UK Government Digital and Data
profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out what you do now, where the
gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this level as **monitoring inbound calls, supporting operators in service resolution
and intervening in difficult calls**. At this level you:

- **own and update the script** for service desk analysts
- **coach and develop one or more service desk analysts**, and manage availability

That is a first-line leadership job wearing an analyst's title. Two of the three published expectations are
about other people, and the third — owning the script — is about how everyone else does the work.

Eight skills. Expect about 25 minutes.

## About this role level

**The role: service desk manager.** A service desk manager is responsible for managing the first- and
second-line technical support for all departmental IT applications and services across sites, including end
user computing — multi-function devices and specialised IT equipment included. The role is also responsible
for ensuring support for existing and emerging information and communications technology services, including
providing technical advice to project teams.

**Your level: senior service desk analyst.** You monitor inbound calls, support operators in service
resolution and intervene in difficult calls, with the accountabilities listed in the introduction.

**Where this level sits.** This role has four levels:

1. Service desk analyst
2. **Senior service desk analyst** — you are here
3. Service desk manager
4. Head of service desk

Your next step is service desk manager.

**Two things worth knowing about this role.**

First, **your level adds two skills the analyst does not have**: problem management, and a **Level 3 service
management framework qualification**. If you stepped up recently and nobody mentioned the qualification, that
is worth raising — it is a named requirement, so the ask is funding and study time rather than a vague
development goal.

Second, **customer service management is the skill that separates you most clearly from the analyst below**.
Theirs is resolving to the service level agreement and empathising. Yours adds **dealing with complex
complaints** — the calls that have already gone wrong before they reach you.

**The eight skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Asset and configuration management
2. Customer service management
3. IT service reporting
4. Ownership and initiative
5. Problem management
6. Service focus
7. Service management framework knowledge
8. User focus

### Skill: Customer service management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: **resolve user requests, maintaining a high level
of customer service**; **deal with complex complaints**; and **use empathy to understand and meet customer
needs**.

**The clearest step up from service desk analyst**, whose version is resolving to the service level agreement,
empathising, and using customer-focused metrics. Yours drops the metrics and adds complaints.

**What that looks like here.** A complex complaint is usually not about the fault. It is about being passed
around, having to repeat themselves, or being told something that turned out not to be true. Resolving it
means dealing with the history as well as the ticket.

**The next step.** At service desk manager it changes shape entirely: translating lessons learnt into
continual service improvement, overseeing the full range of customer service functions including reviewing
satisfaction, and investigating which processes can be automated.

### Skill: IT service reporting

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: produce relevant reports in an appropriate format
and agreed timeframe; work with stakeholders to discuss any changes in the reporting processes; and interpret
a data set and communicate this to others.

**Identical to service desk analyst.** At service desk manager it collapses to one line — using data
analytics skills to make decisions that enhance business performance — and at head of service desk it becomes
using prediction methodologies to maintain a holistic view of trends across multiple processes.

**What that looks like here.** You see the call patterns before anyone else does. Interpreting them — saying
what they mean rather than what they are — is the half that gets skipped.

### Skill: Problem management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: **investigate problems in systems, processes and
services**, with an understanding of the level of a problem — strategic, tactical or operational; and
**contribute to the implementation of remedies and preventative measures**.

**New at your level** — the analyst does not have this skill at all.

**Why it matters here.** The service desk sees every symptom the organisation produces. That makes you the
best-placed person to spot which underlying problem is generating the calls — and usually the person with the
least time to investigate it.

**The three levels of problem.** Operational is today's breakage. Tactical is the pattern behind several.
Strategic is something about how the whole thing is built.

**The next step.** At service desk manager you initiate and monitor actions to investigate patterns and
trends, consult specialists, determine the resolution and determine preventative measures.

### Skill: User focus

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: **identify and engage with users or stakeholders
to collate user needs evidence**; understand and define research that fits user needs; and **use quantitative
and qualitative data about users to turn user focus into outcomes**.

**A significant step up from service desk analyst**, where it was awareness of user experience analysis and
explaining user stories.

**What that looks like here.** You have both kinds of data already — the numbers from the queue and what
people actually said. Turning that into evidence someone acts on is the gap.

**The next step.** At service desk manager it becomes collaborating with user researchers, representing
users internally, championing user research for all users, and guiding others in understanding the user
story.
