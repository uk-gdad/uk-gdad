# Senior infrastructure operations engineer - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a senior infrastructure operations engineer in the UK Government Digital
and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out what you do now,
where the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this level as acting as **second- or third-line support for incidents, problems and
changes** to solutions and services. At this level you:

- provide specialist technical support and assistance to projects, ensuring delivery of non-functional
  requirements and continual service improvement
- are responsible for preparation and support of IT operations solutions and services, physical or virtual,
  according to industry and organisational best practices, standards, service requirements and key
  performance indicators throughout the product life cycle
- **coach and mentor junior team members**
- assist in the provision of first class services and consistent levels of capability

Second and third line is where the difficult things arrive. First line has already tried the obvious.

Twelve skills. Expect about 35 minutes.

## How to fill in this form

This is a static web page. There is nothing to type into and nothing to send from here — your answers never
touch this site.

Print it, copy the questions into a document, or paste them into whatever your organisation uses for
development conversations. Every question is numbered, so answers travel back on their own.

Worth knowing:

- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a finding.
- **"My team does this" is not an answer to "what do you do".** Name your part.
- **"I have not had the chance" is a real answer.**
- **Do not include anything sensitive** — no credentials, no system detail, no colleagues' names.
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers, what they are used for, or how long
  they are kept, ask before you fill it in.

Where a question asks for a rating, use this scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |

| 3 | Practitioner | You do it unsupported, and handle the usual exceptions |
| 4 | Expert | You set the approach and develop others in it |

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not the remit on your job description.
- **A 4 is a claim about other people.** Since coaching and mentoring is written into your accountabilities,
  it is a fair claim to make here if it is true.

A filled-in answer looks like this:

> **Technical specialism**

Names what is done and what is handed on, which is the useful distinction at second and third line.

## About this role level

**The role: infrastructure operations engineer.** An infrastructure operations engineer supports, manages and
maintains the core infrastructure that underpins production services.

**Your level: senior infrastructure operations engineer.** You act as second- or third-line support for
incidents, problems and changes, with the accountabilities listed in the introduction.

**Where this level sits.** This role has five levels:

1. Associate infrastructure operations engineer
2. Infrastructure operations engineer
3. **Senior infrastructure operations engineer** — you are here
4. Lead infrastructure operations engineer
5. Principal infrastructure operations engineer

**Two things worth knowing about this role.**

First, **technical specialism at your level is largely a database skill**: using management system software
and tools, using logical schemata to investigate problems and create reports, carrying out routine
configuration, installation and reconfiguration of **database and related products**, and optimising
performance and forecasting resource needs. Nowhere else in this role does the published text get that
specific. If databases are handled by another team where you work, this is the skill most likely to be a real
gap.

Second, **the qualification requirement is unchanged from the level below** — a Level 3 service management
framework qualification, the same one. If you already hold it, that skill is done for two levels.

**The twelve skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Asset and configuration management
2. Availability and capacity management
3. Change management
4. Coding and scripting
5. Incident management
6. Ownership and initiative
7. Problem management
8. Service focus
9. Service management framework knowledge
10. Technical specialism
11. Testing

### Skill: Technical specialism

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: **use management system software and tools**; **use
logical schemata to investigate problems, collect performance statistics and create reports**; **carry out
the routine configuration, installation and reconfiguration of database and related products**; and
**optimise performance and forecast resource needs**.

**The most specific wording anywhere in this role**, and a complete change from the level below, where it was
assisting in technical support and routine maintenance.

**Worth being honest about.** Two of these four are database work. In many organisations databases belong to
a separate team, which means a senior infrastructure operations engineer can do this job well for years and
never touch the skill as written. That is a real gap for a framework-based conversation, and it is not your
fault — but it is worth naming rather than rating yourself generously on the two parts you do have.

**A logical schema** is the structural description of data — tables, fields, relationships — independent of
how it is physically stored. Reading one tells you where to look when a query is slow or a report is wrong.

**The next step.** At lead infrastructure operations engineer it changes shape again: drafting and
maintaining procedures and documentation, and setting standards for the definition, security and
integrity of objects and ensuring conformity to them.

**Rate yourself.** Rate the four parts separately in your evidence if they differ, because for most people
they will.
