# Lead infrastructure operations engineer - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a lead infrastructure operations engineer in the UK Government Digital and
Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out what you do now, where
the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this level in one line, and it is worth reading carefully: you **ensure the service
and the contractual and regulatory requirements of the organisation are met**. Not the technology — the
requirements. At this level you:

- manage operational support of IT solutions and services
- **manage workload, performance and development of a team** of IT operations specialists, building capability
  to deliver services as required
- **manage third party provision** of IT operations services
- manage provision of expertise to programmes and projects
- manage the development of architectural solutions for IT operations, throughout the service life cycle

People, suppliers, and obligations. The technical skills are still there, but the level description is about
what you are answerable for rather than what you build.

Twelve skills. Expect about 35 minutes.

## Rating scale

Where a question asks for a rating, use this scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | You have not worked with this |
| 1 | Awareness | You know what it is and why it matters |
| 2 | Working | You do it with support |
| 3 | Practitioner | You do it unsupported, and handle the usual exceptions |
| 4 | Expert | You set the approach and develop others in it |

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not the remit on your job description.
- **A 4 is a claim about other people** — that someone else's practice changed because of you and would stay
  changed if you left.

## About this role level

**The role: infrastructure operations engineer.** An infrastructure operations engineer supports, manages and
maintains the core infrastructure that underpins production services.

**Your level: lead infrastructure operations engineer.** You ensure the service and the contractual and
regulatory requirements of the organisation are met, with the accountabilities listed in the introduction.

**Where this level sits.** This role has five levels:

1. Associate infrastructure operations engineer
2. Infrastructure operations engineer
3. Senior infrastructure operations engineer
4. **Lead infrastructure operations engineer** — you are here
5. Principal infrastructure operations engineer

Your next step is principal infrastructure operations engineer: leading and directing IT operations specialist
teams, developing strategic and tactical roadmaps, and owning the operational relationships with suppliers.

**Two things worth knowing about this role.**

First, **your service management qualification requirement rises twice**. At the two levels below it is a
Level 3 qualification. At yours it is that **plus** demonstrated knowledge of the life cycle or capability
elements of ITIL. At principal it becomes an **expert certificate**. That is a three-step escalation with
real cost and study time attached at each step, and it is worth planning rather than discovering.

Second, **five of your twelve skills are worded identically at principal**: change management, coding and
scripting, incident management, ownership and initiative, and testing. What separates you from the top of the
role is concentrated in the other seven, plus the roadmap and supplier ownership in the level description.

**The twelve skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Asset and configuration management
2. Availability and capacity management
3. Change management
4. Coding and scripting
5. Incident management
6. Ownership and initiative

### Skill: User focus

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: **collaborate with user researchers and represent
users internally**; **explain the difference between user needs and the desires of the user**; **champion user
research to focus on all users**; prioritise and define approaches to understand the user story, guiding
others in doing so; and offer recommendations on the best tools and methods to use.

**A large step up from senior**, where it was engaging with users and using data.

**What that looks like here.** "All users" includes the ones who are served worst — colleagues with access
needs, people on poor connections, those using services at three in the morning.

**The next step.** At principal you give direction on tools and methods, demonstrate experience across
channels, bring insight into how needs have changed over time, and apply strategic thinking.
