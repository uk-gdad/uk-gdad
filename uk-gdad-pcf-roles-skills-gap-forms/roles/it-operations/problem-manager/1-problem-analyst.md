# Problem analyst - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a problem analyst in the UK Government Digital and Data profession.
Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out what you do now, where the gaps
are, and what would close them.

The framework describes problem managers as aiming to resolve and control the root causes of incidents
caused by errors within the IT infrastructure, working to prevent those incidents recurring. At this
level you are responsible for maintaining software systems once they are up and running and, where
necessary, supporting the problem manager in co-ordinating the resolution of problems.

Problem work is the least visible and most valuable job in operations. Nobody notices the incident that
did not happen, which makes recording what you prevented the only way anyone will know.

Eleven skills. Expect about 35 minutes.

## About this role level

**The role: problem manager.** Problem managers resolve and control the root causes of incidents caused
by errors within the IT infrastructure, and work to prevent those incidents recurring.

**Your level: problem analyst.** You are responsible for maintaining software systems once they are up
and running and, where necessary, supporting the problem manager in co-ordinating the resolution of
problems.

**Where this level sits.** This role has two levels only:

1. **Problem analyst** — you are here
2. Problem manager

Your next step is problem manager: picking up feeds from numerous sources such as the service desk,
putting continual improvements in place to stop incidents recurring, resolving emerging and recurring
problems, performing root cause analysis, and working closely with major incident managers.

**One thing worth knowing about this role.** Four of your eleven skills are worded **identically at
problem manager** — asset and configuration management, availability and capacity management, service
management framework knowledge, and user focus. The published step up is concentrated in problem
management itself, incident management, change, continuity, service focus, community collaboration and
ownership.

**The eleven skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Asset and configuration management
2. Availability and capacity management
3. Change management
4. Community collaboration
5. Continuity management
6. Incident management
7. Ownership and initiative
8. Problem management
9. Service focus
10. Service management framework knowledge
11. User focus

### Skill: Change management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: implement change requests; and apply change
control procedures **under supervision**.

**What that looks like here.** Fixing a problem means making a change, and a badly made fix creates the
next problem. Being disciplined about your own changes is part of being credible when you ask others to
be.

**The next step.** At problem manager you analyse and assess the impact of change, document change
requests, and action changes from them.

### Skill: Community collaboration

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: contribute to the work of others; motivate
and empower teams; create the right environment for teams to work in, and identify the best team makeup
depending on the situation; and recognise and deal with issues.

**What that looks like here.** Problem work depends on people telling you inconvenient things — that a
workaround exists, that a fix was rushed, that a warning was ignored. That only happens if you are
someone people are not afraid to tell.

**The next step.** At problem manager you work collaboratively while networking with others, adapt
feedback to make it effective and lasting, identify and rectify problems in team dynamics, and identify
issues through health checks.

### Skill: Ownership and initiative

**What the framework expects at this level.** One thing: own an issue until a new owner has been found
or the problem has been mitigated or resolved.

**What that looks like here.** Problems are exactly the things that get dropped, because they are never
urgent. Holding one until it is genuinely closed — or genuinely accepted — is the discipline of this
role.

**The next step.** At problem manager you take accountability for issues that occur, are proactive in
searching for potential problems, and achieve excellent user outcomes.

### Skill: Problem management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: **initiate and monitor actions to investigate
patterns and trends** to resolve problems; effectively consult specialists where required; determine the
appropriate resolution and assist with its implementation; and determine preventative measures.

**What that looks like here.** This is the core of your job and the wording is already demanding. The
practical difficulty is rarely finding the cause — it is getting the fix prioritised once you have.

**The next step.** At problem manager you **ensure the right actions are taken** to investigate, resolve
and anticipate problems, and co-ordinate the team to do it.

### Skill: User focus

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: identify and engage with users or
stakeholders to collate user needs evidence; understand and define research that fits user needs; and
use quantitative and qualitative data about users to turn user focus into outcomes.

**Same wording at problem manager.**

**What that looks like here.** Knowing what a recurring problem costs the people affected is the
strongest argument for fixing it. "Forty people redo their work every Monday" moves a priority in a way
that "recurring batch failure" does not.
