# Incident manager - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as an incident manager in the UK Government Digital and Data
profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out what you do now, where
the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes incident managers as aiming to restore normal service operation as quickly as
possible and minimise adverse effects on business operations. At this level you oversee incidents and
restore normal operations with the least impact on the business or the user. It expects you to work
on-site maintaining hardware and fixing technical problems quickly; provide first-time resolution by
troubleshooting and diagnosing or escalating faults to major incident managers and problem managers;
and coach apprentices and share knowledge with colleagues. The framework also notes that in some
departments your responsibilities may mirror those of an end user computing engineer.

That is an unusually mixed job description — hands-on hardware work, incident oversight, and coaching in
the same paragraph. Which of those dominates depends entirely on where you work, which makes the first
section of this form more useful than usual.

Eleven skills. Expect about 35 minutes.

## About this role level

**The role: incident manager.** Incident managers restore normal service operation as quickly as
possible and minimise adverse effects on business operations, so that service quality and availability
are returned and maintained.

**Your level: incident manager.** You oversee incidents and restore normal operations as quickly as
possible, with the least impact on the business or the user. The framework expects that you:

- work on-site, maintaining the hardware and fixing technical problems as quickly as possible
- provide first-time resolution by troubleshooting and diagnosing or escalating faults to the major
  incident managers and problem managers to investigate and resolve
- coach apprentices and share knowledge with team colleagues

The framework adds that in some departments your responsibilities may mirror those of an end user
computing engineer.

**Where this level sits.** This role has two levels only:

1. **Incident manager** — you are here
2. Major incident manager

Your next step is major incident manager: managing significant outages and crises, reporting issues to
problem management, and working closely with workplace services, infrastructure, applications operators
and communication managers. The framework notes that in some departments the major incident manager is
also the head of service.

**One thing worth knowing about this role.** Four of your eleven skills are worded **identically at
major incident manager** — asset and configuration management, problem management, service management
framework knowledge, and ownership and initiative is close. The step up is concentrated in incident
management itself, continuity management, service focus, change management, availability and capacity,
community collaboration and user focus.

**The eleven skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Asset and configuration management
2. Availability and capacity management
3. Change management
4. Community collaboration
5. Continuity management
6. Incident management
7. Ownership and initiative

9. Service focus
10. Service management framework knowledge
11. User focus

time than any diagnostic tool. Records that drifted from reality cost minutes you do not have.

### Skill: Change management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: implement change requests; and apply change
control procedures **under supervision**.

**What that looks like here.** Most incidents follow a change. Being the person who habitually asks
"what changed?" first, and who can find out, resolves more incidents than deep technical knowledge.

**The next step.** At major incident manager you analyse and assess the impact of change, document
change requests, and action changes from them.

### Skill: Community collaboration

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: contribute to the work of others; motivate

**What that looks like here.** This is unusually leadership-flavoured wording for this level, and it

noticing when someone has been on it too long.

### Skill: Continuity management

**What the framework expects at this level.** One thing: **explain** the basic principles of IT standard
continuity management processes and procedures.

**What that looks like here.** Continuity is the plan for when restoring normally is not possible.
Knowing whether your organisation's plan is real — tested, current, and known to the people who would
have to use it — is worth finding out before you need it.

**The next step.** At major incident manager you manage the runbooks and the processes around service
continuity, and **test the runbooks** to ensure service availability is maintained.

### Skill: Incident management

**What the framework expects at this level.** One thing: **diagnose and prioritise incidents,
investigate their causes and find resolutions.**

**What that looks like here.** Prioritising well is the part that separates good incident management
from fast incident management. The loudest caller is not always the most affected, and knowing who is
actually impacted is what makes a defensible decision.

**The next step.** At major incident manager you **lead** the investigation and resolution of
incidents.

### Skill: Ownership and initiative

**What the framework expects at this level.** One thing: own an issue until a new owner has been found
or the problem has been mitigated or resolved.

**What that looks like here.** Nothing you pick up gets dropped. In incident work that means the
handover at shift change is as important as the diagnosis — a handover that loses context restarts the
investigation.

**The next step.** At major incident manager you take accountability for issues that occur, are
proactive in searching for potential problems, and achieve excellent user outcomes.

### Skill: User focus

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: identify and engage with users or
stakeholders to collate user needs evidence; understand and define research that fits user needs; and
use quantitative and qualitative data about users to turn user focus into outcomes.

**What that looks like here.** Research-flavoured wording for an incident role. The practical version
is knowing who is affected and how badly — which is exactly what good prioritisation needs.

**The next step.** At major incident manager you collaborate with user researchers and represent users
internally, explain the difference between needs and desires, champion research covering all users, and
guide others.
