# IT service manager - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as an IT service manager in the UK Government Digital and Data profession.
Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out what you do now, where the gaps are, and
what would close them.

The framework describes this level as responsible for **defining service level agreements (SLAs) and
operational level agreements (OLAs) across all relevant business functions**. At this level you:

- **manage a fully functioning multi-sourced service support team** with a customer-centric support approach
  across multiple locations, focused on service availability and performance
- ensure that all service and support functions remain responsive to customer needs
- ensure that the service catalogue and wider IT service operations knowledge library are maintained
- ensure that adequate reporting and service standards are met for your specific service
- **ensure that all technical change is communicated and managed, with appropriate governance**
- **ensure that a programme of continual service improvement** is put in place across the wider service
  delivery function

Six accountabilities, five of them beginning "ensure". That is the shape of this level: you are answerable
for things happening rather than for doing them, which is a different job from the one below even where the
skills are worded the same.

Nine skills. Expect about 30 minutes.

## How to fill in this form

This is a static web page. There is nothing to type into and nothing to send from here — your answers never
touch this site.

Print it, copy the questions into a document, or paste them into whatever your organisation uses for
development conversations. Every question is numbered, so answers travel back on their own.

Worth knowing:

- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a finding.
- **"My team does this" is not an answer to "what do you do".** Name your part.
- **"I have not had the chance" is a real answer.**
- **Do not include anything sensitive** — no contract detail, no supplier disputes, no colleagues' names.
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers, what they are used for, or how long
  they are kept, ask before you fill it in.

## Rating scale

Where a question asks for a rating, use this scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | You have not worked with this |
| 1 | Awareness | You know what it is and why it matters |
| 2 | Working | You do it with support |
| 3 | Practitioner | You do it unsupported, and handle the usual exceptions |
| 4 | Expert | You set the approach and develop others in it |

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not the remit on your job description.
- **A 4 is a claim about other people** — that someone else's practice changed because of you.

A filled-in answer looks like this:

> **Strategic thinking**

>
> *Evidence:* I can explain how my service supports the department's objectives when asked. Contributing to
> the development of strategy and policy is in my level and I am not in those meetings — I find out what was
> decided afterwards.

Separates capability from access, which is the honest distinction at this level.

## About this role level

**The role: IT service manager.** IT service managers are responsible for managing the service delivery of
information and communications technology (ICT) services, and working with teams from IT service operations.

**Your level: IT service manager.** You are responsible for defining service level agreements and operational
level agreements across all relevant business functions, with the accountabilities listed in the
introduction.

**Where this level sits.** This role has four levels:

1. IT service analyst
2. **IT service manager** — you are here
3. Senior IT service manager
4. Head of IT service management

Your next step is senior IT service manager.

**Two things worth knowing about this role.**

First, **five of your nine skills are worded identically at the level below**: IT service reporting,
ownership and initiative, stakeholder relationship management, strategic thinking and user focus. The step
from analyst to manager is not mostly a skills step in the published text — it is a step into agreements,
governance and a team. If someone is assessing you against skills alone, that step will look smaller than it
is.

Second, **service focus is where your level genuinely separates from the one below**, and it is also where
the role stops. The analyst shows an awareness of different products and services; you see the bigger picture
across groups of services and investigate how to get the best of the underlying ones. Senior IT service
manager repeats your wording exactly, and head of IT service management **does not carry this skill at all**
— it has eight skills where the rest of the role has nine. Service focus peaks here.

**The nine skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Asset and configuration management
2. Business analysis (IT operations)
3. IT service reporting
4. Ownership and initiative
5. Service focus
6. Service management framework knowledge
7. Stakeholder relationship management (IT operations)
8. Strategic thinking
9. User focus

## Job skills

Nine skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the level expects, what it looks like in

### Skill: Asset and configuration management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: **maintain secure configuration and accurate
information**; **control IT assets in one or more areas**; and **verify the location and state of IT assets**.

**A step up from IT service analyst**, where it was tracking, logging and correcting information.

**The next step.** At senior IT service manager it becomes managing configuration items and related
information, and managing **service compliance and risk** — a reframing from operational accuracy to
compliance position.

**What that looks like here.** Across a multi-sourced service, the asset picture is assembled from several
sources that disagree. Reconciling them is the work.

### Skill: Service management framework knowledge

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: **show you have a Level 3 service management
framework qualification**; and **demonstrate knowledge of the life cycle or capability elements of ITIL**
(Information Technology Infrastructure Library).

**A step up from IT service analyst**, which requires the Level 3 qualification alone.

**The next step.** At senior IT service manager it becomes an **expert certificate** in the service
management framework qualification, and at head of IT service management that plus demonstrating an industry
understanding of how to implement the framework into numerous environments. Three escalating steps, each with
cost and study time attached.

### Skill: Stakeholder relationship management (IT operations)

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: identify important stakeholders and communicate
with them clearly and regularly; tailor communication to stakeholders' needs and work with them to build
relationships, while also meeting user needs; **build and reach consensus**; and work to improve stakeholder
relationships, using evidence to explain decisions made.

**Identical to IT service analyst.** At senior it changes substantially — negotiating with and influencing
stakeholders, dealing with challenging situations, removing blockers, and building long-term strategic
relationships.

**What that looks like here.** Defining agreements is a negotiation. Business functions want more than the
service can deliver, and the honest conversation about what is affordable is this skill, not a commercial
one.
