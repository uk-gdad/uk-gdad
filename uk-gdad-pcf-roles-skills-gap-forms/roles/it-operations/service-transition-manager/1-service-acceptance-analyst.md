# Service acceptance analyst - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a service acceptance analyst in the UK Government Digital and Data
profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out what you do now, where the
gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this level as co-ordinating **across IT operations** and managing **the acceptance
criteria for any changes** within the area. At this level you **validate changes against the acceptance
criteria, gathering evidence and creating materials as necessary**.

That is a gatekeeping job at the first level of a role, which is unusual. You are the person who says whether
something meets the criteria — and you will sometimes be saying it to people considerably more senior than
you, under time pressure, about something they want released today.

Eleven skills. Expect about 30 minutes.

  they are kept, ask before you fill it in.

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you actually do at work**, not what you could probably manage.
- **The gap between 2 and 3 is the exceptions.** Validating against clear criteria is a 2 or 3. Handling the

## About this role level

**The role: service transition manager.** IT service transition managers provide overall planning for service
transitions and co-ordinate the required resources. The role controls the product life cycle of all changes,
enabling beneficial changes to be made with minimum disruption to IT services.

**Your level: service acceptance analyst.** You co-ordinate across IT operations and manage the acceptance
criteria for changes, validating changes against those criteria, gathering evidence and creating materials.

**Where this level sits.** This role has four levels:

1. **Service acceptance analyst** — you are here
2. Service readiness analyst
3. Service transition manager
4. Lead service transition manager

**Something worth knowing about the levels here.** Unlike most roles in this framework, the two lowest levels
have **different job names rather than a junior and senior version of the same one**. A service acceptance
analyst validates against acceptance criteria; a service readiness analyst tests products and is accountable
for application acceptance and security criteria. They read as two related jobs, and moving between them may
be a sideways step as much as a promotion. Worth clarifying with your manager rather than assuming.

**Two things worth knowing about this role.**

First, **seven of your eleven skills are worded identically at service readiness analyst**: asset and
configuration management, continuity management, incident management, problem management, service focus,
service management framework knowledge and user focus. Only four change — availability and capacity, change
management, community collaboration, and ownership and initiative.

Second, **community collaboration jumps sharply above you.** Yours is understanding the work of others and
the importance of team dynamics. The level above is motivating and empowering teams, creating the right
environment for them, and identifying the best team makeup. That is a leadership expectation arriving at an
analyst level, and it is the biggest single change between the two.

**The eleven skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Asset and configuration management

3. Change management
4. Community collaboration
5. Continuity management

### Skill: Community collaboration

**What the framework expects at this level.** One thing: **understand the work of others and the importance of

stops that becoming adversarial.

### Skill: Continuity management

**What the framework expects at this level.** One thing: **explain the basic principles of IT standard
continuity management processes and procedures.**

**Identical at every level of this role** — acceptance analyst, readiness analyst, transition manager and
lead all read the same. It is the only skill here that never rises, which is striking in a role built around
moving services safely into operation.

**In plain terms.** Continuity management is how the organisation keeps working when something serious fails:
what the recovery plan is, how long recovery takes, what data might be lost.

**Why it matters in transition.** A new service without a continuity plan is a future incident with nobody
prepared for it. Whether that is in your criteria is worth checking.

### Skill: User focus

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: **identify and engage with users or stakeholders
to collate user needs evidence**; understand and define research that fits user needs; and **use quantitative
and qualitative data about users to turn user focus into outcomes**.

**Identical at service readiness analyst.**

**What that looks like here.** Acceptance criteria written from the operational side ask whether it can be
run. Criteria written with users in mind also ask whether it can be used. Those are different questions, and
transition is one of the last points at which anyone asks the second one.
