# Senior infrastructure engineer - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a senior infrastructure engineer in the UK Government Digital and Data
profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out what you do now, where the
gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this level as building, administering, supporting and maintaining solutions as
directed and according to departmental policy, working in a team of infrastructure specialists and engineers.
At this level you:

- ensure services are integrated, delivered and operated as required
- work with and support third parties in providing infrastructure services
- **work with technical architects to translate architectural designs into operations**
- **provide input into overall management** — workforce, budget, technology roadmaps, projects, and tasks
- lead and direct infrastructure specialist teams in building, managing, supporting, and maintaining solutions
  according to departmental policy, **if taking a managerial path**

That last clause matters. This is the level where the role forks, and the framework leaves the choice open
rather than splitting it into two files. Which side you are on changes what this form is asking you.

Eleven skills. Expect about 35 minutes.

## Rating scale

Where a question asks for a rating, use this scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | You have not worked with this |
| 1 | Awareness | You know what it is and why it matters |
| 2 | Working | You do it with support |
| 3 | Practitioner | You do it unsupported, and handle the usual exceptions |
| 4 | Expert | You set the approach and develop others in it |

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not the remit on your job description.
- **A 4 is a claim about other people** — that someone else's practice changed because of you.

> part of.

Names the work and separates what exists from what does not.

## About this role level

**The role: infrastructure engineer.** An infrastructure engineer designs, builds, manages and supports the
infrastructure services that underpin all internal user services and services to the public.

**Your level: senior infrastructure engineer.** Working in a team of infrastructure specialists and
engineers, you build, administer, support and maintain solutions as directed and according to departmental
policy — with the additional accountabilities listed in the introduction.

**Where this level sits.** This role has five levels:

1. Associate infrastructure engineer
2. Infrastructure engineer
3. **Senior infrastructure engineer** — you are here
4. Lead infrastructure engineer
5. Principal infrastructure engineer

Your next step is lead infrastructure engineer: managing third party provision of infrastructure services and
the provision of expertise to develop architectural solutions, throughout the service product life cycle, and
overseeing programmes and projects.

**Two things worth knowing about this role.**

First, **your systems design wording is identical to lead infrastructure engineer's** — both design systems
of medium risk, impact and complexity, select standards and methods, and review others' designs. Design is
not what separates you from the level above; the difference is supplier management and programme oversight.

Second, **coding and scripting stays at "describe" for you** — the same wording as the two levels below —
and only becomes reading and writing code at lead. If you write code today, you are already ahead of the
published expectation, and it will not show up in a framework-based conversation unless you raise it.

**The eleven skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Coding and scripting
2. IT infrastructure
3. Information security
4. Modern development standards
5. Ownership and initiative
6. Problem management
7. Service focus
8. Systems design
9. Systems integration
10. Testing
11. Troubleshooting and problem resolution

### Skill: Systems design

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: **design systems characterised by medium levels
of risk, impact, and business or technical complexity**; select appropriate design standards, methods and
tools, and ensure they are applied effectively; and **review the systems designs of others** to ensure the
selection of appropriate technology, efficient use of resources and integration of multiple systems and
technology.

**Identical wording at lead infrastructure engineer.** Design is not what separates the two levels.

**What that looks like here.** Reviewing others' designs is the part most often missing. It requires a forum,
authority and time, and organisations frequently provide none of the three.

**The next step.** At principal you design systems of *high* risk and complexity, control design practice
across an enterprise architecture, and develop implementation and procurement strategies.
