# Programme delivery manager - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a programme delivery manager in the UK
Government Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent
it so that you can set out what you do now, where the gaps are, and what would
close them.

The framework describes this role as accountable for the delivery of complex
products and services that are being delivered by multiple teams, or that carry
high technical or political risk. **Political risk is named explicitly**, which
is unusual and honest — some of what makes this job hard has nothing to do with
technology.

It expects you to manage dependencies of varying complexity, potentially feeding
into larger programmes and portfolios; remove blockers and manage risks,
commercials, budgets and people; balance objectives and redeploy people and
resources as priorities change; have in-depth knowledge of Agile and other
methodologies; understand, manage and communicate to complex stakeholder groups;
be the initial escalation point for the programme; hold an awareness of the
bigger picture; support the programme director by overseeing delivery of their
vision; and support and coach delivery managers.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Nine skills, each with an explanation, a
rating and some questions. Expect about 35 minutes.

## Advisory

This skills gap form is created by AI and is work in progress for educational
purposes. If you intend to use it in any way, please consult with your
organization's appropriate teammates, such as with human resources staff, or

## How to fill in this form

This is a static web page. There is nothing to type into and nothing to send from
here — your answers never touch this site.

Print it, copy the questions into a document, or paste them into whatever your
organisation uses for development conversations. Every question is numbered, so
answers travel back on their own.

Worth knowing:

- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a finding.
- **"The programme does this" is not an answer to "what do you do".** Name your
  part.
- **"I haven't had the chance" is a real answer**, and more useful than a
  generous reading of a thin example.
- **Some of this is politically sensitive.** Describe your programme at a level
  you would be comfortable saying in a corridor. Nothing here needs a name
  attached to it, and no question is asking for one.
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers, what they
  are used for, or how long they are kept, ask before you fill it in.

## Rating scale

Where a question asks for a rating, use this scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | You have not worked with this |
| 1 | Awareness | You know what it is and why it matters |
| 2 | Working | You do it with support |
| 3 | Practitioner | You do it unsupported, and handle the usual exceptions |
| 4 | Expert | You set the approach and develop others in it |

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not the remit on your job
  description.
- **A 4 is a claim about other people** — that someone else's practice changed
  because of you and would stay changed if you left the programme. Several are
  reasonable here, since coaching is written into four of these skills.

A filled-in answer looks like this:

> **Financial management**
>
> - [x] 2 — Working: I do it with support
>
> *Evidence:* I manage the budget I am given and I can make it work, including
> two reprofiling exercises. Influencing or creating a budget across the
> organisation is done by the programme director; I supply the numbers.

Named practice, and a clear line about where it stops.

## About this role level

**The role: programme delivery manager.** You are accountable for the delivery of
complex products and services that are being delivered by multiple teams or have
high technical or political risk.

**Your level: programme delivery manager.** The framework publishes this role with
a single level — the role and the level are the same thing, and the descriptions
are nearly identical. There is no junior version and no senior version. That is
unusual, and it has a practical consequence: a promotion conversation here is
about moving to a different role, not up a ladder within this one.

The framework expects that you:

- manage dependencies of varying complexity, potentially planning and feeding
  into larger programmes and portfolios
- remove blockers and manage risks, commercials, budgets and people
- balance objectives and redeploy people and resources as priorities change
- have an in-depth knowledge of Agile and other methodologies
- be responsible for understanding, managing and communicating to complex
  stakeholder groups, balancing priorities
- be the initial escalation point for the programme
- have an awareness of the bigger picture
- support the programme director by overseeing the delivery of their vision for
  the programme
- support and coach delivery managers

**Two things worth knowing about this role.**

First, *initial escalation point* is doing heavy lifting in that list. Everything
that nobody else can resolve arrives at you first, which shapes the week far more
than any planning process does.

Second, several of your skills are shared with the senior levels of other
delivery roles — Agile and Lean practices is worded exactly as it is for lead
product managers, and life cycle management likewise. Where those overlap, what
distinguishes your job is the programme scale and the commercial and financial
accountability that comes with it.

**The nine skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Agile and Lean practices
2. Commercial management
3. Communicating between the technical and non-technical
4. Community collaboration
5. Financial management
6. Life cycle management
7. Maintaining delivery momentum
8. Making a process work
9. Planning

### Skill: Agile and Lean practices

**What the framework expects.** Four things: coach and lead teams in Agile and
Lean good practices; create and tailor the right approach for a team, challenging,
evaluating and iterating the approach through the life cycle; experiment with new
and innovative ways of working to improve delivery across the organisation; and
act as a recognised expert and advocate for Agile and Lean approaches.

**What that looks like here.** Different teams in your programme work differently
because their work differs, and you can justify each difference. "In-depth
knowledge of Agile *and other methodologies*" is in your accountabilities too —
this role is expected to know when Agile is not the answer, which is a harder
position to hold in a programme than in a team.

### Skill: Commercial management

**What the framework expects.** Two things: act as the escalation point and
resolve large or high-risk commercial management issues; and coach others in
appropriate commercial management.

**What that looks like here.** Contracts, suppliers and the awkward space between
what was procured and what is actually needed. Being the escalation point means
the difficult supplier conversations reach you. Coaching others matters because
delivery managers often learn commercial management by absorbing it badly.

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What the framework expects.** Three things: mediate between people and
strengthen relationships, adopting the appropriate communication method with
stakeholders at all levels; manage stakeholder expectations and moderate
difficult discussions about high-risk and complex topics, even within constrained
timescales; and speak on behalf of, and represent the community to, large
audiences inside and outside the organisation.

**What that looks like here.** Moderating a difficult conversation about high
risk under time pressure is the hardest thing in this skill, and it is where
programmes are saved or lost. Representing the community to large audiences means
public speaking is part of this job, whether or not you enjoy it.

### Skill: Community collaboration

**What the framework expects.** Two things: solve and unblock issues between
teams or departments at the highest level; and coach the organisation on team
dynamics and conflict resolution, while also building and growing the community.

**What that looks like here.** Issues *between departments* is the demanding
part — problems where neither side owns the fix and both have reasons not to
move. Coaching on conflict resolution means treating friction as something to
work with rather than something to route around.

### Skill: Financial management

**What the framework expects.** Two things: influence or create complex budgets
across an organisation, programme or product view; and manage the budget you are
given and make it work.

**What that looks like here.** "Make it work" is a plain way of describing
something difficult — reprofiling, holding a contingency you will be pressured to
release, and being straight about the point where the budget no longer covers the
commitment. Influencing budgets at organisation level is a different exercise from
managing one, and many people in this role do only the second.

### Skill: Life cycle management

**What the framework expects.** Four things: lead and coach teams through
different phases of the life cycle; ensure effective support for the product or
service and its users; predict, prevent, mitigate and manage potential risks at
different phases; and contribute to the assessment of other teams, providing
guidance and support.

**What that looks like here.** Across a programme, teams are usually in different
phases at once, which means holding several different definitions of "on track"
simultaneously. *Ensure effective support for the product and its users* is the
item most often skipped in programmes racing to a launch date.

### Skill: Maintaining delivery momentum

**What the framework expects.** Three things: optimise the delivery flow of
teams; actively address the most complicated risks, issues and dependencies,
including where ownership exists outside the team or where no clear ownership
exists; and identify innovative ways to unblock issues.

**What that looks like here.** *Where no clear ownership exists* is the sentence
that describes most of programme delivery. The problems that reach you are the
ones nobody's job description covers, and picking them up before they calcify is
most of the value of the role.

### Skill: Making a process work

**What the framework expects.** Three things: identify and challenge
organisational processes of increasing complexity and those that are unnecessarily
complicated; add value and coach the organisation to inspect and adapt processes;
and guide teams through the implementation of a new process.

**What that looks like here.** Challenging a process usually means challenging
whoever owns it, and programmes accumulate process the way lofts accumulate
boxes. Coaching the organisation to inspect and adapt is the durable version —
otherwise you become the only person who can remove anything.

### Skill: Planning

**What the framework expects.** Four things: lead a continual planning process in
a very complex environment; plan beyond product delivery; identify dependencies
in plans across services and co-ordinate delivery; and coach other teams as the
central point of expertise.

**What that looks like here.** *Plan beyond product delivery* means the things
that are not software — training, communications, policy changes, legislation,
staffing, contracts ending. Programmes fail on those far more often than on code.
Continual planning means the plan is maintained rather than produced.
