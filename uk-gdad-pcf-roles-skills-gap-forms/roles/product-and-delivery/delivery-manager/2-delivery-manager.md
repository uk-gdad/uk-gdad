# Delivery manager - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a delivery manager in the UK Government
Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that
you can set out what you do now, what you have not had the chance to do, and what
support would be most useful over the coming months.

At this level you are accountable for the performance of the team. The framework
expects you to build and maintain teams so they are motivated and collaborating,
identify obstacles and help the team overcome them, focus the team on what
matters most to delivery, encourage continuous improvement, and coach team
members and others in Agile and Lean tools and techniques. The framework adds
that the complexity or breadth of products or teams varies in this role depending
on context — so two people at this level can have very different jobs.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Each of the nine skills gets an
explanation, a note on what changes at senior, a rating and some questions.
Expect about 40 minutes.

When you have finished, use Export TSV or Export JSON at the top of the
page to take your answers with you, or print the page, or copy them into

is numbered, so answers travel back on their own. Clear answers empties the

Worth knowing:

- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a finding.
- **"The team does this" is not an answer to "what do you do".** Name your part.
- **"I haven't had the chance" is a real answer**, and more useful than a
  generous reading of a thin example.
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers, what they
  are used for, or how long they are kept, ask before you fill it in.

## About this role level

**The role: delivery manager.** A delivery manager is accountable for the
delivery of products and services.

**Your level: delivery manager.** At this level you are accountable for the
performance of the team. The framework expects that you:

- build and maintain teams, ensuring they are motivated, collaborating and
  working well
- identify obstacles and help the team overcome them
- focus the team on what is most important to the delivery of products and
  services
- encourage and facilitate continuous improvement of the delivery team
- coach and mentor team members and others in applying the most appropriate Agile
  and Lean tools and techniques

The framework also notes that the complexity or breadth of products or teams
varies in this role depending on context.

**Where this level sits.** The delivery manager role has four levels:

1. Associate delivery manager
2. **Delivery manager** — you are here
3. Senior delivery manager
4. Head of (Agile) delivery management

Your step up is senior delivery manager: accountable for complex, high-risk
products and services, the main point of escalation, and coaching other delivery
managers. Commercial management makes the biggest jump — from being the supplier
contact to identifying contractual frameworks, selecting suppliers, negotiating,
and getting good value from contracts.

**The skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Agile and Lean practices
2. Commercial management
3. Communicating between the technical and non-technical
4. Financial management
5. Life cycle management
6. Maintaining delivery momentum
7. Making a process work
8. Planning
9. Team dynamics and collaboration

## Job skills

Nine skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the level expects,
what it looks like in practice, and what changes at senior.

### Skill: Agile and Lean practices

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: compare and select the
most appropriate delivery methods and processes; recognise, reflect and adapt
when something does not work, encouraging experimentation; use a blended approach
depending on context; help teams measure, evaluate and visualise outcomes; and
encourage reflective practice to improve ways of working.

**What that looks like here.** You choose the method to fit the work rather than
running the one you know. You have changed something because a retrospective said
so. Your team measures outcomes rather than throughput.

**The next step.** At senior you *coach and lead* teams in Agile and Lean good
practice, create and tailor the right approach and iterate it through the life
cycle, experiment with new ways of working across the organisation, and act as a
recognised expert and advocate.

### Skill: Commercial management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: act as the point of
contact for contracted suppliers; understand appropriate internal contacts and
processes within a government department; and understand how and when third
parties should be brought into digital, data and technology projects. This is the
same wording as associate — the framework does not raise it until senior.

**What that looks like here.** You manage the day-to-day supplier relationship.
You know who to go to internally when something needs to change. You know when
adding a third party would help and when it would just add a handoff.

**The next step.** The biggest jump in this role: at senior you take
responsibility for complex supplier relationships, identify contractual
frameworks and suppliers, negotiate, and get good value out of contracts. If
senior is where you are heading, this is the skill with furthest to travel, and
you cannot practise it alone.

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: listen to and
interpret the needs of technical and non-technical stakeholders and manage their
expectations; manage active and reactive communication; and support or host
difficult discussions within the team or with diverse senior stakeholders.

**What that looks like here.** You tell people about a problem before they hear
it elsewhere. You can host a conversation where a senior stakeholder wants
something the team cannot do. You translate in both directions rather than only
outwards.

**The next step.** At senior you mediate and strengthen relationships at all
levels, moderate discussions about high risk and complexity under constrained
timescales, and speak for the community to large audiences.

### Skill: Financial management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: balance cost versus
value; consider the impact of user needs; report on financial delivery; monitor
cost and budget; and understand how and when to escalate issues.

**What that looks like here.** You know what your team costs and what it is
producing for that. You can report honestly rather than reassuringly. You
escalate a financial problem before it becomes a surprise.

**The next step.** At senior you negotiate, influence or set budgets in complex
environments, and write or contribute to business cases and communicate
business-value propositions.

### Skill: Life cycle management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: manage the delivery of
products and services that add value through different life cycle phases, with
some support; identify whether the product or service should move to the next
phase and how; ensure it is developed in line with the appropriate service
standards for a phase; and use new information to adapt your approach.

**What that looks like here.** You know what phase you are in and what it
requires. You can say when a thing is not ready to move on, and hold that view.
You have adapted your approach because something you learned changed the picture.

**The next step.** At senior you work with the right people to move through the
life cycle, use *evidence* to decide when a team should continue, change
direction or stop, identify tools for each phase, and guide colleagues and
stakeholders through the phases.

### Skill: Maintaining delivery momentum

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: facilitate the delivery
flow of a team, managing the pace and tempo; and actively address internal and
external risks, issues and dependencies, including where ownership exists outside
the team.

**What that looks like here.** You keep work flowing rather than keeping people
busy. You chase dependencies owned by people who do not report to anyone you
report to. You protect a sustainable pace when pressure rises.

**The next step.** At senior you *optimise* delivery flow across teams, address
the most complicated risks and dependencies — including where no clear ownership
exists — and find innovative ways to unblock issues.

### Skill: Making a process work

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: identify and
challenge organisational processes of increasing complexity and those that are
unnecessarily complicated; add value and coach the organisation to inspect and
adapt processes; and guide teams through the implementation of a new process.

**What that looks like here.** You challenge a process that is wasting your
team's time rather than working around it. You have changed something outside
your own team. You help teams adopt a new process without pretending it is
painless.

**The next step.** Identical wording at senior and at head of delivery
management — this skill does not change again in the role.

### Skill: Planning

**What the framework expects at this level.** Six things: understand the
environment and prioritise the most important or high value tasks; use data to
inform planning; manage complex internal and external dependencies; provide
delivery confidence; remove blockers that affect plans and develop a plan for
difficult situations; and ensure teams plan appropriately for their capacity.

**What that looks like here.** Your plans use evidence rather than optimism. You
can give an honest confidence level. You plan for the team you have rather than
the team you wish you had.

**The next step.** At senior you lead a continual planning process in a very
complex environment, plan beyond product delivery, identify dependencies across
services, and coach other teams as the central point of expertise.

### Skill: Team dynamics and collaboration

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: effectively bring
people together to form a motivated team; help create the right environment for a
team to work in and empower them to deliver; recognise and deal with issues; and
help create the best team makeup depending on the situation.

**What that looks like here.** You have built a team rather than inherited one
and hoped. You deal with the difficult conversation rather than waiting for it to
resolve. You know what mix of people the work actually needs.

**The next step.** At senior you identify problems in team dynamics and rectify
them, use team health checks to stimulate the right responses, choose the right
type of feedback at the right time so decisions stick, and accelerate the team
development cycle.
