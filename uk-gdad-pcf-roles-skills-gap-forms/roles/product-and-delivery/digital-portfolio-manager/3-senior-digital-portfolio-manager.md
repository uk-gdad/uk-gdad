# Senior digital portfolio manager - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a senior digital portfolio manager in the UK
Government Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent
it so that you can set out what you do now, where the gaps are, and what would
close them.

At this level you are responsible for co-ordinating and managing a portfolio of
digital projects. The framework expects you to track, analyse and help deliver
outcomes across the portfolio; give guidance and deal with issues so teams can
deliver faster; manage governance and assurance processes and ensure standards
are met; and provide stakeholders with information on performance and related
risks.

Four of the six skills change at your level compared with the one below —
notably planning, which grows from an awareness of forecasting into a full
discipline, and communicating analysis, which now explicitly includes
communicating **negative** information. That last one is where most portfolio
reporting quietly fails.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Each of the six skills gets an
explanation, a note on what changes at head of portfolio, a rating and some
questions. Expect about 35 minutes.

## How to fill in this form

This is a static web page. There is nothing to type into and nothing to send from
here — your answers never touch this site.

Print it, copy the questions into a document, or paste them into whatever your
organisation uses for development conversations. Every question is numbered, so
answers travel back on their own.

Worth knowing:

- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a finding.
- **"The portfolio does this" is not an answer to "what do you do".** Name your
  part.
- **"I haven't had the chance" is a real answer**, and more useful than a
  generous reading of a thin example.
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers, what they
  are used for, or how long they are kept, ask before you fill it in.

## Rating scale

Where a question asks for a rating, use this scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | You have not worked with this |
| 1 | Awareness | You know what it is and why it matters |
| 2 | Working | You do it with support |
| 3 | Practitioner | You do it unsupported, and handle the usual exceptions |
| 4 | Expert | You set the approach and develop others in it |

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not the remit on your job
  description.
- **A 4 is a claim about other people** — that someone else's practice changed
  because of you and would stay changed. At senior, one or two are reasonable.

A filled-in answer looks like this:

> **Governance and assurance**
>
> - [x] 3 — Practitioner: I do it unsupported, and handle the usual exceptions
>
> *Evidence:* I redesigned our stage gates so small projects skip two of them,
> and it stuck. Supporting other portfolios in applying it is something I have
> not been asked to do.

Named practice, and a clear line about where it stops.

## About this role level

**The role: digital portfolio manager.** A digital portfolio manager co-ordinates
and manages a group — a portfolio — of digital projects, using an iterative and
flexible approach to help define governance processes for that portfolio.

**Your level: senior digital portfolio manager.** At this level you are
responsible for co-ordinating and managing the portfolio. The framework expects
that you:

- track, analyse and help deliver outcomes in your portfolio
- give guidance and deal with issues to help teams deliver faster
- manage governance and assurance processes, and ensure standards are met
- provide information to stakeholders on work performance and related risks

**Where this level sits.** This role has four levels:

1. Digital portfolio analyst
2. Digital portfolio manager
3. **Senior digital portfolio manager** — you are here
4. Head of portfolio

Your step up is head of portfolio: leading a team of portfolio managers, building
and leading that team, and supporting the professional development of the
portfolio community including line management and coaching. On the published
skills, only two change — stakeholder relationship management, which becomes
directing the relationship strategy for your teams, and strategic thinking, which
becomes defining strategies and policies. Communicating analysis is worded
*differently* rather than obviously more demanding.

**One thing worth knowing about this role.** *Agile working* is worded identically
at all four levels, including head of portfolio. The framework does not raise
that expectation as you become more senior.

**The skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Agile working
2. Communicating analysis and insight
3. Governance and assurance
4. Planning
5. Stakeholder relationship management
6. Strategic thinking

## Job skills

Six skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the level expects,
what it looks like in practice, and what changes at head of portfolio.

### Skill: Agile working

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: show an awareness of
Agile methodology and how to apply the principles in practice; take an open-minded
approach; explain why iteration is important; and iterate quickly.

**What that looks like here.** You run the portfolio itself iteratively — the
reporting, the governance, the way risks surface — rather than defending last
year's design. You do not force every team into one shape.

**The next step.** Identical wording at head of portfolio. This skill never
changes in this role, which is worth noticing if your job in fact requires you to
lead agile ways of working across teams.

### Skill: Communicating analysis and insight

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: turn complex data into
compelling, clear and actionable stories; share data communication skills with the
team and across government; present analysis and visualisations to communicate
complex messages; **communicate negative and positive information to
stakeholders**; and give tactical and strategic recommendations.

**What that looks like here.** Your reporting causes decisions. You deliver bad
news early and plainly, which is the part most portfolio reporting avoids. You
teach others to present rather than presenting for them.

**The next step.** At head of portfolio the wording changes rather than obviously
rising: communicating relevant, compelling stories using the most appropriate
medium, and making complex recommendations at tactical and strategic level.

### Skill: Planning

**What the framework expects at this level.** Six things: understand the
environment and prioritise the most important or high value tasks; use data to
inform planning; manage complex internal and external dependencies; provide
delivery confidence; remove blockers or impediments that affect plans and develop
a plan for difficult situations; and ensure teams plan appropriately for their
capacity.

**What that looks like here.** This is a large jump from the level below, where
the expectation was only awareness. You give a delivery confidence people rely
on. You manage the dependencies that cross projects. You notice when teams are
planned beyond their capacity and say so.

**The next step.** Identical wording at head of portfolio.

### Skill: Stakeholder relationship management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: work with the team to
develop and maintain an understanding of stakeholders; work with the team to
develop and implement stakeholder communications strategies; identify and resolve
issues, influence stakeholders and manage relationships effectively; and build
long-term strategic relationships, communicating clearly and regularly.

**What that looks like here.** Stakeholder understanding is shared across your
team rather than held in your head. Communication happens on a rhythm. You have
relationships that outlast the current portfolio.

**The next step.** At head of portfolio you *direct* the stakeholder relationship
strategy for your teams, ensure stakeholders' objectives are set and support teams
to meet them, and negotiate with senior stakeholders to unblock progress.

### Skill: Strategic thinking

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: work within a strategic
context and communicate how activities meet strategic goals; and contribute to
the development of strategy and policies.

**What that looks like here.** You can explain the portfolio in terms of what the
organisation is trying to achieve. You have contributed to a strategy rather than
only reporting against one. You say when a project no longer serves the
strategy — which is harder than it sounds when it has a sponsor.

**The next step.** At head of portfolio you *define* strategies and policies,
guide others on working in the strategic context, and evaluate current strategies
against business requirements.

## Legal compliance

Portfolio work handles information about projects, people and money, and portfolio
reports become records that may be disclosable. Portfolio governance is often
where accessibility, equality and public money obligations get checked — or
missed. These questions ask about awareness, training and support, not about
whether you can state the law correctly.

**Q34.** Which of these have you had training or a briefing on? Tick any that
apply.

- [ ] Data protection — the UK General Data Protection Regulation and the Data

- [ ] Records management and the Freedom of Information Act 2000
- [ ] Information security, and how information is classified and handled
- [ ] Accessibility — the accessibility regulations for public sector websites

- [ ] The public sector equality duty, under the Equality Act 2010
- [ ] The Civil Service Code, or your organisation's code of conduct
- [ ] Managing public money, or your organisation's financial rules
- [ ] Government technology and spend controls
