# Head of business analysis - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you are a head of business analysis in the UK Government
Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that
you can set out what you do now, where the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this role simply: you lead the business analysis function
across the enterprise area, and you build business analysis capability for the
organisation. It publishes no separate list of accountabilities for the level —
those two clauses are the whole description, and they are unusually demanding for
their length. "Building capability for the organisation" is a standing job that
nothing will remind you about.

Head of business analysis is the top of this role. Nothing here is scored, marked
or ranked. Each of the eleven skills gets an explanation, a rating and some
questions. Expect about 45 minutes.

## About this role level

**The role: business analyst.** Business analysts help teams analyse and
understand a business problem or opportunity; research how a business area works;
identify improvements, explore options and define success measures; elaborate
business and user needs; support prioritisation decisions; and understand the
business and policy constraints that apply.

**Your level: head of business analysis.** The framework's description is two
clauses: you lead the business analysis function across the enterprise area, and
you build business analysis capability for the organisation. Unlike most levels
in this framework, no separate list of accountabilities is published for you —
the skills below are the whole published expectation.

**There is no level above this one.** The business analyst role has six levels
and this is the highest. Where people go next is a question about other roles —
portfolio, product, delivery or wider leadership — and the framework does not
define it as a next step, so this form does not invent one.

**Where this level sits:**

1. Trainee business analyst
2. Junior business analyst
3. Business analyst
4. Senior business analyst
5. Lead business analyst
6. **Head of business analysis** — you are here

Only two skills change from lead: *adapting to delivery methodologies*, which
becomes setting and iterating standards and experimenting with new ways of
working; and *consultancy*, which becomes leading the definition of guidance for
how the organisation approaches delivery.

**One anomaly worth knowing.** The framework's wording for *digital and data
systems analysis* at your level is narrower than at lead — it reverts to the
senior business analyst text. On that one skill, the published expectation goes
down rather than up. That is the framework's own text, not an error in this form,
and it is worth ignoring in practice if your job needs more.

**The skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Adapting to delivery methodologies
2. Business modelling
3. Business process improvement
4. Consultancy
5. Context, problem and option analysis
6. Enterprise and business architecture (business analyst)
7. Defining and managing business needs, user needs and requirements
8. Stakeholder relationship management
9. Digital and data systems analysis
10. Testing (business analysis)
11. User experience analysis

## Job skills

Eleven skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the framework

### Skill: Adapting to delivery methodologies

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: set and iterate
standards for working within different delivery methodologies; actively promote
collaboration and agreement so the team delivers value at the right time;
experiment with new and innovative ways of working to enhance delivery outcomes;
and research new and evolving delivery methodologies and identify how your role
would work within them.

**What that looks like here.** The standards for how analysis fits delivery are
yours, and you revise them rather than defending them. You have run a real
experiment in how work is done, not just discussed one.

**Different from lead**, which determines the approach for its own role and
negotiates priorities rather than setting organisational standards and
experimenting.

### Skill: Digital and data systems analysis

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: lead the
identification and analysis of IT system capabilities, developing models and
system requirements; use systems analysis to support a deeper understanding of
how processes and systems work and to identify gaps; and work with other digital
professionals to design and develop system and user interfaces, identify gaps in
functionality and assess the effect of proposed software changes on users or the
organisation.

**Narrower than lead business analyst.** The framework's text for you here is the
*senior* business analyst wording — it does not include setting direction or
enabling others, which the lead level does. That is the published text. If your
job requires the wider version, you are working beyond the published expectation.

**What that looks like here.** You still understand the systems well enough to
challenge what you are told, and you can bring business and technical
perspectives together.
