# Trainee business analyst - skills gap form

## Introduction

Hello, and welcome to this form.

It is for you if you work as a trainee business analyst in the UK Government
Digital and Data profession. Someone in your organisation — human resources, or
your manager — has sent it so that you can say what you already do, what you have
not met yet, and what would help you most over the next few months.

Nothing here is scored, marked or graded, and nobody is comparing your answers
with anyone else's. Trainee is a learning level: you receive direction from more
senior business analysts and work with others or under supervision while you
build your skills. Gaps are the point.

The form walks through the seven skills the framework names for your level. Each
is explained before you are asked anything about it, so you will not need another
page open.

Most people take about 35 minutes. You can stop and come back.

## Advisory

This skills gap form is created by AI and is work in progress for educational
purposes. If you intend to use it in any way, please consult with your
organization's appropriate teammates, such as with human resources staff, or
people and organizational development staff, or legal compliance staff.

## How to fill in this form

You can fill this form in on screen. Type into the boxes, tick the boxes that
apply, and pick a rating where one is asked for. Your answers are saved in
this browser, on this device, so you can close the page and come back to them.
Nothing is sent anywhere.

So pick whichever suits you:

- Fill it in here, then use Export TSV or Export JSON at the top of the
  page, or print it. Clear answers empties the form afterwards.
- Copy the questions into a document or an email, and answer there.
- Answer in whatever tool your organisation already uses.

Every question has a number, like **Q7**, so your answers make sense on their own
without the questions attached.

Before you start:

- **"I don't know" is a real answer**, and a useful one — it tells people what to
  teach you.
- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a mark against you.
- **Small examples are fine.** One workshop, one process map, one requirement.
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers or what
  they are used for, ask before you fill it in.

## Rating scale

Some questions ask you to rate yourself. Use this scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | You have not worked with this |
| 1 | Awareness | You know what it is and why it matters |
| 2 | Working | You do it with support |
| 3 | Practitioner | You do it unsupported, and handle the usual exceptions |
| 4 | Expert | You set the approach and develop others in it |

Two things about the scale:

- It is about **what you do at work today**, not what a course covered. If you
  have only read about it, that is a 1.
- **There is no target rating.** A trainee ticking mostly 1s and 2s is describing
  exactly what this level looks like — the framework's own wording for you is
  full of "contribute to", "support" and "under supervision".

## About this role level

**The role: business analyst.** Business analysts help teams to analyse and
understand a business problem or opportunity; research how a business area works,
considering people, organisation, processes, information, data and technology;
identify areas for improvement, explore options, analyse the effects of change
and define success measures; identify and elaborate user and business needs;
support prioritisation and minimum viable product decisions using insight from
analysis; make sure new products and services meet business and user needs and
align with organisational goals; and understand the business and policy
constraints that apply.

**Your level: trainee business analyst.** At this level you receive direction
from more senior business analysts, and work with others or under supervision to
build and develop your skills.

**Where this level sits.** The business analyst role has six levels:

1. **Trainee business analyst** — you are here
2. Junior business analyst
3. Business analyst
4. Senior business analyst
5. Lead business analyst
6. Head of business analysis

Your next step is junior business analyst, and it is an unusual step: the
framework does not change any of your existing skills. What it does is add two
new ones — business modelling, and user experience analysis. The step is about
breadth rather than depth.

**One thing about the framework's own text.** It lists *context, problem and
option analysis* twice for your level, with identical wording both times. That is
a duplication in the published source rather than two different expectations, so
this form treats it as one skill.

**The skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Adapting to delivery methodologies
2. Business process improvement
3. Context, problem and option analysis
4. Defining and managing business needs, user needs and requirements
5. Stakeholder relationship management
6. Digital and data systems analysis
7. Testing (business analysis)

## Job skills

Seven skills, in the framework's order. Each one explains what the skill means,
what is expected of you now, what it looks like in real work, and what changes at
the next level.

### Skill: Adapting to delivery methodologies

**What this means.** Knowing how teams organise work — scrum, kanban, a staged
plan — and how analysis fits each.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: explain the
differences between delivery methodologies such as scrum or kanban; explain how
your approach would change based on the methodology; explain why iteration is
important; and explain how roles in a multidisciplinary team work together to
support delivery.

**What that looks like here.** You can say what your team's ceremonies are for.
You know why analysis in an iterative team looks different from a big document up
front. You know what everyone else in the team does.

**The next step.** Identical wording at junior business analyst. It changes at
business analyst, where you adapt how you work and what you produce to fit the
methodology rather than explaining it.

### Skill: Business process improvement

**What this means.** Understanding how work actually gets done, finding what is
wrong with it, and helping change it.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: contribute to the
analysis and evaluation of business processes to identify problems and
opportunities; contribute to the design and implementation of business process
improvements; and contribute to business process testing and usability
evaluation.

**What that looks like here.** You have mapped a process as it really is rather
than as the documentation claims. You have spotted a step that exists only
because of an old system or an old rule. You have helped check that a change
actually worked.

**The next step.** Identical wording at junior. At business analyst you analyse
and evaluate processes yourself, design and prioritise improvements with some
support, and validate them through testing.

### Skill: Context, problem and option analysis

**What this means.** Working out what the real problem is, what is going on
around it, and what could be done — with more than one option.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: describe the analysis
techniques you could use to understand the context, problem and options; explain
the importance of understanding that internal and external factors influence the
organisation; and contribute to identifying and assessing multiple options and
formulating recommendations, under supervision.

**What that looks like here.** You can name techniques and say what each is for.
You know what is happening around your work — policy, funding, other programmes.
You have helped put together options rather than one answer.

**The next step.** Identical wording at junior. At business analyst you plan and
use the most appropriate techniques yourself, define problem statements, evaluate
multiple options and formulate recommendations considering feasibility, benefits
and impact.

### Skill: Defining and managing business needs, user needs and requirements

**What this means.** Finding out what is actually needed, writing it down so it
can be built and tested, and keeping track of it as things change.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: support identifying,
analysing, capturing and validating business and user requirements; work under
supervision to manage requirements and support their prioritisation within a
defined scope; and provide distinct task-based outputs for parts of the project
as directed, using a predetermined requirements management life cycle.

**What that looks like here.** You can write a requirement someone could build
and test from. You know which requirements are agreed and which are still
someone's wish. You keep the record current.

**The next step.** Identical wording at junior. At business analyst you identify,
analyse, *challenge* and validate requirements, and co-ordinate and review their
prioritisation with limited supervision.

### Skill: Stakeholder relationship management

**What this means.** Knowing who has an interest in the work and what each of
them cares about.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: describe who your
stakeholders are and the importance of managing relationships with them; and
explain what your stakeholders find important and why.

**What that looks like here.** You could list the people your work affects and
say what each wants. You know who has to be consulted and who merely likes to be.
You notice when two stakeholders want incompatible things.

**The next step.** Identical wording at junior. At business analyst you identify
important stakeholders and communicate with them clearly and regularly, tailor
communication to their needs, build consensus, and improve relationships using
evidence.

### Skill: Digital and data systems analysis

**What this means.** Understanding what the systems can and cannot do, and
turning business needs into system requirements.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: support the analysis of
IT system capabilities and work under supervision to identify and specify system
requirements; and support the development of specifications and models for
bespoke systems or software packages.

**What that looks like here.** You know roughly what the systems in your area do
and where their limits are. You have helped write a specification. You can ask a
developer a sensible question about feasibility.

**The next step.** Identical wording at junior. At business analyst you identify
and analyse system capabilities yourself, and develop models and system
requirements with minimal supervision.

### Skill: Testing (business analysis)

**What this means.** Understanding how what was asked for gets checked, and
making sure requirements can be traced through to working functionality.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: understand the stages
and purpose of testing and have an awareness of the tools and techniques used;
and work under supervision to support the representation of business scenarios
and to trace requirements to developed functionality.

**What that looks like here.** You know what the testing stages in your team are
for. You can help turn a requirement into a scenario someone could test. You
understand why traceability matters when someone asks whether a requirement was
actually met.

**The next step.** Identical wording at junior. At business analyst you support
the use of tools and techniques, identify business scenarios and develop
acceptance criteria, and review prototypes, test plans and test reporting.

## Legal compliance

Business analysis shapes decisions with legal consequences: what data a service
collects, who a change affects, and whether a process treats people fairly. At
your level nobody expects you to be responsible for this, but you should know
these duties exist and know who to ask.

law right.
