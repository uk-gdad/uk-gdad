# Junior DevOps engineer - skills gap form

## Introduction

Hello, and welcome to this form.

It is for you if you work as a junior DevOps engineer in the UK Government
Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that
you can say what you already do, what you have not had a chance to try, and what
would help you most over the next few months.

Nothing here is scored, marked or graded, and nobody is comparing your answers
with a colleague's. Junior is a learning level by design: you build real things
and you do it under supervision, so gaps are expected.

The form walks through the nine skills the framework names for a junior DevOps
engineer. Three of them are new at this level compared with apprentice — systems
design, systems integration and user focus. Each is explained before you are
asked anything about it.

Most people take about 35 to 45 minutes. You can stop and come back.

This skills gap form is created by AI and is work in progress for educational

## How to fill in this form

You can fill this form in on screen. Type into the boxes, tick the boxes that
apply, and pick a rating where one is asked for. Your answers are saved in

Nothing is sent anywhere.

When you have finished, use Export TSV or Export as JSON at the top of the
page, or print it, or copy your answers into a document or an email, or answer
in whatever tool your organisation already uses. Every question has a number,
like **Q7**, so your answers make sense on their own. Clear answers empties
the form and deletes the saved copy. If other people use this computer, press
it when you have finished.

Before you start:

- **"I don't know" is a real answer**, and it tells people what to teach you.
- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a mark against you.
- **One real example beats a general claim.** One pipeline, one incident, one
  script.
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers or what
  they are used for, ask before you fill it in.

## About this role level

**The role: development operations (DevOps) engineer.** Commonly called DevOps
engineers, development operations engineers support the development and
operation of software through tools, environments and practices. In this role
you underpin good development processes: managing tools and testing
environments, central code control, maintaining development standards, and
writing software that automates systems.

Your users are mostly other engineers. When a pipeline gets faster or an
environment gets more reliable, the benefit shows up in everybody else's work —
which is also why the job is easy to undervalue and easy to overload.

**Your level: junior DevOps engineer.** A junior DevOps engineer learns on the
job by building software components. The framework expects that you:

- assist in the building of a complex story
- work under supervision
- are involved in implementation but not planning

"Implementation but not planning" still applies at this level. You build; the
shape of what gets built is decided with you rather than by you.

**Where this level sits.** The DevOps engineer role has nine levels:

1. Apprentice DevOps engineer
2. **Junior DevOps engineer** — you are here
3. DevOps engineer
4. Senior DevOps engineer
5. Senior DevOps engineer (management)
6. Lead DevOps engineer
7. Lead DevOps engineer (management)
8. Principal DevOps engineer
9. Principal DevOps engineer (management)

Your next step is DevOps engineer, and it is a substantial one. There, the
framework expects you to transform technical requirements into DevOps processes,
build complex stories *without additional support*, manage live test
environments and resolve issues preventing delivery. A tenth skill appears —
development process optimisation — and several below move up a band.

**The skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Availability and capacity management
2. Information security
3. Modern development standards
4. Programming and build (software engineering)
5. Prototyping
6. Service support
7. Systems design
8. Systems integration
9. User focus

## Job skills

Nine skills, in the framework's order. Each explains the skill, what is expected
at this level, what it looks like in practice, and what changes at DevOps
engineer.

### Skill: Availability and capacity management

**What this means.** Whether services stay up for the people using them and cope
with what is asked of them — including the tools other engineers depend on. If
the build server is down, everyone stops.

**What the framework expects at this level.** That you can *explain* the
processes. This is the same wording as apprentice, so the expectation has not
moved.

**What that looks like here.** You know what your team watches and where. You
have noticed a trend going the wrong way. You could explain to a new starter what
happens when an environment fills up or a queue backs up.

**The next step.** At DevOps engineer this becomes managing service components so
they meet business needs and key performance indicators — from explaining to
being answerable.

### Skill: Information security

**What this means.** Keeping systems and information safe from people who should
not reach them, and from accidents. In DevOps this is concentrated: pipelines
hold credentials, environments hold data, and toolchain access is often access to
everything.

**What the framework expects at this level.** That you *use* information security
practices and the available controls to contribute to protecting solutions and
services. You contribute — you are not responsible for designing the controls.

**What that looks like here.** You put secrets where they belong and never in
code or logs. You follow the access rules rather than working round them. You
have raised something that looked wrong, even if it turned out fine.

**The next step.** At DevOps engineer you design solutions and services with
security controls built in, engineered against specific threats.

### Skill: Modern development standards

**What this means.** The practices that keep code and configuration
understandable and safe to change — version control, small changes, review,
automated tests, automated builds, working in the open. In DevOps they apply to
infrastructure and pipelines as much as to application code.

**What the framework expects at this level.** That you can explain the most
important principles and how they apply to your own work, and that you apply
them under guidance.

**What that looks like here.** Your infrastructure changes go through the same
review as application code. You do not fix things by hand on a server and leave
the repository behind. You can say why the team works this way.

**The next step.** At DevOps engineer the framework repeats this wording, and it
changes at senior, where you apply the standards *and support others in applying
them*.

### Skill: Programming and build (software engineering)

**What this means.** Writing the software: designing a small piece, writing it,
testing it, correcting it and documenting it. In DevOps most of it is automation
— scripts, pipeline configuration, infrastructure definitions, small tools.

**What the framework expects at this level.** That you design, code, test,
correct and document simple programs or scripts under the direction of others.

**What that looks like here.** You write scripts that other people run without
asking you how. You test your automation rather than running it and hoping. You
leave enough documentation that the next person is not stuck.

**The next step.** At DevOps engineer this rises considerably: collaborating on
reviewing specifications, and producing programs or scripts of medium-to-high
complexity using the right standards and tools.

### Skill: Prototyping

**What this means.** Building a rough version to learn from — a throwaway
pipeline, a scratch environment, a spike. The point is the learning, not the
artefact.

**What the framework expects at this level.** That you understand when to use a
specific prototyping technique or method, and that you show the value of
prototyping to your team.

**What that looks like here.** You have stood something up quickly to answer a
question rather than arguing about the answer. You can say when a scratch
environment is worth the effort and when a whiteboard would do.

**The next step.** At DevOps engineer prototyping becomes a team activity you
help run: soliciting prototypes from others, establishing design patterns, and
choosing between several methods.

### Skill: Service support

**What this means.** Keeping live services and the tools around them working —
investigating faults, fixing them, and doing the routine maintenance that
prevents them.

**What the framework expects at this level.** That you help fix service faults
following agreed procedures, and that you carry out maintenance tasks on service
support infrastructure. The move from apprentice is from "do this specific
thing" to "follow the procedure".

**What that looks like here.** You can follow a runbook without someone reading
it to you. You have taken a support ticket through to the end. You do the
unglamorous maintenance — certificates, patching, dependency updates, disk and
log tidying — and know why it matters.

**The next step.** At DevOps engineer this becomes *identify, locate and fix*
service faults: diagnosing things nobody has written a procedure for.

### Skill: Systems design

**What this means.** Deciding the shape of a system — which parts there are,
what each is responsible for, and how they fit together. This skill is new at
your level; it does not appear at apprentice.

**What the framework expects at this level.** That you assist, as part of a team,
in designing components of larger systems. Assisting is the expectation: you take
part in the thinking, you are not holding the pen.

**What that looks like here.** You have been in the room when a design was
discussed and asked a question that changed something. You can draw the part of
the estate you work on. You understand why the pieces are separated the way they
are.

**The next step.** At DevOps engineer this grows a lot: translating logical
designs into physical ones, producing detailed designs, documenting them to a
standard, and designing systems with managed levels of risk.

### Skill: Systems integration

**What this means.** Making separate systems work together — the interfaces
between them, and what happens when the other side is slow, wrong or missing.
This skill is new at your level.

**What the framework expects at this level.** That you can explain the process
and principles of integrating systems, and describe the challenges of designing,
building and testing interfaces between them.

**What that looks like here.** You know which systems your pipelines and
environments depend on — identity, artefact storage, secrets, cloud APIs,
monitoring. You can say what happens when one of them does not answer. You
understand why testing across a boundary is harder than testing inside one.

**The next step.** At DevOps engineer you start building: simple interfaces
yourself, and more complex integration as part of a wider team.

### Skill: User focus

**What this means.** Keeping the people who will use the thing in mind while you
build it. For a DevOps engineer this has two audiences: the public who use the
services your organisation runs, and the engineers who use the toolchain you
build. Both are users. This skill is new at your level.

**What the framework expects at this level.** That you show an awareness or
understanding of user experience analysis and its principles, and that you can
explain the purpose of user stories and the focus on user needs.

**What that looks like here.** You can say who the public users of your services
are and what they are trying to do. You also notice when a pipeline or an
environment is painful for your colleagues, and treat that as a problem rather
than as their fault.

**The next step.** At DevOps engineer this changes shape rather than simply
rising: identifying and engaging with users or stakeholders to collect evidence
of need, defining research that fits those needs, and using quantitative and
qualitative data to turn user focus into outcomes.
