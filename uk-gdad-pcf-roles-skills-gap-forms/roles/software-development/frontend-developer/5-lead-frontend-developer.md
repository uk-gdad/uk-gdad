# Lead frontend developer - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a lead frontend developer in the UK
Government Digital and Data profession. It has come from human resources or your
manager, and it exists to establish where the gap sits between the work you do
now and what the organisation needs from someone at this level.

The framework calls a lead frontend developer an expert practitioner who
influences and mentors others: setting direction and assuring the quality of
development across teams, leading multiple or highly complex services,
contributing to the community, and promoting knowledge sharing and the adoption
of good practice. What the published skills add at this level is startling in
scope. Leadership and guidance now covers changing organisational structures,
leading strategy for a whole organisation, building consensus between
organisations, unblocking issues between departments at the highest level, and
coaching the organisation on team dynamics and conflict resolution. Very little
of that is about code, and most of it leaves no trace in a commit history.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked, and no answer is compared with a
colleague's. Each of the eleven skills gets an explanation, a note on what
changes at head of frontend development, a rating and some questions. Expect
about 45 minutes, and expect the useful answers to be the uncomfortable ones.

This skills gap form is created by AI and is work in progress for educational

You can fill this form in on screen: the boxes below take typing, the tick

Nothing is sent anywhere.

When you have finished, use Export TSV or Export as JSON at the top of the
page to take your answers with you, or print the page, or copy them into
whatever your organisation uses for development conversations. Every question
is numbered, so answers travel back on their own. Clear answers empties the

Worth knowing:

- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a finding.
- **"The teams do this" is not an answer to "what do you do".** Name your part.
- **"I haven't had the chance" is a real answer**, and more useful than a
  generous reading of a thin example.
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers, what they
  are used for, or how long they are kept, ask before you fill it in.

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**. At this level the temptation is to
  rate the remit rather than the practice; resist it.
- **A 4 is a claim about other people** — that someone else's practice changed
  because of you and would stay changed if you moved on. A few are expected

## About this role level

**The role: frontend developer.** A frontend developer designs, builds and
improves website software that meets user needs — writing clean, accessible code
following a progressive enhancement approach, creating code that is open by
default and easy to reuse, and understanding HTML, CSS and JavaScript.

**Your level: lead frontend developer.** An expert practitioner who influences
and mentors others. The framework expects that you:

- set direction and assure the quality of development across teams
- lead multiple or highly complex services
- contribute to the community, providing technical leadership, coaching and
  mentoring
- promote knowledge sharing and the adoption of good practice
- are proficient in a wide range of technical systems and able to identify
  appropriate technology and approaches

*Assure* is the word doing the work in the first one. Setting a direction is a
conversation; assuring quality across teams means knowing whether the direction
is being followed, which requires a way of knowing that does not depend on you
looking.

**Where this level sits.** The frontend developer role has six levels:

1. Apprentice frontend developer
2. Junior frontend developer
3. Frontend developer
4. Senior frontend developer
5. **Lead frontend developer** — you are here
6. Head of frontend development

Your step up is head of frontend development, and it is a narrower skills step
than the one you have just made. Only three of the eleven skills change:
community collaboration becomes unblocking issues between departments at the
highest level and building the community itself; prototyping becomes coaching
others and strategic service design end to end; and web performance becomes
advocacy and coaching rather than resolution. Everything else keeps its wording.
The real difference is in the accountabilities: influencing and developing
organisational strategy with counterparts across government, ensuring the right
conditions and environment for frontend developers to work, and developing
departmental capability through internal mobility, career paths, and recruitment.
That is a job about people and conditions, not about services.

**The skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Accessibility
2. Communicating information
3. Community collaboration

5. Modern development standards

### Skill: Accessibility

**What this means.** Making sure what your teams build works for everyone, and
carrying that argument to people who do not report to you.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: show a strong
understanding of accessibility and act as an advocate for users with
accessibility needs; build user interfaces that meet a predefined set of
standards; champion accessibility to internal *and external* stakeholders; offer
design solutions to mitigate the risk of failing accessibility testing; and
recommend the best tools and methods for accessibility testing.

**What that looks like here.** You bring solutions rather than objections. You
have argued accessibility outside your organisation — with a supplier, a
partner department, a vendor whose component was unusable. Your teams' testing
approach is one you chose and can defend.

### Skill: Community collaboration

**What this means.** Working across the whole organisation and beyond it,
building networks, and dealing with the dynamics that stop teams working.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: work collaboratively
in a group, actively networking with others; adapt feedback so it is effective
and lasting; use your subject to identify problems in the team dynamic and
rectify them; and identify issues through team health checks and stimulate the
right responses.

**What that looks like here.** Your network reaches other departments, not only
other teams. Feedback you give changes behaviour that lasted. You have named and
fixed a dynamic — a review bottleneck, a silent disagreement, a team that had
stopped asking questions.

**The next step.** At head of frontend development this becomes solving and
unblocking issues between teams *or departments* at the highest level, coaching
the organisation on team dynamics and conflict resolution, and building and
growing the community itself.

### Skill: Leadership and guidance (frontend developer)

**What this means.** At this level, leadership in the organisational sense:
structures, strategy, high-risk decisions, consensus between organisations, and
the psychology of how teams work.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Seven things: change organisational
structures to fixable and sustainable designs; lead on the strategy for the whole
organisation, marrying business needs with innovative analysis; make and justify
decisions characterised by high levels of risk, impact and complexity; build
consensus between organisations, public or private, or highly independent
stakeholders; solve and unblock issues between teams or departments at the
highest level; understand the psychology of a team and have strong mediation
skills; and coach the organisation on team dynamics and conflict resolution.

That is an unusually broad list for a technical role, and few people do all of
it. Rate what you actually do and be specific about the parts you do not.

**What that looks like here.** You have changed how teams are arranged, not only
how they work. You have made a decision where being wrong would have been
public and expensive, and you can say how you justified it. You have got two
organisations with different incentives to agree something.

### Skill: Prototyping

**What this means.** Building rough versions to learn from, disposably, so
decisions rest on evidence rather than seniority.

**What the framework expects at this level.** That you approach prototyping as a
team activity, actively soliciting prototypes and testing them with others;
establish design patterns and iterate them; and use a variety of prototyping
methods, choosing the most appropriate.

**What that looks like here.** You ask for prototypes rather than opinions,
including from people senior to you. The patterns your teams share get revised
rather than fossilised. You have used a prototype to change a decision above
your level.

**The next step.** At head of frontend development this shifts to sharing best
practice and coaching others in prototyping, and to looking at strategic service
design end to end — prototyping how a whole service should work rather than how
a component should.

### Skill: Systems integration (frontend developer)

**What this means.** Owning the path from source code to what a browser
downloads: the static assets build, the network configuration, and the tests
around the interface.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: define the static
assets build; co-ordinate all aspects of the integration and take responsibility
for the tests around the user interface; and configure the part of the system
that uses the network and test that it works as expected.

**What that looks like here.** The build across your services is a deliberate
design rather than an accumulation. You have co-ordinated integration across
teams who release on different rhythms. You know what your services do when the
delivery network, the API or the identity provider misbehaves.

### Skill: Web performance optimisation

**What this means.** How quickly services load and respond, what makes them
slow, and what that costs the people with the worst devices and connections.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: identify and resolve
web performance issues; collect data to monitor and resolve them; and guide
others on the best performance optimisation software and methods.

**What that looks like here.** Performance is monitored continuously across your
services and someone looks at it. You can read a trace and find the real cause.
Other teams come to you for the method rather than the fix.

**The next step.** At head of frontend development it becomes advocating for
performance best practice, coaching and guiding others, and actively collecting
data from multiple tools — less resolution, more influence.

## Legal compliance

directly: public sector websites and apps must meet an accessibility standard in
law and publish an accessibility statement. At lead you may be the person whose
assurance others rely on. These questions ask about awareness, training and

on your conduct.
