# Chief digital and information officer - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you are a chief digital and information officer in the UK
Government Digital and Data profession, or are doing that job under another
title. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out what
you do now, where the gaps are, and what would close them.

**One thing to know before you start.** The framework publishes this role and
describes what it involves, but it marks the role level as **not in use** and
publishes no expectations against any of its named skills. That is the
framework's own position, not an omission in this form. There is no published
standard to measure yourself against — so this form asks you to describe your own
practice against the skill names the framework publishes, and to say where the
gaps are.

Expect about 40 minutes.

## Advisory

This skills gap form is created by AI and is work in progress for educational
purposes. If you intend to use it in any way, please consult with your
organization's appropriate teammates, such as with human resources staff, or
people and organizational development staff, or legal compliance staff.

## How to fill in this form

You can fill this form in on screen: the boxes below take typing, the tick
boxes tick, and each skill has a rating to choose. Your answers are saved in
this browser, on this device, so you can close the page and come back to them.
Nothing is sent anywhere.

When you have finished, use Export TSV or Export as JSON at the top of the
page to take your answers with you, or print the page, or copy them into
whatever your organisation uses for development conversations. Every question
is numbered, so answers travel back on their own. Clear answers empties the
form and deletes the saved copy, which is worth doing on a computer other
people use.

Worth knowing:

- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a finding.
- **Do not put anything sensitive on this form** — no unpublished organisational
  risk, no commercially confidential detail, nothing above the classification the
  form will be handled at.
- **"I am accountable for it" is not an answer to "what do you do".** Name your
  part.
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers, what they
  are used for, or how long they are kept, ask before you fill it in.

## Rating scale

Where a question asks for a rating, use this scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | You have not worked with this |
| 1 | Awareness | You know what it is and why it matters |
| 2 | Working | You do it with support |
| 3 | Practitioner | You do it unsupported, and handle the usual exceptions |
| 4 | Expert | You set the approach and develop others in it |

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not the remit on your job
  description.
- **Because the framework publishes no expectation for these skills**, your
  rating is a statement about your own practice rather than a measure against a
  standard. The evidence line is the useful part, not the number.

## About this role level

**The role: chief digital and information officer.** The chief digital and
information officer shapes and enables the delivery of the organisation's
strategy through digital, data and technology, developing partnerships across the
organisation to design and deliver better outcomes that make lives easier. The
framework says that in this role you:

- lead innovative strategies that deliver the organisation's objectives
- identify and act on the changing landscape of digital, data and technology
- enable the use of innovative approaches and emerging technologies while
  ensuring inclusivity, security and ethical practices
- oversee the delivery, management and maintenance of sustainable digital
  solutions that meet strategic goals and user needs
- ensure data and technology architecture decisions support scalability,
  reusability and interoperability across government
- ensure the organisation remains safe, secure and resilient to cyber threats and
  attacks
- negotiate and secure investment for digital, data and technology
- advise on investment priorities across competing demands to maximise benefits
  and outcomes
- ensure the organisation anticipates and adapts to changes in capability needs
- are a trusted advisor to the board on all matters of digital, data and
  technology

**The role level is published as NOT IN USE.** The framework lists this role,
keeps the description above, and marks the level itself — and every skill
expectation under it — as not in use. There is no level above or below within
this role.

**The skills the framework names for this role**, with no expectations published
against any of them:

1. Capability building for digital, data and technology
2. Ensuring resilience of digital, data and technology
3. Financial management for digital, data and technology
4. Innovation in digital, data and technology
5. Leading digital products and services
6. Making strategic performance-led change
7. Optimising technology and data architecture
8. Shaping the direction of organisational strategy through digital, data and
   technology

technology decisions that carry statutory, security and commercial consequences.

can state the law correctly, and not as a check on your conduct.
