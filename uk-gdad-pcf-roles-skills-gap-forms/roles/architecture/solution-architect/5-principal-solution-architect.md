# Principal solution architect - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you are a principal solution architect in the UK
Government Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent
it so that you can set out what you do now, where the gaps are, and what would
close them.

Principal is the top of this role. The framework has you responsible for a large
programme or group of solution architecture projects, or a single very complex or
critical business area: leading teams of more junior solution architects, leading
multiple projects or programmes, building relationships with senior stakeholders
across the organisation *and its partners*, identifying opportunities to improve
the organisation and its partners, working with technology partners to inform
their roadmaps, taking a leading role in the overall direction of business and
digital capabilities, and inspiring other architects.

*Working with technology partners to inform their roadmaps* is unusual and worth
noticing. It means influencing what suppliers build, rather than choosing between
what they have already built — a longer game, and one that only works if you have
something they want.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Each of the eight skills gets an
explanation, a rating and some questions. Expect 45 minutes to an hour.

## About this role level

**The role: solution architect.** A solution architect designs solutions for
problems that affect the organisation — defining the problem and outcomes,
scoping to requirements, working within constraints such as legislation and
finance, designing and documenting solutions that can be implemented, complying
with standards and governance, and managing risks and decisions transparently.

**Your level: principal solution architect.** At this level you can be
responsible for a large programme or group of solution architecture projects, or
a single very complex or critical business area. The framework expects that you:

- lead teams of more junior solution architects
- lead multiple architecture projects or programmes
- build relationships with senior stakeholders across multiple business or
  technical areas in the organisation and its partners
- are proactive in identifying opportunities to improve the organisation and its
  partners
- work with technology partners to inform their roadmaps
- take a leading role in the overall direction of business and digital
  capabilities
- inspire other architects and help them understand how to meet organisational
  goals

**There is no level above this one.** The solution architect role has five levels
and this is the highest. Where people go next is a question about other roles —
enterprise architecture, chief technology or digital leadership — and the
framework does not define it as a next step for this role, so this form does not
invent one.

**Where this level sits:**

1. Associate solution architect
2. Solution architect
3. Senior solution architect
4. Lead solution architect
5. **Principal solution architect** — you are here

Four skills are yours rather than shared with lead. Architect for the whole
context turns outward to societal and industry trends and to informing policy
beyond your organisation. Community collaboration becomes unblocking issues
between departments at the highest level and coaching the organisation on
dynamics. Making architectural decisions rises to high risk and complexity, risks
across the organisation or wider government, leading and evolving governance, and
representing architectural governance within wider governance such as legal or
commercial. And strategy design becomes defining and connecting strategies across
the organisation or wider government.

**How this role relates to enterprise architect.** Seven of your eight skills are
worded identically to the enterprise architect role at the equivalent level. The
published difference is the eighth: yours is technical design throughout the life
cycle; theirs is enterprise architecture. At this level the two jobs are close
enough that it is worth knowing which one your organisation thinks you are
doing.

**The skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Architect for the whole context
2. Architecture communication
3. Commercial perspective
4. Community collaboration
5. Making architectural decisions
6. Problem definition and shaping
7. Strategy design
8. Technical design throughout the life cycle

## Job skills

Eight skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the framework
expects, what it looks like in practice, and where it differs from lead.

### Skill: Architect for the whole context

**What this means.** Looking beyond the organisation — at society, industry and
policy — and designing so that what is coming does not break what you built.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: assess how trends in
society and industry practices might impact the organisation; work with people
outside your organisation to inform policies, strategies and standards;
anticipate changes to policy and build resilience through your architectural
work; and coach others in identifying important trends.

**What that looks like here.** You can name a trend and say what it means for
your designs in three years. You contribute where standards and policies get
made, not only where they get applied. Something you designed absorbed a policy
change that hurt others.

**Different from lead**, which supports wider organisational objectives and
tracks internal and external issues rather than assessing societal trends and
informing policy outside the organisation.

### Skill: Architecture communication

**What this means.** Communicating and mediating across organisations, winning
support for high-risk decisions, and coaching others to do it.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: communicate with
technical and non-technical stakeholders at all levels and across organisations,
using architecture communication techniques; mediate between people in difficult
architectural discussions; gain support from business and technical stakeholders
for architectural topics with high levels of risk, impact and complexity; and
coach and support others in architecture communication.

**What that looks like here.** You hold rooms where people disagree and have
authority. You win backing for decisions whose payoff is years away. Your
architects present without you.

**Same wording as lead solution architect**, and there is no level above.

### Skill: Commercial perspective

**What this means.** Choosing how and from whom your organisation buys, and —
at this level — influencing what partners build in the first place.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: identify appropriate
contractual frameworks and approaches; and identify, evaluate and select
appropriate suppliers.

**What that looks like here.** You choose the purchasing route as deliberately as
the technology. You have selected suppliers and lived with the results. And,
because your accountabilities include informing partners' roadmaps, you use the
relationship rather than only the contract.

### Skill: Community collaboration

**What this means.** Unblocking what is stuck between departments, and coaching
the organisation on how people work together.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: solve and unblock
issues between teams or departments at the highest level; and coach the
organisation on team dynamics and conflict resolution, while also building and
growing the community.

**What that looks like here.** You have resolved something between departments
that had been stuck for months. You coach other leaders on conflict, not only
your own architects. The architecture community is larger and more active than
when you arrived.

**Different from lead**, which networks, gives lasting feedback and addresses
team-level dynamics rather than departmental unblocking and community building.

### Skill: Making architectural decisions

**What this means.** Owning the highest-risk decisions, and owning the governance
that decides how such decisions are made.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: make and guide
architectural design decisions characterised by high levels of risk and
complexity; identify and address architectural risks across the organisation or
wider government; lead and evolve architectural governance and assurance; and
represent architectural governance as part of wider governance, for example legal
or commercial.

**What that looks like here.** You make the calls where being wrong is public and
expensive. Governance is yours to change, and you have changed it. You sit in the
wider governance — investment, legal, commercial — as the architectural voice
rather than being consulted afterwards.

**Different from lead**, which decides at medium risk and contributes to
governance rather than leading and evolving it.

### Skill: Problem definition and shaping

**What this means.** Leading the framing of the hardest problems, and teaching
others to frame their own.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: lead the framing of a
problem characterised by high complexity, complication or risk so that a solution
can be created; coach others in defining problems and describing appropriate
options for solutions; and help others challenge requirements and assumptions and
identify opportunities when defining problems and solution options.

**What that looks like here.** When something is a mess at the top of the
organisation, you are asked what the problem actually is. You coach rather than
reframing on people's behalf. Challenging a requirement is normal in your area
because you made it so.

**Same wording as lead solution architect**, and there is no level above.

### Skill: Strategy design

**What this means.** Defining and connecting strategy across the organisation and
beyond it, and clearing the way for it to happen.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: define and connect
strategies or visions across the organisation or wider government; and enable the
implementation of strategies or visions across the organisation or wider
government, for example by advocating for resources and removing blockers.

**What that looks like here.** You connect strategies written separately that
would otherwise conflict. You spend credibility moving resources and removing
blockers — political work as much as architectural. The strategy is something
people act on rather than a document that exists.

**Different from lead**, which defines strategies across teams and directs
implementation rather than connecting across the organisation and enabling by
advocacy.

### Skill: Technical design throughout the life cycle

**What this means.** Designing at high risk, leading others who design, and
improving the standards designs are held to. This is the skill that makes this
role solution architecture rather than enterprise architecture.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: create technical
designs characterised by high risk, impact and complexity; lead and guide others
in creating technical designs that achieve organisational objectives; and use
feedback to optimise and refine standards for technical designs throughout the
life cycle.

**What that looks like here.** You still design the things where being wrong
would be public. You guide other architects without taking their work over. What
delivery teaches changes the standard rather than one design — and you can name a
standard that exists because something went wrong once.

**Same wording as lead solution architect**, and there is no level above.
