# Trainee business architect - skills gap form

## Introduction

Hello, and welcome to this form.

It is for you if you work as a trainee business architect in the UK Government
Digital and Data profession. Someone in your organisation — human resources, or
your manager — has sent it to you so that you can say what you already do, what
you have not met yet, and what would help you most over the next few months.

This is not a test. Nothing here is scored, marked or graded, and nobody is
comparing your answers with anyone else's. Trainee is a learning level: you
support more senior business architects and you receive training on the work you
support, so that in time you can do it on your own. Gaps are the point.

Business architecture can feel abstract when you are new to it — a lot of
frameworks, diagrams and words like "capability" and "operating model". This
form explains each skill in plain terms before asking you anything about it, so
you will not need another page open.

Most people take about half an hour. You can stop and come back.

## About this role level

**The role: business architect.** A business architect develops an integrated
view of an enterprise — a business area or a whole organisation — and uses that
view to help the organisation put its strategy in place. In this role you:

- understand and apply business architectural principles, techniques and tools
  to business challenges
- ensure design work from projects and programmes aligns with strategy
- ensure business change is implemented effectively to meet strategy
- aggregate and disaggregate complex information from across your organisation
- communicate complex information about the organisation to stakeholders

In plainer words: you build a picture of how the organisation actually works —
what it does, who does it, with what information and which systems — and you use
that picture to help people make better decisions about changing it.

**Your level: trainee business architect.** A trainee business architect
supports the more senior business architect roles, and receives training on the
tasks they support so they can eventually work on their own. The framework
expects that you:

- develop your knowledge of the frameworks, practice and principles of business
  architecture
- work with other business architects to support the production of clear
  business architecture outputs
- support during workshops and meetings with stakeholders in the organisation

Supporting is the job at this level. Being in the room, taking the notes,
drafting the diagram someone else will challenge — that is how the craft is
learned, not a sign of being kept back.

**Where this level sits.** The business architect role has four levels:

1. **Trainee business architect** — you are here
2. Associate business architect
3. Business architect
4. Lead business architect

Your next step is associate business architect. A new skill appears there —
stakeholder relationship management — and every skill below moves from
supporting to doing.

**The skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Business architecture
2. Communicating information
3. Consultancy
4. Making and guiding decisions
5. Methods and tools
6. Strategic thinking

## Job skills

Six skills, in the framework's order. Each one explains what the skill means,
what is expected of you now, what it looks like in real work, and what changes
at the next level.

### Skill: Business architecture

**What this means.** Describing how an organisation works, in a structured way,
so that people can reason about changing it — what it does, how those things fit
together, and where the strategy would change them. Frameworks are the
vocabularies for doing that consistently.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: show an awareness of
business architecture frameworks, principles and techniques; and support the
application of frameworks to business change, under guidance.

**What that looks like here.** You can read your organisation's capability model
and say roughly what it shows. You know which framework your team works to and
why they chose it. You have helped fill in, tidy or extend a model someone else
owned.

**The next step.** At associate you help support the *adoption* of business
architecture, communicate frameworks clearly to others, support the production
of a multidimensional view of the organisation, and assess the impact of
strategies while highlighting risks and issues.

### Skill: Communicating information

**What this means.** Understanding what people are telling you about their part
of the organisation, and being understood in return — often across teams that
use the same words to mean different things.

**What the framework expects at this level.** That you listen to the needs of
design and business stakeholders and interpret that information, and that you
take part in discussions within a multidisciplinary team.

**What that looks like here.** You take notes in a workshop that turn out to be
useful afterwards. You ask what a term means rather than assuming. You can
repeat back what someone said about their work and have them agree with your
summary.

**The next step.** At associate this grows considerably: building relationships,
managing stakeholder expectations, communicating both proactively and reactively,
and hosting or moderating difficult discussions.

### Skill: Consultancy

**What this means.** Being asked for advice and giving it well: understanding
what the real problem is, framing it so others can see it, and recommending what
to do.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: show an understanding
of the need for consulting in your role and its purpose; work under guidance to
identify what advice, guidance and recommendations might be appropriate, usually
by referring to previous work by others; and show an understanding of how to
frame problems that need solving.

**What that looks like here.** You can find how your team advised on a similar
question before, and reuse it sensibly. You can tell the difference between what
someone asked for and what they actually need. You know that advice which is not
usable is not advice.

**The next step.** At associate you provide advice, guidance and recommendations
based on your own specialist knowledge, propose methodologies and approaches, and
frame problems so they can be understood and troubleshot.

### Skill: Making and guiding decisions

**What this means.** Helping decisions get made well — setting out the options,
explaining the reasoning, and noticing when two people disagree about something
technical.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: recommend decisions and
describe the reasoning behind them; and identify and articulate technical
disputes between direct peers and local stakeholders.

**What that looks like here.** You can write down two options and what each one
costs, rather than just naming your favourite. When two people in a meeting are
disagreeing without noticing, you can say what the disagreement is actually
about.

**The next step.** At associate you make decisions of managed risk and
complexity yourself, recommend where risk is higher, and resolve technical
disputes between wider peers and indirect stakeholders.

### Skill: Methods and tools

**What this means.** The techniques and software used to plan, analyse, develop,
test and improve systems and services — modelling notations, mapping techniques,
architecture repositories, workshop methods.

**What the framework expects at this level.** That you follow advice to apply the
most appropriate tools and techniques to support planning, analysis,
development, testing, implementation and improvement.

**What that looks like here.** You use the tools your team uses without needing
step-by-step help. You know why a particular technique was chosen for a
particular question, because someone explained it.

**The next step.** At associate you *select* the most appropriate tools and
techniques yourself, within a project.

### Skill: Strategic thinking

**What this means.** Understanding why your work exists — what the organisation
is trying to achieve, and how the thing you are working on serves it.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: explain the strategic
context of your work and why it is important; and support strategic planning in
an administrative capacity.

**What that looks like here.** You can say what your organisation's strategy
actually says, in your own words. You can connect a piece of work to a strategic
aim. You have helped prepare material for a planning session, even if you did
not run it.

**The next step.** At associate you work within the strategic context and
communicate how activities meet strategic goals, and you contribute to the
development of strategy and policies.

## Legal compliance

Architecture work shapes decisions that carry legal weight — what data a service
holds, how accessible it is, how it is bought, and sometimes how people's jobs
change. At your level nobody expects you to be responsible for that, but you
should know these duties exist and know who to ask.

These questions ask about **training and support**, not about whether you get the
law right.
