# Business architect - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a business architect in the UK Government
Digital and Data profession. It has come from human resources or your manager,
and it exists to establish where the gap sits between what you do now and what
the organisation needs from someone at this level.

At this level the framework has you supporting executive work and the management
of the architecture practice itself, guiding others in the tools and methods,
creating abstract representations of the organisation and knowing when each is
worth using, and developing standard, scalable models for collecting and
breaking down complex information. In other words: you are no longer only
producing architecture, you are shaping how it gets produced and who it reaches.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked, and no answer is compared with a
colleague's. Each of the eight skills gets an explanation, a note on what
changes at lead, a rating and some questions. Expect about 40 minutes.

This skills gap form is created by AI and is work in progress for educational

You can fill this form in on screen: the boxes below take typing, the tick

When you have finished, use Export TSV or Export as JSON at the top of the
page to take your answers with you, or print the page, or copy them into
whatever your organisation uses for development conversations. Every question
is numbered, so answers travel back on their own. Clear answers empties the

Worth knowing:

- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a finding.
- **"I haven't had the chance" is a real answer**, and more useful than a
  generous reading of a thin example.
- **Where a question asks what you did, name your part** rather than the
  practice's.
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers, what they
  are used for, or how long they are kept, ask before you fill it in.

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not what you could pick up or did
  in a previous organisation.
- **A 4 is a claim about other people** — that someone else's practice changed
  because of you and would stay changed. At this level a few are reasonable.

## About this role level

**The role: business architect.** A business architect develops an integrated
view of an enterprise and uses it to help the organisation put its strategy in
place — applying architectural principles and tools to business challenges,
aligning project and programme design with strategy, making sure business change
is implemented effectively, aggregating and disaggregating complex information,
and communicating it to stakeholders.

**Your level: business architect.** At this level you use your knowledge and
experience to understand business scenarios, communicate the issues and
recommend next steps, and you support and guide other business architects. The
framework expects that you:

- support executive level work and the management of the business architecture
  practice
- guide others in using appropriate business architectural tools and methods
- understand and apply business architectural principles, techniques and tools
  to business scenarios
- create business architecture artefacts, including abstract representations of
  the organisation, knowing when to apply them
- develop standard and scalable models to collect, aggregate or disaggregate
  complex information

*Knowing when to apply them* is the phrase that separates this level from the one
below. Producing a model is a craft skill; knowing that this particular question
does not need one is judgement.

**Where this level sits.** The business architect role has four levels:

1. Trainee business architect
2. Associate business architect
3. **Business architect** — you are here
4. Lead business architect

Your step up is lead business architect, and it is narrower than it looks. Four
of your eight skills are worded identically at lead: communicating information,
consultancy, making and guiding decisions, and strategic thinking. Four change,
and they change in the same direction — from doing the work well to building the
capability that does it. Business architecture becomes simplifying frameworks
into clear actions, developing architecture maturity through something like a
community of practice, and identifying strategic misalignment across the
organisation. Methods and tools becomes setting direction and giving others the
authority to advise. Stakeholder relationship management becomes directing the
relationship strategy for teams. Strategic design and business change moves from
supporting definitions to defining them.

**The skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Business architecture
2. Communicating information
3. Consultancy
4. Making and guiding decisions
5. Methods and tools
6. Stakeholder relationship management
7. Strategic design and business change
8. Strategic thinking

## Job skills

Eight skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the level expects,
what it looks like in practice, and what changes at lead.

### Skill: Business architecture

**What this means.** Describing how the organisation works so that people can
reason about changing it, and making that description influential enough that
decisions rest on it.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: apply and develop
business architecture frameworks, principles and techniques in your
organisation; advise and guide the organisation to inform the implementation of
its strategy; share the value of business architecture with the organisation;
and produce clear multidimensional views of your organisation to support
decision making.

**What that looks like here.** Your views get used in decisions rather than filed
after them. You have adapted a framework to fit your organisation rather than
importing it wholesale. You have persuaded a sceptic that this work is worth
funding.

**The next step.** At lead it becomes simplifying complex frameworks into clear
actions, developing architecture maturity across the organisation — through a
community of practice, for instance — leading others in industry best practice,
and identifying strategic misalignment and recommending improvement.

### Skill: Consultancy

shaping how the organisation approaches its work rather than answering one
question at a time.

effectively.

**What that looks like here.** Your advice is sought before decisions rather than
after them. You have written guidance that other people follow. You have changed
how a programme approaches its work, not just what it decided.

### Skill: Making and guiding decisions

**What this means.** Making decisions of real consequence, and getting
independent parties to agree where none of them has to.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: make decisions
characterised by medium levels of risk and complexity, and recommend decisions as
risk and complexity increase; and build consensus between services or
independent stakeholders.

**What that looks like here.** You decide within your remit and recommend beyond
it, with the reasoning written down. You have got two services with different
incentives to agree an approach and stick to it.

**The next step.** Identical wording at lead business architect. What changes at
lead is the accountability around it — being answerable for the whole team's
outputs.

### Skill: Methods and tools

**What this means.** The techniques and software used to plan, analyse, develop
and improve systems and services — and at this level, whether the organisation
uses them consistently and well.

**What the framework expects at this level.** That you build capability in
methods and tools, ensuring they are adopted consistently and used effectively.

**What that looks like here.** You have taught a method rather than only applied
it. There is consistency across the practice's outputs because you worked at it.
You know which tools are used properly and which are used as decoration.

**The next step.** At lead you set direction and give others the means and
authority to advise on the best methodologies and tools, and you assess the
effectiveness of different approaches — Agile or plan-driven — recommending
tools to support collaboration and build capability.

### Skill: Stakeholder relationship management

**What this means.** Building and keeping the relationships the work depends on,
as a deliberate practice rather than as a by-product of meetings.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: work with the team to
develop and maintain an understanding of stakeholders; work with the team to
develop and implement stakeholder communications strategies; identify and resolve
issues, influence stakeholders and manage relationships effectively; and build
long-term strategic relationships, communicating clearly and regularly.

**What that looks like here.** Your team shares an understanding of who matters
and why, rather than each architect holding their own contacts. Communication
happens on a rhythm rather than when something goes wrong. You have relationships
that predate the current programme and will outlast it.

**The next step.** At lead you direct the stakeholder relationship strategy for
your teams, ensure stakeholders' objectives are set and support teams to meet
them, and influence and negotiate with senior stakeholders to unblock progress.

### Skill: Strategic design and business change

**What this means.** Understanding what is happening to the organisation and why,
and shaping the principles, patterns, standards, policies, roadmaps and vision
that guide how it changes. This skill is new at your level.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: demonstrate a good
understanding of business issues, events and activities and their short to long
term impact; support defining principles, patterns, standards, policies,
roadmaps and vision statements; effectively focus on outcomes rather than
solutions; and help to develop, maintain or update strategy in response to
feedback and findings.

**What that looks like here.** You can say what is happening to your organisation
this year and what it means in three years. You have contributed principles or
standards that others apply. When someone brings you a solution, you can find the
outcome underneath it.

**The next step.** At lead the same four move up a notch: a *strong*
understanding, *defining* rather than supporting the definition of principles and
roadmaps, and developing and updating strategy rather than helping to.

### Skill: Strategic thinking

**What this means.** Setting the direction that other people's work follows, and
checking whether it is delivering.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: define strategies and
policies, providing guidance to others on working in the strategic context; and
evaluate current strategies to ensure business requirements are being met and
exceeded where possible.

**What that looks like here.** You have written a strategy or policy that others
work to. You have looked back at one and asked whether it worked, rather than
assuming. You help others see their work in the strategic context rather than
doing that translation for them every time.
