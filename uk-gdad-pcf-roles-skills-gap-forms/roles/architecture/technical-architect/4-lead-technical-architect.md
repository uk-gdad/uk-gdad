# Lead technical architect - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a lead technical architect in the UK
Government Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent
it so that you can set out what you do now, where the gaps are, and what would
close them.

The framework describes a lead technical architect as working with multiple
projects or teams on problems that require broad architectural thinking:
responsible for leading the technical design of systems and services and
justifying the decisions, assuring other services and system quality so that
technical work fits the broader strategy *for government*, exploring the benefits
of cross-government alignment, mentoring within teams, and providing leadership to
other architects.

Cross-government alignment is unusual to see named at this level. It means
looking at whether something has already been solved elsewhere in government
before solving it again — which is easy to agree with and rarely resourced.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Each of the six skills gets an
explanation, a note on what changes at principal, a rating and some questions.
Expect about 40 minutes.

## About this role level

**The role: technical architect.** A technical architect provides technical
leadership and architectural design.

**Your level: lead technical architect.** At this level you work with multiple
projects or teams on problems that require broad architectural thinking. The
framework expects that you:

- are responsible for leading the technical design of systems and services,
  justifying and communicating design decisions
- assure other services and system quality, ensuring the technical work fits into
  the broader strategy for government
- explore the benefits of cross-government alignment
- provide mentoring within teams
- provide leadership to other architects

**Where this level sits.** The technical architect role has five levels:

1. Associate technical architect
2. Technical architect
3. Senior technical architect
4. **Lead technical architect** — you are here
5. Principal technical architect

Your step up is principal technical architect, and only two skills change: making
architectural decisions rises to high risk and complexity, risks across the
organisation or wider government, leading and evolving governance, and
representing architectural governance within wider governance such as legal or
commercial; and strategy design becomes defining and connecting strategies across
the organisation or wider government and enabling their implementation. Everything
else — including architect for the whole context — keeps your wording. Unlike the
solution and enterprise architect roles, technical architecture does not rise to
assessing societal trends at its top level.

**The skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Architect for the whole context
2. Architecture communication
3. Community collaboration
4. Making architectural decisions
5. Strategy design
6. Technical design throughout the life cycle

## Job skills

### Skill: Architect for the whole context

**What this means.** Working for the organisation rather than your own projects,
and tracking the slow-moving things that will matter later.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: work to support wider
organisational objectives beyond your immediate goals; track emerging internal and
external issues over time that could affect the work of teams across the
organisation; and take action to solve or mitigate problems by influencing
colleagues across the organisation.

**What that looks like here.** You have given up a technically better answer
because the organisation needed something else. You track issues rather than
reacting. You change things by influence, having no authority over the people
involved.

### Skill: Architecture communication

**What this means.** Communicating and mediating across organisations, winning
support for high-risk technical decisions, and coaching others to do it.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: communicate with
technical and non-technical stakeholders at all levels and across organisations,
using architecture communication techniques; mediate between people in difficult
architectural discussions; gain support from business and technical stakeholders
for architectural topics with high levels of risk, impact and complexity; and
coach and support others in architecture communication.

**What that looks like here.** You have mediated a technical dispute where both
sides had authority and expertise. You have won backing for something expensive
whose benefit is years away. Your architects present without you rewriting their
material.

### Skill: Community collaboration

**What this means.** Working across teams, giving feedback that lasts, and

### Skill: Making architectural decisions

**What this means.** Making and guiding consequential technical decisions, and
improving the governance that manages architectural risk.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: make and guide
architectural design decisions characterised by medium risk and complexity;
identify and address architectural risks that affect multiple teams or domains;
use architectural governance and assurance to make design decisions and manage
technical risks at the appropriate level; and contribute to the development of
architectural governance and assurance.

This is the first level in the role where the framework stops saying "with
support" — worth noticing if you have effectively been doing this for years.

**What that looks like here.** You guide more decisions than you make. You can
name the technical risks that cross team boundaries and nobody owns. You have
improved a governance process rather than only complying with it.

**The next step.** At principal this rises to high risk and complexity, risks
across the organisation or wider government, leading and evolving governance, and
representing architectural governance within wider governance such as legal or
commercial.

### Skill: Strategy design

**What this means.** Defining technical direction across teams, and the
principles and roadmaps that make it followable.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: define strategies or
visions across teams that align with organisational objectives; direct the
implementation of a strategy or vision, for example by creating roadmaps or
plans; define architectural principles and patterns; and develop or maintain
strategy in response to feedback and findings.

**What that looks like here.** There is a technical strategy, you wrote it, and
teams work to it. The principles in use are yours, and people can say why each
exists. When something is learned, the strategy changes rather than the learning
being noted.

**The next step.** At principal you define and *connect* strategies across the
organisation or wider government, and enable implementation by advocating for
resources and removing blockers.

### Skill: Technical design throughout the life cycle

**What this means.** Designing at high risk, leading others who design, and
improving the standards designs are held to.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: create technical
designs characterised by high risk, impact and complexity; lead and guide others
in creating technical designs that achieve organisational objectives; and use
feedback to optimise and refine standards for technical designs throughout the
life cycle.

**What that looks like here.** You own the designs where being wrong would be
public and expensive. You guide other architects without taking their designs
over. What delivery teaches changes the standard, not just one design.

**The next step.** Identical wording at principal technical architect.
