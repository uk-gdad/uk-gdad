# Senior technical architect - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a senior technical architect in the UK
Government Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent
it so that you can set out what you do now, where the gaps are, and what would
close them.

The framework describes a senior technical architect as working on large or
multiple pieces of work that are complex or risky: defining strategy and being
central to assuring services, collaborating with senior stakeholders and
providing direction *and challenge*, proactively identifying problems and
translating them into non-technical descriptions that can be widely understood,
and mentoring and coaching junior colleagues.

Translating a technical problem into language a non-technical audience can act on
is the skill this level lives or dies by. A risk nobody outside engineering
understands does not get funded.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Each of the six skills gets an
explanation, a note on what changes at lead, a rating and some questions. Expect
about 35 minutes.

## About this role level

**The role: technical architect.** A technical architect provides technical
leadership and architectural design.

**Your level: senior technical architect.** At this level you work on large or
multiple pieces of work that are complex or risky. The framework expects that
you:

- define strategy and are central to assuring services
- regularly collaborate and find agreement with senior stakeholders, providing
  direction and challenge
- are proactive in identifying problems and translating them into non-technical
  descriptions that can be widely understood
- mentor and coach junior colleagues

**Where this level sits.** The technical architect role has five levels:

1. Associate technical architect
2. Technical architect
3. **Senior technical architect** — you are here
4. Lead technical architect
5. Principal technical architect

Your step up is lead technical architect. Four skills change: architect for the
whole context turns to organisation-wide objectives and influencing colleagues
across it; architecture communication adds working across organisations,
mediating and coaching; making architectural decisions finally drops "with
support" and becomes making and guiding decisions at medium risk across multiple
teams; and strategy design moves from supporting to *defining* strategies and
principles. Technical design rises to high risk with leading others and refining
standards.

**Something the framework does at this level that is worth noticing.** Making
architectural decisions and strategy design are worded identically at technical
architect and at senior technical architect — including "engage with
architectural governance and assurance… **with support**". A senior technical
architect who is central to assuring services is still described as needing
support in governance. If you are already deciding and governing unaided, you are
working beyond the published expectation, and that belongs on this form.

**The skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Architect for the whole context
2. Architecture communication
3. Community collaboration
4. Making architectural decisions
5. Strategy design
6. Technical design throughout the life cycle

## Job skills

Six skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the level expects,
what it looks like in practice, and what changes at lead.

### Skill: Architect for the whole context

**What this means.** Making sure your technical work fits with everything else in
motion — other architects, other teams, and how technology is moving.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: align your work with
the work being done by other architects and technical professionals; track
emerging issues, strategies, roadmaps, patterns and technologies over time to
assess opportunities and risks; and identify how other teams contribute to
delivering outcomes through change.

**What that looks like here.** You know what the other architects are building
towards and where your technology choices constrain theirs. You track technology
direction over time rather than sampling it when a decision arrives.

**The next step.** At lead this turns outward: supporting wider organisational
objectives beyond your own goals, tracking internal and external issues that
could affect teams across the organisation, and influencing colleagues across it.

### Skill: Architecture communication

**What this means.** Leading the difficult technical conversations, and making
the message fit the audience.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: lead the communication
of complicated, complex or risky architecture topics with technical and
non-technical stakeholders; communicate with senior stakeholders across your
organisation; adapt your message and communication techniques to your audience;
advocate on behalf of a team to other stakeholders; and manage stakeholder
expectations effectively.

**What that looks like here.** You raise the risky part of the technical picture
yourself rather than waiting to be asked. You can describe a technical debt
problem in terms of delivery consequence and cost. You advocate for your
engineers in rooms they are not in.

**The next step.** At lead it becomes communicating across organisations,
mediating in difficult architectural discussions, gaining support for high-risk
topics, and coaching others in it.

### Skill: Community collaboration

**What this means.** Working across teams, giving feedback that lasts, and
noticing when the way people work is the real technical problem.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: work collaboratively
in a group, actively networking with others; adapt feedback to ensure it is
effective and lasting; use your subject to identify problems or issues in the
team dynamic and rectify them; and identify issues through team health checks and
help stimulate the right responses.

**What that looks like here.** Your network reaches other teams and other
organisations. Your design feedback changes what someone does next time. You have
spotted that a recurring technical problem was really about how two teams
communicated.

**The next step.** Identical wording at lead and at principal technical
architect — this skill does not change again in this role.

### Skill: Making architectural decisions

**What this means.** Making choices that are hard to reverse, seeing the risks
they carry, and working within governance.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: work with others to
make architectural design decisions characterised by managed levels of risk and
complexity; identify and address architectural risks relevant to your team or
domain; and engage with architectural governance and assurance to manage
decisions and risks, with support.

This is word for word the technical architect expectation. At a level that
defines strategy and is central to assurance, the framework still says "with
support" — which is worth naming rather than glossing over.

**What that looks like here.** You decide with others and the reasoning is
recorded. You can name the architectural risks in your domain. In practice you
probably handle governance unaided, whatever the published wording says.

**The next step.** At lead the support drops: making and guiding decisions of
medium risk, addressing risks that affect multiple teams or domains, using
governance at the appropriate level, and contributing to how governance itself
develops.

### Skill: Strategy design

**What this means.** Supporting the direction the organisation sets, and using
the principles and patterns that carry it.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: support the
development of a strategy or vision that aligns with organisational objectives;
challenge requirements and assumptions and identify opportunities to develop
strategy; support implementation, for example through a roadmap or plan; and use
architectural principles, patterns and constraints when appropriate.

Note that your accountabilities say you *define strategy*, while this skill says
you *support* its development. That gap is real, and worth resolving locally.

**What that looks like here.** You have contributed to a technical strategy
rather than only working under one. You challenge assumptions. You use the
principles that exist and say when one no longer fits.

**The next step.** At lead you *define* strategies or visions across teams,
direct implementation through roadmaps and plans, and define architectural
principles and patterns.

### Skill: Technical design throughout the life cycle

**What this means.** Designing, staying with the design, and keeping it coherent
as change arrives — which decides whether the thing is still maintainable in
three years.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: create technical
designs characterised by medium risk, impact and complexity; maintain appropriate
quality and architectural coherence of a technical design in response to change;
and use feedback to optimise and refine technical designs throughout the life
cycle.

**What that looks like here.** You hold the shape of a design while dozens of
reasonable-sounding changes arrive. You can say which you accepted and which you
refused, and why. You use what delivery teaches rather than defending the original
drawing.

**The next step.** At lead the risk band rises to high, you lead and guide others
in creating designs, and you use feedback to refine the *standards* rather than
individual designs.
