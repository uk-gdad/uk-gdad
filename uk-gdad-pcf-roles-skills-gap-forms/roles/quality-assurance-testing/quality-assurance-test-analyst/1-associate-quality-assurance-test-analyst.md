# Associate quality assurance test analyst - skills gap form

## Introduction

Hello, and welcome to this form.

It is for you if you work as an associate quality assurance test analyst in the
UK Government Digital and Data profession. Someone in your organisation — human
resources, or your manager — has sent it to you so that you can say what you
already do, what you have not met yet, and what would help you most over the
next few months.

This is not a test, which is a slightly odd thing to say on a form for a tester.
But it is true: nothing here is scored, marked or graded, and nobody is comparing
your answers with anyone else's. Associate is a learning level — you work with
other test professionals to learn quality assurance testing and analysis
techniques — so gaps are expected and useful.

The form walks through the six skills the framework names for your level. Each
one is explained before you are asked anything about it, so you will not need
another page open.

Most people take about half an hour. You can stop and come back.

This skills gap form is created by AI and is work in progress for educational

## How to fill in this form

This page is a web page. You cannot type into it and there is no button to send
it. Nothing you write goes anywhere near this website.

So pick whichever suits you:

- Print the page and write on it.
- Copy the questions into a document or an email, and answer there.
- Answer in whatever tool your organisation already uses.

Every question has a number, like **Q7**, so your answers make sense on their
own without the questions attached.

Before you start:

- **"I don't know" is a real answer**, and a useful one — it tells people what to
  teach you.
- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a mark against you.
- **Small examples are fine.** One test, one defect, one story.
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers or what
  they are used for, ask before you fill it in.

## About this role level

**The role: quality assurance test analyst.** A quality assurance test analyst
interprets testing needs, creating and executing appropriate tests to make sure
things work and match what was asked for. In this role you:

- plan, design and execute tests
- apply testing techniques and standards to learn about systems and reduce risk
- analyse artefacts and prototypes
- test outcomes and record defects
- seek to improve testing practices to enhance quality and reliability
- recommend testing approaches

Notice *learn about systems*. Good testing is not only checking whether something
matches the specification — it is finding out how the thing actually behaves,
which is often not what anyone wrote down.

**Your level: associate quality assurance test analyst.** At this level you work
with other test professionals to learn quality assurance testing and analysis
techniques. The framework expects that you:

- follow and apply test approaches, plans and strategies, under supervision
- analyse artefacts, such as user stories, prototypes, processes and designs,
  with support
- use basic functional and non-functional testing techniques
- increase your understanding of how to design and execute basic tests, develop
  reports, record outcomes and support the resolution of defects

"Functional" testing asks whether it does what it should. "Non-functional" asks
whether it does it well enough — fast enough, securely enough, accessibly
enough, under enough load.

**Where this level sits.** The quality assurance test analyst role has four
levels:

1. **Associate quality assurance test analyst** — you are here
2. Quality assurance test analyst
3. Senior quality assurance test analyst
4. Lead quality assurance test analyst

Your next step is quality assurance test analyst, where the supervision drops
away: you select techniques, design and execute tests yourself, conduct
exploratory testing, and communicate risks to stakeholders directly.

**One thing worth knowing about this role.** The framework words the *test
engineering* skill identically at all four levels of this role — including at
lead, where it still says "with support". The deeper engineering expectation
lives in the separate test engineer role rather than here. So if automation and
tooling are what interest you most, that is worth raising early: this role will
not grow that skill for you on paper, even as you become more senior.

**The skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Communicating between the technical and non-technical
2. Designing and executing tests
3. Managing, reporting and resolving defects
4. Test analysis
5. Test and quality planning
6. Test engineering

## Job skills

Six skills, in the framework's order. Each one explains what the skill means,
what is expected of you now, what it looks like in real work, and what changes at
the next level.

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What this means.** Explaining technical things to people who are not
technical, and choosing how to say something depending on who needs to hear it.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: explain why it is
important to communicate technical concepts in non-technical language; and
explain the types of communication that can be used with internal and external
stakeholders, and their impact.

**What that looks like here.** You can describe a defect in terms of what a user
would experience, not only what the log says. You know the difference between
raising something in a stand-up, in a ticket and in a message to a stakeholder.

**The next step.** At the next level this grows a great deal: communicating
effectively with technical and non-technical stakeholders, hosting discussions
with difficult dynamics, advocating for the team externally, and managing
differing stakeholder perspectives.

### Skill: Designing and executing tests

**What this means.** Working out what to check and how, building the tests, and
running them.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: contribute to deciding
the most appropriate test types and techniques; follow guidance to design, build
and maintain simple tests that align to user needs and requirements; execute
simple tests with support; and explain the value of automation within testing.

**What that looks like here.** You write test cases that someone else could
follow. You can say why a particular check exists, in terms of the user need. You
can explain why the team automates the tests it automates.

**The next step.** At the next level you set up environments with some support,
select types and techniques yourself, design and execute without the guidance
rail, conduct exploratory testing, and research new techniques.

### Skill: Managing, reporting and resolving defects

**What this means.** Recording what is wrong clearly enough that someone can fix
it, tracking it, checking the fix, and escalating when it matters.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: explain how to report
and track defects; follow a defect management process to report, communicate and
maintain defects with appropriate information; and retest and escalate defects
when needed.

**What that looks like here.** Your defect reports get fixed without three
rounds of questions. You include what you did, what happened and what you
expected. You retest properly rather than assuming the fix worked, and you say
something when a defect matters more than its priority suggests.

**The next step.** At the next level you help create the defect management
process, assess dependencies and risks with support, contribute to mitigation
plans, and communicate the impact of defects to stakeholders.

### Skill: Test analysis

**What this means.** Working out what matters about quality before you test —
reading the story, the design or the process and finding where the risk is.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: describe quality
characteristics and explain why they are important; analyse information such as
user stories, prototypes, processes and designs, with support; and explain what
might be a risk in achieving quality goals.

**What that looks like here.** You can name quality characteristics — usability,
accessibility, performance, security, reliability — and say why each matters for
your service. You read a story and ask the question that reveals the missing
case. You can say what worries you before testing starts.

**The next step.** At the next level you work with stakeholders to decide which
quality characteristics add value, determine what to test, advocate for the test
data, access and environments you need, and analyse information to identify
risks.

### Skill: Test and quality planning

**What this means.** The approach behind the testing — what will be tested, by
whom, when, and how anyone will know it worked.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: explain the value of
quality testing approaches, plans and strategies; explain how different delivery
methodologies affect them; follow them with support; and explain how to measure
their effectiveness and why that matters.

**What that looks like here.** You know what your team's test approach says and
where to find it. You can say how testing in your team differs from testing in a
plan-driven project. You have an opinion about whether the approach is working.

**The next step.** At the next level you create or adapt approaches based on
risk, follow the strategy and contribute to developing it, and contribute to
continuous improvement.

### Skill: Test engineering

**What this means.** The processes, environments and tools that make testing
possible — including automated tests and the machinery that runs them.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: explain why testing
processes, environments and tools are important; follow test engineering
practices and standards, with support; and support the maintenance of automated
tests and tools required for testing.

**What that looks like here.** You can explain why an unreliable test environment
costs the team more than it looks. You keep to the team's practices when you add
a test. You have helped fix or update an automated test.

**The next step.** Unusually, there is no next step within this role: the
framework uses exactly this wording at every level of quality assurance test
analyst, including lead. The deeper expectation lives in the separate test
engineer role. If this is the part you enjoy, say so — it may point at a
different path rather than a promotion.

## Legal compliance

Testing sits close to two legal duties in particular. Public sector services must
meet an accessibility standard in law, and testing is usually where that is
checked. And test data is real data more often than people admit — including in
screenshots attached to defect reports.

These questions ask about **training and support**, not about whether you get the
law right. This is not a check on your conduct.
