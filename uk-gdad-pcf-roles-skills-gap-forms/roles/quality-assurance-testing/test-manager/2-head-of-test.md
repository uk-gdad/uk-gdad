# Head of test - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you are a head of test in the UK Government Digital and
Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set
out what you do now, where the gaps are, and what would close them.

Head of test is the top of this role. The framework has you establishing policy
for the test team and setting the testing strategy: owning continuous
improvement, researching new technology and techniques and implementing change,
making the important decisions, building the quality assurance and testing
community, providing organisational leadership, possibly advocating test
capabilities inside and outside government, and making sure teams have the right
capabilities.

*Ensure that teams have the right capabilities* is the one to sit with. It is a
standing job rather than a project, it is the hardest to evidence, and it is the
first thing to slip when delivery is loud.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Each of the six skills gets an
explanation, a rating and some questions. Expect about 45 minutes.

## Advisory

This skills gap form is created by AI and is work in progress for educational

organization's appropriate teammates, such as with human resources staff, or

## How to fill in this form

This is a static web page. There is nothing to type into and nothing to send
from here — your answers never touch this site.

Print it, copy the questions into a document, or paste them into whatever your
organisation uses for development conversations. Every question is numbered, so
answers travel back on their own.

Worth knowing:

- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a finding.
- **"The organisation does this" is not an answer to "what do you do".** Name
  your part.
- **"I haven't had the chance" is a real answer** at this level too.
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers, what they
  are used for, or how long they are kept, ask before you fill it in.

## Rating scale

Where a question asks for a rating, use this scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | You have not worked with this |
| 1 | Awareness | You know what it is and why it matters |
| 2 | Working | You do it with support |
| 3 | Practitioner | You do it unsupported, and handle the usual exceptions |
| 4 | Expert | You set the approach and develop others in it |

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not the remit on your job
  description and not what you were excellent at three years ago.
- **A 4 is a claim about other people** — that someone else's practice changed
  because of you and would stay changed if you left. Several are reasonable
  here; six would be worth interrogating.

A filled-in answer looks like this:

> **Designing and executing tests**
>
> - [x] 2 — Working: I do it with support
>
> *Evidence:* I set the standards and I chair the decisions, but the strategic
> judgements about techniques come from my two leads and I would struggle to
> challenge them on the detail.

Named practice, and a clear line about where it stops.

## About this role level

**The role: test manager.** A test manager takes ownership of the quality
assurance and testing strategy as part of a broader risk-based approach.

**Your level: head of test.** At this level you take ownership of establishing
policy for the test team and setting the testing strategy. The framework expects
that you:

- own continuous improvement, researching new technology and new techniques to
  implement change
- make important decisions
- build the quality assurance and testing community and provide organisational
  leadership
- may advocate test capabilities, both internally and outside government
- ensure that teams have the right capabilities

**There is no level above this one.** The test manager role has two levels and
this is the highest. Where people go next is a question about other roles —
delivery, digital leadership, or leadership outside the profession — and the
framework does not define it as a next step for this role, so this form does not
invent one.

**Where this level sits:**

1. Test manager
2. **Head of test** — you are here

**Two things worth knowing about how this role is written.**

First, only two skills change between test manager and head of test: designing
and executing tests, and test analysis, both moving from leading across teams to
setting organisational standards and making strategic decisions. Everything else
is worded identically. So the step is mostly in the accountabilities —
continuous improvement, community, organisational leadership and capability.

Second, *test engineering* is worded here exactly as it is for an associate:
"follow test engineering practices and standards, with support". A head of test
is described as needing support to follow test engineering practice. That is what
the framework says. It is not an oversight in this form — but if your organisation
treats you as its technical authority on test automation, it is expecting
something the published framework does not describe, and that gap deserves to be
resourced rather than quietly absorbed.

**The skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Communicating between the technical and non-technical
2. Designing and executing tests
3. Managing, reporting and resolving defects
4. Test analysis
5. Test and quality planning
6. Test engineering

## Job skills

Six skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the framework
expects, what it looks like in practice, and where it differs from test manager.

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What this means.** Mediating, strengthening relationships, and speaking for the
testing community.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: mediate between
people and strengthen relationships, adopting the appropriate communication
method with stakeholders at all levels; manage stakeholder expectations and
moderate difficult discussions about high risk and complex topics, even within
constrained timescales; and speak on behalf of and represent the community to
large audiences inside and outside the organisation.

**What that looks like here.** You have repaired a relationship between testing
and a part of the organisation that had written it off. You hold the line in a
release decision when the pressure is at its highest. You speak for testers at a
level where nobody else will.

**Same wording as test manager**, and there is no level above.

### Skill: Designing and executing tests

**What this means.** Setting how testing is designed and executed across the
organisation, and making the strategic calls about technique.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: set standards and
influence organisational decisions for test types, techniques, design and
execution; coach others in them; advocate for continuous improvement and
refinement; and make strategic decisions on new or improved test types and
techniques used in your area.

**What that looks like here.** The standards exist because you set them, and you
know whether they are followed or merely published. You have retired an approach
that had outlived its usefulness. Your strategic calls about technique are
argued, not delegated and rubber-stamped.

**Different from test manager**, which influences and guides use and contributes
to standards rather than setting them and deciding strategically.

### Skill: Managing, reporting and resolving defects

**What this means.** Improving how defects are handled across the organisation,
and using what they reveal to change how work is done.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: lead and coach others
in improving test and defect management processes; support others in assessing
complex and challenging defects across the organisation; lead and coach others in
using defect patterns and trends to make tactical and strategic recommendations;
and influence improvements to quality processes, informed by those patterns.

**What that looks like here.** Defect and escape data reaches the people who
decide how software gets built, and changes what they decide. Your managers and
leads read the patterns themselves. You know which recurring problem is structural
rather than technical.

**Same wording as test manager**, and there is no level above.

### Skill: Test analysis

**What this means.** Setting how risk is found across the organisation and
getting it found early enough to matter.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: lead and guide
multiple teams in test analysis, ensuring it is implemented early in the life
cycle; advocate for risk-based analysis to drive improvements across many teams;
and set standards and principles for test analysis across the organisation.

**What that looks like here.** Quality enters delivery planning because you
changed something structural — a gate, a governance step, a definition of ready.
Risk-based analysis is the default across teams rather than the enthusiasm of a
few. The principles are written down and used.

**Different from test manager**, which leads work across teams and coaches
individuals rather than setting organisational standards and principles.

### Skill: Test and quality planning

**What this means.** Creating and running the strategies teams work to, and
guiding them with evidence.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: create and manage
multiple quality testing plans, approaches and strategies; lead and guide
multiple teams in adopting quality testing strategy; advocate for early quality
testing involvement in organisational delivery processes; and guide teams across
an organisation in optimising approaches using appropriate data.

**What that looks like here.** There is a strategy, you own it, and people
outside testing can describe what it commits to. Teams adopt it because it helps.
You guide with numbers and you know which of those numbers can be gamed.

**Same wording as test manager**, and there is no level above.

### Skill: Test engineering

**What this means.** The processes, environments and tools that make testing
possible, including automation and pipelines.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things, word for word what it
expects of an associate: explain why testing processes, environments and tools
are important; follow test engineering practices and standards, with support; and
support the maintenance of automated tests and tools required for testing.

**What that looks like here.** You understand the engineering well enough to fund
it, to argue for it at board level, and to know when a technical answer is
convenient rather than true. The building is done by engineers.

**Same wording at every level of this role, and at every level of the test
analyst role.** If you are being relied on as the organisation's technical
authority on test automation, that is beyond what the framework describes for
this job — and it is worth saying so and getting it resourced rather than
carrying it invisibly.

## Legal compliance

Testing carries two direct legal duties — accessibility, and the personal data
that reaches test environments and defect reports. Your role adds suppliers, tool
procurement, and the people decisions that come with capability. At this level
your assurance may be what the organisation relies on. These questions ask about
awareness, training and support, not about whether you can state the law
correctly, and not as a check on your conduct.
