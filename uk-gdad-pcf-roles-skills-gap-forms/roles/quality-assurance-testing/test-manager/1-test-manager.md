# Test manager - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a test manager in the UK Government Digital
and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can
set out what you do now, where the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework has you owning the quality assurance and testing strategy as part
of a broader risk-based approach: owning delivery, creating the strategy and
leading its implementation, being responsible for test improvement and
optimisation, possibly promoting test capabilities inside and outside government,
taking responsibility for talent, succession planning and supplier management,
and making the important decisions about testing within the delivery environment.

Talent, succession and suppliers are the part that makes this a management role
rather than a senior practitioner one, and they are the part least likely to have
been trained for. Several questions below are about them.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Each of the six skills gets an
explanation, a note on what changes at head of test, a rating and some questions.
Expect about 45 minutes.

## About this role level

**The role: test manager.** A test manager takes ownership of the quality
assurance and testing strategy as part of a broader risk-based approach.

**Your level: test manager.** At this level you take ownership of delivery,
create the strategy and lead its implementation. The framework expects that you:

- are responsible for test improvement and optimisation
- may promote and advocate test capabilities internally and outside government
- take responsibility for talent, succession planning and supplier management
- make important decisions relating to test within the context of the delivery
  environment

**Where this level sits.** The test manager role has two levels:

1. **Test manager** — you are here
2. Head of test

Your step up is head of test, and on the published skills it is a narrow one.
Only two of six change: designing and executing tests moves from influencing and
guiding to setting standards and making strategic decisions, and test analysis
moves from leading across teams to setting organisational standards and
principles. Everything else keeps its wording. The rest of the step is in the
accountabilities — owning continuous improvement and research, building the
quality assurance and testing community, providing organisational leadership, and
making sure teams have the right capabilities.

**Two things worth knowing about how this role is written.**

First, your skill profile is a mixture. Communicating, defect management and
quality planning are worded at the level the framework gives a *lead* test
analyst or engineer; designing and executing tests, and test analysis, are worded
at *senior* level. So this is not simply a rung above the lead practitioner
roles — the three roles in this profession run in parallel rather than in a line.

Second, *test engineering* is worded here exactly as it is for an associate:
"follow test engineering practices and standards, with support". A test manager
who owns the testing strategy is described as needing support to follow
engineering practice. That is genuinely what the framework says. If you are
technical, you will exceed it; if you are not, the framework is not asking you to
be — but your engineers still need someone who can tell a good technical answer
from a convenient one.

**The skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Communicating between the technical and non-technical
2. Designing and executing tests
3. Managing, reporting and resolving defects
4. Test analysis
5. Test and quality planning
6. Test engineering

## Job skills

Six skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the level expects,
what it looks like in practice, and what changes at head of test.

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What this means.** Mediating, strengthening relationships, and speaking for the
testing community rather than for yourself.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: mediate between
people and strengthen relationships, adopting the appropriate communication
method with stakeholders at all levels; manage stakeholder expectations and
moderate difficult discussions about high risk and complex topics, even within
constrained timescales; and speak on behalf of and represent the community to
large audiences inside and outside the organisation.

**What that looks like here.** You have repaired a relationship between testing
and a delivery or supplier organisation. You moderate go-live decisions where the
risk is real and the date is political. You speak for testers where they are not
in the room, including to people who think testing is a cost.

**The next step.** Identical wording at head of test.

### Skill: Designing and executing tests

**What this means.** Shaping how testing is designed and run across your area,
without doing it yourself.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: set up suitable
environments; influence and guide the use of appropriate test types and
techniques to mitigate risk early; lead others in designing, building,
maintaining and executing tests; contribute to developing and implementing
standards; and improve test types and techniques through a structured process.

**What that looks like here.** You know enough to influence technique choices
rather than defer to them. You have got environments sorted out — usually a
funding and priority problem rather than a technical one. You improve
deliberately and can show what changed.

**The next step.** At head of test this becomes setting standards and influencing
organisational decisions about test types and techniques, coaching others, and
making strategic decisions about new or improved techniques.

### Skill: Managing, reporting and resolving defects

**What this means.** Improving how defects are handled across the organisation,
and using what they reveal to change how work is done.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: lead and coach others
in improving test and defect management processes; support others in assessing
complex and challenging defects across the organisation; lead and coach others in
using defect patterns and trends to make tactical and strategic recommendations;
and influence improvements to quality processes informed by those patterns.

**What that looks like here.** Defect data reaches people who can change how
software is built, and does change it. You coach your leads to read trends rather
than reading trends for them. You escalate the ones that matter and let go of the
ones that do not.

### Skill: Test analysis

**What this means.** Getting risk found early across teams, and building the
habit in others.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: lead work with
stakeholders across teams to determine which quality characteristics add value;
determine if an approach needs to change based on effort and risk; ensure test
needs are implemented early; use multiple techniques to analyse complex
information to identify risks; and coach others in test analysis.

**What that looks like here.** You get the quality conversation into planning
rather than into the last sprint. You change approach when the risk changes
rather than defending the plan. Your analysts and engineers analyse risk before
they reach for tools.

**The next step.** At head of test this becomes leading and guiding multiple
teams with analysis early in the life cycle, advocating for risk-based analysis
across many teams, and setting standards and principles across the organisation.

### Skill: Test and quality planning

**What this means.** Creating and running the strategies teams work to, and
guiding them with evidence.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: create and manage
multiple quality testing plans, approaches and strategies; lead and guide multiple
teams in adopting quality testing strategy; advocate for early quality testing
involvement in organisational delivery processes; and guide teams across an
organisation in optimising approaches using appropriate data.

**What that looks like here.** There is a strategy, you own it, and people
outside testing can say what it commits to. Teams adopt it because it helps them.
You guide with numbers — escape rate, feedback time, cost of a late defect —
rather than with preference.

### Skill: Test engineering

**What this means.** The processes, environments and tools that make testing
possible, including automation and pipelines.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things, and they are word for
word the associate expectation: explain why testing processes, environments and
tools are important; follow test engineering practices and standards, with
support; and support the maintenance of automated tests and tools required for
testing.

**What that looks like here.** You understand the engineering well enough to fund
it, argue for it, and know when an answer is convenient rather than true. The
building is done by engineers.

**The next step.** Identical wording at head of test. This skill never rises
anywhere in this role, which is worth naming: if the organisation expects you to
be its senior technical authority on test automation, it is expecting something
the framework does not describe, and that gap should be resourced rather than
absorbed.

that ends up in test environments and defect reports. Your role adds two more:

state the law correctly, and not as a check on your conduct.
