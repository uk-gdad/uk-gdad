# Associate test engineer - skills gap form

## Introduction

Hello, and welcome to this form.

It is for you if you work as an associate test engineer in the UK Government
Digital and Data profession. Someone in your organisation — human resources, or
your manager — has sent it to you so that you can say what you already do, what
you have not met yet, and what would help you most over the next few months.

Nothing here is scored, marked or graded, and nobody is comparing your answers
with anyone else's. Associate is a learning level: you work closely with test
engineers and quality assurance test analysts to learn the activities and
techniques that establish the basis of testing. Gaps are expected.

The form walks through the six skills the framework names for your level. Each
one is explained before you are asked anything about it, so you will not need
another page open.

Most people take about half an hour. You can stop and come back.

## About this role level

**The role: test engineer.** A test engineer undertakes test planning activity —
capturing what has been discovered, defining tests, and estimating the effort
testing will take — as part of a broader risk-based approach.

**Your level: associate test engineer.** At this level you work closely with test
engineers and quality assurance test analysts to learn the activities and
techniques required to establish the basis of testing. The framework expects that
you:

- have domain and business knowledge
- develop test scripts under supervision
- raise awareness of and manage defects

*Domain and business knowledge* is listed first, which is unusual for an entry
level and worth taking seriously. A test engineer who knows the code but not the
service will automate the wrong things confidently.

**Where this level sits.** The test engineer role has four levels:

1. **Associate test engineer** — you are here
2. Test engineer
3. Senior test engineer
4. Lead test engineer

Your next step is test engineer, where you write, debug and refactor test code,
work with developers on unit-level coverage, and start creating and maintaining
automated tests yourself.

**How this role relates to the test analyst role.** This is worth understanding
early. Five of your six skills — everything except test engineering — are worded
by the framework *exactly* as they are for a quality assurance test analyst at
the same level. The published difference between the two roles is the test
engineering skill, which grows steadily through this role and does not grow at
all through the analyst role. In practice the difference is that you build the
machinery and they decide what the testing needs to prove. If the building is
what you enjoy, you are in the right role.

**The skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Communicating between the technical and non-technical
2. Designing and executing tests
3. Managing, reporting and resolving defects
4. Test analysis
5. Test and quality planning
6. Test engineering

## Job skills

Six skills, in the framework's order. Each one explains what the skill means,
what is expected of you now, what it looks like in real work, and what changes at
the next level.

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What this means.** Explaining technical things to people who are not
technical, and choosing how to say something depending on who needs to hear it.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: explain why it is
important to communicate technical concepts in non-technical language; and
explain the types of communication that can be used with internal and external
stakeholders, and their impact.

**What that looks like here.** You can say what a failing test means for a user
rather than quoting the stack trace. You know when to raise something in a
stand-up, a ticket, or a message.

**The next step.** At test engineer this grows a lot: communicating effectively
with technical and non-technical stakeholders, hosting discussions with difficult
dynamics, advocating for the team externally, and managing differing
perspectives.

### Skill: Designing and executing tests

**What this means.** Working out what to check and how, building the checks, and
running them.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: contribute to deciding
the most appropriate test types and techniques; follow guidance to design, build
and maintain simple tests that align to user needs and requirements; execute
simple tests with support; and explain the value of automation within testing.

**What that looks like here.** Your tests check something that matters rather
than something that is easy to assert. You can say why the team automates what it
automates and leaves the rest manual.

**The next step.** At test engineer you set up environments with some support,
select types and techniques yourself, design and execute without the guidance
rail, conduct exploratory testing, and try new techniques.

### Skill: Managing, reporting and resolving defects

**What this means.** Recording what is wrong clearly enough that someone can fix
it, tracking it, checking the fix, and escalating when it matters.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: explain how to report
and track defects; follow a defect management process to report, communicate and
maintain defects with appropriate information; and retest and escalate defects
when needed.

**What that looks like here.** Your defect reports include what you did, what
happened and what you expected, so nobody has to ask. You retest properly. You
know the difference between a broken test and a broken service, and say which it
is.

**The next step.** At test engineer you help create the defect process, assess
dependencies and risks with support, contribute to mitigation plans, and
communicate defect impact to stakeholders.

### Skill: Test analysis

**What this means.** Working out what matters about quality before you build the
tests — reading the story or the design and finding where the risk is.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: describe quality
characteristics and explain why they are important; analyse information such as
user stories, prototypes, processes and designs, with support; and explain what
might be a risk in achieving quality goals.

**What that looks like here.** You can name quality characteristics — reliability,
performance, accessibility, security — and say which matter for your service. You
read a story and notice the case nobody has thought about. You can say what you
are worried about before you start automating.

**The next step.** At test engineer you work with stakeholders on which
characteristics add value, decide what to test, advocate for the data and
environments you need, and analyse information to identify risks.

### Skill: Test and quality planning

**What this means.** The approach behind the testing — what will be covered, by
whom, when, and how anyone knows it worked.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: explain the value of
quality testing approaches, plans and strategies; explain how different delivery
methodologies affect them; follow them with support; and explain how to measure
their effectiveness and why that matters.

**What that looks like here.** You know what your team's approach says and where
it lives. You can say how testing in a two-week iteration differs from testing in
a long plan-driven project. You have a view on whether the approach is working.

**The next step.** At test engineer you create or adapt approaches based on risk,
follow the strategy and contribute to developing it, and contribute to continuous
improvement.

### Skill: Test engineering

**What this means.** The processes, environments and tools that make testing
possible — source control, pipelines, frameworks, and the automated tests
themselves. This is the skill that makes this role different from the test
analyst role.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: explain why testing
processes, environments and tools are important; follow test engineering
practices and standards, with support; and support the maintenance of automated
tests and tools required for testing.

**What that looks like here.** You keep to the team's conventions when you add a
test. You have fixed a test that was failing for its own reasons rather than the
service's. You can explain why an unreliable suite costs more than no suite.

**The next step.** At test engineer this becomes substantial: using test
engineering frameworks and tools, following practices such as source control and
continuous integration and deployment pipelines, integrating and executing tests
for early feedback, creating and maintaining automated tests, and writing and
reviewing coded solutions. This is where your role starts to diverge sharply from
the test analyst role.

## Legal compliance

Testing sits close to two legal duties in particular. Public sector services must
meet an accessibility standard in law, and testing is usually where that is
checked. And test data is real data more often than people admit — including in
the fixtures and snapshots that live in a test repository.

These questions ask about **training and support**, not about whether you get the
law right.
