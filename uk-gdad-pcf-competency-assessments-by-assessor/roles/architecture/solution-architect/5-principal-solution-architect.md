# Principal Solution Architect - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This assessment is for the person doing the assessing — a line manager, a
hiring panel member, or a reviewer — judging whether a candidate or employee
possesses the baseline abilities the principal solution architect role level
requires. A principal solution architect can be responsible for a large
programme or group of solution architecture projects, or a single, very
complex or critical business area, leading teams of more junior architects
and taking a leading role in the overall direction of business and digital
capabilities. This is the top of the solution architect ladder.

Work through the competency matrix below, one skill at a time, then complete
the overall determination. This assessment is one input among several into a
hiring, promotion or development decision, never the whole of it — see
`## Advisory`.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating on this
form must never be the sole basis for a hiring, promotion, pay, performance or
disciplinary decision. Before using it, consult your organisation's human
resources, people development, occupational psychology or legal compliance
colleagues.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` before scoring, so you are
assessing against this level's own accountabilities rather than lead
solution architect below it. At this level, evidence should show
organisation-wide or cross-organisation impact and a track record of
developing other architects, not a single strong example. Gather whatever
evidence exists first: a CV or portfolio, a structured interview, a work
sample or task, direct observation, or references, including from people this
person has led or coached.

**Scoring on evidence, not impression.** Every rating must trace to something
the person did, said, produced, or was observed doing. If you cannot point to
evidence for a rating, record it as unknown rather than guess — this matters
more, not less, for a candidate whose seniority makes it tempting to assume
competence.

**Consistency.** Score every candidate for this role level the same way, and
where possible have a second assessor score independently before you compare
notes — this matters most for a hiring or promotion decision, and materially
reduces the risk of an unlawful adverse impact from an unvalidated,
AI-drafted instrument used inconsistently.

**Fairness.** Never let a rating be influenced by anything other than
demonstrated, job-related evidence — see
[Fair and lawful use](../../../../uk-gdad-pcf-competency-assessments/spec/index.md#fair-and-lawful-use)
in the project specification.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

This is a **proficiency** scale, not a frequency scale: it rates how well the
person does the thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it.

A worked example, using **Strategy design**:

- A candidate rated **3 — Advanced** independently defines a strategy across
  the teams they lead, but has not yet connected strategies across the
  organisation or with wider government.
- A candidate rated **4 — Expert** defines and connects strategies or
  visions across the organisation or wider government, and enables their
  implementation by advocating for resources and removing blockers — the
  framework's own bar for this level.

## About this role level

**The role: solution architect.** A solution architect designs solutions for
problems that affect the organisation. In this role, a person will:

- ensure a problem and the desired outcomes are properly defined
- ensure the scope of a solution meets the organisation's requirements
- stay up to date on technology trends and approaches
- understand organisational objectives and external drivers, for example,
  legislation or financial constraints
- work with others to develop business and technical strategies
- work within business and technical constraints
- design and document solutions so they can be implemented by the
  organisation
- comply with standards and governance
- communicate and work effectively with stakeholders
- manage risks and decisions in a transparent way

**This level: principal solution architect.** A principal solution architect
can be responsible for a large programme or group of solution architecture
projects, or a single, very complex or critical business area.

**Where this level sits.** The solution architect role has five levels:

1. Associate solution architect
2. Solution architect
3. Senior solution architect
4. Lead solution architect
5. **Principal solution architect** — this level, the top of the ladder

Three skills carry framework wording identical to lead solution architect
below — Architecture communication, Commercial perspective, Problem
definition and shaping — and Technical design throughout the life cycle is
also unchanged. An assessor should use this level's own accountabilities
(large programme or critical business area, leading role in overall
direction) to judge whether the evidence shows genuinely wider reach. The
remaining four skills — Architect for the whole context, Community
collaboration, Making architectural decisions and Strategy design — carry
new wording that explicitly widens scope to the whole organisation or wider
government.

**Accountabilities at this level.** The framework expects a principal
solution architect to:

- lead teams of more junior solution architects
- lead multiple architecture projects or programmes
- build relationships with senior stakeholders across multiple business or
  technical areas in the organisation and its partners
- be proactive in identifying opportunities to improve the organisation and
  its partners
- work with technology partners to inform their roadmaps
- take a leading role in the overall direction of business and digital
  capabilities
- inspire other architects and help them understand how to meet
  organisational goals

**The skills this assessment covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Architect for the whole context
2. Architecture communication
3. Commercial perspective
4. Community collaboration
5. Making architectural decisions
6. Problem definition and shaping
7. Strategy design
8. Technical design throughout the life cycle

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Architect for the whole context

**What the framework expects at this level**

Assess how trends in society and industry practices might impact the
organisation. Work with people outside of your organisation to inform
policies, strategies and standards. Anticipate changes to policy and build
resilience through your architectural work. Coach others in identifying
important trends.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. The framework names coaching others in this skill
explicitly, and the scope — assessing societal and industry trends, working
with people outside the organisation — is organisation-wide by design.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe a societal or industry trend that could affect
  the organisation.
- 1 — Beginner: Aware of trends but has not connected them to organisational
  policy or resilience.
- 2 — Intermediate: Assesses trends for their own area with support.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently assesses trends and works with people outside
  the organisation to inform policy or strategy, but has not yet coached
  another architect in doing the same.
- 4 — Expert: Anticipates policy changes, builds resilience into
  architectural work as a result, and coaches other architects in
  identifying important trends.

**Evidence to gather**

- A portfolio item: a policy, strategy, or standards document the candidate
  informed through external engagement.
- A structured interview question: "Describe anticipating a policy change
  before it happened, and building resilience into your architectural work
  as a result."
- A reference question: "Has this person coached another architect in
  spotting an important trend?"

### Skill: Architecture communication

**What the framework expects at this level**

Communicate with technical and non-technical stakeholders at all levels, and
across organisations, using architecture communication techniques. Mediate
between people in difficult architectural discussions. Gain support from
business and technical stakeholders for architectural topics with high
levels of risk, impact and complexity. Coach and support others in
architecture communication.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. This wording is identical to lead solution architect's
and already sits at the ceiling there — this level is expected to sustain
that standard across a larger programme or organisational remit, not repeat
it at a smaller scale.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has not mediated a difficult architectural discussion.
- 1 — Beginner: Communicates well one-to-one, without mediating disagreement.
- 2 — Intermediate: Mediates straightforward disagreements with support.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently mediates difficult discussions and gains
  support for high-risk topics, without yet coaching others.
- 4 — Expert: Coaches and supports other architects in architecture
  communication across a large programme or the organisation, with visible
  effect on someone else's practice.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Describe mediating a difficult
  architectural discussion across organisations, not just within your own."
- A reference question, ideally from someone coached: "Has this person's
  coaching changed how you communicate architecture?"

- [ ] 0 — None

### Skill: Commercial perspective

**What the framework expects at this level**

Identify appropriate contractual frameworks and approaches. Identify,
evaluate and select appropriate suppliers.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. This wording is identical to lead solution
architect's, and the framework does not describe coaching others in
commercial judgement at either level — record the repeat rather than
inflating the number on seniority alone.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has not identified a contractual framework or evaluated a
  supplier.
- 1 — Beginner: Contributes to supplier evaluation with support.
- 2 — Intermediate: Identifies contractual frameworks for straightforward
  cases with occasional support.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently identifies, evaluates and selects appropriate
  suppliers and contractual frameworks, at the scale of a large programme or
  critical area.
- 4 — Expert: Not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Describe a supplier or contractual
  decision you made for a large programme. What would you do differently
  now?"
- A document review: a supplier evaluation or contractual approach the
  candidate led at programme scale.

### Skill: Community collaboration

**What the framework expects at this level**

Solve and unblock issues between teams or departments at the highest level.
Coach the organisation on team dynamics and conflict resolution, while also
building and growing the community.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "Coach the organisation" is explicit and unambiguous —
this is teaching and guiding others at the widest possible scope this
framework describes.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has not resolved an issue between teams or departments.
- 1 — Beginner: Contributes to resolving issues within their own team.
- 2 — Intermediate: Resolves issues between two teams with support.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently solves and unblocks issues between teams or
  departments, without yet coaching the wider organisation.
- 4 — Expert: Coaches the organisation on team dynamics and conflict
  resolution, and has grown a community of practice beyond their own
  reporting line.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Describe unblocking a conflict between
  two departments at the highest level. What made it hard, and how did you
  resolve it?"
- A reference question: "Has this person built or grown a community of
  practice across the organisation?"

### Skill: Making architectural decisions

**What the framework expects at this level**

Make and guide architectural design decisions characterised by high levels
of risk and complexity. Identify and address architectural risks across the
organisation or wider government. Lead and evolve architectural governance
and assurance. Represent architectural governance as part of wider
governance, for example, legal or commercial.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "Lead and evolve architectural governance" is the
framework's own marker for teaching or guiding others through the skill,
combined with organisation- or government-wide risk scope.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has not made an architectural decision at organisation-wide
  scope.
- 1 — Beginner: Contributes to high-risk decisions with regular support.
- 2 — Intermediate: Makes decisions of high risk with occasional support.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently makes and guides decisions of high risk and
  complexity across the organisation, without yet evolving governance
  itself.
- 4 — Expert: Leads and evolves architectural governance and assurance, and
  represents it within wider governance such as legal or commercial
  functions.

**Evidence to gather**

- A document review: a governance framework or standard the candidate led
  the evolution of.
- A structured interview question: "Describe representing architectural
  governance within a wider governance process — legal, commercial, or
  similar. What changed as a result?"

### Skill: Problem definition and shaping

**What the framework expects at this level**

Lead the framing of a problem characterised by high complexity, complication,
or risk so that a solution can be created. Coach others in defining problems
and describing appropriate options for solutions. Help others challenge
requirements and assumptions, and identify opportunities when defining
problems and solution options.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. This wording is identical to lead solution architect's
and already names coaching others explicitly — this level sustains that
standard at programme or organisational scale.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has not led the framing of a high-complexity problem.
- 1 — Beginner: Frames problems for their own work, without leading others.
- 2 — Intermediate: Leads framing for medium-complexity problems with
  support.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently leads framing of high-complexity, high-risk
  problems, without yet coaching another architect through it.
- 4 — Expert: Coaches other architects in defining problems and options
  across a large programme or the organisation, and has helped someone
  else challenge a requirement they would otherwise have accepted.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Describe coaching another architect
  through framing a high-complexity, high-risk problem across a large
  programme."
- A portfolio item: a problem definition for a critical, organisation-scale
  piece of work the candidate led.

### Skill: Strategy design

**What the framework expects at this level**

Define and connect strategies or visions across the organisation or wider
government. Enable the implementation of strategies or visions across the
organisation or wider government, for example, by advocating for resources
and removing blockers.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "Across the organisation or wider government" is
explicitly the widest scope the framework describes for this skill, a step
up from lead solution architect's "across teams," and enabling
implementation by removing blockers is a leadership act, not an individual
contribution.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has not defined a strategy beyond their own group of projects.
- 1 — Beginner: Contributes to organisation-level strategy with regular
  support.
- 2 — Intermediate: Defines strategy elements at organisation scale with
  occasional support.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently defines strategy across the teams they lead,
  without yet connecting it across the wider organisation or government.
- 4 — Expert: Defines and connects strategies or visions across the
  organisation or wider government, and enables their implementation by
  advocating for resources and removing blockers.

**Evidence to gather**

- A portfolio item: a strategy or vision document the candidate defined at
  organisation or cross-government scale, with evidence of implementation.
- A structured interview question: "Describe removing a blocker to
  implementing a strategy that was outside your direct control to fix."

### Skill: Technical design throughout the life cycle

**What the framework expects at this level**

Create technical designs characterised by high risk, impact, and complexity.
Lead and guide others in creating technical designs that achieve
organisational objectives. Use feedback to optimise and refine standards for
technical designs throughout the life cycle.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. This wording is identical to lead solution architect's
and already names leading and guiding others explicitly — this level
sustains that standard at the scale of a large programme or critical area.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has not created a technical design of high risk and complexity.
- 1 — Beginner: Contributes to high-risk designs with regular support.
- 2 — Intermediate: Creates designs of medium risk and complexity with
  occasional support.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently creates technical designs of high risk, impact
  and complexity, without yet leading another architect through the process.
- 4 — Expert: Leads and guides other architects in creating technical
  designs that achieve organisational objectives, and refines the standards
  designs are built against across a large programme or the organisation.

**Evidence to gather**

- A portfolio item: a technical design of high risk and complexity the
  candidate led at programme or organisation scale, with evidence others
  followed their guidance.
- A structured interview question: "Describe refining a technical design

at or above its baseline target. At this level, most skills carry a baseline
of Expert by the framework's own wording, so a shortfall anywhere is a
significant finding, not a rounding error — weigh how central the skill is
to this level's accountabilities, consider whether the shortfall is
coachable quickly after appointment, and record the reasoning in the
evidence log rather than only the number. Commercial perspective carries a
lower baseline because neither this level nor lead solution architect's
framework wording describes coaching others in it; do not treat a 3 there as
a shortfall on its own.
