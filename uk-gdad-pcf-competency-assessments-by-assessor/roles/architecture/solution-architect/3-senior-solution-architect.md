# Senior Solution Architect - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This assessment is for the person doing the assessing — a line manager, a
hiring panel member, or a reviewer — judging whether a candidate or employee
possesses the baseline abilities the senior solution architect role level
requires. A senior solution architect is responsible for a single, more
complex solution, usually where risk is moderate, and may lead and coach
other solution architects.

Work through the competency matrix below, one skill at a time, then complete
the overall determination. This assessment is one input among several into a
hiring, promotion or development decision, never the whole of it — see
`## Advisory`.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating on this
form must never be the sole basis for a hiring, promotion, pay, performance or
disciplinary decision. Before using it, consult your organisation's human
resources, people development, occupational psychology or legal compliance
colleagues.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` before scoring, so you are
assessing against this level's own accountabilities — a more complex,
moderate-risk solution, with coaching responsibility — rather than solution
architect below it or lead solution architect above it. Gather whatever
evidence exists first: a CV or portfolio, a structured interview, a work
sample or task, direct observation, or references, including a reference from
someone this person has coached, where one exists.

**Scoring on evidence, not impression.** Every rating must trace to something
the person did, said, produced, or was observed doing. If you cannot point to
evidence for a rating, record it as unknown rather than guess.

**Consistency.** Score every candidate for this role level the same way, and
where possible have a second assessor score independently before you compare
notes — this matters most for a hiring or promotion decision, and materially
reduces the risk of an unlawful adverse impact from an unvalidated,
AI-drafted instrument used inconsistently.

**Fairness.** Never let a rating be influenced by anything other than
demonstrated, job-related evidence — see
[Fair and lawful use](../../../../uk-gdad-pcf-competency-assessments/spec/index.md#fair-and-lawful-use)
in the project specification.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

This is a **proficiency** scale, not a frequency scale: it rates how well the
person does the thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it.

A worked example, using **Community collaboration**:

- A candidate rated **2 — Intermediate** contributes to a positive team
  environment for their own solution, with support for dealing with harder
  issues.
- A candidate rated **3 — Advanced** independently adapts feedback so it
  lasts, uses Agile health checks to surface team-dynamic issues, and
  rectifies problems in how the team works together, unsupported.
- A candidate rated **4 — Expert** coaches other architects on team dynamics
  across an organisation — the bar for principal solution architect, well
  above this level.

## About this role level

**The role: solution architect.** A solution architect designs solutions for
problems that affect the organisation. In this role, a person will:

- ensure a problem and the desired outcomes are properly defined
- ensure the scope of a solution meets the organisation's requirements
- stay up to date on technology trends and approaches
- understand organisational objectives and external drivers, for example,
  legislation or financial constraints
- work with others to develop business and technical strategies
- work within business and technical constraints
- design and document solutions so they can be implemented by the
  organisation
- comply with standards and governance
- communicate and work effectively with stakeholders
- manage risks and decisions in a transparent way

**This level: senior solution architect.** A senior solution architect is
responsible for a single, more complex solution. They usually work on
solutions where the risk is moderate, and they may lead and coach other
solution architects.

**Where this level sits.** The solution architect role has five levels:

1. Associate solution architect
2. Solution architect
3. **Senior solution architect** — this level
4. Lead solution architect
5. Principal solution architect

Four skills carry framework wording identical to solution architect below —
Architect for the whole context, Architecture communication, Commercial
perspective, and Problem definition and shaping. An assessor should use this
level's own accountabilities (a more complex, moderate-risk solution, with
coaching responsibility) to judge whether the evidence reflects senior scope
rather than crediting the shared wording alone. Community collaboration,
Making architectural decisions, and Technical design throughout the life
cycle carry new wording at this level that raises the bar explicitly.

**Accountabilities at this level.** The framework expects a senior solution
architect to:

- build relationships with senior stakeholders across different business or
  technical areas in the organisation
- be proactive in identifying opportunities to improve the organisation
- support multiple architecture projects
- find and use emerging technologies and approaches
- support others to follow best practice for solution architecture

**The skills this assessment covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Architect for the whole context
2. Architecture communication
3. Commercial perspective
4. Community collaboration
5. Making architectural decisions
6. Problem definition and shaping
7. Strategy design
8. Technical design throughout the life cycle

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Architect for the whole context

**What the framework expects at this level**

Align your work with the work being done by other architects and technical
professionals. Track emerging issues, strategies, roadmaps, patterns and
technologies over time to assess opportunities and risks to your work.
Identify how other teams contribute to delivering outcomes through change.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. The framework's text repeats solution architect's
wording, but this level's accountability to "support multiple architecture
projects" and "find and use emerging technologies" describes independent,
Advanced practice across more than one piece of work.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe how their solution relates to wider
  organisational trends or other teams' work.
- 1 — Beginner: Aligns to strategies and roadmaps pointed out to them.
- 2 — Intermediate: Actively tracks emerging issues for their own solution
  with support judging opportunity or risk.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently tracks and assesses emerging issues across
  more than one solution, and proactively identifies opportunities or risks
  before being asked.
- 4 — Expert: Coaches others in identifying important trends and anticipates
  organisation-wide shifts — the bar for principal solution architect, well
  above this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Describe an opportunity or risk you
  spotted from a trend before anyone asked you to look for it. What did you
  do?"
- A reference question: "Has this person's awareness of technology trends
  changed a decision on more than one piece of work?"

### Skill: Architecture communication

**What the framework expects at this level**

Lead the communication of complicated, complex or risky architecture topics
with technical and non-technical stakeholders. Communicate with senior
stakeholders across your organisation. Adapt your message and communication
techniques to your audience. Advocate on behalf of a team to other
stakeholders. Manage stakeholder expectations effectively.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. This wording is shared with solution architect below,
but at this level it is read against "build relationships with senior
stakeholders" and a more complex solution — independent, unsupported
communication with senior stakeholders is the realistic bar here.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has not led communication of a complex or risky topic with a
  senior stakeholder.
- 1 — Beginner: Communicates with support for senior or contested audiences.
- 2 — Intermediate: Leads communication for their solution with occasional
  support for the harder conversations.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently manages senior stakeholder expectations for
  complicated or risky topics, adapting technique to audience, unsupported.
- 4 — Expert: Mediates the most difficult architectural discussions across
  organisations and coaches others — the bar for lead solution architect and
  above.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Describe managing a senior stakeholder's
  expectations on a complex or risky topic where they initially disagreed
  with you."
- A reference question, ideally from a senior stakeholder: "Did this person
  advocate effectively for their solution when it mattered?"

### Skill: Commercial perspective

**What the framework expects at this level**

Understand commercial processes and the appropriate internal contacts within
a government department. Understand different sourcing strategies and when
to apply them.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. This wording is identical to solution architect
below, and nothing in this level's own accountabilities points to a deeper
commercial bar — record the repeat rather than inflating the number on
seniority alone.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot name a sourcing strategy or internal commercial contact
  relevant to their work.
- 1 — Beginner: Aware sourcing strategies exist, needs help judging which
  applies.
- 2 — Intermediate: Knows the appropriate internal commercial contacts and
  can judge which sourcing strategy applies, with support for less common
  cases.
- 3 — Advanced: Identifies appropriate contractual frameworks and evaluates
  suppliers independently — the bar for lead solution architect, above this
  level.
- 4 — Expert: Not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Describe choosing between sourcing
  strategies for a real piece of work. What did you choose, and why?"

### Skill: Community collaboration

**What the framework expects at this level**

Work collaboratively in a group, actively networking with others. Adapt
feedback to ensure it's effective and lasting. Use your topic to identify
problems or issues in the team dynamic and rectify them. Identify issues
through Agile 'health checks' with the team, and help to stimulate the right
responses.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. This is materially new text from solution architect
below — actively rectifying team-dynamic issues and running Agile health
checks is independent, unsupported practice.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has not used a structured method, such as an Agile health check,
  to surface a team-dynamic issue.
- 1 — Beginner: Contributes to a team environment but has not rectified a
  dynamic issue unsupported.
- 2 — Intermediate: Adapts feedback for one-off effect, with support making
  it stick over time.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently identifies and rectifies team-dynamic issues,
  uses Agile health checks to surface problems, and adapts feedback so it
  lasts.
- 4 — Expert: Coaches the organisation on team dynamics and conflict
  resolution — the bar for principal solution architect, well above this
  level.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Describe a team-dynamic issue you
  identified and rectified. How did you know it had actually improved,
  rather than just quietened down?"
- A reference question: "Has this person run or contributed meaningfully to
  a team health check, and did anything change as a result?"

### Skill: Making architectural decisions

**What the framework expects at this level**

Make and guide architectural design decisions characterised by medium risk
and complexity. Identify and address architectural risks that affect
multiple teams or domains. Use architectural governance and assurance to make
design decisions and manage technical risks at the appropriate level.
Contribute to the development of architectural governance and assurance.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. "Make and guide" decisions unsupported, at medium
risk, addressing risks across multiple teams, is a clear, independent step
up from solution architect's "with support" ceiling.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has not made an architectural decision affecting more than one
  team.
- 1 — Beginner: Contributes to decisions with regular support.
- 2 — Intermediate: Makes decisions of managed risk with occasional support,
  for a single team or domain.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently makes and guides decisions of medium risk and
  complexity, addresses risks affecting multiple teams or domains, and
  contributes to how architectural governance itself works.
- 4 — Expert: Leads and evolves architectural governance across the
  organisation — the bar for lead solution architect and above.

**Evidence to gather**

- A document review: a design decision record the candidate made that
  addressed a risk affecting more than one team.
- A structured interview question: "Describe contributing to how
  architectural governance or assurance works, not just working within it."

### Skill: Problem definition and shaping

**What the framework expects at this level**

Frame a problem characterised by medium complexity, complication, or risk so
that a solution can be created. Produce architectural representations that
enable different teams to have a shared understanding of problems throughout
the life cycle. Describe options for solving problems so that appropriate
delivery methods can be decided.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. This wording is identical to solution architect
below, but at senior level it applies to a more complex solution
unsupported, which is the realistic reading given this level's own
accountabilities.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has not independently framed a medium-complexity problem.
- 1 — Beginner: Frames simple problems with support.
- 2 — Intermediate: Frames medium-complexity problems with occasional
  support.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently frames medium-complexity problems, produces
  representations multiple teams can use, and describes options that lead to
  a delivery decision.
- 4 — Expert: Leads framing of high-complexity, high-risk problems and
  coaches others in it — the bar for lead solution architect and above.

**Evidence to gather**

- A portfolio item: a problem definition or option-appraisal document for a
  moderately complex, real solution.
- A structured interview question: "Describe framing a problem where the
  desired outcome was not initially clear. How did you get to clarity?"

### Skill: Strategy design

**What the framework expects at this level**

Support the development of a strategy or vision that aligns with
organisational objectives. Challenge requirements and assumptions, and
identify opportunities to develop strategy. Support the implementation of a
strategy or vision, for example, by using a roadmap or plan. Use
architectural principles, patterns, and constraints when appropriate.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Text unchanged from solution architect, but this
level's "support multiple architecture projects" accountability means the
supporting role here spans more than one strategy, with less need for
oversight — independent, Advanced support.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has not contributed to a strategy or vision.
- 1 — Beginner: Contributes when directed.
- 2 — Intermediate: Supports strategy development for one solution, with
  occasional support.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently supports strategy or vision development and
  implementation across more than one piece of work, regularly challenges
  requirements and assumptions.
- 4 — Expert: Defines strategies or visions across teams — the bar for lead
  solution architect and above.

**Evidence to gather**

- A document review: a strategy, vision, or roadmap document the candidate
  contributed to across more than one project.
- A structured interview question: "Describe a requirement or assumption you
  challenged while supporting a strategy. What changed as a result?"

### Skill: Technical design throughout the life cycle

**What the framework expects at this level**

Create technical designs characterised by medium risk, impact, and
complexity. Maintain appropriate quality and architectural coherence of a
technical design in response to change. Use feedback to optimise and refine
technical designs throughout the life cycle.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. The framework raises this skill's wording explicitly
at this level — from "managed" to "medium" risk, and from "adapt if needed"
to actively maintaining coherence and using feedback to optimise — an
independent, Advanced bar.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has not maintained a technical design's coherence through
  significant change.
- 1 — Beginner: Adapts a design when told to, with support.
- 2 — Intermediate: Creates designs of managed risk and adapts them with
  occasional support.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently creates designs of medium risk and complexity,
  maintains their coherence as circumstances change, and uses feedback to
  refine them over the life cycle.
- 4 — Expert: Creates designs of high risk and impact and leads others in
  doing the same — the bar for lead solution architect and above.

**Evidence to gather**

- A portfolio item: a technical design the candidate maintained through a
  significant change, with before-and-after evidence.
- A structured interview question: "Describe using feedback to refine a
  technical design after it was in delivery. What changed?"
