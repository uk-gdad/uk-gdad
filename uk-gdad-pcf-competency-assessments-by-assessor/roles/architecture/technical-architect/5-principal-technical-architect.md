# Principal Technical Architect - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This assessment is for the person doing the assessing — a line manager, a
hiring panel member, or a reviewer — judging whether a candidate or employee
possesses the baseline abilities the principal technical architect role
level requires. A principal technical architect leads at the highest level
and is responsible for making sure strategy is agreed and followed, networks
with senior stakeholders across organisations, and is responsible for
governance, solving complex and high-risk issues, or delivering architecture
design. This is the top of the technical architect ladder.

Work through the competency matrix below, one skill at a time, then complete
the overall determination. This assessment is one input among several into a
hiring, promotion or development decision, never the whole of it — see
`## Advisory`.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating on this
form must never be the sole basis for a hiring, promotion, pay, performance or
disciplinary decision. Before using it, consult your organisation's human
resources, people development, occupational psychology or legal compliance
colleagues.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` before scoring, so you are
assessing against this level's own accountabilities rather than lead
technical architect below it. At this level, evidence should show
organisation-wide or cross-organisation impact and a track record of
developing other architects, not a single strong example. Gather whatever
evidence exists first: a CV or portfolio, a structured interview, a work
sample or task, direct observation, or references, including from people
this person has led or coached.

**Scoring on evidence, not impression.** Every rating must trace to something
the person did, said, produced, or was observed doing. If you cannot point to
evidence for a rating, record it as unknown rather than guess — this matters
more, not less, for a candidate whose seniority makes it tempting to assume
competence.

**Consistency.** Score every candidate for this role level the same way, and
where possible have a second assessor score independently before you compare
notes — this matters most for a hiring or promotion decision, and materially
reduces the risk of an unlawful adverse impact from an unvalidated,
AI-drafted instrument used inconsistently.

**Fairness.** Never let a rating be influenced by anything other than
demonstrated, job-related evidence — see
[Fair and lawful use](../../../../uk-gdad-pcf-competency-assessments/spec/index.md#fair-and-lawful-use)
in the project specification.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

This is a **proficiency** scale, not a frequency scale: it rates how well the
person does the thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it.

A worked example, using **Community collaboration**:

- A candidate rated **3 — Advanced** independently solves and unblocks
  issues between teams or departments, but has not yet coached the wider
  organisation on team dynamics.
- A candidate rated **4 — Expert** solves and unblocks issues between teams
  or departments at the highest level, and coaches the organisation on team
  dynamics and conflict resolution while building and growing the
  community — the framework's own bar for this level.

## About this role level

**The role: technical architect.** A technical architect provides technical
leadership and architectural design.

**This level: principal technical architect.** A principal technical
architect leads at the highest level and is responsible for making sure the
strategy is agreed and followed.

**Where this level sits.** The technical architect role has five levels:

1. Associate technical architect
2. Technical architect
3. Senior technical architect
4. Lead technical architect
5. **Principal technical architect** — this level, the top of the ladder

Architecture communication and Technical design throughout the life cycle
carry framework wording identical to lead technical architect below, and
both already name coaching or leading others there; this level sustains that
standard at the widest scope the framework describes. The remaining four
skills — Architect for the whole context, Community collaboration, Making
architectural decisions, and Strategy design — carry new wording that
explicitly widens scope to the whole organisation or wider government and
names coaching or leading others directly.

**Accountabilities at this level.** The framework expects a principal
technical architect to:

- network and communicate with senior stakeholders across organisations
- proactively seek opportunities for digital transformation
- support multiple teams, finding and using best practice and emerging
  technologies
- inspire other architects and help them understand how to deliver the
  goals of the organisation
- be responsible for governance, solving complex and high risk issues or
  delivering architecture design

**The skills this assessment covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Architect for the whole context
2. Architecture communication
3. Community collaboration
4. Making architectural decisions
5. Strategy design
6. Technical design throughout the life cycle

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Architect for the whole context

**What the framework expects at this level**

Work to support wider organisational objectives beyond your immediate goals.
Track emerging internal and external issues over time that could affect the
work of teams across the organisation. Take action to solve or mitigate
problems by influencing colleagues across the organisation.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. This level's accountability to "support multiple
teams" and "inspire other architects" describes coaching and guiding others
through this skill at the widest scope the framework gives this role.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe how their work connects to the organisation's
  wider objectives.
- 1 — Beginner: Aware of wider organisational objectives, needs support
  acting on them.
- 2 — Intermediate: Tracks internal and external issues for their own area,
  with support influencing colleagues elsewhere.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently tracks issues affecting teams across the
  organisation and takes action to mitigate problems by influencing
  colleagues, without yet coaching others in this.
- 4 — Expert: Anticipates policy changes, builds resilience into
  architectural work across multiple teams, and coaches other architects
  in identifying important trends.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Describe influencing colleagues across
  more than one team or department to solve or mitigate a problem you saw
  coming."
- A reference question: "Has this person coached another architect in
  spotting an important trend, or only spotted it themselves?"

- [ ] 1 — Beginner
- [ ] 2 — Intermediate
- [ ] 3 — Advanced

### Skill: Architecture communication

**What the framework expects at this level**

Communicate with technical and non-technical stakeholders at all levels, and
across organisations, using architecture communication techniques. Mediate
between people in difficult architectural discussions. Gain support from
business and technical stakeholders for architectural topics with high
levels of risk, impact and complexity. Coach and support others in
architecture communication.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. This wording is identical to lead technical
architect's and already names coaching others explicitly — this level
sustains that standard while networking with senior stakeholders across
organisations.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has not mediated a difficult architectural discussion.
- 1 — Beginner: Communicates well one-to-one, without mediating disagreement.
- 2 — Intermediate: Mediates straightforward disagreements with support.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently mediates difficult discussions and gains
  support for high-risk topics, without yet coaching others.
- 4 — Expert: Coaches and supports other architects in architecture
  communication across the organisation, with visible effect on someone
  else's practice.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Describe mediating a difficult
  architectural discussion across organisations, not just within your own."
- A reference question, ideally from someone coached: "Has this person's
  coaching changed how you communicate architecture?"

- [ ] 3 — Advanced

### Skill: Community collaboration

**What the framework expects at this level**

Solve and unblock issues between teams or departments at the highest level.
Coach the organisation on team dynamics and conflict resolution, while also
building and growing the community.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "Coach the organisation" is explicit and unambiguous —
this is teaching and guiding others at the widest possible scope this
framework describes.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has not resolved an issue between teams or departments.
- 1 — Beginner: Contributes to resolving issues within their own team.
- 2 — Intermediate: Resolves issues between two teams with support.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently solves and unblocks issues between teams or
  departments, without yet coaching the wider organisation.
- 4 — Expert: Coaches the organisation on team dynamics and conflict
  resolution, and has grown a community of practice beyond their own
  reporting line.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Describe unblocking a conflict between
  two departments at the highest level. What made it hard, and how did you
  resolve it?"
- A reference question: "Has this person built or grown a community of
  practice across the organisation?"

### Skill: Making architectural decisions

**What the framework expects at this level**

Make and guide architectural design decisions characterised by high levels
of risk and complexity. Identify and address architectural risks across the
organisation or wider government. Lead and evolve architectural governance
and assurance. Represent architectural governance as part of wider
governance, for example, legal or commercial.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "Lead and evolve architectural governance" is the
framework's own marker for teaching or guiding others through the skill,
combined with organisation- or government-wide risk scope.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has not made an architectural decision at organisation-wide
  scope.
- 1 — Beginner: Contributes to high-risk decisions with regular support.
- 2 — Intermediate: Makes decisions of high risk with occasional support.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently makes and guides decisions of high risk and
  complexity across the organisation, without yet evolving governance
  itself.
- 4 — Expert: Leads and evolves architectural governance and assurance, and
  represents it within wider governance such as legal or commercial
  functions.

**Evidence to gather**

- A document review: a governance framework or standard the candidate led
  the evolution of.
- A structured interview question: "Describe representing architectural
  governance within a wider governance process — legal, commercial, or
  similar. What changed as a result?"

### Skill: Strategy design

**What the framework expects at this level**

Define and connect strategies or visions across the organisation or wider
government. Enable the implementation of strategies or visions across the
organisation or wider government, for example, by advocating for resources
and removing blockers.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "Across the organisation or wider government" is
explicitly the widest scope the framework describes for this skill, a step
up from lead technical architect's "across teams," and enabling
implementation by removing blockers is a leadership act, not an individual
contribution.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has not defined a strategy beyond their own projects or teams.
- 1 — Beginner: Contributes to organisation-level strategy with regular
  support.
- 2 — Intermediate: Defines strategy elements at organisation scale with
  occasional support.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently defines strategy across the teams they lead,
  without yet connecting it across the wider organisation or government.
- 4 — Expert: Defines and connects strategies or visions across the
  organisation or wider government, and enables their implementation by
  advocating for resources and removing blockers.

**Evidence to gather**

- A portfolio item: a strategy or vision document the candidate defined at
  organisation or cross-government scale, with evidence of implementation.
- A structured interview question: "Describe removing a blocker to
  implementing a strategy that was outside your direct control to fix."

### Skill: Technical design throughout the life cycle

**What the framework expects at this level**

Create technical designs characterised by high risk, impact, and complexity.
Lead and guide others in creating technical designs that achieve
organisational objectives. Use feedback to optimise and refine standards for
technical designs throughout the life cycle.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. This wording is identical to lead technical
architect's and already names leading and guiding others explicitly — this
level sustains that standard while being responsible for governance and
complex, high-risk architecture design across the organisation.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has not created a technical design of high risk and complexity.
- 1 — Beginner: Contributes to high-risk designs with regular support.
- 2 — Intermediate: Creates designs of medium risk and complexity with
  occasional support.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently creates technical designs of high risk, impact
  and complexity, without yet leading another architect through the process.
- 4 — Expert: Leads and guides other architects in creating technical
  designs that achieve organisational objectives, and refines the standards
  designs are built against across the organisation.

**Evidence to gather**

- A portfolio item: a technical design of high risk and complexity the
  candidate led at organisation scale, with evidence others followed their
  guidance.
- A structured interview question: "Describe refining a technical design
  standard used across more than one team, based on feedback."

at or above its baseline target. Every skill carries a baseline of Expert at
this level, because the framework's own wording names organisation-wide or
government-wide scope and coaching or leading others for all six — this is
the top of the ladder, and there is no level above it to defer development
to. A shortfall anywhere is a significant finding, not a rounding error:
weigh how central the skill is to this level's accountabilities, consider
whether the shortfall is coachable quickly after appointment, and record the
reasoning in the evidence log rather than only the number.
