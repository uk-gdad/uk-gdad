# Lead Analytics Engineer - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This assessment is for an assessor — a line manager, a hiring panel member, or
a reviewer — judging whether a candidate or an employee meets the baseline
abilities required for the Data role of Analytics engineer, at the Lead
analytics engineer role level. It measures demonstrated skill against the UK
GDAD Profession Capability Framework's own description of this role level, for
a hiring decision, a promotion board, onboarding, or a capability check.

A lead analytics engineer leads the design and deployment of data models
across a team, sets standards for communication, models and documentation, and
promotes the discipline of analytics engineering more widely. This assessment
looks for team leadership, standard-setting, and judgement about timing and
trade-offs, on top of the strong individual delivery expected below.

This assessment is one input among several into a decision, never the whole of
it. Read `## Advisory` before using it.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating on this
form must never be the sole basis for a hiring, promotion, pay, performance or
disciplinary decision. Before using it, consult your organisation's human
resources, people development, occupational psychology or legal compliance
colleagues.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, then gather whatever
evidence exists: a CV or portfolio, a structured interview, a work sample or
task (for example, asking the candidate to critique a data model produced by a
hypothetical team member, or to draft a short standard for the team to
follow), direct observation, or references — particularly references that can
speak to how the candidate has led or set standards for a team.

**Score on evidence, not impression.** Every rating must trace to something
the person did, said, produced, or was observed doing. If you cannot point to
evidence for a rating, record it as unknown rather than guess.

**Consistency.** Score every candidate for this role level the same way. For a
hiring or promotion decision, have more than one assessor score independently
before comparing notes.

**Fairness.** Every rating and every piece of evidence you gather must be
about demonstrated, job-related behaviour — see
[Fair and lawful use](#fair-and-lawful-use) in the project specification.
Never let a rating be coloured by anything other than what the person actually
did, said, or produced.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix below is rated on the same five-point
scale. This is a **proficiency** scale — it rates how well the person does the
thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it or how they feel
about it.

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

**Worked example — Data innovation.** The framework expects a lead analytics
engineer to "identify areas of innovation in data tools and techniques, and
recognise appropriate timing for adoption". A candidate who can name emerging
tools but not judge when the team should actually adopt them is showing
**2 — Intermediate**, below this level's baseline. A candidate who has
identified a genuine opportunity for the team — for example, recommending a
new data transformation tool and correctly judging that now, rather than mid
way through a major delivery, is the right time to introduce it — is showing
**3 — Advanced**, the baseline this level expects. A candidate who does this
routinely across the whole organisation, not just their own team, is showing
evidence towards **4 — Expert**.

## About this role level

The Data profession's Analytics engineer role transforms data relevant to the
organisation into structures that enable analysis and decision-making. Across
the role, an analytics engineer works with subject matter experts to
understand organisational processes and translate these into data structures
optimised for analysis; works with data users to design and build data models
they can use for effective analysis and decision-making, using modelling
techniques such as Kimball or Inmon; supports data quality improvement;
develops standards for data transformation; creates and maintains data
documentation; refines requirements in response to feedback from users and
changes in the organisation; and provides ongoing support to users.

The role has five levels, in order: Trainee analytics engineer, Analytics
engineer, Senior analytics engineer, Lead analytics engineer, and Head of
analytics engineering. This assessment is for the fourth of them.

A **lead analytics engineer** leads the design and deployment of data models
for analysis. At this role level, they will lead and support a team of
analytics engineers to design, build and maintain data models, work with
stakeholders and teams across the organisation to understand relationships
between data and organisational processes and use this to define data
requirements, promote and build awareness and support understanding of
analytics engineering, review the work of other analytics engineers, create
standards for communication, data models and documentation, and define and
improve ways of working in the team.

The framework names eight skills for this role level: Communicating between
the technical and non-technical, Data analysis and synthesis, Data
innovation, Data modelling, cleansing and enrichment, Metadata management,
Problem management, Programming and build (data and analytics engineering),
and Testing.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead analytics engineer is expected to listen to and interpret the needs of
technical and non-technical stakeholders and manage their expectations, manage
active and reactive communication, and support or host difficult discussions
within the team or with diverse senior stakeholders.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Managing stakeholder expectations and hosting
discussions with "diverse senior stakeholders" describes confident,
independent practice at a wider scope than the senior level's team-level
discussions, but the framework reserves speaking for the whole community to
large external audiences for the level above this one.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot interpret conflicting stakeholder needs or manage
  expectations proactively.
- 1 — Beginner: Communicates stakeholder needs upward but does not yet manage
  expectations independently.
- 2 — Intermediate: Manages routine stakeholder expectations, but escalates
  discussions involving senior or diverse stakeholders.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently interprets and manages the needs of technical
  and non-technical stakeholders, including senior ones, and hosts difficult
  discussions without escalating.
- 4 — Expert: Mediates across the whole organisation and represents the
  community to large audiences, inside and outside it.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a time they managed the expectations of a
  senior stakeholder whose request was not realistic, and how they handled it.
- Ask about a discussion they hosted involving people at very different levels
  of seniority or technical understanding.
- Ask a referee how the candidate is regarded by senior stakeholders outside
  their own team.

### Skill: Data analysis and synthesis

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead analytics engineer is expected to understand and help teams apply a
range of techniques for data profiling, carry out source system analysis from
a complex single source, and bring multiple data sources together in a
conformed model for analysis.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Bringing multiple sources together into a conformed
model, and teaching a team a range of profiling techniques, is more demanding
than the single-source profiling expected below, and describes someone who is
both a strong practitioner and starting to build others' capability.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot synthesise more than one data source into a usable model.
- 1 — Beginner: Profiles a single source competently but struggles to combine
  more than one.
- 2 — Intermediate: Combines two related sources with support, but not yet a
  genuinely complex single source or several disparate ones.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently analyses a complex single source and brings
  multiple data sources together in a conformed model, and helps team members
  build their own profiling technique.
- 4 — Expert: Sets the organisation's approach to conforming disparate data
  for analysis.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a time they brought several different data
  sources together into one model for analysis, and what made it hard.
- Ask about how they have taught or supported a team member in developing
  their profiling skills.

**Rate this skill**

- [ ] 0 — None
- [ ] 1 — Beginner
- [ ] 2 — Intermediate
- [ ] 3 — Advanced
- [ ] 4 — Expert

### Skill: Data innovation

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead analytics engineer is expected to identify areas of innovation in data
tools and techniques, and recognise appropriate timing for adoption — judgement
about not just what is new, but when to bring it in.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Recognising the right timing for adoption is a
judgement call that goes beyond understanding a trend's impact, which is what
the level below is baselined against, so this level's baseline moves up a
point.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot identify a genuine opportunity for innovation relevant to
  the team.
- 1 — Beginner: Identifies opportunities for innovation but cannot judge
  timing.
- 2 — Intermediate: Identifies opportunities and has an instinct for timing,
  with some support in deciding.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently identifies genuine opportunities for
  innovation and judges the right time to introduce them, balancing benefit
  against disruption to current delivery.
- 4 — Expert: Horizon-scans across the whole organisation and introduces
  innovation at scale.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a time they recommended adopting a new tool or
  technique, and how they judged that the timing was right.
- Ask about a time they deliberately delayed adopting something new, and why.

**Rate this skill**

- [ ] 0 — None
- [ ] 1 — Beginner
- [ ] 2 — Intermediate
- [ ] 3 — Advanced
- [ ] 4 — Expert

### Skill: Data modelling, cleansing and enrichment

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead analytics engineer is expected to build and review complex data models
ensuring adherence to standards, use data integration tools and languages to
integrate and store data while advising teams on best practice, ensure data
for analysis meets data quality standards and is interoperable with other
data sets, and work with other data professionals to improve modelling and
integration patterns and standards. The framework's own wording for this
skill is unchanged from the senior level below.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. The skill's own text does not move between senior and
lead, but this level's accountabilities — creating standards for the whole
team and reviewing other analytics engineers' work — mean the modelling
practice itself needs to stay at the same strong, independent standard while
now being used as the yardstick others are reviewed against.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot design a complex data model or judge whether one meets
  standards.
- 1 — Beginner: Builds simple models with support.
- 2 — Intermediate: Builds moderately complex models independently but needs
  support reviewing others' models against standards.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently builds and reviews complex data models, ensures
  they meet quality and interoperability standards, and sets the standard the
  rest of the team is reviewed against.
- 4 — Expert: Works across the organisation to improve modelling and
  integration standards themselves.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to review a data model (real or hypothetical) and identify
  where it falls short of good practice.
- Ask about a modelling or integration standard they created for a team to
  follow.

**Rate this skill**

- [ ] 0 — None
- [ ] 1 — Beginner
- [ ] 2 — Intermediate
- [ ] 3 — Advanced
- [ ] 4 — Expert

### Skill: Metadata management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead analytics engineer is expected to design an appropriate metadata
repository, suggest changes to improve current metadata repositories,
understand a range of tools for storing and working with metadata, and advise
less experienced members of the team about metadata management. The
framework's own wording for this skill is unchanged from the senior level
below.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. As with data modelling, the text is unchanged from the
level below, but this level's team-wide standard-setting role means the
baseline holds at the same strong, independent point.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot judge whether a metadata repository is fit for purpose.
- 1 — Beginner: Uses an existing repository correctly but could not design or
  improve one.
- 2 — Intermediate: Suggests small improvements to an existing repository
  with support.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently designs a metadata repository appropriate to a
  defined area, proposes improvements to existing repositories, and advises
  less experienced colleagues on metadata management.
- 4 — Expert: Sets metadata governance for the whole organisation.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a metadata repository they have designed or
  substantially improved, and why.
- Ask about a time they advised a less experienced colleague on metadata
  practice, and what the colleague changed as a result.

### Skill: Problem management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead analytics engineer is expected to ensure that the right actions are
taken to investigate, resolve and anticipate problems, and to co-ordinate the
team to investigate problems, implement solutions and take preventive
measures — coordination across a team, rather than owning an individual
investigation.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Ensuring the right actions are taken and coordinating
a team's response is a step beyond determining and implementing an individual
resolution, which is what the level below is baselined against — the scope
widens even where the general standard of judgement is similar.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Does not coordinate a team's response to a problem, even when
  asked.
- 1 — Beginner: Coordinates a simple, contained problem response with
  guidance.
- 2 — Intermediate: Coordinates the team's response to moderately complex
  problems with some oversight.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently ensures the right actions are taken across the
  team to investigate, resolve and anticipate problems, coordinating several
  people at once.
- 4 — Expert: Builds problem-anticipating capability into the team and the
  wider organisation.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a time they coordinated a team's response to a
  problem that involved more than one person's work.
- Ask about a preventative measure they put in place that stopped a problem
  recurring across the team, not just for their own work.

**Rate this skill**

### Skill: Programming and build (data and analytics engineering)

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead analytics engineer is expected to use agreed standards and tools to
design, code, test, correct and document moderate-to-complex programs and
scripts from agreed specifications and subsequent iterations, and to
collaborate with others to review specifications where appropriate. The
framework's own wording for this skill is unchanged from the senior level
below.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. The text does not move from the level below, and this
level's own role is more about setting standards and reviewing others' work
than writing more programs personally, so the baseline for hands-on
programming holds at the same point rather than climbing further.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot design or build a program from a specification without
  significant direction.
- 1 — Beginner: Builds simple programs from a clear specification, with
  substantial review needed.
- 2 — Intermediate: Builds moderately complex programs with some review.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently designs, builds, tests and documents
  moderate-to-complex programs to agreed standards, and collaborates on
  reviewing specifications.
- 4 — Expert: Sets programming standards and takes overall technical
  responsibility across a project.

**Evidence to gather**

- Review a program or script the candidate has built and ask them to explain
  the design choices.
- Ask about a specification they reviewed and improved before build started.

### Skill: Testing

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead analytics engineer is expected to review requirements and
specifications and define test conditions, identify issues and risks
associated with the work, and analyse and report test activities and results.
The framework's own wording for this skill is unchanged from the senior level
below.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. As with programming, the text is unchanged from the
level below, and the baseline holds at the same strong, independent point.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot define test conditions from a set of requirements.
- 1 — Beginner: Executes test scripts reliably but needs help defining test
  conditions.
- 2 — Intermediate: Defines test conditions for straightforward requirements
  with some support.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently reviews requirements and specifications, defines
  test conditions, identifies risks in the work, and reports results clearly.
- 4 — Expert: Sets testing standards across the team and reviews others' test
  design.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe how they would turn a set of requirements
  into test conditions for a new, complex data model.
- Ask for an example of a risk they identified through testing that would
  otherwise have gone unnoticed.
