# Analytics Engineer - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This assessment is for an assessor — a line manager, a hiring panel member, or
a reviewer — judging whether a candidate or an employee meets the baseline
abilities required for the Data role of Analytics engineer, at the Analytics
engineer role level. It measures demonstrated skill against the UK GDAD
Profession Capability Framework's own description of this role level, for a
hiring decision, a promotion board, onboarding, or a capability check.

At this level, an analytics engineer develops and tests data models assigned
by more senior colleagues, drafts documentation to standard, and starts
supporting trainees and users — real delivery work, but still within models
and specifications set by others.

This assessment is one input among several into a decision, never the whole of
it. Read `## Advisory` before using it.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating on this
form must never be the sole basis for a hiring, promotion, pay, performance or
disciplinary decision. Before using it, consult your organisation's human
resources, people development, occupational psychology or legal compliance
colleagues.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, then gather whatever
evidence exists: a CV or portfolio, a structured interview, a work sample or
task (for example, reviewing a data model the candidate built), direct
observation, or references.

**Score on evidence, not impression.** Every rating must trace to something
the person did, said, produced, or was observed doing. If you cannot point to
evidence for a rating, record it as unknown rather than guess.

**Consistency.** Score every candidate for this role level the same way. For a
hiring or promotion decision, have more than one assessor score independently
before comparing notes.

**Fairness.** Every rating and every piece of evidence you gather must be
about demonstrated, job-related behaviour — see
[Fair and lawful use](../../../../uk-gdad-pcf-competency-assessments-by-assessor/spec/index.md#fair-and-lawful-use) in the project specification.
Never let a rating be coloured by anything other than what the person actually
did, said, or produced.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix below is rated on the same five-point
scale. This is a **proficiency** scale — it rates how well the person does the
thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it or how they feel
about it.

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

**Worked example — Data modelling, cleansing and enrichment.** The framework
expects an analytics engineer to "produce data models and understand where to
use different types of data models" and to "reverse-engineer a data model from
a live system". A candidate who can describe what a data model is but has
never built one is showing **1 — Beginner**, below this level's baseline. A
candidate who has produced a working data model from a given specification,
with some review support, and can explain why they chose a particular
structure is showing **2 — Intermediate** — the baseline this level expects. A
candidate who has independently reverse-engineered a data model from a live,
messy system and defended their design choices unprompted is showing evidence
towards **3 — Advanced**.

## About this role level

The Data profession's Analytics engineer role transforms data relevant to the
organisation into structures that enable analysis and decision-making. Across
the role, an analytics engineer works with subject matter experts to
understand organisational processes and translate these into data structures
optimised for analysis; works with data users to design and build data models
they can use for effective analysis and decision-making, using modelling
techniques such as Kimball or Inmon; supports data quality improvement;
develops standards for data transformation; creates and maintains data
documentation; refines requirements in response to feedback from users and
changes in the organisation; and provides ongoing support to users.

The role has five levels, in order: Trainee analytics engineer, Analytics
engineer, Senior analytics engineer, Lead analytics engineer, and Head of
analytics engineering. This assessment is for the second of them.

An **analytics engineer** develops and tests data models assigned by more
senior analytics engineers, to help people in a defined area access and use
data. At this role level, they will draft documentation of data that meets
standards, work with other analytics engineers to resolve issues and risks,
support trainee analytics engineers, provide training and support for users of
data sets, and work with more experienced analytics engineers to develop their
own skills. The work is real delivery, but scoped and assigned rather than
self-directed.

The framework names eight skills for this role level: Communicating between
the technical and non-technical, Data analysis and synthesis, Data
innovation, Data modelling, cleansing and enrichment, Metadata management,
Problem management, Programming and build (data and analytics engineering),
and Testing.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What the framework expects at this level**

An analytics engineer is expected to communicate effectively with technical
and non-technical stakeholders, support and host discussions within a
multidisciplinary team with potentially difficult dynamics, be an advocate for
the team externally, and manage differing stakeholder perspectives.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. The move from "explain why it matters" at trainee
level to "communicate effectively" and "host discussions" is a step up into
guided practice, but "manage differing stakeholder perspectives" and hosting
"difficult dynamics" ask for more independence than this level's other
accountabilities, so most candidates should be judged against the middle of
the scale rather than the top.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot adapt an explanation for a non-technical audience without
  heavy prompting.
- 1 — Beginner: Explains technical ideas to non-technical stakeholders when
  coached beforehand, but struggles when the conversation goes off-script.
- 2 — Intermediate: Communicates effectively with both technical and
  non-technical stakeholders in routine situations, and can describe how they
  would host a discussion with differing views in a small team.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently hosts discussions with difficult dynamics,
  advocates for the team's work to outsiders, and manages conflicting
  stakeholder expectations without escalation.
- 4 — Expert: Is sought out to mediate the most difficult stakeholder
  situations and coaches others in doing the same.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a time they explained a data model or an
  analysis to someone with no technical background, and how the conversation
  went.
- Present a short scenario: two stakeholders in a meeting want different
  things from the same data set — ask how the candidate would handle it.
- Ask a referee about the candidate's ability to represent their team's work
  to people outside it.

### Skill: Data analysis and synthesis

**What the framework expects at this level**

An analytics engineer is expected to undertake data profiling and source
system analysis, and to present clear insights to colleagues to support the
end use of the data — a step beyond interpreting a given data set, into
profiling a source system.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. Profiling a source system and presenting findings
clearly is guided, real analytical work — more than a trainee's supervised
interpretation, but not yet the independent, multi-source synthesis expected
higher up the ladder.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe what data profiling involves or produce a
  presentable summary of findings.
- 1 — Beginner: With close guidance, profiles a simple source and describes
  basic characteristics such as missing values or duplicate records.
- 2 — Intermediate: Profiles a source system with some independence and
  presents findings to colleagues clearly enough to support a decision.
- 3 — Advanced: Profiles complex or unfamiliar source systems independently
  and synthesises insight across more than one source.
- 4 — Expert: Sets the standard for source system analysis and coaches others
  in synthesising insight for decision-making.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to talk through how they would profile an unfamiliar
  source system — what they would check first, and why.
- Ask for an example of a time they presented a data finding to a colleague,
  and how they made sure it was understood.
- Review a work sample, such as a data profiling report or a short findings
  summary, if one is available.

**Rate this skill**

- [ ] 0 — None
- [ ] 1 — Beginner
- [ ] 2 — Intermediate
- [ ] 3 — Advanced
- [ ] 4 — Expert

### Skill: Data innovation

**What the framework expects at this level**

An analytics engineer is expected to show an awareness of opportunities for
innovation with new tools and uses of data — the framework's wording for this
skill is identical to the trainee level.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. Because the framework's own wording does not change
between trainee and this level for this specific skill, the baseline stays the
same rather than being inflated to match the general seniority of the role
level — this is one of the skills that does not progress at this step in the
ladder.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Shows no interest in or awareness of tools or approaches beyond
  what they use day to day.
- 1 — Beginner: Can name a tool, technique or way of working they have come
  across that seemed useful, when prompted.
- 2 — Intermediate: Proactively raises a new tool or technique with colleagues
  and explains why it might help.
- 3 — Advanced: Evaluates whether a new tool or technique genuinely fits the
  organisation's context before recommending it.
- 4 — Expert: Actively scans the field and introduces genuinely useful
  approaches into team practice.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to name a data tool, technique or trend they have come
  across recently and what interested them about it.
- Ask whether they have suggested a new way of working to a team, and what
  happened.

### Skill: Data modelling, cleansing and enrichment

**What the framework expects at this level**

An analytics engineer is expected to produce data models and understand where
to use different types of data models, understand different tools and be able
to compare different data models, reverse-engineer a data model from a live
system, and understand industry-recognised data modelling patterns and
standards.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. "Produce data models" is genuine, hands-on
production work — a real step up from a trainee's awareness of the concept —
but this level's models are assigned by more senior colleagues, so
independent design judgement is not yet the baseline.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot produce a data model even from a clear specification.
- 1 — Beginner: Produces a simple data model from a clear specification, with
  significant review and correction needed.
- 2 — Intermediate: Produces data models from an assigned specification with
  moderate review, and can reverse-engineer a straightforward model from a
  live system.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently designs data models for new requirements and
  reviews others' models against modelling standards.
- 4 — Expert: Sets the organisation's modelling patterns and standards.

**Evidence to gather**

- Review a data model the candidate has built, or ask them to sketch one from
  a short, given specification.
- Ask them to describe how they would reverse-engineer a model from an
  existing, unfamiliar system.
- Ask about the modelling techniques they know, such as Kimball or Inmon, and
  when they would choose one over the other.

### Skill: Metadata management

**What the framework expects at this level**

An analytics engineer is expected to use metadata repositories to complete
complex tasks such as data and systems integration impact analysis, and to
maintain a metadata repository to ensure information remains accurate and up
to date — a move from directed maintenance to using metadata for real
analytical tasks.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. Using a repository for impact analysis is more
than following instructions to update entries, but it is bounded, task-level
work rather than repository design, so Intermediate fits.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot use a metadata repository to answer a real question about
  data lineage or impact.
- 1 — Beginner: Looks up entries in a metadata repository correctly, with
  guidance on how to interpret them.
- 2 — Intermediate: Uses a metadata repository unaided to support a task such
  as assessing the impact of a system change, and keeps the repository
  accurate for their own area of work.
- 3 — Advanced: Maintains a metadata repository across a wider area
  independently and identifies gaps in its coverage.
- 4 — Expert: Designs metadata repositories and the governance that keeps them
  accurate.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe how they would use a metadata repository to
  work out what would be affected if a source system field changed.
- Ask about their experience keeping metadata records accurate and up to date.

**Rate this skill**

- [ ] 0 — None
- [ ] 1 — Beginner
- [ ] 2 — Intermediate

### Skill: Problem management

**What the framework expects at this level**

An analytics engineer is expected to investigate problems in systems,
processes and services, with an understanding of the level of a problem — for
example, strategic, tactical or operational — and to contribute to the
implementation of remedies and preventative measures. The framework's own
wording for this skill is unchanged from the trainee level.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. As with Data innovation, the framework's wording does
not move between trainee and this level for this specific skill, so the
baseline holds rather than climbing automatically with general seniority.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Does not notice or investigate when something is not working as
  expected.
- 1 — Beginner: With guidance, investigates a contained problem and correctly
  judges roughly whether it is a one-off or a wider issue.
- 2 — Intermediate: Investigates moderately complex problems with some
  independence and contributes a workable remedy.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently investigates and resolves problems and
  distinguishes strategic, tactical and operational causes confidently.
- 4 — Expert: Anticipates problems and builds others' capability to
  investigate them.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a problem they investigated in their current
  or a previous role, and how they worked out its cause and level.
- Present a short scenario (for example, a data pipeline producing wrong
  numbers intermittently) and ask how they would investigate it.

### Skill: Programming and build (data and analytics engineering)

**What the framework expects at this level**

An analytics engineer is expected to design, code, test, correct and document
simple programs or scripts under the direction of others — the framework's own
first mention of this skill, since it does not appear at trainee level.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. This is the first level at which the skill is named at
all, and the framework's wording — "simple programs", "under the direction of
others" — describes early, supervised practice, so the baseline sits at
Beginner rather than Intermediate.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot write or correct a simple script even with direction.
- 1 — Beginner: Writes and tests a simple script under direction, and
  documents what it does when asked.
- 2 — Intermediate: Writes, tests and documents simple-to-moderate scripts
  with only light direction.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently designs, builds and documents moderately
  complex programs from a specification.
- 4 — Expert: Sets coding standards and reviews others' programs for quality
  and maintainability.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to walk through a script or piece of code they have
  written, explaining what it does and why they wrote it that way.
- Give a small, simple coding task (for example, transforming a short data
  file) and observe the approach, if a work sample is appropriate.
- Ask about their experience documenting code so someone else could pick it
  up.

### Skill: Testing

**What the framework expects at this level**

An analytics engineer is expected to correctly execute test scripts under
supervision, and to understand the role of testing and how it works — the
framework's wording is unchanged from the trainee level.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. As with Data innovation and Problem management, this
skill's wording does not move between trainee and this level, so the baseline
holds at Beginner.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot follow a test script accurately, or does not understand why
  testing matters.
- 1 — Beginner: Follows a supplied test script correctly under supervision and
  explains why testing is done.
- 2 — Intermediate: Executes test scripts with minimal supervision and flags
  unexpected results clearly.
- 3 — Advanced: Designs simple test cases as well as executing them.
- 4 — Expert: Sets testing standards and reviews others' test coverage.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to walk through how they would follow a written test
  script for checking a data transformation produces the right output.
- Ask about a time they found an unexpected result while testing, and what
  they did next.
