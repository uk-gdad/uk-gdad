# Head of Analytics Engineering - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This assessment is for an assessor — a line manager, a hiring panel member, or
a reviewer — judging whether a candidate or an employee meets the baseline
abilities required for the Data role of Analytics engineer, at the Head of
analytics engineering role level. It measures demonstrated skill against the
UK GDAD Profession Capability Framework's own description of this role level,
for a hiring decision, a promotion board, onboarding, or a capability check.

A head of analytics engineering oversees the development of a range of data
models that meet analysis needs across the whole organisation, builds
relationships with senior stakeholders, aligns the analytics engineering
team's work with strategic priorities, and builds capability across the
analytics engineering community. This assessment looks for organisation-wide
influence, strategic judgement, and the ability to build capability in others,
not just strong individual delivery.

This assessment is one input among several into a decision, never the whole of
it. Read `## Advisory` before using it.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating on this
form must never be the sole basis for a hiring, promotion, pay, performance or
disciplinary decision. Before using it, consult your organisation's human
resources, people development, occupational psychology or legal compliance
colleagues.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, then gather whatever
evidence exists: a CV or portfolio, a structured interview, a work sample or
task (for example, asking the candidate to describe how they would set
analytics engineering strategy for an organisation, or to critique a proposed
governance process), direct observation, or references — particularly
references that can speak to the candidate's standing with senior stakeholders
and their track record of building capability in others.

**Score on evidence, not impression.** Every rating must trace to something
the person did, said, produced, or was observed doing. Evidence at this level
often takes longer to surface than a single task — a track record across
several years, rather than one example — so allow time for it to emerge, and
record a rating as unknown rather than guess if it cannot.

**Consistency.** Score every candidate for this role level the same way. For a
hiring or promotion decision, have more than one assessor score independently
before comparing notes.

**Fairness.** Every rating and every piece of evidence you gather must be
about demonstrated, job-related behaviour — see
[Fair and lawful use](#fair-and-lawful-use) in the project specification.
Never let a rating be coloured by anything other than what the person actually
did, said, or produced.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix below is rated on the same five-point
scale. This is a **proficiency** scale — it rates how well the person does the
thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it or how they feel
about it.

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

**Worked example — Metadata management.** The framework expects a head of
analytics engineering to "identify how metadata repositories can support
different areas of the organisation", "communicate the value of metadata
repositories" and "set up robust governance processes to keep repositories up
to date". A candidate who has designed a good metadata repository for their
own team, but has never set organisation-wide governance or made the case for
metadata investment to senior leaders, is showing evidence closer to
**3 — Advanced** — strong practice, but not yet this level's baseline. A
candidate who has set up governance that keeps metadata accurate across
several teams, and who can describe how they persuaded senior stakeholders of
its value, is showing **4 — Expert**, the baseline this level expects.

## About this role level

The Data profession's Analytics engineer role transforms data relevant to the
organisation into structures that enable analysis and decision-making. Across
the role, an analytics engineer works with subject matter experts to
understand organisational processes and translate these into data structures
optimised for analysis; works with data users to design and build data models
they can use for effective analysis and decision-making, using modelling
techniques such as Kimball or Inmon; supports data quality improvement;
develops standards for data transformation; creates and maintains data
documentation; refines requirements in response to feedback from users and
changes in the organisation; and provides ongoing support to users.

The role has five levels, in order: Trainee analytics engineer, Analytics
engineer, Senior analytics engineer, Lead analytics engineer, and Head of
analytics engineering. This assessment is for the fifth and most senior of
them.

A **head of analytics engineering** oversees the development of a range of
data models that meet analysis needs across the organisation. At this role
level, they will build relationships with senior stakeholders across the
organisation to determine what the organisation needs, ensure the activities
of the analytics engineering team align with strategic priorities, ensure the
team works to standards for communication, data models and documentation,
advocate for the analytics engineering role to senior leadership and other
organisations, build analytics engineering capability by providing technical
leadership and career development for the community, and ensure appropriate
technology is available for the community.

The framework names eight skills for this role level: Communicating between
the technical and non-technical, Data analysis and synthesis, Data
innovation, Data modelling, cleansing and enrichment, Metadata management,
Problem management, Programming and build (data and analytics engineering),
and Testing.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What the framework expects at this level**

A head of analytics engineering is expected to mediate between people and
strengthen relationships, adopting the appropriate communication method with
stakeholders at all levels, manage stakeholder expectations and moderate
difficult discussions about high risk and complex topics even within
constrained timescales, and speak on behalf of and represent the community to
large audiences inside and outside the organisation.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Speaking for the whole community to large audiences, and
moderating high-risk, time-constrained discussions, is the top of what the
framework describes for this skill across the role's ladder — this level is
where "manage differing stakeholder perspectives" becomes representing and
defending the profession itself.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot represent the analytics engineering function to senior
  stakeholders or defend its position under pressure.
- 1 — Beginner: Communicates competently one to one but struggles in
  high-stakes group settings.
- 2 — Intermediate: Manages moderately difficult discussions with support but
  is not yet the person the organisation turns to for the hardest ones.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently manages difficult discussions and represents
  the team externally, but has not yet consistently represented the whole
  community at scale.
- 4 — Expert: Is trusted to mediate the organisation's hardest, highest-risk
  discussions about data work, and represents the analytics engineering
  community credibly to large internal and external audiences.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe the most difficult, highest-stakes discussion
  they have led about data work, under time pressure, and what they did.
- Ask a referee about the candidate's standing with senior stakeholders and
  whether they have represented a data function externally.
- Ask about a time they had to say no to a senior stakeholder and how they
  managed the relationship afterwards.

- [ ] 1 — Beginner
- [ ] 2 — Intermediate
- [ ] 3 — Advanced

### Skill: Data analysis and synthesis

**What the framework expects at this level**

A head of analytics engineering is expected to understand and help teams
apply a range of techniques for data profiling, carry out source system
analysis from a complex single source, and bring multiple data sources
together in a conformed model for analysis. The framework's own wording for
this skill is unchanged from the lead level below.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Although the role level itself is the most senior in
the ladder, the framework's own wording for this specific hands-on analytical
skill does not move beyond the lead level, so the baseline is held at
Advanced rather than inflated to Expert on the strength of the job title
alone — this level's real step up shows in the other skills below, not this
one.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot synthesise more than one data source into a usable model.
- 1 — Beginner: Profiles a single source competently but struggles to combine
  more than one.
- 2 — Intermediate: Combines related sources with support.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently analyses a complex single source and brings
  multiple data sources together in a conformed model, and helps others build
  the same skill.
- 4 — Expert: Sets the organisation's whole approach to conforming disparate
  data for analysis.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate for an example, even if from earlier in their career, of
  bringing several disparate data sources together for analysis.
- Ask how they keep their own hands-on analytical skill current while working
  at a strategic level.

**Rate this skill**

- [ ] 0 — None
- [ ] 1 — Beginner
- [ ] 2 — Intermediate
- [ ] 3 — Advanced
- [ ] 4 — Expert

### Skill: Data innovation

**What the framework expects at this level**

A head of analytics engineering is expected to investigate emerging trends in
data-related approaches, perform horizon-scanning for the organisation, and
introduce innovative ways of working — the framework's most demanding
description of this skill anywhere in the role's ladder.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Horizon-scanning for the whole organisation, and
actually introducing new ways of working as a result, is the top of this
skill's progression — a clear step beyond the lead level's judgement about
timing for a single team.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Does not track emerging trends beyond their own immediate work.
- 1 — Beginner: Is aware of trends when told about them but does not scan
  proactively.
- 2 — Intermediate: Occasionally investigates a trend and shares findings with
  their own team.
- 3 — Advanced: Regularly investigates trends and recommends adoption within
  their own area, with reasonable success.
- 4 — Expert: Proactively horizon-scans for the whole organisation and has a
  track record of successfully introducing new, innovative ways of working as
  a result.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a change in how the organisation works with
  data that they personally introduced, from spotting the trend to making it
  happen.
- Ask how they keep themselves informed of developments across the wider
  data field, not just their own specialism.

**Rate this skill**

- [ ] 0 — None
- [ ] 1 — Beginner
- [ ] 2 — Intermediate
- [ ] 3 — Advanced
- [ ] 4 — Expert

### Skill: Data modelling, cleansing and enrichment

**What the framework expects at this level**

A head of analytics engineering is expected to understand different ways to
model data to maximise its use and value, ensure data is modelled
appropriately with standards that exist and are complied with, understand data
integration tools and patterns and ensure teams have the support and training
to use the most appropriate methods, and build relationships with other
senior data professionals to share best practice and continually improve
modelling and integration processes and standards.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. This level's accountabilities move from building and
reviewing models personally to ensuring compliance across the organisation and
building cross-discipline relationships with other senior data professionals —
governance and influence at organisational scale, which is the top of this
skill's progression.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe how the organisation's modelling standards are
  kept consistent.
- 1 — Beginner: Follows modelling standards personally but has not shaped
  them.
- 2 — Intermediate: Contributes to modelling standards within their own team.
- 3 — Advanced: Sets modelling standards for a team and ensures compliance
  within it.
- 4 — Expert: Ensures modelling standards exist and are complied with across
  the organisation, ensures teams are trained and supported to use the right
  methods, and builds relationships with other senior data professionals to
  keep improving them.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe how they ensure modelling standards are
  followed across more than one team, and what they do when they are not.
- Ask about a relationship they have built with a senior data architect,
  engineer or scientist, and what came of it.

### Skill: Metadata management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A head of analytics engineering is expected to identify how metadata
repositories can support different areas of the organisation, communicate the
value of metadata repositories, and set up robust governance processes to
keep repositories up to date.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Setting up governance and making the case for
investment across the organisation is the top of this skill's progression —
well beyond designing or improving a single repository, which is what the
level below is baselined against.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot explain why metadata management matters to the
  organisation.
- 1 — Beginner: Understands the value of good metadata for their own work.
- 2 — Intermediate: Advocates for good metadata practice within their own
  team.
- 3 — Advanced: Designs and improves a metadata repository and advises others
  on practice.
- 4 — Expert: Identifies how metadata repositories can support different
  parts of the organisation, makes the case for their value to senior
  stakeholders, and sets up governance that keeps them accurate and useful.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe how they made the case for investment in
  metadata management to a senior stakeholder, and what happened.
- Ask about a governance process they set up to keep metadata accurate across
  more than one team.

### Skill: Problem management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A head of analytics engineering is expected to anticipate problems and defend
against them at the right time, understand how a problem fits into the larger
picture, identify and describe problems and help others describe them, and
build problem-solving capabilities in others.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Anticipating problems before they occur, seeing how they
fit the bigger picture, and building capability in others is the top of this
skill's progression, and matches the framework's own wording most closely
with organisation-wide, capability-building responsibility.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Reacts to problems only after they have caused visible harm.
- 1 — Beginner: Investigates problems reactively when raised.
- 2 — Intermediate: Coordinates a team's reactive response to problems.
- 3 — Advanced: Ensures the right actions are taken and coordinates the team
  to investigate, resolve and anticipate problems.
- 4 — Expert: Anticipates problems before they surface, understands how each
  one fits the larger organisational picture, and deliberately builds
  problem-solving capability in others.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a significant problem they anticipated and
  headed off before it caused harm, and how they saw it coming.
- Ask about a time they helped someone else learn to identify and describe a
  problem more clearly.

### Skill: Programming and build (data and analytics engineering)

**What the framework expects at this level**

A head of analytics engineering is expected to set local or team-based
standards for programming tools and techniques and select appropriate
development methods, advise on the application of standards and methods and
ensure compliance, and take technical responsibility for all stages and
iterations in a software development project, providing method-specific
technical advice and guidance to project stakeholders.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Setting standards, ensuring compliance, and taking
overall technical responsibility for a project is exactly the framework's own
description of expert-level practice — a clear step beyond building
moderate-to-complex programs personally, which is where the level below is
baselined.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot advise a project on which development methods or standards
  to use.
- 1 — Beginner: Follows agreed programming standards but has not set them.
- 2 — Intermediate: Contributes to setting standards for their own team.
- 3 — Advanced: Builds moderate-to-complex programs independently to agreed
  standards.
- 4 — Expert: Sets standards for programming tools and techniques, selects
  appropriate development methods, advises projects on compliance, and takes
  overall technical responsibility across all stages of a development
  project.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a set of programming standards they set for a
  team or a project, and how they ensured compliance.
- Ask about a time they took technical responsibility for a whole project,
  from design through to delivery.

### Skill: Testing

**What the framework expects at this level**

A head of analytics engineering is expected to review requirements and
specifications and define test conditions, identify issues and risks
associated with the work, and analyse and report test activities and results.
The framework's own wording for this skill is unchanged from the senior and
lead levels below.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. As with Data analysis and synthesis, this hands-on
skill's wording plateaus below the top of the role's ladder, so the baseline
holds at Advanced rather than being inflated for the sake of the job title —
this level's genuine step up in testing shows through ensuring the whole
team's practice meets this bar, which belongs in the surrounding
accountabilities rather than in the skill's own wording.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot define test conditions from a set of requirements.
- 1 — Beginner: Executes test scripts reliably but needs help defining test
  conditions.
- 2 — Intermediate: Defines test conditions for straightforward requirements
  with some support.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently reviews requirements and specifications, defines
  test conditions, identifies risks in the work, and reports results clearly,
  and can explain how they ensure this standard holds across a team they
  oversee.
- 4 — Expert: Sets testing standards across the whole organisation.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate how they satisfy themselves that testing practice across
  their whole team or community meets a good standard, given they are not
  doing all of it personally.
- Ask for an example of test-derived evidence that changed a significant
  decision.
