# Data Governance Manager - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This assessment is for an assessor — a line manager, a hiring panel member, or
a reviewer — judging whether a candidate or an employee meets the baseline
abilities required for the Data role of Data governance manager, at the Data
governance manager role level. It measures demonstrated skill against the UK
GDAD Profession Capability Framework's own description of this role level, for
a hiring decision, a promotion board, onboarding, or a capability check.

A data governance manager plans, monitors and enforces the management of data
assets. At this, the first of the role's three levels, the job is already real
governance work — designing practical data governance solutions, building
recommendations to improve data quality and trustworthiness, and helping the
organisation manage data risk — but it sits under a Lead data governance
manager and a Head of data governance, and it is written for someone still
building independent judgement in several of the wider disciplines the role
touches, such as strategy and risk.

This assessment is one input among several into a decision, never the whole of
it. Read `## Advisory` before using it.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating on this
form must never be the sole basis for a hiring, promotion, pay, performance or
disciplinary decision. Before using it, consult your organisation's human
resources, people development, occupational psychology or legal compliance
colleagues.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, then gather whatever
evidence exists: a CV or portfolio, a structured interview, a work sample or
task (for example, sketching part of a data governance framework or working
through a short risk scenario), direct observation of the candidate in a
governance meeting or discussion, or references.

**Score on evidence, not impression.** Every rating must trace to something
the person did, said, produced, or was observed doing. Where you cannot point
to evidence for a rating, record it as unknown rather than guess.

**Consistency.** Score every candidate for this role level the same way. For a
hiring or promotion decision, have more than one assessor score independently
before comparing notes.

**Fairness.** An unvalidated, AI-drafted instrument used inconsistently can
produce unlawful adverse impact. Consistent, evidence-based administration by
more than one assessor materially reduces that risk. Every rating and every
piece of evidence you gather must be about demonstrated, job-related
behaviour — see [Fair and lawful use](../../../../uk-gdad-pcf-competency-assessments-by-assessor/spec/index.md#fair-and-lawful-use) in the project
specification. Never let a rating be coloured by anything other than what the
person actually did, said, or produced.

**Sensitive material.** Data governance work touches organisational risk
registers and sensitive data sets. Do not record classified material, live
security detail, or live risk detail in the evidence log below — describe the
governance behaviour you observed or were told about, not the substance of a
confidential incident, control or data set.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix below is rated on the same five-point
scale. This is a **proficiency** scale — it rates how well the person does the
thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it or how they feel
about it.

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

**Worked example — Data governance.** The framework expects a data governance
manager to "develop an end-to-end data governance framework (the processes,
operational policies and standards needed to support data management)" and to
"work effectively with technical teams and other stakeholders to implement" it.
A candidate who can describe what a data governance framework is for, but
cannot point to any part of one they have built, is showing **1 — Beginner** —
below this level's baseline. A candidate who, with some support, has drafted
one policy or process within a wider framework and can explain how it fits is
showing **2 — Intermediate**, still short of the baseline. A candidate who has
independently developed an end-to-end framework — processes, policies and
standards together — and worked with technical teams to implement it and
secure compliance is showing **3 — Advanced**, the baseline this level
expects.

## About this role level

The Data profession's Data governance manager role plans, monitors and
enforces the management of data assets. Across the role, a data governance
manager catalogues, monitors and identifies data assets that are essential to
the organisation's operations or services; assigns ownership of data, so that
a clear RACI (responsible, accountable, consulted and informed) matrix exists
in the organisation; incorporates governance of data into routine processes;
supports or manages risk processes around data; advocates for better use and
management of data within the organisation; and encourages better data
literacy within the organisation.

The role has three levels, in order: Data governance manager, Lead data
governance manager, and Head of data governance. This assessment is for the
first of them.

At the **data governance manager** role level, a person defines, implements
and manages data governance frameworks, policies and standards. At this role
level, they will work on the design and implementation of practical data
governance solutions to support organisational needs, identify opportunities
and make recommendations to improve the quality and trustworthiness of data
throughout the organisation, implement and establish wider data management
competencies within the organisation (such as data quality, metadata
management, master data management, data modelling, and development of data
standards and data strategy), apply data governance to optimise the safety and
value of data throughout the data life cycle, and work within the organisation
to minimise data risks and increase data literacy.

The framework names thirteen skills for this role level: Communicating
analysis and insight, Communicating between the technical and non-technical,
Data ethics and privacy, Data governance, Data innovation, Data life cycle,
Data literacy improvement, Data management, Data maturity, Data standards,
Enabling and informing risk-based decisions, Stakeholder relationship
management, and Strategic thinking.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Communicating analysis and insight

**What the framework expects at this level**

A data governance manager is expected to choose the appropriate medium to
communicate findings, shape and share communications relevant to the
audience, present analysis and visualisations in clear ways to both specialist
and non-specialist audiences with support, and give tactical recommendations
based on their analysis and insight to inform decision making.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. Presenting "with support" caps the practical
expectation, while choosing a medium, shaping communications, and giving
tactical recommendations describe real, if still developing, independent
practice, so the baseline sits at Intermediate rather than Beginner.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot say why a particular audience would need a different
  explanation of a governance finding.
- 1 — Beginner: With guidance, picks a broadly suitable format for one finding
  (for example, a short memo) and drafts it with a colleague's review.
- 2 — Intermediate: Chooses an appropriate medium unprompted, presents a
  governance analysis to a mixed specialist and non-specialist audience with
  support, and offers a tactical recommendation tied to the finding.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently shapes and delivers analysis and recommendations
  that change how a stakeholder group acts, without needing a colleague's
  review first.
- 4 — Expert: Sets the standard for how governance analysis is communicated
  across the organisation and coaches others in it.

**Evidence to gather**

- Give the candidate a short governance finding, such as a data quality issue
  found in one system, and ask them to prepare a brief summary for a
  non-specialist audience.
- Ask about a time they gave a recommendation based on an analysis, and what
  happened after they gave it.
- Review a report, slide deck, or briefing note the candidate has produced, if
  one is available.

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What the framework expects at this level**

A data governance manager is expected to communicate effectively with
technical and non-technical stakeholders, support and host discussions within
a multidisciplinary team with potentially difficult dynamics, be an advocate
for the team externally, and manage differing stakeholder perspectives.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. Supporting and hosting discussions, and managing
differing perspectives, ask for practical facilitation skill beyond basic
communication but stop short of independently leading the hardest dynamics
alone, which fits Intermediate.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Struggles to get a technical governance point across to a
  non-technical stakeholder even with a colleague's help.
- 1 — Beginner: Communicates a straightforward governance point to a
  non-technical stakeholder, with support preparing the explanation.
- 2 — Intermediate: Hosts part of a multidisciplinary discussion, including
  one with some difficult dynamics, and manages at least one differing
  stakeholder perspective without escalating it.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently leads discussions across a multidisciplinary
  team through difficult dynamics and represents the team's position
  externally.
- 4 — Expert: Is sought out to mediate the most difficult disputes between
  technical and non-technical parties.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a recent disagreement between a technical team
  and a business stakeholder that they were part of, and what they did.
- Ask them to explain a governance concept, such as data ownership, as they
  would to a non-technical director, and then as they would to an engineer.
- Ask about, or observe, their role in a multidisciplinary meeting with
  competing views.

### Skill: Data ethics and privacy

**What the framework expects at this level**

A data governance manager is expected to apply fundamental principles of data
ethics and privacy in their work under supervision, and to share data ethics
and privacy risks through appropriate channels.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. "Under supervision" is explicit in the framework's own
wording for this skill at this level, so the baseline sits at the first rung
above None rather than higher.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot identify a data ethics or privacy principle relevant to a
  piece of work they are doing.
- 1 — Beginner: Applies a fundamental data ethics or privacy principle in
  their own work when a supervisor points it out, and, when a concern arises,
  uses the right channel to share it after checking with someone senior.
- 2 — Intermediate: Applies fundamental data ethics and privacy principles to
  their own work without prompting, and raises a concern through the right
  channel unprompted.
- 3 — Advanced: Proactively spots data ethics or privacy risk in someone
  else's work and advises them on it.
- 4 — Expert: Shapes the organisation's approach to data ethics and privacy and
  is the point of escalation for others' concerns.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a data ethics or privacy principle relevant to
  the organisation's work, and how they applied it recently.
- Ask what channel they would use to raise a data ethics or privacy concern,
  and why.
- Do not ask the candidate to disclose the details of a live or sensitive
  privacy case; ask about the process they followed instead.

### Skill: Data governance

**What the framework expects at this level**

A data governance manager is expected to demonstrate up-to-date knowledge of
data governance theory, standard methodology and the practical considerations,
develop an end-to-end data governance framework (the processes, operational
policies and standards needed to support data management), and work
effectively with technical teams and other stakeholders to implement a data
governance framework, making recommendations to ensure compliance.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. This is the skill the role level's own description
names as its core purpose — "defines, implements and manages data governance
frameworks, policies and standards" — and the framework's own wording asks for
an end-to-end framework to be developed and implemented, which is independent
work, so the baseline sits at Advanced.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe what a data governance framework is for.
- 1 — Beginner: Describes, at a basic level, current data governance theory
  and methodology, and points to parts of an existing framework.
- 2 — Intermediate: With support, drafts part of a data governance framework,
  such as one policy or one process, and explains how it fits the wider
  framework.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently develops an end-to-end data governance
  framework — the processes, policies and standards a function needs — and
  works with technical teams to implement it, making recommendations to
  secure compliance.
- 4 — Expert: Is recognised across the organisation as the authority on data
  governance framework design and advises other functions setting up their
  own.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to walk through a data governance framework they have
  worked on or designed — its policies, processes and standards — and their
  part in it.
- Give a short scenario, such as a new data source arriving with no clear
  owner, and ask how they would start building governance around it.
- Ask a referee about a specific governance framework or policy the candidate
  helped implement.

### Skill: Data innovation

**What the framework expects at this level**

A data governance manager is expected to understand the impact on the
organisation of emerging trends in data tools, analysis techniques and data
usage.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. This is a single "understand the impact" bullet,
which describes awareness rather than demonstrated application, so the
baseline sits at Beginner.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Is unaware of a data tool, technique, or usage trend that is
  materially changing similar organisations' practice.
- 1 — Beginner: Names an emerging trend in data tools, analysis techniques or

  when asked.
- 2 — Intermediate: Tracks emerging trends as a matter of course and raises a

- 3 — Advanced: Assesses the governance implications of an emerging trend in
  enough depth to inform a policy or process change.
- 4 — Expert: Is sought out by others in the organisation to interpret what an
  emerging data trend means for governance.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to name an emerging trend in data tools, analysis
  techniques, or data usage, and what it might mean for the organisation's
  governance.
- Ask how they keep up to date with developments in data tools and practice.

### Skill: Data life cycle

**What the framework expects at this level**

A data governance manager is expected to understand and apply data governance
over a data life cycle, ensuring best practice at each phase.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. Applying governance across the whole life cycle,
not just understanding it, is real applied work, so the baseline sits above

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot name the phases of a data life cycle relevant to their

- 1 — Beginner: Names the phases of a data life cycle and, with guidance,

  contested phases.
- 4 — Expert: Defines the organisation's data life cycle governance model and
  trains others to apply it.

- Ask the candidate to describe the phases of the data life cycle for a data
  asset they know well, and one governance practice at each phase.

### Skill: Data literacy improvement

### Skill: Data management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A data governance manager is expected to follow organisational data
governance, including policies on data access, sharing, dissemination and
protection, participate in or deliver data management across services or
products, and use appropriate data management tools, procedures and methods
with some support.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. "With some support" is explicit in the framework's
own wording, and matches the definition of Intermediate — knowing the basics
and performing tasks with some guidance — directly.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Does not follow the organisation's data governance policies on
  data access, sharing, dissemination or protection.
- 1 — Beginner: Follows organisational data governance policy in their own
  work when reminded, and takes a small, defined part in a data management
  activity for one service or product.
- 2 — Intermediate: Follows data governance policy consistently without
  reminders, and, with some support, uses appropriate data management tools,
  procedures and methods to deliver data management work across a service or
  product.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently delivers data management across multiple
  services or products, choosing the right tools, procedures and methods
  without support.
- 4 — Expert: Sets the data management approach other teams follow across
  services and products.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a data management tool, procedure, or method
  they have used, and how it fitted the organisation's wider policy.
- Give a short scenario describing a service that shares data with a third
  party, and ask what data governance policy points they would check.

### Skill: Data standards

**What the framework expects at this level**

A data governance manager is expected to use data policies, processes and
standards effectively, work with subject matter experts to develop standards,
policies and guidance to protect data, monitor compliance with policies and
standards in a team and take action if needed, and analyse the impact if a
standard is breached.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. Using standards effectively and contributing to
developing them alongside subject matter experts describes solid, guided
practice; independently leading standard development and owning breach
response sit above this level's baseline.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot say which data standard applies to a piece of work they are
  doing.
- 1 — Beginner: Uses an existing data policy, process, or standard correctly
  in their own work, with guidance on which one applies.
- 2 — Intermediate: Uses data policies, processes and standards effectively
  without guidance, contributes to developing a standard alongside subject
  matter experts, and monitors compliance in a team, escalating where action
  is needed.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently leads the development of a new standard with
  subject matter experts, and analyses the impact of a breach, recommending a
  proportionate response.
- 4 — Expert: Owns the organisation's data standards portfolio and is the
  final point of judgement on how a breach is handled.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a data standard they use regularly, and an
  example of monitoring compliance with it.
- Give a scenario where a data standard has been breached, and ask them to
  describe the impact and what they would do next.
- Ask a referee about the candidate's role in developing or maintaining a data
  standard.

### Skill: Enabling and informing risk-based decisions

**What the framework expects at this level**

A data governance manager is expected to work with risk owners to advise and
give feedback, advise on risk impact and whether it is within risk tolerance,
and describe different risk methodologies and how these are applied, as well
as the proportionality of risk.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. "Work with risk owners to advise" describes
supported engagement with risk decisions rather than owning them outright, so
the baseline sits at Intermediate.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe what "risk tolerance" means in a data context.
- 1 — Beginner: With support, describes a risk methodology and gives a basic
  view on whether a data risk sits within tolerance.
- 2 — Intermediate: Works with a risk owner to advise on and give feedback on
  a data risk, and independently describes different risk methodologies and
  how proportionality applies.
- 3 — Advanced: Advises risk owners on complex, ambiguous data risk with
  confidence, and is trusted to judge proportionality without checking with
  someone senior.
- 4 — Expert: Shapes the organisation's approach to data risk methodology and
  trains risk owners in using it.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a data risk they helped a risk owner think
  through, and what advice they gave.
- Give a short, generalised scenario and ask whether, in their view, the
  described risk sits within a stated risk tolerance, and why.
- Do not ask the candidate to describe a live, unresolved risk or its
  confidential detail; use a generalised or past example instead.

### Skill: Stakeholder relationship management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A data governance manager is expected to identify important stakeholders and
communicate with them clearly and regularly, tailor communication to
stakeholders' needs and work with them to build relationships while meeting
user needs, build and reach consensus with stakeholders, and work to improve
stakeholder relationships using evidence to explain decisions.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. Identifying stakeholders, communicating regularly,
and tailoring communication describe solid, applied practice; building
consensus on a contested issue and repairing a strained relationship are the
harder end of this skill and sit above the baseline.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot name the stakeholders relevant to a piece of governance
  work.
- 1 — Beginner: Identifies important stakeholders for a piece of work, with
  guidance, and communicates with them when prompted.
- 2 — Intermediate: Identifies stakeholders and communicates with them clearly
  and regularly without prompting, tailors communication to their needs, and
  builds towards consensus on a straightforward issue.
- 3 — Advanced: Builds and reaches consensus with stakeholders on a contested
  issue, and uses evidence to repair a strained stakeholder relationship.
- 4 — Expert: Is the person other data governance colleagues bring in to
  manage the organisation's hardest stakeholder relationships.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe the stakeholders for a governance initiative
  they worked on, and how they tailored communication to each one.
- Ask about a time they built consensus with a stakeholder who initially
  disagreed with a governance recommendation.
