# Data Governance Manager (Head of Data Governance) - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This assessment is for an assessor — a line manager, a hiring panel member, or
a reviewer — judging whether a candidate or an existing employee has the
baseline abilities required for the Head of Data Governance role level, within
the Data Governance Manager role in the data profession. It measures
demonstrated skill against the UK GDAD Profession Capability Framework's own
description of this role level: what someone at this level is expected to do,
and how well they can be expected to do it. Use it for a hiring decision, a
promotion board, onboarding into the role, or a capability check against the
framework.

Head of Data Governance is the most senior of the three role levels in the
Data Governance Manager role. It sits above Data governance manager and Lead
data governance manager, and the person doing it works directly with the
chief data officer, providing strategic oversight of data governance across
the whole organisation and, in places, as a senior advisor across government.
The competency matrix below reflects that seniority: most baselines sit at
Advanced or Expert, and the behavioural indicators describe judgement,
influence and organisation-wide accountability rather than close-supervision
tasks.

This assessment is one input to a decision, not the decision itself. Read
`## Advisory` before using it, and read `## How to use this assessment` for
how to administer it fairly.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` before you score anything, so
you know what the framework itself says this level is accountable for. Then
gather whatever evidence exists for the person you are assessing: a CV or
portfolio, a structured interview, a work sample or task (for example, a
governance framework document or a board-level risk paper they have written),
direct observation of them at work, or references from people who have seen
them operate at this level.

**Score on evidence, not impression.** Every rating you give must trace to
something the person actually did, said, produced, or was observed doing. If
you cannot point to evidence for a rating, record it as unknown rather than
guess — a guess dressed up as a score is worse than an honest gap.

**Be consistent.** Score every candidate for this role level the same way,
using the same evidence types where you can. Where the decision matters — a
hiring or promotion decision especially — have more than one assessor score
independently before comparing notes and agreeing a final view.

**Stay fair.** This is an unvalidated, AI-drafted instrument. Used
inconsistently, or by a single assessor relying on impression rather than
evidence, it can produce unlawful adverse impact against people with
protected characteristics under the Equality Act 2010, even unintentionally.
Consistent, evidence-based administration by more than one assessor
materially reduces that risk. Every rating and every note must be about
demonstrated, job-related behaviour only — never about a protected
characteristic, health, a diagnosis, trade union membership, political
opinion, immigration status, criminal record, caring responsibilities or
financial circumstances — and nothing here compares or ranks one candidate
against another; the only comparison is against the framework's own
description of this role level.

**Handle sensitive material with care.** Head of Data Governance carries
organisational risk and works with sensitive datasets in scope. When you
record evidence in `## Assessor notes and evidence log`, describe what the
person did and the judgement they showed — never write down classified
material or live security or risk detail, even where the candidate offers it
as an example.

## Rating scale

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

This is a **proficiency** scale, not a frequency scale: it rates how well the
person performs the skill, on the evidence available, not how often they do
it or how they feel about it.

**Worked example — Data governance.**

- At **1 — Beginner**, someone can describe what a data governance framework
  is and why an organisation needs one, but has not designed or run one
  themselves; they might explain the idea of a RACI matrix in the abstract
  without having assigned data ownership on it.
- At **3 — Advanced**, someone has designed and implemented a data governance
  framework for a team or directorate, working largely independently, and can
  point to policies, standards or compliance processes they put in place and
  maintained.
- At **4 — Expert**, someone has defined the data governance strategy for an
  entire organisation, connected it explicitly to the organisation's wider
  strategy, and can describe how they set the direction that other technical
  leaders and senior stakeholders implement and are held to — the standard
  this role level's own accountabilities describe.

## About this role level

A data governance manager plans, monitors and enforces the management of an
organisation's data assets. Across the role, this means cataloguing and
monitoring data assets essential to the organisation's operations or
services, assigning clear ownership of data through a RACI (responsible,
accountable, consulted and informed) matrix, building governance into routine
processes, supporting or managing data risk processes, advocating for better
use and management of data, and encouraging better data literacy across the
organisation.

Head of Data Governance is the third and most senior of the role's three
levels, above Data governance manager (level 1) and Lead data governance
manager (level 2). Where those levels build and run governance within a team
or directorate, the head of data governance works with the chief data officer
across the whole organisation and, at times, across government, providing
strategic oversight and leadership in every aspect of data governance and
use, and making sure standards stay in line with regulation and ethical
requirements.

At this role level, you will:

- work with the chief data officer to provide strategic oversight of data
  governance throughout the organisation
- set and monitor adherence to data governance policies, frameworks and
  standards
- be accountable for understanding and managing the organisation's data risks
  and issues, and co-ordinate with data owners to accept or resolve them
- act as a senior advisor across government on the best practice of data
  security development, in accordance with the data enterprise architecture
- maintain effective long-term relationships at a senior level with important
  decision-makers and influencers
- advocate for improved data risk management and practices across the
  organisation
- encourage better data literacy and understanding of data governance across
  the organisation

The framework names 13 skills at this role level: Communicating analysis and
insight, Communicating between the technical and non-technical, Data ethics
and privacy, Data governance, Data innovation, Data life cycle, Data literacy
improvement, Data management, Data maturity, Data standards, Enabling and
informing risk-based decisions, Stakeholder relationship management, and
Strategic thinking.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Communicating analysis and insight

**What the framework expects at this level**

At this level, someone communicates relevant, compelling stories using
whichever medium best suits the audience, and makes complex recommendations
at both a tactical and a strategic level.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Communication at Head of Data Governance routinely
operates at both tactical and strategic levels, up to and including advising
the chief data officer and government-facing audiences, so the framework's
own wording sets the bar at mastery rather than competence.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot put together a coherent narrative from a data set or an
  analysis; presents raw findings without a story or a recommendation.
- 1 — Beginner: Can summarise a straightforward analysis for a familiar
  audience, but the story is unclear or the chosen medium does not suit the
  audience, and needs help from someone more senior to make it land.
- 2 — Intermediate: Produces a clear, accurate summary of an analysis for one
  audience, such as their own team, with some coaching on framing and medium.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently builds a compelling narrative from complex
  analysis, chooses the right medium for a directorate-level audience, and
  makes a tactical recommendation that stakeholders act on.
- 4 — Expert: Turns complex, ambiguous analysis into a compelling strategic
  recommendation pitched correctly for a board, the chief data officer or a
  cross-government audience, and can be handed a difficult story — a
  governance failure, a maturity gap — and land it without over- or
  under-stating the risk.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to bring a real board or senior-committee paper they
  wrote, and to explain why they chose that structure and medium for that
  audience.
- In a structured interview, ask them to summarise a piece of complex data
  governance analysis, real or a case study you supply, for two audiences: a
  technical peer and a non-technical board member, and compare the two.
- Ask a referee who has sat on a board or committee with the candidate what
  stayed with them from a presentation the candidate gave.

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What the framework expects at this level**

At this level, someone mediates between people and strengthens relationships,
adopting the right communication method for stakeholders at every level;
manages stakeholder expectations and moderates difficult discussions about
high risk and complex topics, even within constrained timescales; and speaks
on behalf of, and represents the community to, large audiences inside and
outside the organisation.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. This level names moderating high-risk discussions under
time pressure and representing the whole data governance community
externally — both mastery-level activities the two levels below this one are
not asked to do.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Struggles to translate a technical governance issue for a
  non-technical audience, or the reverse; discussions stall or escalate
  rather than resolve.
- 1 — Beginner: Can explain a simple technical point to a non-technical
  colleague one-to-one, with preparation, but avoids high-stakes or
  contested conversations.
- 2 — Intermediate: Mediates routine disagreements between technical and
  non-technical colleagues on their own team, adjusting language for the
  audience with some support.
- 3 — Advanced: Moderates a difficult discussion about a high-risk or complex
  governance topic between stakeholders at different levels, and reaches a
  workable outcome unsupervised.
- 4 — Expert: Is sought out to defuse a genuinely difficult, time-constrained,
  high-risk discussion between senior stakeholders, and represents the
  organisation's data governance position to large internal or external
  audiences, including outside the organisation.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of moderating a high-risk, time-constrained discussion
  between stakeholders who disagreed, and what outcome they reached.
- Ask about a time they represented the organisation externally — a
  conference, a cross-government forum, a public body — and what challenge
  they had to field.
- Ask a referee about a specific difficult conversation the candidate managed
  between technical and non-technical senior stakeholders.

### Skill: Data ethics and privacy

**What the framework expects at this level**

At this level, someone oversees compliance with data ethics standards and
legislation across the organisation; develops, manages and continuously
improves an ethical framework within their area; develops a data ethics
culture and influences others to act on it; and makes sure data ethics is
applied properly when policies and programmes are formulated, implemented and
evaluated.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Overseeing compliance across the whole organisation,
and building an ethical framework and culture rather than only following one,
are organisation-wide leadership accountabilities.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot identify a data ethics or privacy risk in a scenario, or
  applies ethics and legislation inconsistently.
- 1 — Beginner: Recognises an obvious data ethics or privacy issue, such as
  using data outside its stated purpose, once pointed to it, but needs
  guidance on what to do about it.
- 2 — Intermediate: Applies an existing data ethics framework correctly to a
  routine piece of work, with some support on edge cases.
- 3 — Advanced: Reviews and improves a data ethics framework for their own
  area, and can explain to a policy team where a proposed programme falls
  short of it.
- 4 — Expert: Owns compliance with data ethics standards and relevant
  legislation across the whole organisation, has built or substantially
  reshaped the organisation's ethical framework, and has visibly shifted how
  people think about data ethics, not just what they do.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a data ethics framework they built or
  substantially changed, and what changed as a result.
- Present a scenario where a proposed programme is efficient but ethically
  borderline, and ask how they would assess and respond to it.
- Ask a referee for an example of the candidate influencing organisational
  policy on ethical grounds, not just flagging a compliance issue.

### Skill: Data governance

**What the framework expects at this level**

At this level, someone defines the data governance strategy for the
organisation and connects it with the wider organisational strategy; designs,
implements and monitors data governance within a large organisation
throughout the data life cycle; sets the strategy for applying end-to-end
data governance frameworks, processes, operational policies, standards,
compliance and assurance; and engages and works effectively with technical
leaders and senior stakeholders to implement data governance and ensure
compliance.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. This is the role level's core purpose: the summary's
own level description names strategic oversight of data governance
throughout the organisation as this level's defining accountability.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe what a data governance strategy is, or why an
  organisation needs one distinct from individual policies.
- 1 — Beginner: Understands individual governance policies but has not
  connected them into a coherent strategy, or has worked only within a
  strategy someone else designed.
- 2 — Intermediate: Implements and monitors an existing data governance
  framework for a team or system, with guidance on how it fits the wider
  strategy.
- 3 — Advanced: Designs and runs data governance for a large part of the
  organisation across the data life cycle, largely independently.
- 4 — Expert: Has defined a data governance strategy for a whole
  organisation, connected it explicitly to the organisation's wider
  strategy, and set the direction that technical leaders and senior
  stakeholders across the organisation implement and are held to.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for a real data governance strategy document the candidate authored or
  led, and how they connected it to the organisation's wider strategic goals.
- In a structured interview, present a large organisation with fragmented
  data governance and ask how they would set an end-to-end strategy for it.
- Ask a referee, ideally someone in a chief data officer or equivalent role,
  how the candidate's governance strategy work changed outcomes.

### Skill: Data innovation

**What the framework expects at this level**

At this level, someone investigates emerging trends in data-related
approaches, performs horizon-scanning for the organisation, and introduces
innovative ways of working.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. The framework asks this level to scan and introduce
new approaches, not to have already made the organisation a recognised
innovator, so Advanced — independent, credible horizon-scanning that leads to
real change — is the fair baseline rather than Expert.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Unaware of emerging trends in data governance or data management
  approaches relevant to their organisation.
- 1 — Beginner: Can name a trend or emerging approach they have read about,
  but has not assessed its relevance or tried it.
- 2 — Intermediate: Investigates an emerging approach and reports on its
  relevance to the organisation, with support in judging priority.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently horizon-scans, identifies which emerging
  approaches matter for the organisation, and introduces at least one new way
  of working as a result.
- 4 — Expert: Is a recognised source of horizon-scanning insight beyond their
  own organisation, and has introduced innovations that other teams or
  organisations have gone on to adopt.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of an emerging data governance or data management
  approach the candidate identified and introduced, and what changed.
- Ask how they keep up with developments in the field, and to name something
  they investigated and decided not to adopt, and why.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate has been a source of new ideas rather
  than only implementing others' ideas.

### Skill: Data life cycle

**What the framework expects at this level**

At this level, someone takes responsibility for data governance structures
and principles, and makes sure they are defined and applied with best
practice throughout a data life cycle; and directs continuous improvements to
optimise data governance over a data life cycle.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Taking responsibility and directing continuous
improvements across the whole data life cycle is an organisation-wide
ownership accountability, distinct from applying life cycle practice within
one system.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe the stages of a data life cycle or where
  governance applies at each stage.
- 1 — Beginner: Can describe the data life cycle in general terms, but has
  not applied governance consistently across its stages.
- 2 — Intermediate: Applies governance structures at specific stages of a
  data life cycle for a system or team, with guidance on the stages they have
  not covered.
- 3 — Advanced: Ensures governance structures and best practice are applied
  consistently across the whole data life cycle for a significant part of
  the organisation.
- 4 — Expert: Owns the organisation's data governance structures and
  principles across the entire data life cycle, and directs continuous
  improvement, able to point to a specific change they drove that measurably
  optimised governance over the life cycle.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of a continuous improvement they directed that changed
  how data was governed across its life cycle, and how they measured the
  effect.
- Present a scenario with a gap in governance at one stage of the life cycle,
  for example disposal or archiving, and ask how they would identify and
  close it organisation-wide.
- Review a governance framework document they authored for whether it
  addresses the full life cycle or only part of it.

### Skill: Data literacy improvement

**What the framework expects at this level**

At this level, someone takes responsibility for improving the organisation's
data literacy; encourages a data-driven culture by strengthening data
literacy skills among all employees; and persuades senior decision-makers

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Taking organisation-wide responsibility for literacy,

- Ask how they would measure whether an organisation's data literacy had

### Skill: Data maturity

At this level, someone oversees the implementation of the organisation's data

### Skill: Enabling and informing risk-based decisions

**What the framework expects at this level**

At this level, someone acts as a point of escalation; is trusted by senior
risk owners as an expert in security; and applies risk methodologies at the
most complex levels of risk.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "The most complex levels of risk" and being the named
escalation point is the explicit ceiling of this skill in the framework's own
wording.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot apply a risk methodology to a data governance scenario, or
  defers every risk judgement upward.
- 1 — Beginner: Applies a simple, well-defined risk methodology to a routine
  scenario, with supervision.
- 2 — Intermediate: Assesses moderate-complexity data risks and proposes a
  course of action, with some guidance from a more senior colleague.
- 3 — Advanced: Is trusted to assess and act on genuinely difficult data risk
  scenarios independently, without needing to escalate.
- 4 — Expert: Is the named escalation point when others cannot resolve a
  risk, is trusted by senior risk owners as the expert, and applies risk
  methodologies at the most complex levels — several interacting risks,
  incomplete information, organisation-wide consequences.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of a genuinely complex risk scenario escalated to them,
  and how they reasoned through it.
- Ask a referee who is a senior risk owner whether they have relied on the
  candidate's judgement on a difficult data risk decision.
- Present a scenario with several interacting, incomplete-information risks,
  and ask them to talk through how they would decide and who they would
  involve.

### Skill: Stakeholder relationship management

**What the framework expects at this level**

At this level, someone directs the stakeholder relationship strategy for
their teams; makes sure stakeholders' objectives are set and supports teams
to meet them; and influences and negotiates with senior stakeholders to
resolve issues and enable progress.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Directing a stakeholder relationship strategy and
negotiating with senior stakeholders to resolve issues is a senior leadership
accountability, consistent with the level description's "effective long-term
relationships at a senior level with important decision-makers and
influencers".

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has no consistent approach to managing stakeholder relationships;

  progress.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of a difficult negotiation with a senior stakeholder

- Ask how they set and track stakeholders' objectives across several

- Ask a referee, ideally a senior stakeholder the candidate has worked with

  of work to organisational strategy.
- 1 — Beginner: Understands the organisation's strategy when it is
  explained, but has not contributed to shaping it.
- 2 — Intermediate: Contributes ideas to strategy or policy discussions, and
  evaluates whether a specific initiative meets business requirements, with
  guidance.
- 3 — Advanced: Leads the design of strategy or policy for a significant part
  of the organisation, largely independently.
- 4 — Expert: Leads the design and implementation of strategy at
  organisational scale, and directs the evaluation of strategies and
  policies to confirm business requirements are actually being met, not just
  that the strategy was written.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for a strategy or policy document the candidate led the design of, and
  how they evaluated whether it met business requirements after
  implementation.
- Present a scenario where a strategy is not delivering its intended
  outcomes, and ask how they would diagnose and respond.
- Ask a referee for an example of the candidate directing an evaluation that
  led to a strategy or policy being changed.
