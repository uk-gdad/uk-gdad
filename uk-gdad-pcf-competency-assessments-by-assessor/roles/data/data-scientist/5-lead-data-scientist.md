# Lead Data Scientist - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This assessment is for an assessor — a line manager, a hiring panel member, or
a reviewer — judging whether a candidate or an employee meets the baseline
abilities required for the Data role of Data scientist, at the Lead data
scientist role level. It measures demonstrated skill against the UK GDAD
Profession Capability Framework's own description of this role level, for a
hiring decision, a promotion board, onboarding, or a capability check.

At this level, a lead data scientist is a leader, coach and champion of data
science, with responsibility for managing and developing teams, building
organisational capability, and setting and communicating direction across an
organisation.

This assessment is one input among several into a decision, never the whole of
it. Read `## Advisory` before using it.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating on this
form must never be the sole basis for a hiring, promotion, pay, performance or
disciplinary decision. Before using it, consult your organisation's human
resources, people development, occupational psychology or legal compliance
colleagues.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, then gather whatever
evidence exists: a CV or portfolio, a structured interview, a work sample or
task (for example, reviewing a scalable data science product the candidate
took responsibility for delivering), direct observation, or references from
people they have led, coached or worked alongside.

**Score on evidence, not impression.** Every rating must trace to something
the person did, said, produced, or was observed doing. If you cannot point to
evidence for a rating, record it as unknown rather than guess.

**Consistency.** Score every candidate for this role level the same way. For a
hiring or promotion decision, have more than one assessor score independently
before comparing notes.

**Fairness.** Every rating and every piece of evidence you gather must be
about demonstrated, job-related behaviour — see
[Fair and lawful use](../../../../uk-gdad-pcf-competency-assessments-by-assessor/spec/index.md#fair-and-lawful-use)
in the project specification. An unvalidated, AI-drafted instrument used
inconsistently can produce unlawful adverse impact; consistent, evidence-based
administration by more than one assessor materially reduces that risk. Never
let a rating be coloured by anything other than what the person actually did,
said, or produced.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix below is rated on the same five-point
scale. This is a **proficiency** scale — it rates how well the person does the
thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it or how they feel
about it.

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

**Worked example — Developing data science capability.** The framework expects
a lead data scientist to "set the direction of CPD (continuous professional
development) within your team" and to act "as a leader or technical
specialist, providing detailed support and guidance within the organisation
and helping colleagues to develop skills". A candidate who manages their own
CPD well, and occasionally helps one colleague when asked, is showing evidence
towards **2 — Intermediate**, below this level's baseline. A candidate who
regularly provides detailed support and guidance to colleagues developing
their skills, and can confidently explain the benefits of data science to a
potential customer, is showing **3 — Advanced** — close to this level's
baseline but not yet all of it. A candidate who, beyond that, has set the CPD
direction for a whole team — deciding what the team needs to learn next and
why, and matching new developments in the field to real opportunities in the
organisation — is showing **4 — Expert**, the baseline this skill sets at this
level.

## About this role level

The Data profession's Data scientist role explores and applies statistical
tools and techniques — including machine learning and predictive analytics —
to existing and new data, finding patterns and turning them into
organisational insight. Across the role, a data scientist is inquisitive;
explores and visualises data; makes recommendations that address complex
problems and inform strategic and operational decision-making; uses data
ethically and appropriately; is innovative and adaptable; and works, often as
part of a multidisciplinary team, to use data and analytics in support of
organisational goals.

The role has six levels, in order: Trainee data scientist, Associate data
scientist, Data scientist, Principal data scientist, Lead data scientist, and
Head of data science. This assessment is for the fifth of them.

A **lead data scientist** is a leader, coach and champion of data science.
They are responsible for managing and developing teams, for building
organisational capability, and for setting and communicating direction. At
this role level, they will have experience identifying opportunities to
deploy extensive knowledge of data science techniques, and in challenging the
status quo; design and implement scalable data science outputs and products,
driving collaboration with others in support of organisational objectives;
select the most appropriate tools and technologies; navigate ethical and data
challenges; communicate and present effectively; and challenge delivery plans
and priorities.

The framework names eight skills for this role level: Applied maths,
statistics and scientific practices; Data engineering; Data ethics and
privacy; Data science innovation; Delivering business impact; Developing data
science capability; Programming and build (data science); and Understanding
product delivery.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Applied maths, statistics and scientific practices

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead data scientist is expected to identify opportunities to develop
statistical insight, reports and models that support organisational
objectives, collaborating effectively across the organisation to do so; to
critique statistical analyses; to use a variety of data analytics techniques —
such as data mining, and prescriptive and predictive analytics — for complex
analysis through the whole data life cycle; to use model outputs to produce
evidence that helps design services and policies; and to understand a broad
range of statistical tools, particularly those the organisation uses, well
enough to use them appropriately and to help other people use them too.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. The framework's own wording closes this skill on "help
others to use them" — teaching people across the organisation to use
statistical tools, on top of independently using model outputs to design
services and policies, is full mastery applied to guiding others, not solely
applying the skill oneself.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot use a statistical technique to analyse a real
  organisational data set without step-by-step direction.
- 1 — Beginner: Runs a statistical technique on a well-specified problem under
  supervision, and needs help interpreting what the output means for the
  organisation.
- 2 — Intermediate: Independently runs standard analyses and produces a
  report, but needs support to critique someone else's statistical work or to
  choose confidently between competing techniques.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently critiques a colleague's statistical analysis,
  selects and applies an appropriate technique for a complex, whole-life-cycle
  problem, and uses the resulting model output to help design a service or a
  policy.
- 4 — Expert: Is sought out across the organisation to teach or advise others
  on which statistical tool fits a problem, and their model outputs routinely
  shape organisational-level services or policy.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to critique a piece of statistical analysis that is not
  their own and to say what they would change and why.
- Ask them to describe a time a model output they produced directly shaped the
  design of a service or a policy.
- Ask a referee about a time the candidate helped a colleague, outside their
  immediate team, learn to use a statistical tool properly.

- [ ] 1 — Beginner

### Skill: Data engineering

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead data scientist is expected to help identify the data engineering
requirements for a data science product, working with data engineers and
other data scientists to design and deliver those products into the
organisation effectively; to understand the need to cleanse and prepare data
before it goes into a data science product, and to put reusable processes and
checks in place; and to understand a broad range of architectures — cloud and
on-premise — and the data manipulation and transformation tools the
organisation uses, well enough to use them appropriately and help other people
do the same.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. This skill's own bullets frame the lead data
scientist as working alongside dedicated data engineers to identify
requirements, rather than owning data engineering practice outright — a
strong, independent grasp that puts reusable checks in place and spans more
than one architecture, without the organisation-wide standard-setting the
framework gives explicitly to other skills at this level.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe what data preparation a data science product would
  need before it could be built reliably.
- 1 — Beginner: Identifies an obvious data quality problem once it is pointed
  out, but needs help specifying the engineering work required to fix it.
- 2 — Intermediate: Works with a data engineer to specify requirements for a
  straightforward product, with review support.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently identifies the data engineering requirements for
  a complex, multi-source product, sets up reusable cleansing and quality
  checks, and works confidently across more than one architecture the
  organisation uses.
- 4 — Expert: Is the reference point other teams call on for how data
  engineering and data science should fit together across the organisation.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe the data engineering requirements they
  identified for a specific data science product, and how they worked with
  data engineers to get it delivered.
- Ask about a reusable data quality check or process they put in place, and
  what problem it went on to prevent.
- Ask which architectures — cloud, on-premise, or both — they have worked
  across, and for an example of a choice they made between them.

### Skill: Data ethics and privacy

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead data scientist is expected to oversee compliance with data ethics
standards and legislation across the organisation; to develop, manage and
continuously improve an ethical framework within their area; to develop a
data ethics culture and influence other people to act on it; and to ensure
data ethics is applied appropriately when their organisation formulates,
implements and evaluates policies and programmes.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Overseeing compliance across the whole organisation,
owning and improving an ethical framework, and building a culture that
influences how other people behave, is organisation-wide leadership of this
skill, not independent application of it.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot identify an ethical or privacy concern in a data science
  product without it being pointed out.
- 1 — Beginner: Recognises a flagged ethical or privacy concern and follows
  existing guidance to address it, with support.
- 2 — Intermediate: Identifies ethical and privacy concerns in their own work
  and raises them with stakeholders appropriately.
- 3 — Advanced: Owns and improves an ethical framework for their own area, and
  constructively challenges a proposed policy or programme on data ethics
  grounds.
- 4 — Expert: Oversees data ethics compliance across the whole organisation,
  and has visibly shifted how colleagues outside their own team think and act
  on data ethics.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe the ethical framework they own or have
  improved, and one specific change they made to it and why.
- Ask them to describe a time they influenced how a policy or a programme
  handled data ethics, beyond their own immediate work.
- Ask a referee about a time the candidate changed how a colleague, or a team
  other than their own, approached a data ethics or privacy question.

### Skill: Data science innovation

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead data scientist is expected to be a leader in the data science space;
to have in-depth knowledge of data science tools and techniques, using them to
solve problems creatively and to create opportunities for their team; to act
as a coach, inspiring curiosity and creativity in others; to keep up to date
with changes in the data science profession; and to challenge the status quo,
always looking for ways to improve data science.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "Act as a coach, inspiring curiosity and creativity in
others" is, in this scale's own terms, teaching and guiding people directly,
and sits alongside being named a leader in the field — full mastery applied
to developing other people's thinking, not only the candidate's own.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Applies a data science technique only in the way it was
  originally taught, with no evidence of adapting it creatively.
- 1 — Beginner: Recognises when an established technique might be adapted for
  a new problem, with support in working out how.
- 2 — Intermediate: Adapts a known technique to a moderately novel problem
  independently, but has not yet led others through the same kind of
  thinking.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently and creatively solves a genuinely new problem
  with no established template, and keeps demonstrably current with the
  field.
- 4 — Expert: Actively coaches other data scientists to be more curious and
  creative, creates opportunities for their team by spotting where a
  technique applies, and is recognised as someone who challenges and improves
  how the organisation does data science.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a genuinely new problem they solved
  creatively, with no established technique to follow, and how they arrived
  at their approach.
- Ask them to describe a time they coached someone to be more curious or
  creative, and what changed for that person afterwards.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate is someone others go to when a data
  science approach needs rethinking, and for a specific example.

### Skill: Delivering business impact

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead data scientist is expected to champion the role of data science within
the organisation; to understand and champion user research, and to design and
manage processes to gather and establish user needs; to identify and create
opportunities to develop and deliver data science products that support
organisational objectives, collaborating across the organisation to fulfil
meaningful goals; and to take responsibility for delivering scalable data
science products into the organisation, and for establishing their
maintenance support.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. This skill's bullets describe accountable delivery
leadership — designing the process for establishing user needs, and taking
personal responsibility for a product's delivery and its ongoing maintenance
support — a strong, independent grasp exercised across the organisation,
without the explicit coaching or standard-setting language the framework
gives to skills such as Data ethics and privacy or Developing data science
capability at this level.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot connect a data science product to a specific
  organisational objective it is meant to support.
- 1 — Beginner: Contributes to a data science product with a clear business
  purpose, under someone else's direction.
- 2 — Intermediate: Identifies an opportunity to apply data science to an
  organisational objective and delivers a bounded piece of it, with review
  support.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently champions data science's role in the
  organisation, designs the process for establishing user needs for a
  product, and takes personal responsibility for delivering it at scale and
  arranging its maintenance support.
- 4 — Expert: Is the person other parts of the organisation come to when they
  need data science to change how an entire organisational area operates.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to walk through a data science product they took
  responsibility for delivering at scale, including how they arranged its
  maintenance support afterwards.
- Ask them to describe how they designed a process to gather and establish
  user needs for a product, and what it changed about the result.
- Ask a referee about a time the candidate championed data science to a
  sceptical or unfamiliar part of the organisation, and what happened.

### Skill: Developing data science capability

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead data scientist is expected to act as a leader or a technical
specialist, providing detailed support and guidance within the organisation
and helping colleagues develop their skills; to set the direction of
continuous professional development (CPD) within their team; to keep up to
date with new developments in data science and match them to opportunities in
their organisation; to talk confidently about the benefits of data science
approaches to existing and potential customers; to have an in-depth
understanding of a wide range of data science techniques — such as machine
learning and natural language processing — and detailed knowledge of at least
one specialism; and to use these techniques to build data science solutions,
including reports, models and dashboards.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "Set the direction of CPD within your team" is
direction-setting for other people's development, not just contributing to
it, and combined with "detailed knowledge of at least one specialism" this is
full mastery deployed to guide the team's growth as a whole.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has no active plan for their own development and has not helped
  anyone else develop their data science skills.
- 1 — Beginner: Follows a development plan set by someone else, and has
  occasionally helped a colleague informally.
- 2 — Intermediate: Manages their own CPD and helps an individual colleague
  develop a specific skill when asked.
- 3 — Advanced: Provides detailed support and guidance to colleagues
  developing their skills, and confidently explains the benefits of data
  science approaches to people outside the team, including potential
  customers.
- 4 — Expert: Sets the direction of CPD for the whole team, matches emerging
  developments in data science to real opportunities in the organisation, and
  has detailed, specialist-level knowledge that other data scientists draw
  on.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe the CPD direction they set for a team, and
  how they decided what the team needed to learn.
- Ask them to explain, as if to a potential customer, why a data science
  approach would help with a described problem.
- Ask about their specialism, and for an example of a data science solution —
  a report, a model, or a dashboard — they built using it.

### Skill: Programming and build (data science)

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead data scientist is expected to write complex programs and scripts; to
seek to make code open source where appropriate; to supervise junior analysts
and set coding standards for their team; to understand software architecture
and how to write efficient, optimised code; and to perform user testing on
products before launch.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "Supervise junior analysts and set coding standards for
your team" is, in this scale's own terms, teaching and guiding others
directly, sitting alongside independently writing complex, optimised
programs — full mastery applied to setting the standard the team works to.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot write a program of any complexity without significant
  support.
- 1 — Beginner: Writes and tests simple scripts under direction, and follows
  coding standards someone else has set.
- 2 — Intermediate: Writes moderately complex programs independently, but
  needs support to judge software architecture trade-offs or to review
  someone else's code.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently writes complex, efficient, optimised programs,
  considers making code open source where it fits, and reviews a colleague's
  code against existing standards.
- 4 — Expert: Sets the coding standards their team works to, supervises
  junior analysts' code and practice, and makes software architecture
  decisions other people rely on.

**Evidence to gather**

- Review a code sample the candidate wrote or led, and ask them to explain a
  software architecture or efficiency decision in it.
- Ask about the coding standards they set or maintain for their team, and how
  they got the team to adopt them.
- Ask a referee, or a junior colleague if appropriate, about the candidate's
  experience supervising and reviewing others' code.
- Ask how they have approached user testing a product before launch.

### Skill: Understanding product delivery

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead data scientist is expected to understand the differences between
delivery methods, such as Agile and waterfall, and to set out how their team
should use and adapt them; to lead a team through the different phases of the
product delivery life cycle; to collaborate with the product manager to
influence the direction of work; to identify and involve the relevant teams
needed to deliver data science products smoothly into the organisation, so
those products inform decision-making; to ensure products are monitored,
maintained and continually improved, engaging and working with other people
where necessary; and to have oversight of any data science features
implemented within products or services.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "Set out how your team should use and adapt these
methods" and "lead a team through the different phases of the product
delivery life cycle" are both direction-setting for other people, not just
delivering within a method someone else chose — full mastery of delivery
applied to leading a team through it.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot explain why one delivery method might suit a product
  better than another.
- 1 — Beginner: Follows a delivery method chosen by someone else, with
  guidance on how it applies to their work.
- 2 — Intermediate: Contributes to a team's delivery of a product through
  more than one phase of its life cycle, with review support.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently leads a defined phase of a product's delivery,
  working effectively with the product manager and the other teams involved.
- 4 — Expert: Sets out how their team should use and adapt delivery methods,
  leads the team through the full product delivery life cycle, and keeps
  oversight of a product after launch to make sure it is monitored,
  maintained and continually improved.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a product they led a team through, from an
  early phase of its life cycle to a later one, and a decision they made
  about method along the way.
- Ask them to describe how they worked with a product manager to influence
  the direction of a piece of work.
- Ask a referee about the candidate's oversight of a product after launch —
  how they made sure it kept being monitored, maintained and improved.
