# Head of Data Engineering - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This assessment is for an assessor — a line manager, a hiring panel member, or
a reviewer — judging whether a candidate or an existing employee possesses the
baseline abilities the UK GDAD Profession Capability Framework sets out for
the **Head of data engineering** level of the **Data engineer** role. It is
built for a hiring decision, a promotion board, onboarding into the role, or a
periodic capability check against the framework, and it is completed by the
assessor, not by the person being assessed.

Head of data engineering is a senior leadership level: the framework describes
someone who leads multi-functional delivery teams, builds engineering
capability across a community, and works with other senior colleagues to plan
and deliver data services for their department, other government departments,
and private sector partners. The matrix below turns the framework's own
description of that level into ten skills, each with a baseline and
observable indicators, so that a rating can be traced back to evidence rather
than to impression.

This assessment is one input among several into a wider decision, never the
whole of it. Read `## Advisory` below before using it, and keep it in view
throughout.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` before you score anything, so
the framework's own description of the level is fresh in mind. Then gather
whatever evidence exists before you begin: a CV or portfolio, a structured
interview, a work sample or task, direct observation of the person doing the
job, and references. The more of these you have, the more confidently you can
rate.

**Scoring on evidence, not impression.** Every rating must trace back to
something the person did, said, produced, or was observed doing. If you
cannot point to evidence for a rating, record it as unknown rather than
estimate it from general impression, seniority elsewhere, or how the
conversation felt.

**Consistency.** Score the same way for every candidate or employee assessed
against this role level. Where possible, have more than one assessor score
independently and compare notes afterwards, particularly for a hiring or
promotion decision — independent scoring surfaces disagreement that a single
assessor's impression would hide.

**Fairness.** This is an unvalidated, AI-drafted instrument. Used
inconsistently, or scored on anything other than demonstrated, job-related
evidence, it can produce unlawful adverse impact. Consistent, evidence-based
administration, ideally by more than one assessor, materially reduces that
risk — see [Fair and lawful use](../../../../uk-gdad-pcf-competency-assessments-by-assessor/spec/index.md#fair-and-lawful-use) in the project
specification for what this rules out.

**Sensitive systems.** A head of data engineering works with data services
that may touch classified material, personal data, or systems whose exact
configuration is itself sensitive. When you complete the evidence log below,
record what the person demonstrated — for example, "designed an access
control model for a shared data platform" — and never the classified detail
itself, a live vulnerability, or a security weakness discussed as evidence.
The evidence log is not the place to hold that information.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point scale.
This is a **proficiency** scale: it rates how well the person does the thing,
on the evidence available, not how often they do it or how they feel about
doing it.

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

**Worked example — Data modelling.** The framework expects a head of data
engineering to explain the concepts and principles of data modelling, produce
and maintain models for the organisation's needs, and reverse-engineer a model
from a live system.

- **Rating 2 (Intermediate)** might look like: the person can walk through an
  existing entity-relationship model and explain what each part represents,
  and can build a simple model with guidance from a more experienced
  colleague, but has not independently reverse-engineered a model from an
  unfamiliar live system.
- **Rating 3 (Advanced)** might look like: the person has independently
  produced and maintained data models for at least one organisation's live
  systems, and can reverse-engineer a model from a system they did not build,
  working alone.
- **Rating 4 (Expert)** might look like: everything at rating 3, plus the
  person has taught data modelling concepts to others — for example, running
  a walkthrough for a team new to a domain, or reviewing and correcting
  colleagues' models against modelling principles they can articulate clearly.

## About this role level

**The role.** A data engineer develops and constructs data products and
services, and integrates them into systems and business processes. That one
sentence covers every level of the role, from someone new to the discipline
through to its most senior practitioner.

**The role's levels.** The framework defines four levels for data engineer, in
order: data engineer, senior data engineer, lead data engineer, and — the
level this assessment covers — head of data engineering. Head of data
engineering sits at the top of that ladder. Where the levels below it build
and deliver data services directly, this level is defined by leading the
teams that do that work, building the capability of the wider engineering
community, and representing that community both inside and outside the
organisation.

**This level.** A head of data engineering leads multi-functional delivery
teams to deliver robust data services for their department, other government
departments, and private sector partners. The framework names three
accountabilities at this level:

- inspire best practice for data products and services within your teams
- build data engineering capability by providing technical leadership and
  career development for the community
- work with other senior team members to identify, plan, develop, and deliver
  data services

**The skills this level names.** The framework names ten skills at this level:
communicating between the technical and non-technical, data analysis and
synthesis, data development process, data innovation, data integration
design, data modelling, metadata management, problem management, programming
and build (data and analytics engineering), and testing. Each is set out in
full in the competency matrix below.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a head of data engineering to mediate between people and
strengthen relationships, adopting the communication method that suits
stakeholders at every level. It expects them to manage stakeholder
expectations and moderate difficult discussions about high-risk, complex
topics, even under constrained timescales. It also expects them to speak on
behalf of, and represent, the data engineering community to large audiences
inside and outside the organisation.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. The framework has this level representing the community
externally and moderating high-risk discussions under time pressure — that is
mastery exercised on behalf of others, not communication carried out with
support.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe a recent example of translating a technical
  data engineering issue for a non-technical stakeholder, or has never been
  asked to.
- 1 — Beginner: Can explain a technical topic to a non-technical colleague
  one-to-one when prompted, but needs help judging what to leave out and finds
  disagreement hard to navigate.
- 2 — Intermediate: Adapts explanations to different audiences without
  prompting, and can manage a straightforward stakeholder disagreement with
  guidance from a more senior colleague.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently moderates a difficult conversation about a
  high-risk or complex data topic, keeping it constructive under a tight
  deadline, and manages the stakeholder relationship afterwards.
- 4 — Expert: Represents the data engineering community to a large internal
  or external audience — for example, presenting at a departmental board or a
  cross-government forum — and is sought out by colleagues to mediate the
  hardest stakeholder conversations.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for a specific example of moderating a disagreement between technical
  and non-technical stakeholders about a high-risk data decision, and what
  the person did differently in the room to keep it constructive.
- Ask a referee whether they have seen this person represent the data
  engineering function to an audience outside their immediate team, and how
  that went.
- Observe the person explaining a live data architecture or integration
  decision to a panel that includes at least one non-technical member.

### Skill: Data analysis and synthesis

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a head of data engineering to understand a range of
data profiling techniques and help teams apply them, to analyse a complex
single source system, and to bring multiple data sources together into a
conformed model for analysis.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. The framework's wording at this level is about
helping teams apply the technique and working with a complex source, which is
independent practice extended into coaching others — advanced rather than a
distinct organisation-wide mastery claim.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe profiling a data source or combining more than
  one source into a single model for analysis.
- 1 — Beginner: Has profiled a simple, well-documented data source with close
  supervision, but has not combined multiple sources into one model.
- 2 — Intermediate: Profiles a single complex source independently, and can
  combine two well-understood sources into a conformed model with guidance.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently analyses a complex single source system and
  brings multiple, differently structured sources together into a conformed
  model fit for analysis.
- 4 — Expert: Everything at rating 3, plus actively coaches other engineers
  through profiling and source analysis on a live piece of work, and their
  approach is adopted by the team as the way to do it.

**Evidence to gather**

- Give a work sample: a description of two dissimilar data sources (for
  example, a legacy relational database and a semi-structured API feed) and
  ask the person to describe how they would profile and conform them.
- Ask for a real example of analysing a complex single source system, and
  what made it complex.
- Ask a referee whether they have seen this person help another engineer
  improve their own data profiling or synthesis approach.

### Skill: Data development process

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a head of data engineering to establish enterprise-scale
data integration procedures across the whole data development life cycle, and
to ensure teams adhere to them. It also expects them to manage resources so
that data services work effectively at enterprise level.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "Establish" and "ensure teams adhere to" are the
language of setting the standard for an enterprise, not following one — this
is mastery exercised across multiple teams.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe a data development process they have followed, let
  alone one they have set.
- 1 — Beginner: Follows a data development process set by someone else, with
  supervision, and cannot yet say why each stage exists.
- 2 — Intermediate: Follows an established process independently and can
  suggest small improvements to it within their own team.
- 3 — Advanced: Defines a data integration procedure for their own team and
  gets the team to adopt it consistently.
- 4 — Expert: Establishes a data development process at enterprise scale,
  spanning more than one team, and puts in place a way of checking that teams
  actually adhere to it — for example, a review gate, an audit, or a
  standards board.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for a document — a process definition, a standard, or a runbook — the
  person wrote or substantially owns, and how it is enforced across more than
  one team.
- Ask a structured interview question: "Tell me about a data development
  process you established that other teams had to adopt. How did you get
  buy-in, and how do you know it is being followed?"
- Ask a referee how resources were managed across data services at
  enterprise level under this person's direction.

### Skill: Data innovation

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a head of data engineering to investigate emerging
trends in data-related approaches, perform horizon-scanning for the
organisation, and introduce innovative ways of working.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Horizon-scanning "for the organisation" and introducing
new ways of working are organisation-level activities that only someone with
full command of the discipline is positioned to lead.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot name a recent development in data engineering practice or
  describe how they keep up with the field.
- 1 — Beginner: Follows news or updates about data engineering practice, but
  has not yet connected any of it to a change at work.
- 2 — Intermediate: Brings a new technique or tool to their own team's
  attention and helps the team try it out.
- 3 — Advanced: Runs a structured horizon-scan — for example, evaluating an
  emerging approach against the organisation's needs — and writes up a
  recommendation.
- 4 — Expert: Has introduced an innovative way of working that changed
  practice across more than one team or across the organisation, and can
  explain how they judged it was worth the change.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of an emerging data engineering trend the person
  investigated, and what recommendation, if any, came out of it.
- Ask a structured interview question: "Describe a time you introduced a new
  way of working in data engineering. What did you change, and how did you
  know it had worked?"
- Ask a referee to describe the organisational impact of an innovation this
  person introduced, if any.

### Skill: Data integration design

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a head of data engineering to establish data
integration standards, keep them up to date, and ensure adherence to them,
and to keep abreast of best practice in industry and across government.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Establishing and maintaining the standard, and holding
others to it, is the defining leadership activity of this skill at this
level, not a task carried out under someone else's standard.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe a data integration standard they have used or
  contributed to.
- 1 — Beginner: Applies an existing integration standard when told to, with
  supervision.
- 2 — Intermediate: Applies an integration standard independently and flags
  where it does not fit a particular case.
- 3 — Advanced: Contributes substantively to defining or revising an
  integration standard for their own team or service.
- 4 — Expert: Owns an integration standard that other teams are expected to
  follow, keeps it current against industry and government practice, and can
  point to how they check adherence.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask to review a data integration standard or guidance document the person
  owns or has substantially shaped.
- Ask a structured interview question: "How do you keep a data integration
  standard current, and how do you find out about relevant practice
  elsewhere in government or industry?"
- Ask a referee for an example of the person enforcing an integration
  standard when another team wanted to deviate from it.

### Skill: Data modelling

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a head of data engineering to explain the concepts and
principles of data modelling, to produce, maintain, and update relevant data
models for an organisation's specific needs, and to reverse-engineer data
models from a live system.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. The framework's wording here stays close to the
craft — producing, maintaining, and reverse-engineering models — rather than
setting an organisation-wide modelling standard, so it calls for strong
independent practice rather than an organisation-level mastery claim.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe the difference between, for example, a
  conceptual, logical, and physical data model.
- 1 — Beginner: Can follow an existing data model and make a small,
  supervised change to it.
- 2 — Intermediate: Produces a straightforward data model for a well
  understood need, with guidance from a more experienced colleague.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently produces, maintains, and updates data models
  for the organisation's specific needs, and can reverse-engineer a model
  from a live system they did not design.
- 4 — Expert: Everything at rating 3, plus teaches data modelling concepts
  and principles to others — for example, reviewing colleagues' models
  against clearly articulated principles, or running a session for engineers
  new to the discipline.

**Evidence to gather**

- Give a work sample: a description of a live system with no documented
  model, and ask the person to describe how they would reverse-engineer one.
- Ask to review a data model the person produced or substantially maintains,
  and the reasoning behind its design.
- Ask a referee whether they have seen this person explain data modelling
  concepts to someone less experienced.

### Skill: Metadata management

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a head of data engineering to identify how metadata
repositories can support different areas of the organisation, to communicate
the value of metadata repositories, and to set up robust governance processes
to keep repositories up to date.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Identifying support across "different areas of the
organisation" and setting up governance processes are organisation-wide,
ongoing responsibilities, not a single team's task.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe what a metadata repository is used for, or has
  never worked with one.
- 1 — Beginner: Uses an existing metadata repository as instructed, without
  yet understanding why it is structured the way it is.
- 2 — Intermediate: Maintains entries in a metadata repository for their own
  team's data and can explain their purpose to a colleague.
- 3 — Advanced: Identifies a new area of the organisation that would benefit
  from metadata repository support, and makes the case for it.
- 4 — Expert: Has set up or substantially reformed a governance process that
  keeps a metadata repository up to date across more than one team, and can
  describe how it is sustained once they step back.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of a metadata governance process the person designed or
  put in place, and how it is kept current.
- Ask a structured interview question: "Describe a time you had to persuade
  a part of the organisation that did not already value metadata management
  to invest in it. What did you say, and did it work?"
- Ask a referee to describe the state of metadata governance before and
  after this person's involvement.

### Skill: Problem management

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a head of data engineering to anticipate problems and
defend against them at the right time, to understand how a problem fits into
the larger picture, to identify and describe problems and help others describe
them, and to build problem-solving capabilities in others.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "Build problem-solving capabilities in others" is
explicitly a teaching and capability-building accountability, which the
rating scale places at expert.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe a problem they identified and worked through
  before it caused an incident.
- 1 — Beginner: Recognises a problem when it is pointed out, with support in
  working out what to do about it.
- 2 — Intermediate: Identifies and describes a problem independently, and
  can place it in the context of the immediate piece of work.
- 3 — Advanced: Anticipates a problem before it materialises and acts to
  defend against it, and can explain how the problem connects to the wider
  service or organisation.
- 4 — Expert: Everything at rating 3, plus has visibly built problem-solving
  capability in others — for example, coaching a colleague through
  structuring an ambiguous problem, or establishing a way the team surfaces
  and works through problems together.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of a problem the person anticipated and headed off
  before it became an incident, and how they judged the timing.
- Ask a structured interview question: "Tell me about a time you helped a
  colleague get better at identifying or describing a problem. What did you
  do, and what changed?"
- Ask a referee for an example of this person connecting a local problem to
  a larger organisational picture that others had missed.

### Skill: Programming and build (data and analytics engineering)

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a head of data engineering to set local or team-based
standards for programming tools and techniques and to select appropriate
development methods, to advise on the application of standards and methods
and ensure compliance, and to take technical responsibility for all stages
and iterations of a software development project, providing method-specific
technical advice and guidance to project stakeholders.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Setting standards, ensuring compliance, and holding
technical responsibility across "all stages and iterations" of a project is
full technical ownership exercised over other people's work, not solely one's
own.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe setting a programming standard or taking
  technical responsibility for a project beyond their own code.
- 1 — Beginner: Follows programming standards set by someone else, with
  supervision on non-trivial changes.
- 2 — Intermediate: Applies programming standards independently on their own
  work and can explain the reasoning behind them.
- 3 — Advanced: Sets a programming or build standard for their own team and
  advises colleagues on applying it.
- 4 — Expert: Holds technical responsibility across the full lifecycle of a
  build — from design through to iteration — provides method-specific
  advice to stakeholders, and checks that standards are actually being
  complied with, not just published.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask to review a programming or build standard the person authored or owns,
  and how they check compliance with it.
- Give a work sample: a short design decision on a data pipeline or build
  process, and ask the person to advise a hypothetical stakeholder on the
  method and its trade-offs.
- Ask a referee whether they have seen this person take technical
  responsibility for a project end to end, including where it went wrong.

### Skill: Testing

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a head of data engineering to review requirements and
specifications and define test conditions, to identify issues and risks
associated with work, and to analyse and report test activities and results.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. The framework's wording here stays at the level of
reviewing, defining, and reporting on testing for a piece of work, rather than
setting a testing standard across teams, so it calls for strong independent
practice rather than the organisation-wide mastery seen in some of this
level's other skills.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe reviewing a requirement or specification in order
  to define what should be tested.
- 1 — Beginner: Runs tests defined by someone else, with supervision, and has
  not yet defined test conditions independently.
- 2 — Intermediate: Defines test conditions for a straightforward requirement,
  with guidance on judging what risk to test for.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently reviews requirements and specifications,
  defines test conditions, identifies issues and risks in the work, and
  analyses and reports the results.
- 4 — Expert: Everything at rating 3, plus is sought out to review other
  people's test approach, and has changed how a team defines test conditions
  or reports results based on their input.

**Evidence to gather**

- Give a work sample: a short specification for a data pipeline change, and
  ask the person to define the test conditions and the risks they would
  check for.
- Ask for a real example of a test report the person produced, and what
  issue or risk it surfaced.
- Ask a referee whether they have seen this person's review of a requirement
  or specification change what was tested.

One or two skills rated below target is not automatically a fail. A reasonable
assessor weighs how central that skill is to the level's accountabilities —
for example, a shortfall in data innovation matters differently than a
shortfall in data modelling, given how the framework frames each one at this
level — considers whether the gap is coachable quickly once the person is in
post, and records that reasoning against the skill, not just the number.
Where several skills central to leading and building the community (problem
management, communicating between the technical and non-technical, data
development process) fall short together, that pattern is worth more weight
than any single low rating on its own.
