# Digital Evaluator - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This assessment is for the person judging whether a candidate or an employee meets the
baseline a UK Government Digital and Data (GDAD) digital evaluator needs. It is written
for an assessor — a line manager, a panel member, or a reviewer — not for the person
being assessed.

A digital evaluator supports the evaluation of products and services with a good
understanding of the role and some practical experience, working with a more senior
digital evaluator rather than fully independently. This assessment measures
demonstrated capability against the framework's own description of that level, across
the seven skills the framework names for it. It is one input among several into a
hiring, onboarding or development decision, never the whole of one — see `## Advisory`
for why.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for educational
purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has not been piloted, normed
or checked for adverse impact — and a rating on this form must never be the sole basis
for a hiring, promotion, pay, performance or disciplinary decision. Before using it,
consult your organisation's human resources, people development, occupational
psychology or legal compliance colleagues.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` before you score anything. Gather
whatever evidence exists: a CV or portfolio, a structured interview, a work sample or
task, direct observation, or references. At this level, expect a mix of guided and
semi-independent work — the framework still has this person working with a more senior
digital evaluator and receiving guidance and training, so evidence of unsupervised
delivery on a full evaluation would be unusual.

**Scoring on evidence, not impression.** Every rating must trace to something the
person did, said, produced, or was observed doing. If you cannot point to evidence for a
rating, record it as unknown rather than guess.

**Consistency.** Score every candidate for this role level the same way, using the same
kinds of evidence where possible. For a hiring or promotion decision, have more than one
assessor score independently before comparing notes.

**Fairness.** Never let a rating be influenced by anything other than demonstrated,
job-related evidence. See [Fair and lawful use](../../../../uk-gdad-pcf-competency-assessments/spec/index.md#fair-and-lawful-use)
in the project specification for what this rules out.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point proficiency scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

This rates how well the person does the thing, on the evidence available — not how often
they do it. A worked example, using this level's skill of Quality assurance of data and
analysis:

- **1 — Beginner**: when shown a dataset with an obvious error, they could point to it
  once it was flagged, but did not spot it unprompted.
- **2 — Intermediate**: with limited guidance, they prepared and cleansed a dataset for
  analysis and checked it looked plausible before handing it on.
- **3 — Advanced**: they set up how a dataset should be collected, collated and
  prepared, decided independently whether it was fit for purpose, and peer-reviewed a
  colleague's output.

A digital evaluator's baseline for this skill, below, is 2.

## About this role level

**The role: digital evaluator.** A digital evaluator assesses the design, implementation
and outcomes of a digital product or service, and whether it delivers value for the
public and society. Some are also members of a government analytical profession where
evaluation is a core skill. In this role, you work closely with delivery and policy
teams; develop theories of change for products and services that explain the links
between activities, outputs, intended outcomes and unintended effects; follow
established monitoring and evaluation methods and government guidance; produce
evaluation plans; design quantitative and qualitative research; collect and analyse
data; and communicate findings, including by publishing reports.

**The level: digital evaluator.** The framework describes this level as having a good
understanding of the role and some practical experience. It is the second of five
levels in this role: junior digital evaluator, digital evaluator, senior digital
evaluator, lead digital evaluator, and head of digital evaluation. At this level, you
work with a more senior digital evaluator to plan and deliver evaluations, manage the
day-to-day development of monitoring, evaluation and analytical tools, support the
design of quantitative and qualitative research, help with research activities and
other data collection, report on the performance of products and services, communicate
findings of evaluations (for example through written reports), and receive guidance and
training to support your work and develop your skills.

**The skills this level names**, in the summary's order: Communicating analysis and
insight; Data ethics and privacy; Evaluation delivery; Evaluation planning and strategy;
Monitoring and evaluation across the product life cycle; Product and service monitoring;
and Quality assurance of data and analysis.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Communicating analysis and insight

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a digital evaluator to choose the appropriate medium to communicate
findings, shape and share communications relevant to the audience, present analysis and
visualisations clearly to both specialist and non-specialist audiences (with support),
and give tactical recommendations based on their analysis and insight to inform
decision making.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. Three of the four bullets describe active, independent
communication work; only the presenting-visualisations bullet keeps "with support" —
together this sits above pure Beginner and matches "can perform tasks with some
guidance."

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not chosen a communication medium or shaped a message for an audience.
- 1 — Beginner: with close direction, has helped format a findings report chosen by
  someone else.
- 2 — Intermediate: has chosen a format for a set of findings, shaped the message for a
  named audience, and given a tactical recommendation, with some support presenting
  visualisations.
- 3 — Advanced: independently chooses medium, shapes communications and gives tactical
  recommendations for a real evaluation, without needing support on visualisations.
- 4 — Expert: is trusted to develop other evaluators' communication skills.

**Evidence to gather**

- A work sample: give them a small findings summary and ask them to choose how they
  would present it to a non-specialist stakeholder, and why.
- A structured interview question: "Tell me about a report or presentation you shaped
  for a specific audience. What choices did you make and why?"
- Review an actual written report or slide deck they produced, if one exists.

### Skill: Data ethics and privacy

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a digital evaluator to apply fundamental principles of data ethics
and privacy in their work under supervision, and to share data ethics and privacy risks
through appropriate channels.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. "Apply... under supervision" is active application with
guidance, a step up from the junior level's purely explanatory wording, matching
Intermediate.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not applied a data ethics or privacy principle in real work.
- 1 — Beginner: can explain a data ethics or privacy principle but has not yet applied
  one in their own work.
- 2 — Intermediate: has applied a fundamental data ethics or privacy principle under
  supervision, and has shared a risk through the right channel.
- 3 — Advanced: applies data ethics and privacy principles to their own work without
  needing supervision, and proactively raises risks.
- 4 — Expert: advises colleagues on applying data ethics and privacy principles.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Give me an example of a data ethics or privacy
  principle you've applied to a piece of work, and how you checked you'd got it right."
- Ask them to describe a time they identified and raised a privacy risk, and what
  happened next.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate has needed close supervision or lighter guidance
  when applying data ethics principles.

### Skill: Evaluation delivery

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework's wording for a digital evaluator's Evaluation delivery is identical to
the junior digital evaluator's: evaluate interventions with support, collect
quantitative and qualitative data with support, analyse and interpret data with
support, and use some analytical approaches including descriptive and inferential
analysis. The published expectation does not step up between these two levels for this
particular skill — the level's overall accountabilities move on (planning and delivering
evaluations with a more senior colleague), but this skill's own text repeats what the
junior level asks for.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. Because the framework repeats the junior level's "with support"
wording word for word here, the honest baseline is the same one used for the junior
level, not a higher figure implied by the level's title alone.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not collected or analysed evaluation data, even under close direction.
- 1 — Beginner: with a colleague setting the task, has collected data and produced a
  guided first-pass analysis for an evaluation.
- 2 — Intermediate: collects and analyses data for a small evaluation with periodic
  check-ins rather than step-by-step direction.
- 3 — Advanced: independently runs data collection and analysis for a straightforward
  evaluation, choosing and justifying an appropriate technique.
- 4 — Expert: designs and runs evaluation delivery for a complex intervention and
  teaches the techniques to others.

**Evidence to gather**

- A work sample: a small, realistic dataset for them to collect further data on and
  analyse, then talk through.
- A structured interview question: "Talk me through an evaluation you've helped
  deliver. What parts did you lead, and where did you need a more senior colleague?"
- Ask a referee what level of support the candidate typically needed running data
  collection or analysis.

### Skill: Evaluation planning and strategy

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a digital evaluator to work with stakeholders to understand their
needs, translate stakeholder needs into potential research questions and evaluation
approaches, present options for monitoring and evaluation of products and services, and
influence decisions on evaluation plans with support.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. This moves from the junior level's descriptive wording
("describe how you might...") to actual doing ("work with", "translate", "present"),
with only the influencing bullet keeping "with support" — a clear step to Intermediate.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not worked directly with a stakeholder on evaluation needs.
- 1 — Beginner: has sat in on a stakeholder conversation about evaluation needs but not
  yet led any part of it.
- 2 — Intermediate: has worked with a stakeholder to understand their needs, translated
  those needs into research questions, and presented options for evaluation, with some
  support influencing the final decision.
- 3 — Advanced: independently agrees an evaluation plan's direction with a stakeholder.
- 4 — Expert: is trusted to secure resources and agreement from senior stakeholders for
  an evaluation strategy.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Describe a time you translated what a stakeholder
  said they wanted into a specific evaluation approach."
- A scenario: give a fictional stakeholder brief and ask them to sketch two options for
  evaluating it.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate has presented evaluation options directly to a
  stakeholder.

### Skill: Monitoring and evaluation across the product life cycle

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a digital evaluator to ensure the team is measuring performance as
appropriate for the relevant service standard phase, manage the delivery of evaluation
of services or products at different phases, and plan and look at data requirements in
future phases.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. All three bullets describe genuine ownership of a piece of
work ("ensure", "manage the delivery", "plan"), a clear step up from the junior level's
single descriptive bullet, and matching "can perform tasks with some guidance" rather
than full independence.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not been involved in matching evaluation activity to a life cycle phase.
- 1 — Beginner: can describe what phase a product is in and what evaluation activity
  might suit it, without having run any of it.
- 2 — Intermediate: has helped ensure a team measured performance appropriately for its
  current phase, and has looked ahead at data needs for the next phase, with guidance.
- 3 — Advanced: independently manages evaluation delivery across more than one life
  cycle phase.
- 4 — Expert: advises other teams on evaluation and data requirements as they move
  through life cycle phases.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Tell me about a time you had to plan evaluation
  activity for a service moving from one phase to the next."
- Ask them to describe how they checked the team was measuring the right things for
  the phase the product was in.
- Ask a referee about the candidate's role in planning data requirements ahead of a
  phase change.

### Skill: Product and service monitoring

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a digital evaluator to develop a theory of change for products or
services through discussion with stakeholders (with support), identify and present
options for measuring indicators to stakeholders, turn some identified theory of change
indicators into measurement frameworks for a digital service, and support the
development of a performance dashboard.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. Active verbs throughout ("develop", "identify and present",
"turn... into"), with "with support" attached to the theory of change bullet — the
overall pattern is doing with guidance, matching Intermediate.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not contributed to a theory of change or a measurement framework.
- 1 — Beginner: has observed or helped populate a theory of change diagram someone else
  led.
- 2 — Intermediate: has developed part of a theory of change with support, presented
  indicator options to stakeholders, and turned some indicators into a measurement
  framework.
- 3 — Advanced: independently develops a theory of change through stakeholder
  discussion and builds the resulting measurement framework.
- 4 — Expert: sets the approach other evaluators use for theory of change and indicator
  development.

**Evidence to gather**

- A work sample: give a short service description and ask them to sketch a theory of
  change and propose two indicators.
- A structured interview question: "Tell me about a performance dashboard or
  measurement framework you helped build. What was your part in it?"
- Ask a referee about the candidate's role in developing indicators or a dashboard.

### Skill: Quality assurance of data and analysis

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a digital evaluator to identify appropriate ways to collect, collate
and prepare data, decide if data and analysis is accurate and fit for purpose, perform
data preparation and cleansing with limited guidance, undertake quality assurance
processes, and peer-review colleagues' outputs from data analysis to ensure quality.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. "With limited guidance" and active ownership across five
bullets, including peer review of others' work, places this above Beginner — "limited
guidance" is a good match for "can perform tasks with some guidance."

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not prepared, cleansed or quality-checked a dataset.
- 1 — Beginner: has cleansed or prepared a small dataset only with close, step-by-step
  direction.
- 2 — Intermediate: prepares and cleanses data with limited guidance, decides whether a
  dataset is fit for purpose, and has peer-reviewed a colleague's analysis output.
- 3 — Advanced: independently sets up how a dataset should be collected, collated and
  prepared for a real evaluation.
- 4 — Expert: designs and owns a quality assurance process for others to follow.

**Evidence to gather**

- A work sample: a dataset with a few realistic quality issues, asking them to prepare
  it and say whether it is fit for purpose.
- A structured interview question: "Tell me about a time you peer-reviewed someone
  else's analysis. What did you check, and what did you find?"
- Ask a referee how much guidance the candidate typically needed to prepare data for
  analysis.

The baseline is normally met when every skill is rated at or above its baseline target.
Note that Evaluation delivery's target stays at 1 — the framework's own wording for this

for scoring the same on this skill as a strong junior candidate would.

One or two skills below target is not automatically a fail. Weigh how central the skill
is to this level's core accountabilities — planning and delivering evaluations with a
more senior colleague, and reporting on performance — consider whether the shortfall is
coachable quickly given the guidance and training this level still provides, and record
the reasoning behind the determination, not only the number.
