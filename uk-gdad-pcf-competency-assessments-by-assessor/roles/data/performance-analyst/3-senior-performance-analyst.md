# Senior Performance Analyst - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This assessment is for the person judging whether a candidate or an employee meets the
baseline a UK Government Digital and Data (GDAD) senior performance analyst needs. It
is written for an assessor — a line manager, a panel member, or a reviewer — not for the
person being assessed.

A senior performance analyst looks to delegate, automate or upskill others in analysis
where possible and appropriate, provides leadership within their team, and proactively
uses analysis to improve services. This assessment measures demonstrated capability
against the framework's own description of that level, across the seven skills the
framework names for it. It is one input among several into a hiring, promotion or
development decision, never the whole of one — see `## Advisory` for why.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for educational
purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has not been piloted, normed
or checked for adverse impact — and a rating on this form must never be the sole basis
for a hiring, promotion, pay, performance or disciplinary decision. Before using it,
consult your organisation's human resources, people development, occupational
psychology or legal compliance colleagues.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` before you score anything. Gather
whatever evidence exists: a CV or portfolio, a structured interview, a work sample or
task, direct observation, or references. This level may manage one or more performance
analysts and is proficient in a wide range of tools — expect evidence of both technical
range and some line or task leadership.

**Scoring on evidence, not impression.** Every rating must trace to something the
person did, said, produced, or was observed doing. If you cannot point to evidence for a
rating, record it as unknown rather than guess.

**Consistency.** Score every candidate for this role level the same way, using the same
kinds of evidence where possible. For a hiring or promotion decision, have more than one
assessor score independently before comparing notes.

**Fairness.** Never let a rating be influenced by anything other than demonstrated,
job-related evidence. See [Fair and lawful use](../../../../uk-gdad-pcf-competency-assessments-by-assessor/spec/index.md#fair-and-lawful-use)
in the project specification for what this rules out.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point proficiency scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

This rates how well the person does the thing, on the evidence available — not how often
they do it. A worked example, using this level's skill of Quality assurance of data and
analysis:

- **2 — Intermediate**: they cleansed and prepared a moderately complex dataset with
  some guidance from a colleague.
- **3 — Advanced**: they independently set up a system to get data ready for use,
  specified how it should be cleansed, and communicated the limitations of the result to
  a stakeholder who wanted a cleaner answer than the data allowed.
- **4 — Expert**: they built the assurance system three teams now use to bring data
  together from different sources.

A senior performance analyst's baseline for this skill, below, is 3.

## About this role level

**The role: performance analyst.** A performance analyst conducts analysis. In this
role, you develop performance measurement frameworks — key performance indicators
(KPIs), goals, user needs and benefits — and analyse the performance of a service or
product against these, adapting your approach and framework appropriately and in line
with any changes.

**The level: senior performance analyst.** The framework describes this level as
looking to delegate, automate or upskill others in analysis where possible and
appropriate, providing leadership within their team, and proactively using analysis to
improve services. It is the third of five levels in this role: associate performance
analyst, performance analyst, senior performance analyst, lead performance analyst, and
head of performance analysis. At this level, you may manage one or more performance
analysts; are proficient in a wide range of tools; ensure the team is equipped with the
required skills according to the strategy set out by the head of performance analysis;
are skilled and experienced at carrying out the day-to-day activities of a performance
analyst, including setting up performance measurement frameworks; carry out increasingly
complex analysis, working with other teams where necessary; and actively contribute to
the performance analysis community within your department or organisation, while
forging links with other analytical disciplines. The framework also notes that, as a
"technical specialist senior performance analyst", you would look to develop tools and
techniques to aid others in analysis, and would be responsible for the accuracy and
quality of data and analysis, and for how data and analysis are used.

**The skills this level names**, in the summary's order: Analysis and insight;
Communicating analysis and insight; Performance measurement; Quality assurance of data
and analysis; Technical understanding (performance analyst); Understanding analysis
across the product life cycle; and User-centred analysis.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Analysis and insight

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a senior performance analyst to understand and help teams to apply a
range of techniques to analyse data and provide insight, be proactive and present
compelling findings that inform wider decisions, and apply innovative approaches to
resolve problems. This wording is identical to the performance analyst level below.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. The text repeats the level below's, so the honest baseline for
this specific skill stays the same — this level's real step up shows in Communicating
analysis and insight and in team leadership, not in a higher claim this text does not
make.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not applied more than one technique for analysis and insight
  independently.
- 1 — Beginner: has applied a single technique with close direction.
- 2 — Intermediate: has applied a range of techniques with some guidance and presented
  a finding a colleague acted on.
- 3 — Advanced: independently applies a range of techniques, proactively presents
  compelling findings that inform a wider decision, and has applied an innovative
  approach to resolve a problem.
- 4 — Expert: teaches others across a team or community to apply a range of analysis
  techniques.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for a proactively surfaced finding that changed a decision, not one they were
  asked to produce.
- A structured interview question: "Tell me about a problem where the obvious
  analytical approach didn't work, and what you tried instead."
- Ask a referee about the range of techniques the candidate has independently applied.

### Skill: Communicating analysis and insight

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a senior performance analyst to turn complex data into compelling,
clear and actionable stories; share data communication skills with the team and across
government; present analysis and visualisations clearly to communicate complex
messages; communicate both negative and positive information to stakeholders; and give
tactical and strategic recommendations.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Sharing data communication skills with others, and giving
strategic as well as tactical recommendations, is a strong, independent step beyond
the levels below.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not communicated complex findings to a senior stakeholder unsupported.
- 1 — Beginner: has drafted part of a complex findings report with close direction.
- 2 — Intermediate: has presented complex findings to a specialist audience with some
  support adapting for a non-specialist one.
- 3 — Advanced: independently turns complex data into a compelling, actionable story,
  including delivering unwelcome findings clearly, and gives both tactical and
  strategic recommendations.
- 4 — Expert: is sought out across government to coach others on communicating
  analysis.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for a real report or briefing where they communicated a difficult or negative
  finding, and how the stakeholder responded.
- A structured interview question: "Tell me about a time your analysis wasn't what the
  stakeholder wanted to hear."
- Ask a referee whether the candidate has shared communication skills with others on
  the team.

### Skill: Performance measurement

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a senior performance analyst to lead the development of performance
measurement frameworks including key performance indicators (KPIs), and to demonstrate
a deep understanding of business goals that they can turn into tangible performance
measures.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. "Lead the development" and a deep, not just working,
understanding of business goals is a strong, independent step up from the level below's
guided participation.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not led the development of a performance measurement framework.
- 1 — Beginner: has contributed a single measure to a framework someone else led.
- 2 — Intermediate: has helped develop a framework with some guidance.
- 3 — Advanced: independently leads the development of a performance measurement
  framework, including KPIs, from a deep understanding of the business goals it serves.
- 4 — Expert: oversees the consistency of measurement frameworks across multiple
  projects.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for a performance measurement framework they led the development of, and how it
  tied to business goals.
- A structured interview question: "Tell me about turning a broad business goal into a
  set of tangible KPIs."
- Ask a referee about the candidate's role leading a framework's development, not just
  contributing to one.

### Skill: Quality assurance of data and analysis

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a senior performance analyst to set up a system to get data ready
for use, specify how data should be cleansed and prepared, bring data together from
different sources, and communicate the limitations of data.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Setting up a system and specifying process, rather than
following one, is ownership-level, independent work.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not designed a data preparation system or specified a cleansing
  approach.
- 1 — Beginner: has followed a data preparation process someone else designed.
- 2 — Intermediate: has prepared and combined data from more than one source with some
  guidance.
- 3 — Advanced: independently sets up a system for getting data ready for use,
  specifies how it should be cleansed, brings sources together, and communicates the
  limitations of the resulting data.
- 4 — Expert: designs quality assurance systems used across multiple teams.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Tell me about a data quality system or process you
  set up. How did you decide what it needed to check?"
- Ask about communicating a data limitation to a stakeholder who wanted a cleaner
  answer than the data allowed.
- Review a documented data preparation or cleansing specification they produced, if
  available.

### Skill: Technical understanding (performance analyst)

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a senior performance analyst to show a deep understanding of the
technical concepts required for the role and understand how they fit into the wider
technical landscape, and to understand the limitations of digital technology.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. "Deep understanding" and seeing "how they fit into the wider
technical landscape" is a strong, independent grasp beyond applying core concepts.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot place a technical concept within the wider technical landscape.
- 1 — Beginner: understands core technical concepts but not how they fit together
  more broadly.
- 2 — Intermediate: has explained how one technical concept relates to another, with
  some guidance.
- 3 — Advanced: independently demonstrates deep understanding of technical concepts
  required for the role, places them in the wider technical landscape, and understands
  the limitations of digital technology.
- 4 — Expert: identifies opportunities to use new digital technologies to enhance
  analytical capability.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Explain how the analytics platform you've used
  fits into the wider technical landscape of the services it measures."
- Ask them to describe a limitation of digital technology that affected an analysis
  they did.
- Ask a referee about the candidate's depth of technical understanding relative to the
  team.

### Skill: Understanding analysis across the product life cycle

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a senior performance analyst to apply experience in multiple parts
of the product life cycle, recognise when to move forward and when to stop, recognise
the appropriate deliverables and engage the right people to meet them, work with other
Agile delivery disciplines throughout the product life cycle, plan and proactively
engage with stakeholders at the appropriate stage of the project, and identify which
tools and techniques should be used at each stage.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Six bullets of active, judgement-based ownership — recognising
stop-or-proceed points and choosing tools per stage — is a strong, independent grasp.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not made a stop-or-proceed recommendation at a life cycle stage.
- 1 — Beginner: has contributed evidence to a stop-or-proceed decision someone else
  made.
- 2 — Intermediate: has recommended a course of action at a life cycle stage with
  support.
- 3 — Advanced: independently recognises when to move forward or stop, applies
  experience across multiple life cycle stages, works with other Agile disciplines, and
  chooses the right tools and techniques for each stage.
- 4 — Expert: develops sustainable support models and champions best practice across
  the life cycle.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Tell me about recognising a stage where a service
  should stop, or move forward. What was the evidence?"
- Ask how they chose which tools and techniques to use for a given life cycle stage.
- Ask a referee about a life cycle transition the candidate managed independently.

### Skill: User-centred analysis

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a senior performance analyst to understand the difference between
user needs and the desires of the user, demonstrate experience in meeting user needs
across a variety of channels, integrate digital analytics with qualitative data such as
user surveys and user research to develop hypotheses for testing, and offer
recommendations on the best tools and methods to use.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Distinguishing needs from desires, integrating digital
analytics with qualitative research to develop hypotheses, and offering method
recommendations is judgement-heavy, independent work.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not distinguished user needs from user desires in an analysis.
- 1 — Beginner: has used a piece of user data with close direction.
- 2 — Intermediate: has integrated quantitative and qualitative user data with some
  guidance.
- 3 — Advanced: independently distinguishes user needs from desires, integrates digital
  analytics with qualitative research to develop testable hypotheses, and recommends
  the best tools and methods for a given problem.
- 4 — Expert: sets the strategic approach to user-centred analysis across projects and
  considers the political implications of the resulting insight.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Tell me about a time what users said they wanted
  and what the data showed they needed diverged. How did you handle it?"
- Ask for an example of turning qualitative user research into a testable hypothesis
  with digital analytics.
- Ask a referee about the candidate's recommendations on tools or methods for a real
  project.

- [ ] 2 — Intermediate

The baseline is normally met when every skill is rated at or above its baseline target.
Note that Analysis and insight's target reflects wording unchanged from the performance
analyst level below — this level's genuine step up in that skill is limited, and shows
more clearly in the other six skills and in the team leadership named in this level's
own accountabilities.

One or two skills below target is not automatically a fail. Weigh how central the skill
is to this level's core purpose — being skilled and experienced at the day-to-day
activities of a performance analyst, and looking to delegate, automate or upskill others
— consider whether the shortfall is coachable quickly, and record the reasoning behind
the determination, not only the number.
