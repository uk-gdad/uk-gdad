# Head of Command and Control - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This assessment is for the person doing the assessing — a line manager, a hiring panel member, or a reviewer — who needs to judge whether a candidate or employee has the baseline abilities the head of command and control role level requires. It covers the IT operations role of command and control centre manager at its most senior level: someone responsible for the strategy roadmap, integration and building the capability of the team, within a function that proactively monitors live services and performance trends to identify potential problems or areas for improvement.

The competency matrix below turns the framework's own description of this role level into a rated, evidence-based instrument across the ten skills it names. It is one input among several into a hiring, onboarding or development decision, never the whole of one — see `## Advisory` for why.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating on this form must never be the sole basis for a hiring, promotion, pay, performance or disciplinary decision. Before using it, consult your organisation's human resources, people development, occupational psychology or legal compliance colleagues.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` before you score anything. Gather whatever evidence exists ahead of time: a CV or portfolio, a structured interview, a work sample or task, direct observation, or references. At this level, look for a sustained track record — a strategy roadmap the candidate actually authored, a capability the team gained under their leadership — rather than a single project.

**Scoring on evidence, not impression.** Every rating must trace to something the person did, said, produced, or was observed doing. Job title alone is not evidence; ask for the specific strategy, decision or investigation the candidate personally led.

**Consistency.** Score every candidate for this role level the same way, and for a hiring or promotion decision have more than one assessor score independently before comparing notes — especially important at this level, where a single assessor's personal view of "strategic" can otherwise dominate the rating.

**Fairness.** An unvalidated, AI-drafted instrument like this one can produce unlawful adverse impact if it is used inconsistently or if ratings drift away from demonstrated, job-related evidence. Score against the behavioural indicators below, keep your evidence log, and use more than one assessor wherever you can — consistent, evidence-based administration is what materially reduces that risk. See [Fair and lawful use](../../../../uk-gdad-pcf-competency-assessments/spec/index.md#fair-and-lawful-use) for the full constraints this assessment is written to.

## Rating scale

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

This is a proficiency scale, not a frequency scale — it rates how well the person does the thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it.

**Worked example — Service management framework knowledge.** A candidate who holds only a Level 3 qualification, without the expert-level certificate the framework names at this level, sits at 2 or 3. A candidate who holds the expert-level certificate and applies it consistently sits at 3. A candidate who holds the expert certificate and is regularly asked to settle disputes about how the framework should be applied, or trains others in it, sits at 4 — full mastery, demonstrated by teaching.

## About this role level

Command and control centre managers proactively monitor live services and performance trends to identify potential problems or areas for improvement that can then be investigated. The role runs across four levels: operations analyst, senior operations analyst, operational control manager, and head of command and control.

The head of command and control is the top of the ladder. The framework describes this level as responsible for the strategy roadmap, integration and building the capability of the team. At this level, you ensure all tools are fit for purpose and meet business requirements, and you may still be required to address issues across IT operations personally.

The framework names ten skills at this level: asset and configuration management (managing configuration items and related information, and managing service compliance and risk), availability and capacity management (ensuring the correct implementation of availability and capacity management standards and procedures, identifying capacity issues and implementing required changes, and initiating remedial action), change management (managing high impact, complex change requests, and ensuring that release policies, procedures and processes are applied), community collaboration (working collaboratively in a group and actively networking with others, adapting feedback to ensure it is effective and lasting, using their topic to identify and rectify problems in the team dynamic, and identifying issues through Agile "health checks" with the team), continuity management (managing the runbooks and the processes around service continuity, and testing the runbooks to ensure that service availability is maintained), incident management (leading the investigation and resolution of incidents), ownership and initiative (taking accountability for issues that occur and being proactive in searching for potential problems, and achieving excellent user outcomes), problem management (ensuring the right actions are taken to investigate, resolve and anticipate problems, and co-ordinating the team to investigate problems, implement solutions and take preventive measures), service focus (seeing the bigger picture by taking groups of services and investigating how to get the best of underlying services), service management framework knowledge (holding an expert certificate in the service management framework qualification), and user focus (giving direction on which tools or methods to use, demonstrating experience in meeting the needs of users across a variety of channels, bringing insight and expertise in how user needs have changed over time to ensure they're met by the business, and applying strategic thinking to provide the best service for the end user).

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Asset and configuration management

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a head of command and control to manage configuration items and related information, and to manage service compliance and risk.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "Manage service compliance and risk" is organisation-level accountability, not day-to-day maintenance, matching full mastery at the top of the role's ladder.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe what service compliance or risk means for configuration management.
- 1 — Beginner: Manages configuration items for a single area with support.
- 2 — Intermediate: Manages configuration items independently but has not yet owned compliance or risk at an estate level.
- 3 — Advanced: Manages configuration compliance and risk across more than one area, catching issues before they become material.
- 4 — Expert: Owns configuration compliance and risk policy for the whole operations centre, and other managers escalate their hardest configuration risk decisions to this person.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe how they identify and manage configuration-related risk across the whole operations estate.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate has owned a compliance issue that reached senior management attention.
