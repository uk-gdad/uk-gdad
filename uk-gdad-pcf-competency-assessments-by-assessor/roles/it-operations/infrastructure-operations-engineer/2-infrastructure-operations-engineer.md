# Infrastructure Operations Engineer - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This assessment is for an assessor — a line manager, an interview panel
member, or anyone else evaluating a candidate or an employee — who needs to
judge whether a person has the baseline abilities the IT operations
profession's infrastructure operations engineer role level requires. It
measures demonstrated skill against the UK GDAD Profession Capability
Framework's own description of this level, for a hiring decision, a promotion
or progression board, onboarding a new starter, or a periodic capability
check. It is one input into that decision, never the whole of it — see
`## Advisory` before you begin.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating on this
form must never be the sole basis for a hiring, promotion, pay, performance or
disciplinary decision. Before using it, consult your organisation's human
resources, people development, occupational psychology or legal compliance
colleagues.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you know what the
framework actually expects before you look at any evidence. Then gather what
exists for this person: a CV or portfolio, a structured interview, a work
sample or task, direct observation of them at work, or references from people
who have worked with them. At this level the framework expects documentation
to be created and maintained, routine tasks performed to process and
checklists, and infrastructure and applications services monitored with
appropriate action taken, so look for evidence of disciplined, process-driven
delivery rather than only technical knowledge.

**Score on evidence, not impression.** Every rating must trace to something
the person did, said, produced, or was observed doing. If you cannot point to
evidence for a rating, record it as unknown rather than guess — an honest gap
teaches you more than a guess dressed up as a score.

**Stay consistent.** Score every candidate for this role level the same way,
using the same evidence sources wherever you can. For a hiring or promotion
decision, have more than one assessor score independently, then compare notes,
rather than settling on a single view first.

**Fairness.** An unvalidated, AI-drafted instrument like this one, used
inconsistently, can produce unlawful adverse impact even when nobody intends
it. Consistent, evidence-based administration by more than one assessor
materially reduces that risk. Never let a rating be influenced by anything
other than demonstrated, job-related evidence.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point
proficiency scale — how well the person does the thing, on the evidence
available, not how often they do it.

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

**Worked example.** Take Skill: Availability and capacity management. At 1 —
Beginner, a candidate monitors a service component's KPI with a colleague
interpreting what it means. At 2 — Intermediate, the baseline for this level,
they manage service components to ensure they meet business needs and key
performance indicators — the framework's own wording for this level — flagging
a breach unassisted. At 3 — Advanced, they act proactively to keep components
within their KPIs before anyone asks them to check. Calibrate against
evidence like this before you start scoring.

## About this role level

IT operations role: Infrastructure operations engineer. An infrastructure
operations engineer supports, manages and maintains the core infrastructure
that underpins production services.

The role has five levels, in order: associate infrastructure operations
engineer, infrastructure operations engineer, senior infrastructure
operations engineer, lead infrastructure operations engineer, and principal
infrastructure operations engineer. This assessment covers infrastructure
operations engineer, the second of those.

Role level: Infrastructure operations engineer. An infrastructure operations
engineer is responsible for the preparation and support of IT operations
solutions and services, physical or virtual. They do so according to
industry and organisational best practices, standards, service requirements
and Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) throughout the product life cycle.

At this role level, the framework expects someone to:

- create and maintain documentation
- perform all routine tasks according to process and checklists
- monitor infrastructure and applications services
- alert and take appropriate action

The framework names twelve skills for this level: Asset and configuration
management, Availability and capacity management, Change management, Coding
and scripting, Incident management, Ownership and initiative, Problem
management, Service focus, Service management framework knowledge, Technical
specialism, Testing, and User focus.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Asset and configuration management

**What the framework expects at this level**

An infrastructure operations engineer maintains secure configuration and
accurate information, controls IT assets in one or more areas, and verifies
the location and state of IT assets.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. Controlling assets in one or more areas and
verifying their location and state without being told each step is routine,
guided work.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot say where the asset register is or what it is for.
- 1 — Beginner: updates the asset register when told exactly what to change.
- 2 — Intermediate: keeps configuration and asset records accurate for their
  own area day to day, and can verify an asset's location and state without
  prompting.
- 3 — Advanced: spots and corrects a discrepancy in the asset register before
  it causes a problem.
- 4 — Expert: owns asset and configuration accuracy across a whole service.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe how they keep an asset or configuration record
  accurate day to day, with an example.
- Review an asset or configuration record they maintained.
- Ask a referee for an example of the candidate catching an inaccurate asset
  record before it caused an incident.

### Skill: Availability and capacity management

**What the framework expects at this level**

An infrastructure operations engineer manages service components to ensure
they meet business needs and key performance indicators (KPIs).

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. Managing service components against business
needs and KPIs, with monitoring and alerting the level's own core duty, is
applied, guided accountability rather than fully independent, proactive
ownership.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot describe what a service component's KPI measures.
- 1 — Beginner: monitors a service component's KPI with a colleague
  interpreting what it means.
- 2 — Intermediate: manages service components to ensure they meet business
  needs and KPIs, and flags a breach without being told to check.
- 3 — Advanced: manages service components proactively, spotting a likely
  breach before it happens.
- 4 — Expert: redesigns how a service's availability or capacity is managed
  to prevent recurring KPI breaches.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe how they managed a service component to keep
  it within its KPI.
- Review a monitoring alert or capacity report they own.
- Ask a referee for an example of the candidate acting appropriately when a
  KPI was at risk.

### Skill: Incident management

**What the framework expects at this level**

An infrastructure operations engineer identifies and registers incidents,
gathering the required information and allocating it to the appropriate
channel.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. Identifying, registering and correctly routing an
incident unassisted is guided-but-independent triage work, matching this
level's monitor-and-alert accountability.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot say what information an incident record needs.
- 1 — Beginner: registers an incident someone else has already identified,
  with the details checked before it is filed.
- 2 — Intermediate: identifies an incident, gathers the information needed and
  allocates it to the right channel without prompting.
- 3 — Advanced: spots that an incident is really a symptom of something wider
  and flags it before being asked to.
- 4 — Expert: is the person colleagues check with when an incident does not
  fit the usual channels.

**Evidence to gather**

- Walk through a real or simulated monitoring alert and observe how the
  candidate gathers information and decides where to route it.
- Ask for an example of an incident they registered and what happened to it
  next.
- Ask a referee about the accuracy of the candidate's incident records.

### Skill: Ownership and initiative

**What the framework expects at this level**

An infrastructure operations engineer owns an issue until a new owner has been
found or the problem has been mitigated or resolved.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. Holding onto an issue through a handover rather
than dropping it once escalated is basic, guided-but-independent
professionalism expected of every working-level engineer.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: drops an issue once it is out of their hands.
- 1 — Beginner: keeps an issue open on their own list but needs reminding to
  chase it.
- 2 — Intermediate: tracks an issue through to a confirmed handover or
  resolution without being chased.
- 3 — Advanced: proactively checks that a handed-over issue actually landed
  with its new owner and was picked up.
- 4 — Expert: is the person others rely on to never let an issue slip, across
  a whole shift or team.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of an issue the candidate followed through to resolution
  or a confirmed handover.
- Ask a referee whether issues assigned to the candidate have gone missing or
  stalled.
- Review a ticket history showing the candidate's ownership of an issue over
  time.

### Skill: Problem management

**What the framework expects at this level**

An infrastructure operations engineer investigates problems in systems,
processes and services, with an understanding of the level of a problem — for
example, strategic, tactical or operational — and contributes to the
implementation of remedies and preventative measures.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. Investigating with an understanding of a
problem's level and contributing to remedies is applied, guided work, in
keeping with this level's process-driven, monitor-and-alert accountability.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot distinguish a problem from an incident.
- 1 — Beginner: investigates a problem when told exactly what to look at.
- 2 — Intermediate: investigates a problem unassisted, correctly judges
  whether it is strategic, tactical or operational, and contributes ideas to
  the remedy.
- 3 — Advanced: leads the investigation of a problem and proposes the
  preventative measure that is adopted.
- 4 — Expert: spots patterns across multiple problems that others have
  missed.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a problem they investigated and how they
  judged its level.
- Review a problem record or root-cause note they contributed to.
- Ask a referee about the candidate's contribution to a remedy that was
  implemented.

### Skill: User focus

**What the framework expects at this level**

An infrastructure operations engineer identifies and engages with users or
stakeholders to collate user needs evidence, understands and defines research
that fits user needs, and uses quantitative and qualitative data about users
to turn user focus into outcomes.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. Collating user needs evidence and turning data
into outcomes for a specific case is applied, guided work — a real step up
from the awareness-level expectation at the associate level.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: resolves the reported issue without asking about the user's
  wider impact.
- 1 — Beginner: asks the user basic clarifying questions when prompted to.
- 2 — Intermediate: engages the user or stakeholder directly to understand
  the actual need, and uses that to resolve the issue in a way that works for
  them.
- 3 — Advanced: spots a pattern in what several users need and feeds it back
  so the service improves.
- 4 — Expert: shapes how the wider team engages with users.

**Evidence to gather**

- Observe or ask the candidate to describe an interaction where they found
  out more than the user's initial report.
- Ask for an example where understanding a user's actual need changed how
  they resolved an issue.
- Ask a referee how users or colleagues describe the candidate's manner with
  users.
