# Senior Infrastructure Operations Engineer - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This assessment is for an assessor — a line manager, an interview panel
member, or anyone else evaluating a candidate or an employee — who needs to
judge whether a person has the baseline abilities the IT operations
profession's senior infrastructure operations engineer role level requires.
It measures demonstrated skill against the UK GDAD Profession Capability
Framework's own description of this level, for a hiring decision, a promotion
or progression board, onboarding a new starter, or a periodic capability
check. It is one input into that decision, never the whole of it — see
`## Advisory` before you begin.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating on this
form must never be the sole basis for a hiring, promotion, pay, performance or
disciplinary decision. Before using it, consult your organisation's human
resources, people development, occupational psychology or legal compliance
colleagues.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you know what the
framework actually expects before you look at any evidence. Then gather what
exists for this person: a CV or portfolio, a structured interview, a work
sample or task, direct observation of them at work, or references from people
who have worked with them. At this level the framework expects second- or
third-line support for incidents, problems and changes, specialist support to
projects for non-functional requirements and continual service improvement,
and coaching or mentoring of junior team members, so look for evidence across
both the deeper technical support and the coaching side of the role.

**Score on evidence, not impression.** Every rating must trace to something
the person did, said, produced, or was observed doing. If you cannot point to
evidence for a rating, record it as unknown rather than guess — an honest gap
teaches you more than a guess dressed up as a score.

**Stay consistent.** Score every candidate for this role level the same way,
using the same evidence sources wherever you can. For a hiring or promotion
decision, have more than one assessor score independently, then compare notes,
rather than settling on a single view first.

**Fairness.** An unvalidated, AI-drafted instrument like this one, used
inconsistently, can produce unlawful adverse impact even when nobody intends
it. Consistent, evidence-based administration by more than one assessor
materially reduces that risk. Never let a rating be influenced by anything
other than demonstrated, job-related evidence.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point
proficiency scale — how well the person does the thing, on the evidence
available, not how often they do it.

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

**Worked example.** Take Skill: Incident management. At 2 — Intermediate, a
candidate identifies and registers an incident and allocates it to the right
channel unassisted. At 3 — Advanced, the baseline for this level, they
diagnose and prioritise an incident, investigate its cause and find a
resolution — the framework's own wording for this level, and exactly the
second- or third-line support the level description names. At 4 — Expert,
they are the escalation point when a diagnosis has resisted others. Calibrate
against evidence like this before you start scoring.

## About this role level

IT operations role: Infrastructure operations engineer. An infrastructure
operations engineer supports, manages and maintains the core infrastructure
that underpins production services.

The role has five levels, in order: associate infrastructure operations
engineer, infrastructure operations engineer, senior infrastructure
operations engineer, lead infrastructure operations engineer, and principal
infrastructure operations engineer. This assessment covers senior
infrastructure operations engineer, the third of those.

Role level: Senior infrastructure operations engineer. A senior
infrastructure operations engineer acts as second- or third-line support for
incidents, problems and changes to solutions and services.

At this role level, the framework expects someone to:

- provide specialist technical support and assistance to projects, ensuring
  delivery of non-functional requirements and continual service improvement
- be responsible for preparations and support of IT operations solutions and
  services — physical or virtual — according to industry and organisational
  best practices, standards, service requirements and Key Performance
  Indicators (KPIs) throughout the product life cycle
- coach and mentor junior team members
- assist in the provision of first-class services and consistent levels of
  capability, as required by the organisation

The framework names twelve skills for this level: Asset and configuration
management, Availability and capacity management, Change management, Coding
and scripting, Incident management, Ownership and initiative, Problem
management, Service focus, Service management framework knowledge, Technical
specialism, Testing, and User focus.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Asset and configuration management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior infrastructure operations engineer maintains secure configuration
and accurate information, controls IT assets in one or more areas, and
verifies the location and state of IT assets.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. The framework's wording is unchanged from the
level below, so the baseline stays at Intermediate too — this level's step up
shows in Incident management, Change management and coaching, not here.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot say where the asset register is or what it is for.
- 1 — Beginner: updates the asset register when told exactly what to change.
- 2 — Intermediate: keeps configuration and asset records accurate for their
  own area day to day, and can verify an asset's location and state without
  prompting.
- 3 — Advanced: spots and corrects a discrepancy in the asset register before
  it causes a problem.
- 4 — Expert: owns asset and configuration accuracy across a whole service.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe how they keep an asset or configuration record
  accurate day to day, with an example.
- Review an asset or configuration record they maintained.
- Ask a referee for an example of the candidate catching an inaccurate asset
  record before it caused an incident.

### Skill: Change management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior infrastructure operations engineer analyses and assesses the impact
of change, documents change requests, and actions changes from change
requests.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. This is a clear step up from implementing change
requests under supervision at the level below: assessing impact and actioning
changes independently is strong, independent grasp of the skill.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot describe what assessing the impact of a change involves.
- 1 — Beginner: documents a change request when given the details to record.
- 2 — Intermediate: assesses the impact of a straightforward change with a
  colleague checking the assessment.
- 3 — Advanced: independently analyses and assesses the impact of a change,
  documents the request properly, and actions it.
- 4 — Expert: is consulted on the impact assessment for the highest-risk
  changes in the service.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to walk through a change they assessed, documented and
  actioned, including what they judged the impact to be and why.
- Review a change record showing their impact assessment.
- Ask a referee about a change the candidate actioned that had a wider impact
  than first expected, and how they handled it.

### Skill: Incident management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior infrastructure operations engineer diagnoses and prioritises
incidents, investigates their causes and finds resolutions.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Diagnosing, prioritising and resolving incidents
unassisted is exactly the second- or third-line support this level's own
description names, and matches independent, strong-grasp work.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot diagnose a fault beyond following a runbook.
- 1 — Beginner: diagnoses a familiar incident with a colleague checking the
  reasoning.
- 2 — Intermediate: diagnoses and resolves familiar incidents unassisted, but
  needs help prioritising against a busy queue.
- 3 — Advanced: independently diagnoses and prioritises incidents against
  competing work, investigates the cause and finds the resolution.
- 4 — Expert: is the escalation point for incidents that have resisted
  diagnosis by others, and teaches the diagnostic approach to junior
  colleagues.

**Evidence to gather**

- Walk through a real or simulated incident scenario and observe how the
  candidate prioritises and diagnoses it.
- Ask for an example of an incident they investigated from first report to
  resolution.
- Ask a referee for an example of the candidate correctly prioritising one
  incident over another under pressure.

### Skill: Problem management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior infrastructure operations engineer initiates and monitors actions to
investigate patterns and trends to resolve problems, effectively consults
specialists where required, determines the appropriate resolution and assists
with its implementation, and determines preventative measures.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Determining the resolution and preventative measures,
rather than only investigating and contributing as at the level below, is
independent, judgement-driven ownership of the whole problem.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot distinguish a problem from an incident.
- 1 — Beginner: investigates a problem when told exactly what to look at.
- 2 — Intermediate: investigates a problem and contributes ideas to a remedy,
  but relies on a colleague to decide the resolution.
- 3 — Advanced: initiates and monitors an investigation into a pattern or
  trend, consults specialists appropriately, determines the resolution and
  the preventative measure, and assists with implementing it.
- 4 — Expert: spots a pattern across problems that others have missed and
  changes how the team investigates as a result.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a problem investigation they initiated, who
  they consulted, and what resolution and preventative measure they
  determined.
- Review a problem record or root-cause report they own.
- Ask a referee for an example of a preventative measure the candidate
  determined that stopped a recurring issue.

### Skill: Technical specialism

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior infrastructure operations engineer uses management system software
and tools, uses logical schemata to investigate problems, collect performance
statistics and create reports, carries out the routine configuration,
installation and reconfiguration of database and related products, and
optimises performance and forecasts resource needs.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. This is a marked step up from "assisting" and
"routine maintenance" at the level below: using management tools, optimising
performance and forecasting resource needs is independent specialist work.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot use the team's management system software beyond basic
  tasks.
- 1 — Beginner: uses management system software for routine tasks with
  guidance.
- 2 — Intermediate: uses management system software and logical schemata to
  investigate a familiar problem, with a colleague checking the outcome.
- 3 — Advanced: independently uses management tools and schemata to
  investigate problems, configures and reconfigures database and related
  products, and forecasts resource needs.
- 4 — Expert: is consulted on the hardest performance and capacity forecasts
  in the service.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a resource forecast or performance
  optimisation they carried out, and what data they used.
- Observe or ask them to walk through a routine database configuration task.
- Ask a referee for an example of the candidate's forecast proving accurate,
  or where it needed correcting.

A senior infrastructure operations engineer reviews requirements and

associated with work, and analyses and reports test activities and results.

### Skill: User focus

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior infrastructure operations engineer identifies and engages with users
or stakeholders to collate user needs evidence, understands and defines
research that fits user needs, and uses quantitative and qualitative data
about users to turn user focus into outcomes.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. The framework's wording is unchanged from the
level below, so the baseline holds; this level's growth is expressed through
Incident management and coaching rather than a wider User focus expectation.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: resolves the reported issue without asking about the user's
  wider impact.
- 1 — Beginner: asks the user basic clarifying questions when prompted to.
- 2 — Intermediate: engages the user or stakeholder directly to understand
  the actual need, and uses that to resolve the issue in a way that works for
  them.
- 3 — Advanced: spots a pattern in what several users need and feeds it back
  so the service improves.
- 4 — Expert: shapes how the wider team engages with users.

**Evidence to gather**

- Observe or ask the candidate to describe an interaction where they found
  out more than the user's initial report.
- Ask for an example where understanding a user's actual need changed how
  they resolved an issue.
- Ask a referee how users or colleagues describe the candidate's manner with
  users.
