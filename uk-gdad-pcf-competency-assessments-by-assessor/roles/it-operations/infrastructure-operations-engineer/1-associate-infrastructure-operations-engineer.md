# Associate Infrastructure Operations Engineer - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This assessment is for an assessor — a line manager, an interview panel
member, or anyone else evaluating a candidate or an employee — who needs to
judge whether a person has the baseline abilities the IT operations
profession's associate infrastructure operations engineer role level
requires. It measures demonstrated skill against the UK GDAD Profession
Capability Framework's own description of this level, for a hiring decision,
a promotion or progression board, onboarding a new starter, or a periodic
capability check. It is one input into that decision, never the whole of it
— see `## Advisory` before you begin.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating on this
form must never be the sole basis for a hiring, promotion, pay, performance or
disciplinary decision. Before using it, consult your organisation's human
resources, people development, occupational psychology or legal compliance
colleagues.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you know what the
framework actually expects before you look at any evidence. Then gather what
exists for this person: a CV or portfolio, a structured interview, a work
sample or task, direct observation of them at work, or references from people
who have worked with them. This is a trainee level, working under an
infrastructure operations engineer's direction, so look for evidence of
learning quickly and working well under direction rather than independent
delivery — that is not what the framework expects here yet.

**Score on evidence, not impression.** Every rating must trace to something
the person did, said, produced, or was observed doing. If you cannot point to
evidence for a rating, record it as unknown rather than guess — an honest gap
teaches you more than a guess dressed up as a score.

**Stay consistent.** Score every candidate for this role level the same way,
using the same evidence sources wherever you can. For a hiring or promotion
decision, have more than one assessor score independently, then compare notes,
rather than settling on a single view first.

**Fairness.** An unvalidated, AI-drafted instrument like this one, used
inconsistently, can produce unlawful adverse impact even when nobody intends
it. Consistent, evidence-based administration by more than one assessor
materially reduces that risk. Never let a rating be influenced by anything
other than demonstrated, job-related evidence.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point
proficiency scale — how well the person does the thing, on the evidence
available, not how often they do it.

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

**Worked example.** Take Skill: Incident management. At 1 — Beginner, the
baseline for this level, a candidate identifies and registers an incident,
gathering the required information and allocating it to the appropriate
channel, with a colleague checking each step — the framework's own wording for
this level, at close-supervision depth. At 2 — Intermediate, they identify and
register an incident unassisted, gathering the information needed without
prompting. At 3 — Advanced, they diagnose the incident themselves rather than
only registering and routing it. Calibrate against evidence like this before
you start scoring.

## About this role level

IT operations role: Infrastructure operations engineer. An infrastructure
operations engineer supports, manages and maintains the core infrastructure
that underpins production services.

The role has five levels, in order: associate infrastructure operations
engineer, infrastructure operations engineer, senior infrastructure
operations engineer, lead infrastructure operations engineer, and principal
infrastructure operations engineer. This assessment covers associate
infrastructure operations engineer, the first of those.

Role level: Associate infrastructure operations engineer. As a trainee, an
associate infrastructure operations engineer works in an established team and
supports infrastructure operations engineers in their daily work, under their
direction. The framework does not list separate day-to-day accountability
bullets for this level beyond this description; its expectations are carried
in the skills below.

The framework names four skills for this level: Incident management,
Ownership and initiative, Problem management, and User focus.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Incident management

**What the framework expects at this level**

An associate infrastructure operations engineer identifies and registers
incidents, gathering the required information and allocating it to the
appropriate channel.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. Identifying and registering an incident, under a
colleague's direction as this trainee level implies, is close-supervision
work by the framework's own description.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot say what information an incident record needs.
- 1 — Beginner: identifies and registers an incident with a colleague
  checking the details before it is filed.
- 2 — Intermediate: identifies and registers an incident unassisted, gathering
  the required information and allocating it to the right channel.
- 3 — Advanced: spots that an incident is really a symptom of something wider
  and flags it before being asked to.
- 4 — Expert: is the person colleagues check with when an incident does not
  fit the usual channels.

**Evidence to gather**

- Walk through a real or simulated fault report and observe how the candidate
  gathers information and decides where to route it.
- Ask for an example of an incident they registered and what happened to it
  next.
- Ask a referee about the accuracy of the candidate's incident records.

### Skill: Ownership and initiative

**What the framework expects at this level**

An associate infrastructure operations engineer shows an awareness of problem
resolution processes and passes problems on to their team.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. Passing a problem on, rather than owning it through
to resolution, is exactly the close-supervision description the framework
gives this level.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: does not know who to pass a problem to.
- 1 — Beginner: recognises a problem is beyond them and passes it to the
  team, with an awareness of the resolution process.
- 2 — Intermediate: passes a problem on with useful context attached,
  unprompted.
- 3 — Advanced: owns a straightforward problem through to resolution before
  needing to escalate.
- 4 — Expert: is trusted to judge which problems to own and which to
  escalate.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a problem they recognised was beyond them,
  and what they did next.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate passes problems on with useful context.
- Ask for an example of the candidate's awareness of the team's problem
  resolution process.

- [ ] 4 — Expert

### Skill: Problem management

**What the framework expects at this level**

An associate infrastructure operations engineer investigates problems in
systems, processes and services, with an understanding of the level of a
problem — for example, strategic, tactical or operational — and contributes
to the implementation of remedies and preventative measures.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. Investigating with an understanding of a
problem's level and contributing to remedies is applied, guided work — a step
above pure awareness, though this level does not yet determine the resolution
alone.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot distinguish a problem from an incident.
- 1 — Beginner: investigates a problem when told exactly what to look at.
- 2 — Intermediate: investigates a problem unassisted, correctly judges
  whether it is strategic, tactical or operational, and contributes ideas to
  the remedy.
- 3 — Advanced: leads the investigation of a straightforward problem.
- 4 — Expert: spots patterns across multiple problems that others have
  missed.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a problem they investigated and how they
  judged its level.
- Review a problem record or root-cause note they contributed to.
- Ask a referee about the candidate's contribution to a remedy that was
  implemented.

**Rate this skill**

- [ ] 0 — None
- [ ] 1 — Beginner
- [ ] 2 — Intermediate
- [ ] 3 — Advanced
- [ ] 4 — Expert

### Skill: User focus

**What the framework expects at this level**

An associate infrastructure operations engineer shows an awareness or
understanding of user experience analysis and its principles, and can explain
the purpose of user stories and the focus on user needs.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. Showing awareness and explaining purpose, rather than
engaging users directly to collate evidence, is foundational knowledge suited
to close supervision.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot describe what a user story is for.
- 1 — Beginner: explains the basic purpose of a user story and shows
  awareness of user experience analysis principles when asked.
- 2 — Intermediate: describes how a real user story shaped a piece of work
  they were involved in.
- 3 — Advanced: contributes user-focused input to a piece of work
  unprompted.
- 4 — Expert: helps colleagues understand how to apply user focus principles.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to explain, in their own words, the purpose of a user
  story.
- Ask for an example of a user experience analysis principle they have seen
  applied.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate's understanding of user focus concepts
  is accurate.
