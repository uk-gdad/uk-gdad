# Lead Infrastructure Operations Engineer - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This assessment is for an assessor — a line manager, an interview panel
member, or anyone else evaluating a candidate or an employee — who needs to
judge whether a person has the baseline abilities the IT operations
profession's lead infrastructure operations engineer role level requires. It
measures demonstrated skill against the UK GDAD Profession Capability
Framework's own description of this level, for a hiring decision, a promotion
or progression board, onboarding a new starter, or a periodic capability
check. It is one input into that decision, never the whole of it — see
`## Advisory` before you begin.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating on this
form must never be the sole basis for a hiring, promotion, pay, performance or
disciplinary decision. Before using it, consult your organisation's human
resources, people development, occupational psychology or legal compliance
colleagues.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you know what the
framework actually expects before you look at any evidence. Then gather what
exists for this person: a CV or portfolio, a structured interview, a work
sample or task, direct observation of them at work, or references from people
who have worked with them. At this level the framework expects someone to
manage a team of IT operations specialists, third-party provision, and the
development of architectural solutions throughout the service life cycle, and
to ensure the organisation's own contractual and regulatory requirements are
met, so look for evidence at that management and accountability scale, not
only individual technical delivery.

**Score on evidence, not impression.** Every rating must trace to something
the person did, said, produced, or was observed doing. If you cannot point to
evidence for a rating, record it as unknown rather than guess — an honest gap
teaches you more than a guess dressed up as a score.

**Stay consistent.** Score every candidate for this role level the same way,
using the same evidence sources wherever you can. For a hiring or promotion
decision, have more than one assessor score independently, then compare notes,
rather than settling on a single view first.

**Fairness.** An unvalidated, AI-drafted instrument like this one, used
inconsistently, can produce unlawful adverse impact even when nobody intends
it. Consistent, evidence-based administration by more than one assessor
materially reduces that risk. Never let a rating be influenced by anything
other than demonstrated, job-related evidence.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point
proficiency scale — how well the person does the thing, on the evidence
available, not how often they do it.

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

**Worked example.** Take Skill: Incident management. At 3 — Advanced, a
candidate diagnoses and prioritises an incident independently and finds a
resolution. At 4 — Expert, the baseline for this level, they lead the
investigation and resolution of incidents — the framework's own wording for
this level — co-ordinating other people through the response rather than
working alone, and are the person others learn incident leadership from.
Calibrate against evidence like this before you start scoring.

## About this role level

IT operations role: Infrastructure operations engineer. An infrastructure
operations engineer supports, manages and maintains the core infrastructure
that underpins production services.

The role has five levels, in order: associate infrastructure operations
engineer, infrastructure operations engineer, senior infrastructure
operations engineer, lead infrastructure operations engineer, and principal
infrastructure operations engineer. This assessment covers lead
infrastructure operations engineer, the fourth of those.

Role level: Lead infrastructure operations engineer. A lead infrastructure
operations engineer ensures the service and the contractual and regulatory
requirements of the organisation are met.

At this role level, the framework expects someone to:

- manage operational support of IT solutions and services
- manage workload, performance and development of a team of IT operations
  specialists by building capability to deliver services as required
- manage third-party provision of IT operations services
- manage provision of expertise to programmes and projects
- manage the development of architectural solutions for IT operations
  solutions, throughout the service life cycle

The framework names twelve skills for this level: Asset and configuration
management, Availability and capacity management, Change management, Coding
and scripting, Incident management, Ownership and initiative, Problem
management, Service focus, Service management framework knowledge, Technical
specialism, Testing, and User focus.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Asset and configuration management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead infrastructure operations engineer maintains secure configuration and
accurate information, controls IT assets in one or more areas, and verifies
the location and state of IT assets.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. The framework's wording is unchanged since the level
below, but by the time someone manages a team, third parties and architectural
development, foundational configuration control is assumed to be strong and
independent rather than merely guided — its absence from this level's extra
wording reflects that it is no longer a growth area, not a lowered
expectation.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot say where the asset register is or what it is for.
- 1 — Beginner: updates the asset register when told exactly what to change.
- 2 — Intermediate: keeps configuration and asset records accurate for their
  own area, and can verify an asset's location and state without prompting.
- 3 — Advanced: spots and corrects a discrepancy in the asset register before
  it causes a problem, and improves how the team keeps records.
- 4 — Expert: owns asset and configuration accuracy across a whole service
  and is the person others check a difficult record against.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe how they have improved asset or configuration
  accuracy for a team, not just for themselves.
- Review an asset or configuration standard they set or improved.
- Ask a referee for an example of the candidate catching a systemic
  configuration problem before it caused an incident.

### Skill: Availability and capacity management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead infrastructure operations engineer manages service components to
ensure they meet business needs and key performance indicators (KPIs).

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. The framework's wording is unchanged from the level
below, but a lead who owns architectural development for IT operations is
expected to manage this proactively across a wider set of components — Advanced
reflects that without over-reading a static line of text.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot describe what a service component's KPI measures.
- 1 — Beginner: monitors a service component's KPI with a colleague
  interpreting what it means.
- 2 — Intermediate: manages service components to ensure they meet business
  needs and KPIs, and flags a breach without being told to check.
- 3 — Advanced: manages service components proactively across a wider set of
  services, spotting a likely breach before it happens.
- 4 — Expert: redesigns how availability or capacity is managed across the
  service to prevent recurring KPI breaches.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe how they managed availability or capacity
  across more than one service component.
- Review a capacity or availability strategy document they own.
- Ask a referee for an example of the candidate acting proactively before a
  KPI breach happened.

### Skill: Change management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead infrastructure operations engineer manages high-impact, complex change
requests and ensures that release policies, procedures and processes are
applied.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Managing high-impact, complex changes and being
responsible for release policy application, rather than just assessing and
actioning changes, is ownership at a scale that fits full mastery — the
person others rely on to get the highest-risk changes right.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot describe what makes a change high-impact or complex.
- 1 — Beginner: contributes to part of a complex change under a colleague's
  direction.
- 2 — Intermediate: manages a moderately complex change with a senior
  colleague available to check progress.
- 3 — Advanced: manages a high-impact, complex change independently, and
  applies release policies correctly.
- 4 — Expert: manages the highest-impact, most complex changes in the
  service, and ensures the wider team applies release policy consistently.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a high-impact, complex change they managed
  end to end, including how they handled the risk.
- Review a change record or post-implementation review for a complex change
  they led.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate's management of complex changes has
  needed correction, and how release policy compliance was checked.

### Skill: Incident management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead infrastructure operations engineer leads the investigation and

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Leading the investigation and resolution, rather than

### Skill: Ownership and initiative

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead infrastructure operations engineer takes accountability for issues
that occur and is proactive in searching for potential problems, and achieves
excellent user outcomes.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Proactively searching for problems before they occur,
rather than only owning an issue once it exists, is a genuine step up from
the levels below, matching independent, strong-grasp practice.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: only acts once an issue has already been raised.
- 1 — Beginner: owns an existing issue through to resolution when reminded to.
- 2 — Intermediate: owns issues through to resolution unprompted, but has not
  yet proactively surfaced a potential problem before it occurred.
- 3 — Advanced: takes accountability for issues that occur, proactively looks
  for potential problems, and consistently achieves a good outcome for users.
- 4 — Expert: builds a habit of proactive problem-searching into how the
  whole team works.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of a potential problem the candidate spotted and
  addressed before it became an incident.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate takes accountability when something
  goes wrong, or shifts blame.
- Review evidence of a user outcome the candidate achieved that went beyond
  the minimum fix.

### Skill: Problem management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead infrastructure operations engineer initiates and monitors actions to
investigate patterns and trends to resolve problems, effectively consults
specialists where required, determines the appropriate resolution and assists
with its implementation, and determines preventative measures.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. The framework's wording is unchanged from the senior
level, so the baseline holds; this level's added weight sits in Change
management, Incident management and team management instead.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot distinguish a problem from an incident.
- 1 — Beginner: investigates a problem when told exactly what to look at.
- 2 — Intermediate: investigates a problem and contributes ideas to a remedy,
  but relies on a colleague to decide the resolution.
- 3 — Advanced: initiates and monitors an investigation into a pattern or
  trend, consults specialists appropriately, determines the resolution and
  the preventative measure, and assists with implementing it.
- 4 — Expert: spots a pattern across problems that others have missed and
  changes how the team investigates as a result.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a problem investigation they initiated, who
  they consulted, and what resolution and preventative measure they
  determined.
- Review a problem record or root-cause report they own.
- Ask a referee for an example of a preventative measure the candidate
  determined that stopped a recurring issue.

### Skill: Service management framework knowledge

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead infrastructure operations engineer shows they have a Level 3 service
management framework qualification and demonstrates knowledge of the life
cycle or capability elements of ITIL (Information Technology Infrastructure
Library).

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. The framework adds ITIL life cycle or capability
knowledge on top of the qualification held at levels below — a genuine
widening of the skill that supports an Advanced baseline.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: holds no service management framework qualification and cannot
  describe ITIL concepts.
- 1 — Beginner: holds the Level 3 qualification but cannot yet explain ITIL
  life cycle or capability elements.
- 2 — Intermediate: holds the qualification and describes ITIL life cycle or
  capability elements with some prompting.
- 3 — Advanced: holds the qualification and demonstrates working knowledge of
  ITIL life cycle or capability elements, applying them in day-to-day
  decisions.
- 4 — Expert: is asked to explain ITIL concepts to colleagues or shape how
  the team applies them.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to explain how an ITIL life cycle or capability element
  applies to a decision they made recently.
- Ask them to show their Level 3 service management framework certificate or
  equivalent evidence.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate has helped colleagues understand ITIL
  concepts.

A lead infrastructure operations engineer drafts and maintains procedures and

**Baseline for this role level**

### Skill: User focus

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead infrastructure operations engineer collaborates with user researchers
and can represent users internally, explains the difference between user
needs and the desires of the user, champions user research to focus on all
users, prioritises and defines approaches to understand the user story while
guiding others in doing so, and offers recommendations on the best tools and
methods to use.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Guiding others through the approach to the user story
and championing user research across the team is teaching-and-guiding work —
a clear jump from independently engaging users at the levels below, and the
scale's own definition of Expert.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: resolves the reported issue without asking about the user's
  wider impact.
- 1 — Beginner: engages users directly when directed to.
- 2 — Intermediate: engages users to understand their actual need and uses
  that to resolve issues well.
- 3 — Advanced: champions user research and can explain, to a colleague, the
  difference between what a user asks for and what they need.
- 4 — Expert: represents users internally, prioritises and defines the
  approach to the user story, guides colleagues through it, and recommends
  which tools or methods the team should use.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of the candidate guiding a colleague through
  understanding a user's actual need.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate is seen internally as representing
  users' interests.
- Review a recommendation the candidate made about which tools or methods the
  team should use for user focus work.
