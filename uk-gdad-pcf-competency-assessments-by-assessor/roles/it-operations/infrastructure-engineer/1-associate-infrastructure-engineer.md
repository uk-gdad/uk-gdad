# Associate Infrastructure Engineer - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This assessment is for an assessor — a line manager, an interview panel
member, or anyone else evaluating a candidate or an employee — who needs to
judge whether a person has the baseline abilities the IT operations
profession's associate infrastructure engineer role level requires. It
measures demonstrated skill against the UK GDAD Profession Capability
Framework's own description of this level, for a hiring decision, a promotion
or progression board, onboarding a new starter, or a periodic capability
check. It is one input into that decision, never the whole of it — see
`## Advisory` before you begin.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating on this
form must never be the sole basis for a hiring, promotion, pay, performance or
disciplinary decision. Before using it, consult your organisation's human
resources, people development, occupational psychology or legal compliance
colleagues.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you know what the
framework actually expects before you look at any evidence. Then gather what
exists for this person: a CV or portfolio, a structured interview, a work
sample or task, direct observation of them at work, or references from people
who have worked with them. This is a trainee level, working under an
infrastructure engineer's direction, so look for evidence of learning quickly
and working well under direction rather than independent delivery — that is
not what the framework expects here yet.

**Score on evidence, not impression.** Every rating must trace to something
the person did, said, produced, or was observed doing. If you cannot point to
evidence for a rating, record it as unknown rather than guess — an honest gap
teaches you more than a guess dressed up as a score.

**Stay consistent.** Score every candidate for this role level the same way,
using the same evidence sources wherever you can. For a hiring or promotion
decision, have more than one assessor score independently, then compare notes,
rather than settling on a single view first.

**Fairness.** An unvalidated, AI-drafted instrument like this one, used
inconsistently, can produce unlawful adverse impact even when nobody intends
it. Consistent, evidence-based administration by more than one assessor
materially reduces that risk. Never let a rating be influenced by anything
other than demonstrated, job-related evidence.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point
proficiency scale — how well the person does the thing, on the evidence
available, not how often they do it.

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

**Worked example.** Take Skill: Troubleshooting and problem resolution. At 0 —
None, a candidate cannot describe where to start with a fault. At 1 —
Beginner, the baseline for this level, they show an awareness of different
technology capabilities and demonstrate basic troubleshooting on a familiar
fault, with a colleague checking their approach — the framework's own wording
for this level. At 2 — Intermediate, they troubleshoot a familiar fault
unassisted, but still need help with anything unfamiliar. Calibrate against
evidence like this before you start scoring.

## About this role level

IT operations role: Infrastructure engineer. An infrastructure engineer
designs, builds, manages and supports the infrastructure services that
underpin all internal user services and services to the public.

The role has five levels, in order: associate infrastructure engineer,
infrastructure engineer, senior infrastructure engineer, lead infrastructure
engineer, and principal infrastructure engineer. This assessment covers
associate infrastructure engineer, the first of those.

Role level: Associate infrastructure engineer. As a trainee, an associate
infrastructure engineer works in an established team and supports
infrastructure engineers in their daily work, under their direction. The
framework does not list separate day-to-day accountability bullets for this
level beyond this description; its expectations are carried in the skills
below.

The framework names eight skills for this level: IT infrastructure,
Information security, Modern development standards, Ownership and initiative,
Problem management, Systems design, Systems integration, and Troubleshooting
and problem resolution.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: IT infrastructure

**What the framework expects at this level**

An associate infrastructure engineer supports infrastructure solutions and
services, and other computer, storage, networking, physical infrastructure,
software, commercial-off-the-shelf (COTS) software and open source packages
and solutions, and supports virtual and cloud computing, including
Infrastructure as a Service (IaaS), Platform as a Service (PaaS) and Software
as a Service (SaaS).

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. "Support," under an infrastructure engineer's
direction, is close-supervision work by the framework's own description of
this trainee level.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot describe what infrastructure support involves.
- 1 — Beginner: supports infrastructure solutions and services when directed,
  with a colleague checking the work.
- 2 — Intermediate: supports a familiar infrastructure area unassisted, but
  needs guidance with an unfamiliar one.
- 3 — Advanced: supports infrastructure across several areas without
  prompting.
- 4 — Expert: is relied on for infrastructure support across the team.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe an infrastructure support task they carried
  out, and what direction they were given.
- Observe or ask them to walk through supporting a cloud or virtual computing
  service.
- Ask a referee how much direction the candidate still needs on infrastructure
  support tasks.

### Skill: Information security

**What the framework expects at this level**

An associate infrastructure engineer can explain information security and the
security controls available to protect solutions and services.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. Explaining security concepts, rather than applying
controls independently, is foundational knowledge appropriate to close
supervision.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot describe what information security means for
  infrastructure.
- 1 — Beginner: explains the basic purpose of information security and names
  a security control when asked.
- 2 — Intermediate: explains several security controls accurately and links
  them to a real solution they have worked on.
- 3 — Advanced: applies security control knowledge to spot a gap in a
  solution's protection.
- 4 — Expert: advises colleagues on which security controls to apply.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to explain, in their own words, what a named security
  control protects against.
- Ask for an example of a security control they have seen applied to an
  infrastructure solution.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate's explanations of security concepts are
  accurate.

### Skill: Ownership and initiative

**What the framework expects at this level**

An associate infrastructure engineer shows an awareness of problem resolution
processes and passes problems on to their team.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. Passing a problem on, rather than owning it through
to resolution, is exactly the close-supervision description the framework
gives this level.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: does not know who to pass a problem to.
- 1 — Beginner: recognises a problem is beyond them and passes it to the
  team, with an awareness of the resolution process.
- 2 — Intermediate: passes a problem on with useful context attached,
  unprompted.
- 3 — Advanced: owns a straightforward problem through to resolution before
  needing to escalate.
- 4 — Expert: is trusted to judge which problems to own and which to
  escalate.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a problem they recognised was beyond them,
  and what they did next.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate passes problems on with useful context.
- Ask for an example of the candidate's awareness of the team's problem
  resolution process.

### Skill: Problem management

**What the framework expects at this level**

An associate infrastructure engineer investigates problems in systems,
processes and services, with an understanding of the level of a problem — for
example, strategic, tactical or operational — and contributes to the
implementation of remedies and preventative measures.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. Investigating with an understanding of a
problem's level and contributing to remedies is applied, guided work — a step
above pure awareness, though this level does not yet determine the resolution
alone.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot distinguish a problem from an incident.
- 1 — Beginner: investigates a problem when told exactly what to look at.
- 2 — Intermediate: investigates a problem unassisted, correctly judges
  whether it is strategic, tactical or operational, and contributes ideas to
  the remedy.
- 3 — Advanced: leads the investigation of a straightforward problem.
- 4 — Expert: spots patterns across multiple problems that others have
  missed.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a problem they investigated and how they
  judged its level.
- Review a problem record or root-cause note they contributed to.
- Ask a referee about the candidate's contribution to a remedy that was
  implemented.

**What the framework expects at this level**

An associate infrastructure engineer shows an awareness of different

**Baseline for this role level**

One or two skills below target is not automatically a fail. Weigh how central

Troubleshooting and problem resolution sit at the centre of the day-to-day
work, while an Information security or Modern development standards gap is
often the most coachable quickly, since the framework only asks this level to
explain the concepts rather than apply them. Record your reasoning, not just
the numbers: a panel or manager reading this later needs to know why a
