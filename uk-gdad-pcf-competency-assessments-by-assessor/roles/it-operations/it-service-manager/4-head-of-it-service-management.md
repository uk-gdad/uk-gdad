# Head of IT Service Management - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This assessment is for an assessor — a hiring panel member, a senior manager,
or a reviewer — judging whether a candidate or an employee holds the baseline
abilities the IT operations profession's **head of IT service management**
role level requires. It is built from the UK GDAD Profession Capability
Framework's own description of this role level: its accountabilities and the
seven skills it names.

The head of IT service management sets the vision and strategy for service
management, and ensures processes are owned and maturing across the
organisation. This assessment turns that description into a structured matrix
an assessor can score against observed or reported evidence, for a hiring
decision, a promotion board, or a capability review against the framework.

This assessment is one input among several into a decision, never the whole of
one. Read `## Advisory` before using it.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has not
been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating on this form
must never be the sole basis for a hiring, promotion, pay, performance or
disciplinary decision. Before using it, consult your organisation's human
resources, people development, occupational psychology or legal compliance
colleagues.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` before you score anything.
Then gather whatever evidence exists: a CV or portfolio, a structured
interview, a work sample or task (for example, reviewing a service management
strategy document the candidate has written), direct observation of the
person leading a senior stakeholder escalation, or references from a peer
head of function or a senior sponsor.

**Score on evidence, not impression.** Every rating in the competency matrix
must trace to something the person did, said, produced, or was reliably
reported to have done. At this seniority, favour evidence that shows a
sustained track record — a strategy that shaped an organisation's service
management maturity over years, a senior relationship rebuilt after a serious
failure — over a single task. If you cannot point to evidence for a rating,
record it as unknown rather than guess.

**Consistency.** Score every candidate for this role level the same way, using
the same evidence sources where possible. For a hiring or promotion decision,
have more than one assessor score independently, then compare notes and
discuss any large gaps before agreeing a final rating.

**Fairness.** An unvalidated, AI-drafted instrument like this one, used
inconsistently, can produce unlawful adverse impact against candidates who
share a protected characteristic — even unintentionally. Consistent,
evidence-based administration by more than one assessor materially reduces
that risk. See [Fair and lawful use](../../../../uk-gdad-pcf-competency-assessments/spec/index.md#fair-and-lawful-use)
for what this means in practice, and never let a rating be influenced by
anything other than demonstrated, job-related evidence.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

This is a **proficiency** scale, not a frequency scale: it rates how well the
person does the thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it.

**Worked example — Strategic thinking.** At 3 — Advanced, a candidate might
describe defining a service management policy for their own area and
evaluating whether it exceeded requirements. At 4 — Expert, a candidate for
head of IT service management should be able to describe leading the design
and implementation of a strategy across the whole service management
function, directing how it was evaluated, and showing how it changed the
organisation's maturity over a sustained period — that scale of leadership,
not just successful delivery of one's own policy, is what the top rating
requires at this level.

## About this role level

The IT operations profession's **IT service manager** role covers the people
who manage the delivery of information and communications technology (ICT)
services and work with IT service operations teams. It has four levels:

1. IT service analyst
2. IT service manager
3. Senior IT service manager
4. **Head of IT service management** — this level

The **head of IT service management** is the most senior level of this role.
They are responsible for setting the vision and strategy for service
management, ensuring processes are owned and maturing across the
organisation.

At this role level, you will:

- lead a team, ensuring adequate resources and capacity
- represent the IT service management function at a senior level
- act as an escalation point for business stakeholders

This role level names seven skills: Asset and configuration management,
Business analysis (IT operations), IT service reporting, Ownership and
initiative, Service management framework knowledge, Stakeholder relationship
management (IT operations), Strategic thinking, and User focus. Unlike the
levels below it, this level's summary does not separately name Service focus
as a skill.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Asset and configuration management

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a head of IT service management to manage configuration
management for the organisation, ensuring value for the business and
adherence to company policies, ensure that changes to assets are recorded and
controlled appropriately, and advocate organisational commitment to asset
control.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. The scope has widened from managing configuration items
and compliance risk at the senior IT service manager level to owning
configuration management "for the organisation" and advocating for it at
policy level — full organisational ownership, matching an expert rating.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Delegates configuration management entirely without setting
  direction or standards.
- 1 — Beginner: Understands organisational configuration policy but does not
  yet shape it.
- 2 — Intermediate: Owns configuration management for a defined part of the
  organisation, with support for the wider policy conversation.
- 3 — Advanced: Sets and defends configuration policy across the
  organisation, ensuring it delivers business value.
- 4 — Expert: Has changed organisational culture around asset control —
  colleagues at every level treat configuration discipline as core to their
  own work because of the advocacy this person has done.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of setting or changing an organisation-wide configuration
  management policy, and how they secured commitment to it.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate is known, across the organisation, as
  an advocate for asset control.

### Skill: Business analysis (IT operations)

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a head of IT service management to lead investigative
work into problems and opportunities in existing processes, lead the
collection of information and creation of recommendations for improvements,
and absorb large amounts of conflicting information and use it to produce
solutions.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. The wording repeats the senior IT service manager
level's, but the head of function is expected to apply it at organisational
scale and to be personally accountable for the recommendations that follow,
matching the framework's escalation-point and vision-setting accountabilities.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Relies entirely on others to resolve conflicting analysis before
  seeing it.
- 1 — Beginner: Can follow an investigation others lead, with support to
  reach a recommendation.
- 2 — Intermediate: Leads investigations of moderate scope independently.
- 3 — Advanced: Leads organisation-wide investigations involving genuinely
  conflicting sources, producing sound recommendations.
- 4 — Expert: Is the person senior stakeholders trust to settle the most
  contested, highest-stakes service management questions the organisation
  faces.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of leading analysis on a genuinely contested,
  organisation-wide service management question.
- Ask how the candidate decided which recommendation to back when the
  evidence was genuinely mixed.

### Skill: IT service reporting

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a head of IT service management to use data analytics
skills to make decisions that enhance business performance.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. The wording is unchanged from the senior IT service
manager level, but at this level the decisions being made from analytics
carry organisational weight and are the head of function's own accountability
to defend.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Makes decisions without reference to service data.
- 1 — Beginner: Uses analytics for individual decisions, with support.
- 2 — Intermediate: Independently uses analytics for decisions of moderate
  scope.
- 3 — Advanced: Uses analytics to make and defend decisions that affect the
  wider service delivery function.
- 4 — Expert: Has built or embedded an analytics capability that
  demonstrably improved organisational performance, and can defend that
  record under senior scrutiny.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of a major, organisation-level decision the candidate
  made using data analytics, and how they defended it under challenge.
- Ask a referee about the candidate's reputation for evidence-based
  decision-making at senior level.

### Skill: Ownership and initiative

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a head of IT service management to take accountability
for issues that occur and be proactive in searching for potential problems,
and to achieve excellent user outcomes.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. The wording is unchanged from the senior IT service
manager level, but this level's explicit accountability to act as an
escalation point for business stakeholders means the ownership expected here
extends across the whole function, not one platform.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Passes escalations back down without taking personal
  accountability.
- 1 — Beginner: Accepts escalations but relies heavily on the team to resolve
  them.
- 2 — Intermediate: Takes accountability for escalated issues of moderate
  complexity.
- 3 — Advanced: Reliably takes accountability for the most serious
  escalations and proactively searches for systemic risk across the function.
- 4 — Expert: Is the final point of accountability the organisation trusts
  when nothing else has worked, and their proactive risk-searching has
  prevented major incidents at organisational scale.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of taking personal accountability for a serious,
  organisation-level service failure, and what the candidate did about it.
- Ask a referee about the candidate's reputation as an escalation point of
  last resort.

### Skill: Service management framework knowledge

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a head of IT service management to show they have an
expert certificate in the service management framework qualification, and to
demonstrate an industry understanding of how to implement the framework into
numerous environments.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. The addition of "industry understanding" and
implementation "into numerous environments" goes beyond the senior IT service
manager's expert qualification, asking for breadth across different
organisational contexts as well as depth.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Holds no expert-level qualification and shows no breadth beyond
  one environment.
- 1 — Beginner: Holds the expert qualification but has only applied it in one
  environment.
- 2 — Intermediate: Has applied the framework in more than one environment,
  with support adapting it.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently adapts the framework to different
  organisational contexts, drawing on broad industry understanding.
- 4 — Expert: Is recognised, inside and outside the organisation, as an
  authority on implementing the framework across varied environments.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for examples of implementing a service management framework in more
  than one type of environment, and what had to change between them.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate is seen as an industry-level authority
  on the framework.

### Skill: Stakeholder relationship management (IT operations)

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a head of IT service management to manage long-term
strategic relationships with stakeholders, identifying where new connections
need to be made and existing ones nurtured, direct the strategic approach for
stakeholder relationships and ensure stakeholder objectives are clearly
represented, act as a point of escalation if stakeholder relationships break
down or become challenging for more junior members of the team, influence
important senior stakeholders and arbitrate when blockers are escalated, and
facilitate discussions across high-risk and complex areas or projects under
constrained timelines.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. This is the fullest and most demanding description of
this skill anywhere in the role — directing strategy, arbitrating escalations
from junior colleagues, and facilitating high-risk work under time pressure —
squarely matching an expert-level baseline.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Manages only their own stakeholder relationships, without setting
  direction for others.
- 1 — Beginner: Manages senior relationships adequately but struggles to
  arbitrate when a junior colleague's relationship breaks down.
- 2 — Intermediate: Directs stakeholder strategy for part of the function,
  with support on the most difficult escalations.
- 3 — Advanced: Directs stakeholder strategy across the function and
  successfully arbitrates most escalated relationship breakdowns.
- 4 — Expert: Is the trusted arbiter for the organisation's most
  high-risk, time-constrained stakeholder situations, and consistently gets a
  workable outcome.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of stepping in to rescue a stakeholder relationship a
  more junior colleague could not manage.
- Ask a referee, ideally a senior business stakeholder, how the candidate is
  regarded when things get difficult.

### Skill: Strategic thinking

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a head of IT service management to lead the design and
implementation of strategy, directing the evaluation of strategies and
policies to ensure business requirements are being met.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "Lead the design and implementation" is the strongest
strategic wording in this role's ladder, matching the level's accountability
for setting the whole function's vision.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Works within a strategy set entirely by others.
- 1 — Beginner: Contributes to strategy design but does not yet lead it.
- 2 — Intermediate: Leads strategy design for a defined part of the function.
- 3 — Advanced: Leads the design and implementation of strategy across the
  function, with some support directing evaluation.
- 4 — Expert: Has led strategy that visibly changed the organisation's
  service management maturity, and personally directs how success is
  evaluated.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a strategy they led from design through to
  implementation and evaluation, and its measurable effect.
- Ask a referee, ideally a sponsor or peer head of function, how the
  candidate's strategic leadership is regarded.

### Skill: User focus

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a head of IT service management to give direction on
which tools or methods to use, demonstrate experience in meeting the needs of
users across a variety of channels, bring insight and expertise in how user
needs have changed over time to ensure they are met by the business, and
apply strategic thinking to provide the best service for the end user.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. This level's wording moves from championing and guiding
user research (below) to directing tools and methods and bringing longitudinal
insight into how needs change — a strategic, organisation-shaping use of user
focus.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Sets service direction without reference to how user needs have
  changed.
- 1 — Beginner: Understands user needs have changed over time, but does not
  yet act on that insight strategically.
- 2 — Intermediate: Directs tool or method choices for a defined part of the
  service.
- 3 — Advanced: Directs tool and method choices across the function, informed
  by longitudinal insight into user needs.
- 4 — Expert: Has used insight into how user needs evolved to change the
  business's overall service strategy, with a demonstrable effect on outcomes.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of using longitudinal insight into changing user needs
  to shape a strategic decision.
- Ask how the candidate has directed tool or method choices across a whole
  function, and why.
