# Junior DevOps Engineer - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This is a competency assessment for the role of Development operations
(DevOps) engineer, at the Junior DevOps engineer level. It is written for an
assessor — a line manager, an interview panel, or a reviewer — who needs to
judge whether a candidate or an employee possesses the baseline abilities this
role level requires, for a hiring decision, a promotion board, onboarding, or
a capability check against the framework.

This assessment is one input among several into that judgement, never the
whole of it. Read `## Advisory` below before you use any part of this
document.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has not
been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating on this form
must never be the sole basis for a hiring, promotion, pay, performance or
disciplinary decision. Before using it, consult your organisation's human
resources, people development, occupational psychology or legal compliance
colleagues.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` before you score anything, so
that every rating is anchored to what a junior DevOps engineer is actually
expected to do. Gather whatever evidence exists before you start: a CV or
portfolio, a structured interview, a work sample or task, direct observation
of the person's day-to-day work, or references.

**Scoring on evidence, not impression.** Every rating in the competency matrix
must trace to something the person did, said, produced, or was observed
doing. If you cannot point to evidence for a rating, record it as unknown
rather than guess.

**Consistency.** Score every candidate for this role level the same way.
Where possible, have more than one assessor score independently and compare
notes afterwards, especially for a hiring or promotion decision.

**Fairness.** This is an unvalidated, AI-drafted instrument. Used
inconsistently, it can produce unlawful adverse impact — treating one
candidate less favourably than another for reasons that have nothing to do
with the job. Consistent, evidence-based administration by more than one
assessor materially reduces that risk. Never let a rating be influenced by
anything other than demonstrated, job-related evidence.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same four-point scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 1 | Awareness | You can describe skill fundamentals and show basic knowledge of related tools |
| 2 | Working | You can apply the skill with some support and use appropriate tools |
| 3 | Practitioner | You can apply the skill independently, determine the best tools, and share your experience |
| 4 | Expert | You can lead organisational best practice and teach advanced techniques |

This is a **proficiency** scale, not a frequency scale: it rates how well the
person does the thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it.

**Worked example — Systems design.** At Awareness (1), a candidate could
describe having watched the team design part of a larger system, without
having contributed themselves. At Working (2) — this level's baseline —
they can describe assisting, as part of a team, in designing a component of a
larger system: naming the component, what it needed to do, and how their
contribution fitted into the wider design. At Practitioner (3), above this
level's baseline, they could describe producing a design largely on their
own, which is more independence than a junior engineer is expected to show.

## About this role level

Development operations (DevOps) engineers — commonly called "DevOps
engineers" — support the development and operation of software through tools,
environments and practices. The role is responsible for underpinning good
development processes, including managing tools and testing environments,
central code control, maintaining development standards, and writing software
that automates systems.

This assessment covers the **Junior DevOps engineer** level, the second step
of the role's ladder. The full ladder, in order, is: Apprentice DevOps
engineer, Junior DevOps engineer (this level), DevOps engineer, Senior DevOps
engineer (technical and management track), Lead DevOps engineer (technical
and management), and Principal DevOps engineer (technical and management).

A junior DevOps engineer learns on the job by building software components.
At this level, a person is expected to:

- assist in the building of a complex story
- work under supervision
- be involved in implementation but not planning

This level names nine skills: Availability and capacity management,
Information security, Modern development standards, Programming and build
(software engineering), Prototyping, Service support, Systems design, Systems
integration, and User focus.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Availability and capacity management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A junior DevOps engineer is expected to be able to explain availability and
capacity management processes.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Awareness. The wording matches the apprentice level's, and this
level's own accountabilities keep the person working under supervision, so
explanatory understanding remains the reasonable expectation.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 1 — Awareness: can explain what availability and capacity management
  processes are for, in their own words.
- 2 — Working: can describe how a specific service they have worked near
  is monitored for availability or capacity, and why that monitoring matters.
- 3 — Practitioner: not expected at this level.
- 4 — Expert: not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to explain why a team tracks a service's capacity before
  it becomes a problem.
- Ask about a service they have been near — even briefly — and what they
  noticed about how its availability was tracked.

**Rate this skill**

- [ ] 1 — Awareness
- [ ] 2 — Working
- [ ] 3 — Practitioner
- [ ] 4 — Expert

### Skill: Information security

**What the framework expects at this level**

A junior DevOps engineer is expected to use information security practices
and available security controls to contribute to protecting solutions and
services.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Working. This is a step up from the apprentice level's
"explain" wording to actually using practices and controls, matching the
junior's move into building software components, albeit still under
supervision.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 1 — Awareness: can name a security control relevant to their work but has
  not yet applied one themselves.
- 2 — Working: has applied an existing security control (for example,
  using a secrets manager instead of hard-coding a credential) while building
  a component, under supervision.
- 3 — Practitioner: independently spots where a security control is missing from
  a piece of work and raises it, without being prompted.
- 4 — Expert: not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a security control they have applied while
  building something, and what it protected against.
- Give a short code sample with an obvious security gap (such as a
  hard-coded password) and ask what they would do about it.

**Rate this skill**

- [ ] 1 — Awareness
- [ ] 2 — Working
- [ ] 3 — Practitioner
- [ ] 4 — Expert

### Skill: Modern development standards

**What the framework expects at this level**

A junior DevOps engineer is expected to explain the most important principles
of modern development standards and how they apply to their work, and to
apply those principles under guidance.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Working. The framework asks for both explanation and
guided application, which sits above pure explanation and matches "knows the
basics and can perform tasks with some guidance."

**Behavioural indicators**

- 1 — Awareness: can explain a development standard in general terms but has
  not applied it themselves.
- 2 — Working: has applied a specific standard (such as a commit message
  convention or a code style rule) to their own work, with guidance available.
- 3 — Practitioner: applies standards consistently without needing to be
  reminded, and can explain why each one exists.
- 4 — Expert: not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- Review a piece of code or a commit history the candidate has produced, and
  check whether it follows a stated standard.
- Ask them to explain, for one standard they follow, what problem it
  prevents.

**Rate this skill**

- [ ] 1 — Awareness
- [ ] 2 — Working
- [ ] 3 — Practitioner
- [ ] 4 — Expert

### Skill: Programming and build (software engineering)

**What the framework expects at this level**

A junior DevOps engineer is expected to design, code, test, correct and
document simple programs or scripts under the direction of others.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Working. This is a step up from the apprentice level's
"basic knowledge" to actually producing simple programs or scripts, matching
the level's "assist in the building of a complex story" accountability.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 1 — Awareness: can follow step-by-step direction to write a very small piece
  of code, with heavy support.
- 2 — Working: can design, code, test, correct and document a simple
  script or program under general direction, needing occasional guidance.
- 3 — Practitioner: can do the same work with minimal direction, and helps
  correct a peer's simple mistakes.
- 4 — Expert: not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- Set a small, well-scoped coding task and observe the candidate's process:
  do they test what they wrote, and can they explain what it does?
- Ask to see a simple script or program they built recently, and walk through
  how they tested and documented it.

**Rate this skill**

- [ ] 1 — Awareness
- [ ] 2 — Working
- [ ] 3 — Practitioner
- [ ] 4 — Expert

### Skill: Prototyping

**What the framework expects at this level**

A junior DevOps engineer is expected to understand when to use a specific
prototyping technique or method, and to show the value of prototyping to
their team.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Awareness. The framework still asks for understanding and
demonstrating value rather than running prototyping activities, so the
baseline stays close to the apprentice level's.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 1 — Awareness: can identify, for a simple example, whether a quick prototype
  would help before committing to a full build.
- 2 — Working: has explained to a colleague or in a team discussion why
  a specific prototype was worth building, and been understood.
- 3 — Practitioner: not expected at this level.
- 4 — Expert: not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- Give a short scenario (a new feature idea) and ask whether they would
  prototype it first, and why.
- Ask about a time they helped a team decide to build a rough version of
  something before the real one.

**Rate this skill**

- [ ] 1 — Awareness
- [ ] 2 — Working
- [ ] 3 — Practitioner
- [ ] 4 — Expert

### Skill: Service support

**What the framework expects at this level**

A junior DevOps engineer is expected to help fix service faults following
agreed procedures, and to carry out maintenance tasks on service support
infrastructure.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Working. This is more independent than the apprentice's
"help under direction" — following agreed procedures without needing each
step spelled out — while still short of leading an investigation.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 1 — Awareness: can follow an agreed procedure step by step with someone
  checking their work as they go.
- 2 — Working: can follow an agreed procedure to help fix a fault, and
  carry out routine maintenance tasks, with only occasional check-ins.
- 3 — Practitioner: adapts a procedure sensibly when a fault does not quite match
  the documented steps, and flags this to the team.
- 4 — Expert: not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to walk through a real fault they helped fix, focusing
  on which agreed procedure they followed.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate carries out routine maintenance tasks
  reliably without close supervision.

**Rate this skill**

- [ ] 1 — Awareness
- [ ] 2 — Working
- [ ] 3 — Practitioner
- [ ] 4 — Expert

### Skill: Systems design

**What the framework expects at this level**

A junior DevOps engineer is expected to assist, as part of a team, in the
design of components of larger systems.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Awareness. "Assist... as part of a team" is a supporting
contribution, not an independent one, so the baseline sits at the low end of
the scale.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 1 — Awareness: has taken part in a team discussion about designing a system
  component, contributing a small idea or observation.
- 2 — Working: has helped produce part of a component's design (for
  example, sketching how two parts connect) under a more senior person's
  direction.
- 3 — Practitioner: not expected at this level.
- 4 — Expert: not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a system component they helped design, and
  what specifically they contributed.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate contributes useful ideas in design
  discussions, even in a supporting role.

**Rate this skill**

- [ ] 1 — Awareness
- [ ] 2 — Working
- [ ] 3 — Practitioner
- [ ] 4 — Expert

### Skill: Systems integration

**What the framework expects at this level**

A junior DevOps engineer is expected to explain the process and principles of
integrating systems, and to describe the challenges of designing, building
and testing interfaces between systems.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Awareness. Explanation and description are asked for, not
hands-on integration work, matching this level's implementation-not-planning
accountability.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 1 — Awareness: can explain, in general terms, what systems integration
  involves and why interfaces between systems are tricky to get right.
- 2 — Working: can describe a specific integration challenge they have
  seen (for example, two systems disagreeing on a data format) and what made
  it hard.
- 3 — Practitioner: not expected at this level.
- 4 — Expert: not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to explain, using a simple example, why connecting two
  systems together is harder than it sounds.
- Ask about any integration work they have observed, and what challenge stood
  out to them.

**Rate this skill**

- [ ] 1 — Awareness
- [ ] 2 — Working
- [ ] 3 — Practitioner
- [ ] 4 — Expert

### Skill: User focus

**What the framework expects at this level**

A junior DevOps engineer is expected to show an awareness or understanding of
user experience analysis and its principles, and to explain the purpose of
user stories and the focus on user needs.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Awareness. Awareness and explanation are asked for, not
conducting research or writing user stories independently.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 1 — Awareness: can explain, in simple terms, what a user story is for and
  why user needs matter to the work.
- 2 — Working: can point to a specific user need that shaped a piece of
  work they were involved in, and explain the connection.
- 3 — Practitioner: not expected at this level.
- 4 — Expert: not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to explain, in their own words, what a user story is and
  why their team uses them.
- Ask about a piece of work they built, and whether they can connect it back
  to a user need.

**Rate this skill**

- [ ] 1 — Awareness
- [ ] 2 — Working
- [ ] 3 — Practitioner
- [ ] 4 — Expert
