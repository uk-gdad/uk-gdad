# Principal DevOps Engineer - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This is a competency assessment for the role of Development operations
(DevOps) engineer, at the Principal DevOps engineer level, **technical
track**. The framework splits this level into two roles — technical and
management — and this document covers the technical track; a separate
document covers the management track for the same numbered level.

It is written for an assessor — a line manager, an interview panel, or a
reviewer — who needs to judge whether a candidate or an employee possesses
the baseline abilities this role level requires, for a hiring decision, a
promotion board, onboarding, or a capability check against the framework.

This assessment is one input among several into that judgement, never the
whole of it. Read `## Advisory` below before you use any part of this
document.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has not
been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating on this form
must never be the sole basis for a hiring, promotion, pay, performance or
disciplinary decision. Before using it, consult your organisation's human
resources, people development, occupational psychology or legal compliance
colleagues.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` before you score anything, so
that every rating is anchored to what a principal DevOps engineer on the
technical track is actually expected to do. Gather whatever evidence exists
before you start: a CV or portfolio, a structured interview, a work sample or
task, direct observation of the person's day-to-day work, or references.

**Scoring on evidence, not impression.** Every rating in the competency matrix
must trace to something the person did, said, produced, or was observed
doing. At this level, expect the strongest evidence to be a track record
rather than a single task — ask for more than one example. If you cannot
point to evidence for a rating, record it as unknown rather than guess.

**Consistency.** Score every candidate for this role level the same way.
Where possible, have more than one assessor score independently and compare
notes afterwards, especially for a hiring or promotion decision.

**Fairness.** This is an unvalidated, AI-drafted instrument. Used
inconsistently, it can produce unlawful adverse impact — treating one
candidate less favourably than another for reasons that have nothing to do
with the job. Consistent, evidence-based administration by more than one
assessor materially reduces that risk. Never let a rating be influenced by
anything other than demonstrated, job-related evidence.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

This is a **proficiency** scale, not a frequency scale: it rates how well the
person does the thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it.

**Worked example — Information security.** At Advanced (3), a candidate could
describe consistently designing solutions with appropriate, threat-specific
security controls across their own work. At Expert (4) — this level's
baseline — they design, review and quality assure other people's solutions
and services for security, and lead others across large or multiple teams in
using appropriate information security practices, not just applying good
practice themselves. Evidence for this should span more than one team's work.

## About this role level

Development operations (DevOps) engineers — commonly called "DevOps
engineers" — support the development and operation of software through tools,
environments and practices. The role is responsible for underpinning good
development processes, including managing tools and testing environments,
central code control, maintaining development standards, and writing software
that automates systems.

This assessment covers the **Principal DevOps engineer** level, technical
track — the top of the role's ladder alongside a parallel management track.
The full ladder, in order, is: Apprentice DevOps engineer, Junior DevOps
engineer, DevOps engineer, Senior DevOps engineer (technical and management),
Lead DevOps engineer (technical and management), and Principal DevOps
engineer (this level, technical track, alongside a separate management
track). There is no level above this one.

A principal DevOps engineer leads and plans development across large or
multiple teams, defining the strategic vision for delivery. At this level, a
person is expected to:

- identify, test and champion the adoption of emerging technologies
- ensure that security, stability and capacity are embedded in the
  development and deployment of services
- develop a team's capability by rewarding high performers
- shape career paths and recruit the right talent
- identify skills gaps and important dependencies within technical teams

This level names ten skills: Availability and capacity management,
Development process optimisation, Information security, Modern development
standards, Programming and build (software engineering), Prototyping, Service
support, Systems design, Systems integration, and User focus.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Availability and capacity management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal DevOps engineer is expected to ensure the correct implementation
of availability and capacity management standards and procedures, to
identify capacity issues and implement the required changes, and to initiate
remedial action.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. This wording is unchanged from the lead level, and
the level's own strategic-vision and multi-team accountabilities support a
strong, independent grasp continuing to be the reasonable expectation.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot describe what standards or procedures govern availability
  and capacity management across the teams they influence.
- 1 — Beginner: knows the standards exist but has not checked they are
  followed.
- 2 — Intermediate: checks standards are followed in one team, with
  guidance.
- 3 — Advanced: ensures correct implementation across large or multiple
  teams, identifies capacity issues before they bite, and initiates remedial
  action.
- 4 — Expert: sets availability and capacity strategy across the whole
  delivery organisation.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a time they identified a capacity issue
  spanning more than one team, and how they resolved it.
- Ask how they ensure availability and capacity standards are embedded
  across large or multiple teams, not just their own.

### Skill: Development process optimisation

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal DevOps engineer is expected to analyse current processes,
identify and implement opportunities to optimise them, lead and develop a
team of experts to deliver service improvements, and help evaluate and
establish requirements for the implementation of changes by setting policy
and standards.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. "Lead and develop a team of experts" and
"setting policy and standards" match this level's strategic-vision and
capability-development accountabilities.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not led a team through a process improvement.
- 1 — Beginner: contributes to a process improvement led by someone else.
- 2 — Intermediate: leads a small, well-defined process improvement with
  guidance.
- 3 — Advanced: analyses current processes, leads and develops a team of
  experts to deliver improvements, and helps set the policy or standards a
  change is implemented against, across large or multiple teams.
- 4 — Expert: sets development process optimisation strategy for the whole
  delivery organisation.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a service improvement they led a team of
  experts to deliver across more than one team.
- Ask about a policy or standard they established that other teams now
  follow.

### Skill: Information security

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal DevOps engineer is expected to design, review and quality assure
solutions and services, ensuring that they are specifically engineered to
mitigate security threats, and to lead others in using appropriate
information security practices.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "Review and quality assure" other people's solutions,
and "lead others" in security practice, go beyond the lead level's personal
design-and-set-expectations wording to full accountability for security
practice across the teams they influence — matching this level's "ensure that
security... [is] embedded in the development and deployment of services"
accountability.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: does not design with security in mind, and does not review
  others' work for it.
- 1 — Beginner: applies security controls to their own work with support.
- 2 — Intermediate: applies controls consistently to their own designs.
- 3 — Advanced: designs solutions with strong security controls and reviews
  a colleague's design occasionally.
- 4 — Expert: designs, reviews and quality assures solutions across large or
  multiple teams for security, and leads others in using appropriate
  information security practices, so that security is embedded rather than
  bolted on.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe how security is embedded — not just
  reviewed at the end — across a large or multi-team delivery they led.
- Ask about a time they led another team through improving its information
  security practice.

### Skill: Modern development standards

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal DevOps engineer is expected to identify and apply modern
development standards to support complex projects and programmes, and to
lead others in applying modern development standards.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. This wording is unchanged from the lead level, and
this level's "identify, test and champion the adoption of emerging

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: does not apply or lead on development standards.
- 1 — Beginner: applies standards to their own work with reminders.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate about a modern development standard or emerging practice
  they championed the adoption of, and the evidence they used to justify it.
- Ask how they led adoption of a standard across teams they did not directly
  manage.

### Skill: Programming and build (software engineering)

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal DevOps engineer is expected to collaborate with others when
necessary to review specifications, and to use the agreed specifications to
design, code, test and document programs or scripts of medium-to-high
complexity, using the right standards and tools.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Worth noting plainly, not smoothing over: this
level's own wording for this skill has not visibly escalated beyond the
senior or lead levels — still "medium-to-high complexity" — so the baseline
holds at Advanced rather than rising to Expert on the strength of this
level's other, more senior accountabilities alone.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot independently build from an agreed specification.
- 1 — Beginner: needs significant support even on medium-complexity work.
- 2 — Intermediate: builds medium-complexity programs with occasional
  guidance.
- 3 — Advanced: designs, codes, tests, corrects and documents programs or
  scripts of medium-to-high complexity independently, and remains a credible
  technical authority despite a largely strategic role.
- 4 — Expert: not required by this level's own wording, though many
  principals retain it from earlier levels.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate about a recent piece of hands-on technical work, to
  confirm they remain a credible technical authority.
- Ask how they balance hands-on credibility against a largely strategic
  workload at this level.

### Skill: Prototyping

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal DevOps engineer is expected to use a variety of prototyping
methods, to share best practice and coach others, and to look at strategic
service design end to end.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "Share best practice and coach others" and "look at
strategic service design end to end" are a clear escalation beyond the lead
level's team-activity wording, matching full mastery and this level's
strategic-vision accountability.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not coached anyone on prototyping practice.
- 1 — Beginner: uses one prototyping method without adapting it to the
  situation.
- 2 — Intermediate: uses a variety of methods with guidance on which fits a
  situation.
- 3 — Advanced: chooses appropriate methods independently and shares
  practice informally.
- 4 — Expert: uses a variety of prototyping methods, actively coaches others
  in choosing and running them, and looks at strategic service design end to
  end rather than one component at a time.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a time they coached someone else on choosing
  or running a prototyping method.
- Ask how they have approached prototyping a service end to end, rather than
  one part of it.

### Skill: Service support

A principal DevOps engineer is expected to identify, locate and fix complex

service support.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. This matches the senior and lead levels' wording, and

this level's own accountabilities are largely strategic.

- 1 — Beginner: needs significant support to diagnose a complex fault.
- 2 — Intermediate: diagnoses complex faults with some guidance.

  faults.

**Evidence to gather**

### Skill: Systems design

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal DevOps engineer is expected to design systems characterised by
medium levels of risk, impact, and business or technical complexity, to
select appropriate design standards, methods and tools and ensure they are
applied effectively, and to review the systems designs of others to ensure
the selection of appropriate technology, efficient use of resources and
integration of multiple systems and technology.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Worth noting plainly, not smoothing over: this
level's own wording for this skill has not visibly escalated beyond the
senior or lead levels either, so the baseline holds at Advanced.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot design a system of medium risk or complexity
  independently.
- 1 — Beginner: designs simple systems with heavy support.
- 2 — Intermediate: designs medium-complexity systems with some guidance, and
  does not yet review others' work.
- 3 — Advanced: independently designs systems of medium risk, impact and
  complexity, and reviews others' designs across large or multiple teams.
- 4 — Expert: not required by this level's own wording, though evidence of
  it should not count against a candidate.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask to see a design the candidate reviewed for a team they did not
  directly manage, and what they changed.
- Ask about a design decision that balanced risk, impact and complexity
  across more than one system.

### Skill: Systems integration

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal DevOps engineer is expected to establish standards and
procedures across a service product life cycle, including the development
product life cycle, ensuring that practitioners adhere to these, and to
manage resources to ensure that the systems integration function works
effectively.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. This matches the lead level's escalated wording, and
establishing standards across a whole product life cycle and managing
resources for the function fits this level's strategic-vision
accountability.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot describe what standards or procedures govern systems
  integration across their teams.
- 1 — Beginner: follows established standards without having set any.
- 2 — Intermediate: proposes a small standard or procedure with guidance.
- 3 — Advanced: establishes standards and procedures for part of a service
  product life cycle, and checks adherence.
- 4 — Expert: establishes standards and procedures across a full service
  product life cycle, ensures practitioners adhere to them across large or
  multiple teams, and manages resources so the systems integration function
  works effectively.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a standard or procedure they established
  across a product life cycle that spans more than one team.
- Ask how they resourced systems integration work to prevent it becoming a
  bottleneck across teams.

### Skill: User focus

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal DevOps engineer is expected to collaborate with user researchers
and represent users internally, to explain the difference between user needs
and the desires of the user, to champion user research to focus on all
users, to prioritise and define approaches to understand the user story
while guiding others in doing so, and to offer recommendations on the best
tools and methods to use.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. This matches the senior and lead levels' wording, and
a strong, independent grasp remains the reasonable expectation, evidenced at
this level across large or multiple teams rather than a single one.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: does not represent user needs in decisions across the teams they
  influence.
- 1 — Beginner: uses user evidence when given it, but does not champion
  research.
- 2 — Intermediate: engages with user researchers and uses evidence, with
  some guidance on prioritising approaches.
- 3 — Advanced: represents users internally, champions research across large
  or multiple teams, and guides others on the right approach to understanding
  a user story.
- 4 — Expert: not required by this level's own wording.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate about a time they championed user research that shaped a
  strategic decision spanning more than one team.
- Ask how they guide others across teams on distinguishing user needs from
  stated user desires.
