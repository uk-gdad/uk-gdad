# Lead Quality Assurance Test Analyst - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This assessment is for an assessor — a line manager, a hiring panel member, or
a reviewer — who needs to judge whether a candidate or employee has the
baseline abilities required for the lead quality assurance test analyst role
level. It measures demonstrated skill against the UK GDAD Profession
Capability Framework's own description of this role level, working through a
competency matrix of the skills the framework names.

A lead quality assurance test analyst sets the strategy for test analysis and
influences test analysis practices across a broad area. They develop, monitor
and evaluate test analysis standards and methods, and make strategic
decisions that improve test analysis in the organisation. This is the top of
the quality assurance test analyst ladder — the level at which someone sets
direction for other testers and for the organisation's approach to test
analysis, rather than only guiding a defined area.

This assessment is one input among several into a hiring, promotion or
development decision. It is never the whole of that decision — read
`## Advisory` below before using it.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating on this
form must never be the sole basis for a hiring, promotion, pay, performance or
disciplinary decision. Before using it, consult your organisation's human
resources, people development, occupational psychology or legal compliance
colleagues.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` before you score anything, so
you know what this level is actually accountable for. Gather whatever
evidence exists before you start: a CV or portfolio, notes from a structured
interview, a work sample or task, direct observation of the person leading or
mentoring others, or references — for this level, a track record over time
often tells you more than a single task. Do not score from memory of a
general impression.

**Scoring on evidence, not impression.** Every rating in the competency
matrix must trace to something the person did, said, produced, or was
observed doing. If you cannot point to evidence for a rating, record it as
unknown rather than guess — an unknown is honest; a guess dressed up as a
score is not.

**Consistency.** Score every candidate for this role level the same way,
using the same evidence sources where you can. Where possible, have more than
one assessor score independently and compare notes afterwards, especially for
a hiring or promotion decision — independent scoring surfaces disagreement
before it hardens into a single, unchallenged view.

**Fairness.** This is an unvalidated, AI-drafted instrument. Used
inconsistently — different evidence standards for different people, or
ratings influenced by anything other than demonstrated, job-related
behaviour — it can produce unlawful adverse impact under the Equality Act
2010. Consistent, evidence-based administration by more than one assessor
materially reduces that risk. See [Fair and lawful use](#fair-and-lawful-use)
in the project specification for what this rules out.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point scale.
This is a **proficiency** scale — how well the person does the thing, on the
evidence available — not a frequency scale of how often they do it.

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

**Worked example — Test and quality planning.** At 3 (Advanced), a candidate
develops and implements a quality testing approach for a defined area with a
team and contributes a genuine idea to the wider organisational strategy — but
someone else still owns that organisational strategy. At 4 (Expert), the level
expected here, they create and manage multiple quality testing plans,
approaches and strategies at once, lead and guide multiple teams in adopting
a shared strategy, and guide teams across the organisation in optimising
their approach using data. The evidence that separates 3 from 4 is scope and
ownership: has the person shaped a strategy for their own team, or set and
carried a strategy across several teams that did not report to them and had
to be persuaded, not instructed?

## About this role level

A quality assurance test analyst interprets testing needs, creating and
executing appropriate tests to ensure quality and alignment with
requirements. Across the role, this means planning, designing and executing
tests; applying testing techniques and standards to learn about systems and
mitigate risk; analysing artefacts and prototypes; testing outcomes and
recording defects; seeking to improve testing practices to enhance quality
and reliability; and recommending testing approaches.

The role has four levels, in order: associate quality assurance test
analyst, quality assurance test analyst, senior quality assurance test
analyst, and **lead quality assurance test analyst** (this level). This is
the top rung: a lead quality assurance test analyst sets the strategy for
test analysis and influences test analysis practices across a broad area,
developing, monitoring and evaluating test analysis standards and methods,
and making strategic decisions that improve test analysis in the
organisation. Where the senior level guides a defined area, this level sets
direction and standards across the whole organisation and mentors the
practitioners below them.

At this role level, a person will:

- lead on setting standards and good practices across the test analysis
  community
- lead and guide teams across the organisation in test analysis
- define and implement test strategies
- mentor and coach other quality assurance test analysts
- negotiate and escalate major risks, issues and defects

The framework names six skills at this level:

- Communicating between the technical and non-technical
- Designing and executing tests
- Managing, reporting and resolving defects
- Test analysis
- Test and quality planning
- Test engineering

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What the framework expects at this level**

At this level, a person is expected to mediate between people and strengthen
relationships, adopting the appropriate communication method with
stakeholders at all levels, manage stakeholder expectations and moderate
difficult discussions about high-risk and complex topics even within
constrained timescales, and speak on behalf of, and represent the community
to, large audiences inside and outside the organisation.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Representing the whole test analysis community to large
internal and external audiences, and mediating high-risk discussions under
time pressure, is full mastery — this is not something a person works
towards while being coached, it is what they are trusted to do unsupervised
at scale.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Avoids representing the team or community beyond their immediate
  colleagues.
- 1 — Beginner: Can present testing outcomes to a familiar audience with
  preparation and support.
- 2 — Intermediate: Manages routine communication with senior stakeholders
  independently, but relies on others for genuinely high-stakes moments.
- 3 — Advanced: Moderates a difficult discussion about a high-risk defect
  with senior stakeholders under time pressure, and reaches a resolution.
- 4 — Expert: Represents the test analysis community at an organisation-wide
  or external forum, and is the person colleagues ask to mediate the most
  difficult stakeholder relationships in the organisation.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask a structured interview question: "Tell me about representing your
  discipline to an audience outside your immediate team or organisation.
  What was at stake?"
- Ask for a work sample: a briefing, a talk, or a written communication the
  candidate produced for a large or senior audience.
- Ask a referee whether the person is sought out specifically to mediate
  difficult stakeholder situations, and by whom.

### Skill: Designing and executing tests

**What the framework expects at this level**

At this level, a person is expected to set standards and influence
organisational decisions for test types, techniques, design and execution,
coach others in test types, techniques, design and execution, advocate for
continuous improvement and refinement of test types and techniques, and make
strategic decisions on new or improved test types and techniques used in
their area.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Setting organisational standards, coaching others, and
making strategic technique decisions are all mastery-level accountabilities
that shape how other people, not just the person themselves, design and
execute tests.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has no influence over testing standards beyond their own work.
- 1 — Beginner: Suggests a technique informally but does not set or change
  standards.
- 2 — Intermediate: Contributes to a team's testing standard when asked, and
  applies it consistently themselves.
- 3 — Advanced: Leads test design and technique choices for a defined area
  and coaches individual testers within it.
- 4 — Expert: Sets an organisation-wide standard for test design or
  technique, gets it adopted across multiple teams that do not report to
  them, and coaches senior testers through applying it.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask a structured interview question: "Tell me about a testing standard or
  approach you set that was adopted beyond your own team."
- Ask for a work sample: an organisational testing standard, framework, or
  strategy document the candidate authored.
- Ask a referee whether the person's decisions about test technique have
  shaped practice across the organisation, not just their own team.

### Skill: Managing, reporting and resolving defects

**What the framework expects at this level**

At this level, a person is expected to lead and coach others in improving
test and defect management processes, support others in assessing complex
and challenging defects across the organisation, lead and coach others in
using defect patterns and trends to make tactical and strategic
recommendations, and influence improvements to quality processes informed by
defect patterns and trends.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Every bullet is about leading, coaching or influencing
other people's defect management practice at an organisational scale, not
managing the person's own defects — the definition of full mastery on this
scale.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Manages only the defects directly assigned to them.
- 1 — Beginner: Helps a colleague think through a complex defect when asked.
- 2 — Intermediate: Manages complex, cross-team defects independently within
  a defined area.
- 3 — Advanced: Coaches another senior tester through assessing a complex,
  organisation-spanning defect.
- 4 — Expert: Uses defect trend data to influence an organisation-wide
  quality process change, and coaches multiple teams' testers in applying the
  new approach.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask a structured interview question: "Tell me about a process change you
  drove based on defect patterns or trends. How did you get it adopted
  beyond your own team?"
- Ask for a work sample: an analysis of defect trends and the resulting
  recommendation, or a process document the candidate authored.
- Ask a referee whether the person is asked to advise on the organisation's
  most complex or contentious defects.

### Skill: Test analysis

**What the framework expects at this level**

At this level, a person is expected to lead and guide multiple teams in test
analysis, ensuring it is implemented early in the life cycle, advocate for
risk-based analysis to drive improvements across many teams, and set
standards and principles for test analysis across the organisation.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Leading multiple teams and setting organisation-wide
standards and principles is the clearest possible expression of mastery on
this scale — the person defines what good test analysis looks like for
others, rather than practising it well themselves.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Analyses only work directly assigned to them.
- 1 — Beginner: Applies test analysis principles someone else set, without
  question.
- 2 — Intermediate: Leads test analysis within a single defined area,
  applying an existing organisational standard.
- 3 — Advanced: Guides more than one team in test analysis and coaches
  senior colleagues through complex analysis.
- 4 — Expert: Sets the organisation's principles for test analysis, gets
  early involvement embedded across multiple teams' ways of working, and can
  point to a measurable improvement that resulted.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask a structured interview question: "Tell me about setting a principle or
  standard for test analysis that multiple teams adopted. What changed as a
  result?"
- Ask for a work sample: an organisational test analysis standard or set of
  principles the candidate authored.
- Ask a referee whether the person has shaped how test analysis happens
  across the organisation, not just within one team.

### Skill: Test and quality planning

**What the framework expects at this level**

At this level, a person is expected to create and manage multiple quality
testing plans, approaches and strategies, lead and guide multiple teams in
adopting quality testing strategy, advocate for early quality testing
involvement in organisational delivery processes, and guide teams across an
organisation in optimising quality testing approaches, plans and strategies
using appropriate data.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Managing multiple strategies at once and guiding teams
across the whole organisation is a scope and ownership level beyond
independent working — this is setting direction others follow.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has no involvement in strategy beyond their own team's plan.
- 1 — Beginner: Contributes an idea to a strategy owned by someone senior.
- 2 — Intermediate: Implements an organisational testing strategy within
  their own defined area.
- 3 — Advanced: Develops a quality testing strategy for a defined area and
  influences the wider organisational one.
- 4 — Expert: Owns and manages several quality testing strategies at once
  across the organisation, and uses data to guide multiple teams in
  optimising their own approaches.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask a structured interview question: "Tell me about a quality testing
  strategy you set for more than one team. How did you get it adopted, and
  how did you know it worked?"
- Ask for a work sample: an organisational quality testing strategy the
  candidate created or owned.
- Ask a referee whether the person has managed testing strategy across more
  than one team or area at the same time.

### Skill: Test engineering

**What the framework expects at this level**

At this level, the framework repeats the wording used at every level below
it: a person is expected to explain why testing processes, environments and
tools are important, follow test engineering practices and standards with
support, and support the maintenance of automated tests and tools required
for testing.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. The framework has not differentiated this specific
skill's wording anywhere in this role's ladder, so the text on its own gives
no basis for an Expert baseline. This assessment sets the baseline at Advanced
rather than higher, reflecting that a lead is accountable for setting
standards and leading teams across the organisation — which requires a
strong, largely independent grasp of the engineering underneath testing to
speak with authority — without claiming the framework evidences hands-on
mastery it has never described for this role.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has no working knowledge of test environments or tooling.
- 1 — Beginner: Explains why testing tools and environments matter, but has
  limited hands-on experience.
- 2 — Intermediate: Sets up and troubleshoots routine test environment or
  tooling problems independently.
- 3 — Advanced: Diagnoses non-obvious engineering problems across more than
  one team's testing setup, and advises other teams on fixing them.
- 4 — Expert: Is the organisation's go-to authority for the hardest test
  engineering problems, and actively builds or redesigns the tooling other
  teams rely on.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask a structured interview question: "Tell me about a test engineering
  problem you helped another team solve, not just your own."
- Ask for a work sample or observation: evidence of the candidate advising on
  or improving test tooling or environments beyond a single team.
- Ask a referee whether other teams come to the person for test engineering
  authority, and what kind of problems they bring.

The baseline for this role level is normally met when every skill is rated at
or above its baseline target. One or two skills rated below target is not
automatically a fail: weigh how central that skill is to this level's
accountabilities (a shortfall in setting standards, mentoring, or leading
test analysis strikes at the core of what a lead exists to do; a shortfall
in test engineering, which the framework still frames modestly at every
level of this role, is more likely a smaller gap around a capable person),
and consider whether it is coachable quickly or points to a genuine mismatch
with the level. Record your reasoning, not just the numbers — the notes
matter more than the table for anyone reading this later, and at this
seniority the evidence is often a track record rather than a single task, so
say what that track record actually showed.
