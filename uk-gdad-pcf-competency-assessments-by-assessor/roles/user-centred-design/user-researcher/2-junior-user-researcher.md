# Junior User Researcher - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This assessment is for the person judging whether a candidate or an employee meets the
baseline a UK Government Digital and Data (GDAD) junior user researcher needs. It is
written for an assessor — a line manager, a panel member, or a reviewer — not for the
person being assessed.

User researchers plan, design and carry out research activities with users, helping
teams get a deep understanding of the people who use government services, so that
policy, propositions, services, content and interaction design work well for users and
achieve policy intent. A junior user researcher is embedded in a multidisciplinary team
to carry out user research activities, working in combination with a more senior user
researcher. This assessment measures demonstrated capability against the framework's
own description of that level, across the seven skills the framework names for it.

It is one input among several into a hiring, promotion or development decision, never
the whole of one — see `## Advisory` for why.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for educational
purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has not been piloted, normed
or checked for adverse impact — and a rating on this form must never be the sole basis
for a hiring, promotion, pay, performance or disciplinary decision. Before using it,
consult your organisation's human resources, people development, occupational
psychology or legal compliance colleagues.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` before you score anything. Gather
whatever evidence exists: a CV or portfolio, a structured interview, a work sample or
task, direct observation, or references. This is the second of six levels the framework
publishes for this role, and the framework describes it as having "some practical
experience but need[ing] regular guidance and training" — expect evidence of small,
guided contributions to real or training research activities, working alongside a more
senior researcher, rather than evidence of leading a project alone.

**Scoring on evidence, not impression.** Every rating must trace to something the
person did, said, produced, or was observed doing. If you cannot point to evidence for a
rating, record it as unknown rather than guess. A low rating at this level is a normal,
expected result, not a red flag on its own.

**Consistency.** Score every candidate for this role level the same way, using the same
kinds of evidence where possible. For a hiring or promotion decision, have more than one
assessor score independently before comparing notes.

**Fairness.** Never let a rating be influenced by anything other than demonstrated,
job-related evidence. See [Fair and lawful use](../../../../uk-gdad-pcf-competency-assessments/spec/index.md#fair-and-lawful-use)
in the project specification for what this rules out.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point proficiency scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

This rates how well the person does the thing, on the evidence available — not how often
they do it. A worked example, using this level's skill of User research methods:

- **1 — Beginner**: they could explain a basic user research method, such as an
  interview or a usability test, but had not yet applied one to a real project.
- **2 — Intermediate**: with support from a more senior researcher, they planned and
  conducted part of a user research project, applied a basic method correctly, and
  involved their team in the research activity.

A junior user researcher's baseline for this skill, below, is 2.

## About this role level

**The role: user researcher.** User researchers plan, design and carry out research
activities with users, helping teams get a deep understanding of the people who use
government services. This research informs policy, proposition, service, content and
interaction design, so that services work well for users and achieve policy intent.

**The level: junior user researcher.** The framework describes this level as embedded
in a multidisciplinary team to carry out user research activities. It is the second of
six levels the framework publishes for this role, above associate user researcher and
below user researcher, senior user researcher, lead user researcher, and head of user
research. At this level, you have some practical experience but need regular guidance
and training to produce your best work and develop your skills, and you work in
combination with a more senior user researcher.

**The skills this level names**, in the summary's order: Agile research practices;
Analysis and synthesis; Inclusive research; Research management, leadership and
assurance; Stakeholder relationship management; User research methods; and
User-centred practice and advocacy. Five of these — Agile research practices,
Inclusive research, Research management, leadership and assurance, Stakeholder
relationship management, and User-centred practice and advocacy — carry text identical
to the associate user researcher level below; only Analysis and synthesis and User
research methods have new, more developed text at this level, and that is where the
real step up from associate to junior shows.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Agile research practices

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a junior user researcher to describe agile working and the roles
in a multidisciplinary digital team. This wording is identical to the associate user
researcher level below.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. Because the text repeats the associate level's wording word for
word, the honest baseline stays the same as that level — this level's real step up
shows in Analysis and synthesis and User research methods, and in this level's own
accountability to work in combination with a more senior researcher, not in a claim
this text does not make.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot describe what agile working means or name any roles in a
  multidisciplinary digital team.
- 1 — Beginner: describes agile working in general terms, and can name some roles in
  a multidisciplinary digital team, such as a delivery manager or a developer.
- 2 — Intermediate: describes agile working accurately and explains how at least one
  named role, such as a user researcher, fits into it, with some prompting.
- 3 — Advanced: independently explains how agile working and the roles in a
  multidisciplinary team affect how user research gets planned and used.
- 4 — Expert: is not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "What is agile working, and which roles have you
  worked alongside in a multidisciplinary digital team?"
- Ask about a sprint or ceremony they took part in, and what they noticed about how
  different roles contributed.
- Ask them to describe how a more senior colleague explained agile working to them,
  and what stuck.

### Skill: Analysis and synthesis

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a junior user researcher to understand how to apply basic
techniques for the analysis of research data and synthesis of findings; effectively
involve their team in analysis and synthesis; and present clear findings that
colleagues can understand and use.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. This level's text is a genuine step up from the associate
level's mere "awareness" wording — it now asks for applying basic techniques and
presenting findings, with the framing "with support" implicit in a level that "needs
regular guidance and training".

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot apply a basic analysis technique to research data, even with
  support.
- 1 — Beginner: has watched a more senior researcher analyse data and synthesise
  findings, without applying a technique themselves.
- 2 — Intermediate: applies a basic analysis technique to research data with support,
  involves their team in the analysis, and presents findings colleagues can understand.
- 3 — Advanced: independently applies a range of analysis techniques and produces
  clear findings without close support.
- 4 — Expert: is not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Tell me about a time you helped analyse research
  data and turned it into a finding your team could use."
- Ask for a findings document or presentation they contributed to, if available.
- Ask how they involved the wider team in an analysis session, and what that looked
  like.

- [ ] 1 — Beginner
- [ ] 2 — Intermediate
- [ ] 3 — Advanced

### Skill: Inclusive research

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a junior user researcher to demonstrate some awareness of the
diversity of users of government services, and of the need to make government services
usable and accessible for everyone. This wording is identical to the associate user
researcher level below.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 0 — None. The text repeats the associate level's wording word for word, so
the honest baseline stays the same — an awareness expectation, not yet an applied one.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: shows no awareness of the diversity of users of government services.
- 1 — Beginner: can name, when asked, some ways users of government services differ
  from each other and why accessibility matters.
- 2 — Intermediate: has taken part in a research session that included a user with an
  access need or a diverse background, with support from a more senior researcher.
- 3 — Advanced: is not expected at this level.
- 4 — Expert: is not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Tell me about a research session you took part in
  with a user whose needs were different from your own. What did you notice?"
- Ask about a course, talk, or piece of reading on accessibility or inclusive research
  they have engaged with.
- Ask them to describe one way a government service might not work well for someone
  different from themselves.

### Skill: Research management, leadership and assurance

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a junior user researcher to explain the importance of working to a
user research plan, and of following standards for user research. This wording is
identical to the associate user researcher level below.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. The text repeats the associate level's wording word for word,
so the honest baseline stays the same — explaining why a plan and standards matter,
not yet leading or assuring one.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot explain why a user research plan or research standards matter.
- 1 — Beginner: explains, in general terms, why working to a user research plan
  matters, and why following standards for user research is important.
- 2 — Intermediate: explains the importance of a plan and standards with a specific
  example from a project they were part of.
- 3 — Advanced: is not expected at this level.
- 4 — Expert: is not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Why does a research plan matter on a project
  you've been part of, and what standards did the team follow?"
- Ask them to describe a time following the plan or a standard changed how a piece of
  research was carried out.
- Ask a referee — ideally the more senior researcher they worked with — whether the
  candidate understood the plan and standards they were working to.

### Skill: User research methods

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a junior user researcher to plan and conduct a user research
project, with support; understand when to use basic user research methods and how to
apply them correctly; and effectively involve their team in research activities.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. This level's text is a genuine step up from the associate
level's "explain some basic methods" — it now asks for planning and conducting a
project, applying methods correctly, with the framework's own wording making explicit
that this is done "with support".

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot apply a basic user research method, even with support.
- 1 — Beginner: has observed a research session run by a more senior researcher,
  without planning or conducting one themselves.
- 2 — Intermediate: plans and conducts a user research project with support, applies a
  basic method correctly, and involves their team in research activities.
- 3 — Advanced: independently plans and conducts a research project using a range of
  basic methods, without needing close support.
- 4 — Expert: is not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Tell me about a research project you helped plan
  and conduct. What method did you use, and what support did you have?"
- Ask for a discussion guide, moderation script, or research plan they contributed to.
- Ask a referee — ideally the more senior researcher they worked with — how much
  support the candidate needed to conduct a session correctly.

### Skill: User-centred practice and advocacy

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a junior user researcher to describe how government services and
technology affect how people behave, and to explain how user-centred practices are
used to build services that adapt to changing user behaviour and needs. This wording
is identical to the associate user researcher level below.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. The text repeats the associate level's wording word for word,
so the honest baseline stays the same — describing and explaining, not yet advocating
independently.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot describe how a government service or technology affects how people
  behave.
- 1 — Beginner: describes, in general terms, how a government service or technology
  can affect how people behave, and explains why user-centred practices matter.
- 2 — Intermediate: gives a specific example, from a project they were part of, of a
  service that changed because of user-centred practice.
- 3 — Advanced: is not expected at this level.
- 4 — Expert: is not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Can you describe a service you researched, and how
  user-centred practice shaped how it changed?"
- Ask about a moment on a project where a research insight changed a team's mind
  about how users would behave.
- Ask them to describe why a service that ignores how people behave might fail.

One or two skills below target is not automatically a fail. Weigh how central the skill
is to this level's core purpose — carrying out user research activities embedded in a
multidisciplinary team, working in combination with a more senior researcher — consider
whether the shortfall is coachable quickly, and record the reasoning behind the
determination, not only the number.
