# Associate User Researcher - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This assessment is for the person judging whether a candidate or an employee meets the
baseline a UK Government Digital and Data (GDAD) associate user researcher needs. It is
written for an assessor — a line manager, a panel member, or a reviewer — not for the
person being assessed.

User researchers plan, design and carry out research activities with users, helping
teams get a deep understanding of the people who use government services, so that
policy, propositions, services, content and interaction design work well for users and
achieve policy intent. An associate user researcher is a trainee in an entry-level
role, working with more experienced user researchers, who needs to show an
understanding of the role and potential rather than demonstrated independent practice.
This assessment measures demonstrated capability against the framework's own
description of that level, across the seven skills the framework names for it.

It is one input among several into a hiring, promotion or development decision, never
the whole of one — see `## Advisory` for why.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for educational
purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has not been piloted, normed
or checked for adverse impact — and a rating on this form must never be the sole basis
for a hiring, promotion, pay, performance or disciplinary decision. Before using it,
consult your organisation's human resources, people development, occupational
psychology or legal compliance colleagues.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` before you score anything. Gather
whatever evidence exists: a CV or portfolio, a structured interview, a work sample or
task, direct observation, or references. This is the most junior of six levels the
framework publishes for this role, and the framework itself describes it as needing
guidance and training to produce good work — expect evidence of potential and of
understanding the role, from academic projects or training exercises as much as from
paid work, rather than evidence of independent delivery.

**Scoring on evidence, not impression.** Every rating must trace to something the
person did, said, produced, or was observed doing. If you cannot point to evidence for a
rating, record it as unknown rather than guess. A low rating at this level is a normal,
expected result, not a red flag on its own.

**Consistency.** Score every candidate for this role level the same way, using the same
kinds of evidence where possible. For a hiring or promotion decision, have more than one
assessor score independently before comparing notes.

**Fairness.** Never let a rating be influenced by anything other than demonstrated,
job-related evidence. See [Fair and lawful use](../../../../uk-gdad-pcf-competency-assessments/spec/index.md#fair-and-lawful-use)
in the project specification for what this rules out.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point proficiency scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

This rates how well the person does the thing, on the evidence available — not how often
they do it. A worked example, using this level's skill of Research management,
leadership and assurance:

- **0 — None**: they could not say why a user research plan matters, or what
  following a standard for research is for.
- **1 — Beginner**: in a training exercise or interview, they explained why working to
  a user research plan matters, and why following standards for user research is
  important, even though they have not yet run a project of their own.

An associate user researcher's baseline for this skill, below, is 1.

## About this role level

**The role: user researcher.** User researchers plan, design and carry out research
activities with users, helping teams get a deep understanding of the people who use
government services. This research informs policy, proposition, service, content and
interaction design, so that services work well for users and achieve policy intent.

**The level: associate user researcher.** The framework describes this level as a
trainee in an entry-level role, working with more experienced user researchers, who
needs to have an understanding of the role and show potential, while needing guidance
and training to produce good work and develop their skills. It is the first, most
junior of six levels the framework publishes for this role, below junior user
researcher, user researcher, senior user researcher, lead user researcher, and head of
user research. The summary for this level has no separate "At this role level, you
will" accountability list — the level description above is the whole of what the
framework states for it.

**The skills this level names**, in the summary's order: Agile research practices;
Analysis and synthesis; Inclusive research; Research management, leadership and
assurance; Stakeholder relationship management; User research methods; and
User-centred practice and advocacy.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Agile research practices

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks an associate user researcher to describe agile working and the
roles in a multidisciplinary digital team.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. Being able to describe agile working and its roles is
knowledge someone can hold before doing the job unsupervised, which is exactly what
this entry-level, needs-guidance level requires.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot describe what agile working means or name any roles in a
  multidisciplinary digital team.
- 1 — Beginner: describes agile working in general terms, and can name some roles in
  a multidisciplinary digital team, such as a delivery manager or a developer.
- 2 — Intermediate: describes agile working accurately and explains how at least one
  named role, such as a user researcher, fits into it, with some prompting.
- 3 — Advanced: independently explains how agile working and the roles in a
  multidisciplinary team affect how user research gets planned and used.
- 4 — Expert: is not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "What is agile working, and which roles have you
  come across in a multidisciplinary digital team?"
- Ask about a training course, university module, or team induction that covered agile
  working, and what they took from it.
- Ask them to describe a team they have been part of, even in a non-work setting, and
  identify roles that resemble those in a digital team.

### Skill: Analysis and synthesis

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks an associate user researcher to show an awareness of the need for
careful analysis of research data to produce clear findings.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 0 — None. This is an awareness expectation, not a describe-or-explain one —
the wording is lighter here than for this level's other skills, so the honest baseline
is that no demonstrated skill is expected yet, only that awareness be visible when
asked.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: shows no awareness that research data needs careful analysis before it
  produces clear findings.
- 1 — Beginner: can say, when asked, that research data needs careful analysis to
  produce clear findings, without yet having tried it.
- 2 — Intermediate: has helped analyse a small, simple data set as part of a training
  exercise or a supervised task.
- 3 — Advanced: is not expected at this level.
- 4 — Expert: is not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Why do you think careful analysis matters when
  turning research data into findings?"
- Ask for an example, from a course or a work-shadowing experience, of seeing analysis
  done poorly or well, and what the difference was.
- Ask them to look at a short, anonymised extract of research notes and say what they
  would want to check before drawing a conclusion from it.

**Rate this skill**

- [ ] 0 — None
- [ ] 1 — Beginner
- [ ] 2 — Intermediate
- [ ] 3 — Advanced
- [ ] 4 — Expert

### Skill: Inclusive research

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks an associate user researcher to demonstrate some awareness of the
diversity of users of government services, and of the need to make government services
usable and accessible for everyone.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 0 — None. As with Analysis and synthesis, this is framed as an awareness
expectation rather than a describe-or-explain one, so the honest baseline is that no
demonstrated skill is required yet.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: shows no awareness of the diversity of users of government services.
- 1 — Beginner: can name, when asked, some ways users of government services differ
  from each other and why accessibility matters.
- 2 — Intermediate: has taken part in a research session, or a training exercise,
  that included a user with an access need or a diverse background, with support.
- 3 — Advanced: is not expected at this level.
- 4 — Expert: is not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Why does it matter that government services work
  for people with very different needs and backgrounds?"
- Ask about a course, talk, or piece of reading on accessibility or inclusive design
  they have engaged with.
- Ask them to describe one way a government service might not work well for someone
  different from themselves.

- [ ] 2 — Intermediate

### Skill: Research management, leadership and assurance

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks an associate user researcher to explain the importance of working
to a user research plan, and of following standards for user research.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. Explaining why a plan and standards matter is knowledge-level
demonstration appropriate to an entry-level, needs-guidance level.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot explain why a user research plan or research standards matter.
- 1 — Beginner: explains, in general terms, why working to a user research plan
  matters, and why following standards for user research is important.
- 2 — Intermediate: explains the importance of a plan and standards with a specific
  example of what could go wrong without them.
- 3 — Advanced: is not expected at this level.
- 4 — Expert: is not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Why does a user researcher need a research plan,
  and why follow standards for how research is done?"
- Ask about a course or training exercise that covered research planning or standards.
- Ask them to describe what might go wrong on a research project that had no plan.

- [ ] 1 — Beginner
- [ ] 2 — Intermediate
- [ ] 3 — Advanced
- [ ] 4 — Expert

### Skill: Stakeholder relationship management

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks an associate user researcher to describe who their stakeholders
are and the importance of managing relationships with them, and to explain what their
stakeholders find important and why.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. Describing and explaining who stakeholders are, and what
matters to them, is knowledge-level demonstration appropriate to this entry level.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot describe who a stakeholder in a research project might be.
- 1 — Beginner: describes who stakeholders typically are on a research project, and
  explains in general terms why managing relationships with them matters.
- 2 — Intermediate: explains what one type of stakeholder finds important and why,
  drawing on a specific example from a course or a supervised task.
- 3 — Advanced: is not expected at this level.
- 4 — Expert: is not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Who do you think a user researcher's stakeholders
  usually are, and why does managing those relationships matter?"
- Ask about a group project, at university or elsewhere, where they had to consider
  what different people needed from the outcome.
- Ask them to describe what a policy team or a service manager might care about when
  research findings are shared with them.

### Skill: User research methods

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks an associate user researcher to explain some basic user research
methods.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. Explaining basic methods, rather than applying them, is
knowledge-level demonstration appropriate to this entry level.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot name or explain a basic user research method.
- 1 — Beginner: explains at least one or two basic user research methods, such as an
  interview or a usability test, in general terms.
- 2 — Intermediate: explains several basic methods and, when asked, has a view on when
  each might be used.
- 3 — Advanced: is not expected at this level.
- 4 — Expert: is not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "What user research methods have you come across,
  and what do you understand about how each one works?"
- Ask about a course, book, or piece of independent study on user research methods.
- Ask them to describe a method they might use to find out why people struggle to
  complete an online form.

- [ ] 1 — Beginner

### Skill: User-centred practice and advocacy

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks an associate user researcher to describe how government services
and technology affect how people behave, and to explain how user-centred practices are
used to build services that adapt to changing user behaviour and needs.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. Describing and explaining, rather than applying, is
knowledge-level demonstration appropriate to this entry level.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot describe how a government service or technology affects how people
  behave.
- 1 — Beginner: describes, in general terms, how a government service or technology
  can affect how people behave, and explains why user-centred practices matter.
- 2 — Intermediate: gives a specific example of a service that changed because of
  user-centred practice, and explains what changed and why.
- 3 — Advanced: is not expected at this level.
- 4 — Expert: is not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Can you describe a government service, or any
  digital service, that changed how it worked based on how people actually used it?"
- Ask about a course or piece of reading on user-centred design that shaped their
  understanding.
- Ask them to describe why a service that ignores how people behave might fail.

One or two skills below target is not automatically a fail. Weigh how central the skill

developed with guidance and training — consider whether the shortfall is coachable
quickly, and record the reasoning behind the determination, not only the number.
