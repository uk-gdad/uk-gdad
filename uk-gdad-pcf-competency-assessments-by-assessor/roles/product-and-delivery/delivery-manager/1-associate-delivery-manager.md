# Associate Delivery Manager - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This assessment is for an assessor — a line manager, a panel, or a reviewer —
judging whether a candidate or employee meets the baseline abilities expected
of an **associate delivery manager** in the product and delivery profession.
It measures demonstrated skill against the framework's own description of
this role level, for a hiring decision, an early-careers or apprenticeship
onboarding, or a periodic capability check.

This is one input among several into that decision, never the whole of it. See
`## Advisory` below for why.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating on this
form must never be the sole basis for a hiring, promotion, pay, performance or
disciplinary decision. Before using it, consult your organisation's human
resources, people development, occupational psychology or legal compliance
colleagues.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` before you score anything, so
you know what an associate delivery manager is accountable for — learning on
the job, on a small or mature Agile team, potentially shadowing a more senior
delivery manager. Then gather whatever evidence exists: a CV or portfolio, a
structured interview, a small work sample or task, direct observation, or
references.

**Score on evidence, not impression.** Every rating must trace to something
the person did, said, produced, or was observed doing. Evidence at this level
is often thin — a recent entrant to the profession may have little on-the-job
history — so look for evidence from study, structured exercises, or
supervised practice rather than marking a skill down because workplace
evidence does not yet exist. If you cannot point to evidence for a rating,
record it as unknown rather than guess.

**Be consistent.** Score every candidate for this role level the same way.
Where possible, have more than one assessor score independently before
comparing notes — this matters especially for a hiring decision.

**Stay fair.** See [Fair and lawful use](#fair-and-lawful-use) below. Never let
a rating be influenced by anything other than demonstrated, job-related
evidence.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

This is a **proficiency** scale, not a frequency scale: it rates how well the
person does the thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it or
how they feel about it.

**Worked example — Agile and Lean practices.** At 0, a candidate cannot say
what Agile principles are or name a basic Agile tool. At 2 — the baseline for
this role level — a candidate can describe applying Agile principles in
practice from direct, if supervised, experience on a team, can point to a
tool such as a task board they have used to make the team's work visible, and
shows awareness of how those tools help. At 4, by contrast, a candidate
coaches teams in Agile and Lean good practice and is recognised as an expert
advocate for the approach — evidence well beyond what an associate delivery
manager is expected to show, and a signal the candidate might be ready for a
more senior assessment instead.

## About this role level

The product and delivery profession's delivery manager role exists to make a
team accountable for the delivery of products and services. A delivery
manager builds and maintains an effective team, removes obstacles, keeps the
team focused on what matters most, and helps the team improve how it works.

The role has four levels: associate delivery manager, delivery manager,
senior delivery manager, and head of (Agile) delivery management. This
assessment is for the first of those.

An **associate delivery manager** works on a small or mature Agile team, and
learns on the job. The framework says the person may work alongside a
delivery manager on a larger team — shadowing and providing support — or work
to deliver an element of the team's work under guidance and mentorship. This
is explicitly a learning role: there is no independent accountability for the
overall performance of a team at this level, and the assessment below
reflects that by setting baselines low and treating supervised, hands-on
practice as strong evidence in its own right.

The framework names nine skills for this level: Agile and Lean practices,
Commercial management, Communicating between the technical and non-technical,
Financial management, Life cycle management, Maintaining delivery momentum,
Making a process work, Planning, and Team dynamics and collaboration.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Agile and Lean practices

**What the framework expects at this level**

An associate delivery manager is expected to demonstrate experience in
applying Agile principles in practice, to provide a clear, open and
transparent framework in which teams can deliver, to show awareness of Agile
tools and start using them intelligently, and to visualise and make visible
the work of the team.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. "Demonstrate experience... in practice" and
"starting to use [tools] intelligently" describe real, if guided, hands-on
practice rather than pure knowledge, so the baseline sits above Beginner.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe an Agile principle or name a tool used to make
  team work visible.
- 1 — Beginner: Can describe Agile principles from study, but has not applied
  them on a real team.
- 2 — Intermediate: Has applied Agile principles on a real, if small or
  supervised, team, used a tool such as a task board to make the team's work
  visible, and can explain why that visibility mattered.
- 3 — Advanced: Not expected at this level. Comparing and selecting the most
  appropriate delivery methods for a context belongs to a later level.
- 4 — Expert: Not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a team they worked on and how the team's work
  was made visible day to day.
- Ask them to explain, in their own words, why a clear and transparent
  framework helps a team deliver.
- Ask a supervisor or referee how actively the candidate has used Agile tools
  in practice, not just described them.

**Rate this skill**

- [ ] 0 — None
- [ ] 1 — Beginner
- [ ] 2 — Intermediate
- [ ] 3 — Advanced
- [ ] 4 — Expert

### Skill: Commercial management

**What the framework expects at this level**

An associate delivery manager is expected to act as the point of contact for
contracted suppliers, to understand appropriate internal contacts and
processes within a government department, and to understand how and when
third parties should be brought into digital, data and technology projects.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. "Understand" describes knowledge, and even "act as
the point of contact" is a narrow, procedural task rather than independent
commercial judgement, so the baseline sits low.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe why a project might need to work with a
  contracted supplier.
- 1 — Beginner: Can describe, in general terms, when a third party might be
  brought into a project, and knows who the internal commercial contacts are
  in their organisation.
- 2 — Intermediate: Has acted as the day-to-day point of contact for a
  supplier on a small piece of work, under guidance.
- 3 — Advanced: Not expected at this level. Taking responsibility for complex
  supplier relationships belongs to a later level.
- 4 — Expert: Not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe when they would bring a third party into a
  project, and why.
- Ask whether they know the internal process for engaging a contracted
  supplier in their organisation.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate has fielded supplier queries reliably
  under guidance.

**Rate this skill**

- [ ] 0 — None
- [ ] 1 — Beginner
- [ ] 2 — Intermediate
- [ ] 3 — Advanced
- [ ] 4 — Expert

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What the framework expects at this level**

An associate delivery manager is expected to communicate effectively with
technical and non-technical stakeholders, to support and host discussions
within a multidisciplinary team with potentially difficult dynamics, to be an
advocate for the team externally, and to manage differing stakeholder
perspectives.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. "Support and host discussions" and "be an
advocate" describe real, active participation, though "support" signals this
is not yet done fully independently.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Struggles to explain a technical concept to a non-technical
  audience or vice versa.
- 1 — Beginner: Can communicate simple, low-stakes information clearly to
  both technical and non-technical people.
- 2 — Intermediate: Has supported or co-hosted a discussion within a
  multidisciplinary team, including one with some tension in it, and has
  spoken up for the team's position to someone outside it.
- 3 — Advanced: Not expected at this level. Independently mediating
  high-stakes discussions and moderating difficult conversations belongs to a
  later level.
- 4 — Expert: Not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to explain a technical concept from their own experience
  to a non-technical audience, live in the interview.
- Ask about a time they helped smooth a disagreement within a team, even in a
  supporting role.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate communicates confidently with people
  outside their immediate discipline.

### Skill: Financial management

**What the framework expects at this level**

An associate delivery manager is expected to understand the financial impact
of the work they do and how cost and budgets are created.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. The framework asks only for understanding at this
level — there is no accountability yet for monitoring or reporting on a
budget.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe why the team's work has a cost.
- 1 — Beginner: Can describe, in general terms, how the team's work
  translates into cost, and has some awareness of how a budget is put
  together.
- 2 — Intermediate: Not expected at this level. Balancing cost against value
  or monitoring a budget belongs to a later level.
- 3 — Advanced: Not expected at this level.
- 4 — Expert: Not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to explain, in their own words, how the work they do
  costs money, using a real or plausible example.
- Ask whether they have seen a budget for a piece of work they were part of,
  and what it covered.
- Ask a supervisor whether the candidate shows curiosity about the financial
  side of the team's work.

### Skill: Life cycle management

**What the framework expects at this level**

An associate delivery manager is expected to explain what each phase of the
product or service life cycle is for, and to explain how the needs of the
product or service vary across phases of the life cycle, and how a team
responds to them.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. The framework asks only for explanation at this
level, not for managing a product or service through a phase.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot name the phases of a product or service life cycle.
- 1 — Beginner: Can name the phases of a typical life cycle (for example
  discovery, alpha, beta, live) and give a general description of what each
  is for.
- 2 — Intermediate: Not expected at this level. Managing delivery through a
  phase with some support belongs to a later level.
- 3 — Advanced: Not expected at this level.
- 4 — Expert: Not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to name and describe the phases of a service life cycle
  they are aware of, from study or experience.
- Ask how they think a team's priorities might change between an early and a
  later phase.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate shows awareness of what phase a piece of
  work they were part of was in, and why that mattered.

- [ ] 0 — None

### Skill: Maintaining delivery momentum

**What the framework expects at this level**

An associate delivery manager is expected to actively address internal risks
and issues and know when to escalate them, to set the team cadence and tempo
ensuring it is sustainable, to track, manage, escalate and communicate
dependencies, to actively remove or minimise risks, issues or dependencies
where possible, and to understand how risks, issues or dependencies impact
the work of a team.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. This is a substantial list of active verbs for a
first-level role, but every one of them is bounded to internal risks and a
small or mature team — real applied practice within a supported, contained
scope.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe what a risk, issue or dependency is in a delivery
  context.
- 1 — Beginner: Can identify a risk or dependency when pointed at it, but has
  not tracked or escalated one independently.
- 2 — Intermediate: Has tracked a dependency, flagged a risk and known when to
  escalate it, and has helped set or maintain a sustainable team cadence, on a
  small or mature team.
- 3 — Advanced: Not expected at this level. Facilitating delivery flow across
  a team and handling risks with ownership outside the team belongs to a later
  level.
- 4 — Expert: Not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of a risk or dependency the candidate spotted and what
  they did about it.
- Ask how they know when to escalate something rather than handle it
  themselves.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate has helped keep a team's pace
  sustainable, or has flagged when it was not.

### Skill: Making a process work

**What the framework expects at this level**

An associate delivery manager is expected to guide teams to focus on the
output rather than the process, to help the team find a process that works for
them, and to support teams in establishing a process.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. "Guide", "help" and "support" describe assisting an
existing process rather than independently diagnosing or designing one.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe the difference between a team's process and its
  output.
- 1 — Beginner: Can explain why a team should focus on its output rather than
  rigidly following a process, and has supported a team in following an
  established process.
- 2 — Intermediate: Not expected at this level. Identifying and challenging
  overly complex organisational processes belongs to a later level.
- 3 — Advanced: Not expected at this level.
- 4 — Expert: Not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate for an example of a team process they helped a team
  follow or settle into.
- Ask them to explain, with an example, why focusing on output rather than
  process matters.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate has noticed and flagged when a process
  was getting in the way of a team's output.

### Skill: Planning

**What the framework expects at this level**

An associate delivery manager is expected to understand the importance of
planning and forecasting, and to show awareness of the different ways to
develop a plan.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. The framework asks only for understanding and
awareness, not for producing or owning a plan.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot explain why planning matters to a delivery team.
- 1 — Beginner: Can explain why planning and forecasting matter, and can name
  more than one way a team might develop a plan.
- 2 — Intermediate: Not expected at this level. Using data to inform planning
  and managing dependencies belongs to a later level.
- 3 — Advanced: Not expected at this level.
- 4 — Expert: Not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate why they think planning matters to a delivery team, with
  a real or hypothetical example.
- Ask them to name two different ways a team could plan its work and when
  each might suit.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate has taken part in a team's planning
  activities, even as an observer or supporter.

### Skill: Team dynamics and collaboration

**What the framework expects at this level**

An associate delivery manager is expected to explain the importance of team
dynamics, collaboration and empowering delivery teams, and to understand the
importance of feedback.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. The framework asks only for explanation and
understanding — this level is not yet accountable for shaping a team's
dynamics itself.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot explain why team dynamics or feedback matter to delivery.
- 1 — Beginner: Can explain, in general terms, why healthy team dynamics and
  regular feedback help a team deliver.
- 2 — Intermediate: Not expected at this level. Actively bringing people
  together to form a motivated team belongs to a later level.
- 3 — Advanced: Not expected at this level.
- 4 — Expert: Not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a time team dynamics helped or hindered a
  piece of work they were part of.
- Ask why they think feedback matters to a delivery team, with an example.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate contributes positively to team
  dynamics, even without formal responsibility for them.
