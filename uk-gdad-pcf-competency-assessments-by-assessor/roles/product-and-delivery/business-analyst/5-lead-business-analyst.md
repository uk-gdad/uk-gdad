# Lead Business Analyst - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This assessment is for an assessor — a line manager, a panel, or a reviewer —
judging whether a candidate or employee meets the baseline abilities expected
of a **lead business analyst** in the product and delivery profession. It
measures demonstrated skill against the framework's own description of this
role level, for a hiring decision, a promotion from senior business analyst,
or a periodic capability check.

This is one input among several into that decision, never the whole of it. See
`## Advisory` below for why.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating on this
form must never be the sole basis for a hiring, promotion, pay, performance or
disciplinary decision. Before using it, consult your organisation's human
resources, people development, occupational psychology or legal compliance
colleagues.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` before you score anything.
This level operates in the enterprise arena, across highly complex problem
spaces spanning multiple products or services, with leadership across
government and externally — the evidence you need is about influence and
track record at that scale, not a single well-run project.

**Score on evidence, not impression.** Every rating must trace to something
the person did, said, produced, or was observed doing. At this level, evidence
often takes longer to surface than at a junior level: a reputation as a
go-to person, a piece of guidance others adopted, a mentoring relationship
that changed someone's trajectory. Ask for specifics rather than accepting a
general claim of seniority. If you cannot point to evidence for a rating,
record it as unknown rather than guess.

**Be consistent.** Score every candidate for this role level the same way.
Where possible, have more than one assessor score independently before
comparing notes — this matters especially for a hiring or promotion decision.

**Stay fair.** See [Fair and lawful use](#fair-and-lawful-use) below. Never let
a rating be influenced by anything other than demonstrated, job-related
evidence.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

This is a **proficiency** scale, not a frequency scale: it rates how well the
person does the thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it or
how they feel about it.

**Worked example — Consultancy.** At 2, a candidate can offer an opinion on a
problem when asked, drawing on their own recent experience. At 4 — the
baseline for this role level — a candidate provides advice, guidance and
recommendations based on genuinely specialist knowledge and experience,
proposes methodologies and approaches to implementation that others adopt, and
frames ambiguous problems so that people outside the discipline can understand
and act on them. A candidate who can only describe consultancy in the
abstract, without a specific example of advice they gave that changed a
decision, has not yet shown evidence at this level, whatever their years of
experience.

## About this role level

The product and delivery profession's business analyst role exists to help
teams analyse and understand a business problem or opportunity: to research
how a business or business area works — its people, organisation, processes,
information, data and technology — identify areas for improvement, explore
feasible options, analyse the effects of change and define success measures.
Business analysts identify and elaborate user and business needs so that
services and business change can be effectively designed, developed and
tested; they make prioritisation and minimum viable product decisions using
analysis-led insights; they ensure new products and services meet business and
user needs and align with organisational goals; and they understand the
business and policy constraints a piece of work sits within, and assess the
implications.

The role has six levels: trainee business analyst, junior business analyst,
business analyst, senior business analyst, lead business analyst, and head of
business analysis. This assessment is for the fifth of those.

A **lead business analyst** has a good understanding of the enterprise arena
and works in highly complex problem spaces, typically spanning multiple
products or services. At this role level, the framework says the person will
be a leader in the business analysis community across government and
externally, have functional management or people management accountabilities
or a combination of both, actively share knowledge and be seen as a go-to
person in the field, mentor others, and own the main stakeholder
relationships. This is a step up from senior business analyst in scope
(enterprise-wide and cross-organisational rather than project or programme
level) and in the kind of influence expected — the framework now looks for
someone whose judgement and guidance shape how other business analysts work,
not only their own delivery.

The framework names eleven skills for this level: Adapting to delivery
methodologies, Business modelling, Business process improvement, Consultancy,
Context, problem and option analysis, Enterprise and business architecture
(business analyst), Defining and managing business needs, user needs and
requirements, Stakeholder relationship management, Digital and data systems
analysis, Testing (business analysis), and User experience analysis.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Adapting to delivery methodologies

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead business analyst is expected to determine the approach for their role
within the team's delivery methodology, to define and agree the scope of
delivery for their role within a multidisciplinary team, to negotiate and
agree delivery priorities with the team and relevant stakeholders, and to
clearly communicate the value of iterative delivery and realising benefits
early.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. The framework's wording for this skill is unchanged
from the senior business analyst level; the leadership this role level adds
shows up in the other skills, particularly Consultancy and Enterprise and
business architecture, rather than in a further step-change here.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe how they would shape their role's scope within a
  delivery methodology.
- 1 — Beginner: Follows an agreed approach within a methodology.
- 2 — Intermediate: Adapts their own working practice to a chosen
  methodology, with some support negotiating scope.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently determines their approach, negotiates delivery
  priorities with the team and stakeholders across an enterprise-scale piece
  of work, and communicates the value of iterative delivery convincingly to
  senior, sceptical audiences.
- 4 — Expert: Not distinctly expected at this level by the framework's own
  wording for this skill; a candidate operating at this depth would likely be
  setting methodology standards, which belongs elsewhere in the matrix.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example where the candidate negotiated delivery scope or
  priorities across multiple products or services, not just one team.
- Ask how they have communicated the value of iterative delivery to a senior
  stakeholder outside the delivery team.
- Ask a referee how much the candidate's approach shapes delivery decisions
  beyond their own immediate team.

### Skill: Business modelling

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead business analyst is expected to determine the scope and methodology
for the representation of complex programmes across an organisation, and to
document conceptual models to support strategic planning and decision making.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "Determine the scope and methodology... across an
organisation" and modelling for strategic planning describe mastery exercised
at enterprise scale — the framework's own wording places this beyond
independent working into setting direction for others.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has never scoped or designed a modelling approach for
  organisation-wide work.
- 1 — Beginner: Produces a model within a scope and methodology someone else
  has set.
- 2 — Intermediate: Leads modelling for a single project or programme, with
  limited direction.
- 3 — Advanced: Leads modelling across a project or programme independently,
  and manages the impact of proposed changes on what has been modelled.
- 4 — Expert: Determines the scope and methodology for representing complex
  programmes across an organisation, and produces conceptual models that
  genuinely inform strategic planning and decision making at that level.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example where the candidate set the modelling scope and
  methodology for organisation-wide, not just project-level, work.
- Ask to see a conceptual model the candidate produced that fed into a
  strategic decision, and what that decision was.
- Ask a referee whether senior leaders have used the candidate's modelling
  work directly in planning.

### Skill: Business process improvement

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead business analyst is expected to lead the identification of large-scale
opportunities to deliver business performance improvements, to set the
direction for analysis, design and evaluation of business process
improvements including methods, tools and standards, to set the direction for
design, execution and assessment of business process tests and usability
evaluations, and to coach others in designing, testing, implementing and
assessing business process improvements.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "Set the direction... including methods, tools and
standards" and "coach others" are, by the scale's own definition, expert-level
behaviour: mastery exercised in service of other people's practice, not just
the candidate's own delivery.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has never set direction for process improvement work beyond their
  own project.
- 1 — Beginner: Leads a single process improvement with support.
- 2 — Intermediate: Leads process improvement work independently for one
  project or programme.
- 3 — Advanced: Leads process improvement across more than one project or
  programme, with real independence, but has not yet set organisation-wide
  standards.
- 4 — Expert: Leads identification of large-scale improvement opportunities,
  sets direction for methods, tools and standards used across the
  organisation, and coaches other business analysts in the discipline.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of a large-scale improvement opportunity the candidate
  identified and led, beyond a single project's scope.
- Ask what standards, methods or tools for process improvement the candidate
  has set or influenced across an organisation.
- Ask a referee for an example of the candidate coaching someone else through
  a process improvement.

### Skill: Consultancy

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead business analyst is expected to provide advice, guidance and
recommendations based on their specialist knowledge and experience, to
propose methodologies to follow and approaches to implementation, and to
frame problems so they can easily be understood, and troubleshoot where
required, to support the business in operating more effectively.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. This skill first appears at this level, and its
wording — specialist advice, proposing methodology, framing problems for
others — is the framework's description of what a go-to expert does, matching
the level description's own "go-to person in the field".

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has never been asked to provide specialist advice on a business
  problem.
- 1 — Beginner: Offers an opinion on a familiar problem when directly asked.
- 2 — Intermediate: Provides advice within their own area of recent
  experience, though not yet sought out proactively by others.
- 3 — Advanced: Is regularly sought out for advice within their business area
  and proposes practical approaches to implementation.
- 4 — Expert: Provides advice, guidance and recommendations based on genuine
  specialist knowledge across a range of problems, proposes methodologies
  that others adopt, and frames ambiguous problems so non-specialists can
  understand and act on them.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of specific advice the candidate gave that changed how a
  business problem was approached.
- Ask them to frame a genuinely ambiguous, real problem from their experience
  in a way a non-specialist could understand and act on.
- Ask a referee who in the organisation seeks the candidate out for advice,
  and on what kind of problem.

### Skill: Context, problem and option analysis

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead business analyst is expected to advise on approaches to options
analysis, feasibility and impact assessments to ensure best practice is
followed, to ensure all streams of work align to the organisation's strategic

influence strategic plans in a changing environment.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "Advise on approaches... to ensure best practice" and

  assessment approach, ensures work streams align to strategic goals, and
  influences strategic plans as the environment changes.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example where the candidate advised on analysis approach or best
  practice beyond their own project.
- Ask how they have influenced a strategic plan as circumstances changed.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate's advice on approach has shaped how

### Skill: Enterprise and business architecture (business analyst)

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead business analyst is expected to work with limited direction to
translate business drivers, goals and constraints into business objectives,
and to help define required capabilities and support organisational changes
to create operating models that meet business objectives.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. "With limited direction" signals real, independent
competence at translating strategy into objectives and capabilities, though

**Behavioural indicators**

  setting direction for others.

  driver into a concrete business objective.

### Skill: Defining and managing business needs, user needs and requirements

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead business analyst is expected to direct the approach to requirements

ensure proposed solutions align with the organisational strategy and vision.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "Direct the approach" and "enable others" describe

the scale's own definition of expert practice.

- 1 — Beginner: Manages requirements for their own piece of work.
- 2 — Intermediate: Advises on requirements approach for a single project or

  independently.

  enables other business analysts to select and apply appropriate life cycle
  methods, and influences prioritisation of complex, competing requirement
  sets to balance strategic and tactical needs.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example where the candidate set a requirements management

- Ask how they balanced a long-term strategic requirement against a short-term

### Skill: Digital and data systems analysis
