# Trainee Business Analyst - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This assessment is for an assessor — a line manager, a panel, or a reviewer —
judging whether a candidate or employee meets the baseline abilities expected
of a **trainee business analyst** in the product and delivery profession. It
measures demonstrated skill against the framework's own description of this
role level, for a hiring decision, an apprenticeship or early-careers
onboarding, or a periodic capability check.

This is one input among several into that decision, never the whole of it. See
`## Advisory` below for why.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating on this
form must never be the sole basis for a hiring, promotion, pay, performance or
disciplinary decision. Before using it, consult your organisation's human
resources, people development, occupational psychology or legal compliance
colleagues.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` before you score anything, so
you know what a trainee business analyst is accountable for and how the
framework distinguishes them from a junior business analyst. Then gather
whatever evidence exists: a CV or portfolio, a structured interview, a small
work sample or task, direct observation of the candidate at work, or
references.

**Score on evidence, not impression.** Every rating must trace to something
the person did, said, produced, or was observed doing. Trainee-level evidence
is often thin — a recent graduate or career-changer may have little on-the-job
history — so look for evidence from study, structured exercises, or supervised
practice rather than marking a skill down because workplace evidence does not
yet exist. If you genuinely cannot point to evidence for a rating, record it as
unknown rather than guess.

**Be consistent.** Score every candidate for this role level the same way.
Where possible, have more than one assessor score independently before
comparing notes — this matters especially for a hiring decision, where a
single assessor's impression can carry disproportionate weight.

**Stay fair.** See [Fair and lawful use](#fair-and-lawful-use) below. Never let
a rating be influenced by anything other than demonstrated, job-related
evidence.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

This is a **proficiency** scale, not a frequency scale: it rates how well the
person does the thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it or
how they feel about it.

**Worked example — Stakeholder relationship management.** At 0, a candidate
cannot say who a stakeholder is or why managing that relationship matters. At
1 — the baseline for this role level — a candidate can describe, in a
structured interview, who the stakeholders on a recent piece of coursework or
supervised work were, and why keeping them informed mattered, but has not yet
had to manage a live relationship unsupervised. At 3, by contrast, a candidate
has independently identified stakeholders on a piece of work, kept them
updated without prompting, and adjusted their approach when a stakeholder's
priorities changed — evidence well beyond what a trainee is expected to show,
and a strong signal the person may be ready to progress faster than the role
level implies.

## About this role level

The product and delivery profession's business analyst role exists to help
teams analyse and understand a business problem or opportunity: to research
how a business or business area works — its people, organisation, processes,
information, data and technology — identify areas for improvement, explore
feasible options, analyse the effects of change and define success measures.
Business analysts identify and elaborate user and business needs so that
services and business change can be effectively designed, developed and
tested; they make prioritisation and minimum viable product decisions using
analysis-led insights; they ensure new products and services meet business and
user needs and align with organisational goals; and they understand the
business and policy constraints a piece of work sits within, and assess the
implications.

The role has six levels: trainee business analyst, junior business analyst,
business analyst, senior business analyst, lead business analyst, and head of
business analysis. This assessment is for the first of those.

A **trainee business analyst** receives direction from more senior business
analysts. At this level, the framework expects someone to work with others or
under supervision to build and develop their skills. There are no independent
accountabilities listed for this level beyond that: it is explicitly a
learning role, and the assessment below reflects that — every baseline is set
low, and evidence of learning under supervision counts for as much as evidence
of independent delivery, because independent delivery is not yet what this
level is for.

The framework names six skills for this level: Adapting to delivery
methodologies, Business process improvement, Context, problem and option
analysis, Defining and managing business needs, user needs and requirements,
Stakeholder relationship management, Digital and data systems analysis, and
Testing (business analysis).

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Adapting to delivery methodologies

**What the framework expects at this level**

A trainee business analyst is expected to explain the differences between
delivery methodologies such as scrum or kanban, to explain how their approach
would change based on the delivery methodology in use, to explain why
iteration is important, and to explain how roles in a multidisciplinary team
work together to support delivery.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. The framework asks only for explanation, not for
independent adaptation of working practice — this is knowledge a trainee is
expected to be acquiring, not a skill they are expected to apply unsupervised
yet.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot name a delivery methodology or say what scrum or kanban are
  for.
- 1 — Beginner: Can describe, in general terms, the difference between an
  Agile and a waterfall approach, and can say why teams work in iterations,
  though the explanation may be textbook rather than grounded in direct
  experience.
- 2 — Intermediate: Has taken part in a scrum or kanban team's ceremonies —
  stand-ups, retrospectives, sprint planning — and can describe how their own
  tasks fitted into that rhythm.
- 3 — Advanced: Can explain how a multidisciplinary team's roles interact
  within a chosen methodology, using a real example from their own experience,
  and can say what they would do differently under a different methodology.
- 4 — Expert: Not expected at this level. A candidate showing this would be
  coaching others on methodology choice, which is well beyond a trainee's
  accountabilities.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a project or piece of coursework and how the
  team organised its work — what rhythm it followed and why.
- Give a short scenario ("your team currently plans in two-week sprints but
  keeps missing the sprint goal — what might be going on?") and ask them to
  reason through it out loud.
- Ask a referee or supervisor whether the candidate has taken part in
  Agile ceremonies and how actively.

### Skill: Business process improvement

**What the framework expects at this level**

A trainee business analyst is expected to contribute to the analysis and
evaluation of business processes to identify problems and opportunities, to
contribute to the design and implementation of business process improvements,
and to contribute to business process testing and usability evaluation.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. Every verb the framework uses at this level is
"contribute to" — this is supporting work under someone else's direction, not
independent process analysis.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has never taken part in mapping or improving a business process.
- 1 — Beginner: Has helped document an existing process — for example drawing
  a process map or listing the steps in a task — under direction, and can
  explain what the process was for.
- 2 — Intermediate: Has contributed a specific observation or suggestion to a
  process improvement exercise, such as spotting a duplicated step or an
  unclear handover point.
- 3 — Advanced: Not expected at this level. This would mean independently
  designing and implementing a process improvement, which the framework
  reserves for later levels.
- 4 — Expert: Not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of a process map, workflow diagram, or "as-is" process
  description the candidate has helped produce, even in a training context.
- Ask what they noticed while documenting a process that they thought could be
  better, and why.
- A short task: give the candidate a simple, deliberately clunky process (for
  example, a multi-step approval chain) and ask them to describe it back and
  flag anything odd.

### Skill: Context, problem and option analysis

**What the framework expects at this level**

A trainee business analyst is expected to describe the analysis techniques
they could use to help understand the context, problem and options to meet
business and user needs, to explain the importance of understanding that
internal and external factors influence the organisation, and to contribute to
identifying and assessing multiple options and formulating recommendations,
under supervision.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. The framework asks for description and explanation,
plus supervised contribution — knowledge of technique, not independent
judgement about which option to recommend.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot name an analysis technique or explain why context matters
  to a business problem.
- 1 — Beginner: Can name one or two analysis techniques (for example a
  SWOT analysis or a stakeholder map) and explain, in general terms, why
  internal and external factors matter to a problem.
- 2 — Intermediate: Has contributed, under supervision, to identifying options
  for a real or simulated problem, and can explain the reasoning behind at
  least one option considered.
- 3 — Advanced: Not expected at this level. Independently formulating and
  defending a recommendation is a level-3 accountability under this
  framework's wording.
- 4 — Expert: Not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a problem they have analysed — in study, in a
  previous role, or in a simulated exercise — and what technique they used to
  understand it.
- Present a short, simplified business scenario and ask which of two or three
  named techniques they would reach for first, and why.
- Ask a supervisor or referee whether the candidate has contributed usefully
  to identifying options in a supervised setting.

**Rate this skill**

- [ ] 0 — None
- [ ] 1 — Beginner
- [ ] 2 — Intermediate

### Skill: Defining and managing business needs, user needs and requirements

**What the framework expects at this level**

A trainee business analyst is expected to support identifying, analysing,
capturing and validating business and user requirements, to work under
supervision to manage requirements and support their prioritisation within a
defined scope, and to provide distinct task-based outputs for parts of a
project as directed, using a predetermined requirements management life
cycle.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. Every accountability here is "support", "under
supervision" or "as directed" — the framework is explicit that requirements
work at this level is task-based and closely guided.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has never captured or documented a requirement.
- 1 — Beginner: Has captured a small number of requirements under close
  direction, using a template or format someone else set, and can explain what
  the requirement was for.
- 2 — Intermediate: Has captured requirements with less hand-holding, can
  distinguish a well-written requirement from a vague one, and has supported a
  prioritisation discussion by presenting information clearly.
- 3 — Advanced: Not expected at this level. Independently managing a
  requirements set is a later accountability.
- 4 — Expert: Not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask to see, or ask the candidate to talk through, a requirement or user
  story they have written, even from a training exercise.
- A short task: give a brief, informal description of a business need (for
  example "staff need to know when their leave request has been approved")
  and ask the candidate to turn it into one or two clear requirements.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate has produced requirements-related
  outputs reliably when given a clear brief.

### Skill: Stakeholder relationship management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A trainee business analyst is expected to describe who their stakeholders are
and the importance of managing relationships with them, and to explain what
their stakeholders find important and why.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. The framework asks for description and explanation
only — this level does not yet own or independently manage a stakeholder
relationship.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot identify who a stakeholder is on a piece of work, or why
  their views matter.
- 1 — Beginner: Can name the stakeholders on a recent piece of work — real or
  from study — and give a plausible reason why each one cares about the
  outcome.
- 2 — Intermediate: Has had some direct, supervised contact with a
  stakeholder — for example taking notes in a meeting or fielding a simple
  question — and can describe what they learned from it.
- 3 — Advanced: Not expected at this level. Independently managing a
  stakeholder relationship comes later.
- 4 — Expert: Not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to map the stakeholders on a project they have been part
  of, even a small or academic one, and explain each one's interest.
- Ask about a time a stakeholder's priorities were unclear or conflicting, and
  what the candidate noticed or did.
- Ask a supervisor or referee whether the candidate handles simple stakeholder
  contact professionally and knows when to escalate rather than improvise.

### Skill: Digital and data systems analysis

**What the framework expects at this level**

A trainee business analyst is expected to support the analysis of IT system
capabilities and work under supervision to identify and specify system
requirements, and to support the development of specifications and models for
bespoke IT systems or software packages.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. Supervised support for someone else's analysis, not
independent systems analysis.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has no exposure to describing or documenting how an IT system
  works.
- 1 — Beginner: Has helped document what an existing system does — for
  example listing its main functions or screens — under supervision.
- 2 — Intermediate: Has contributed a specific, useful observation about a
  system's capability or a gap in it, under supervision.
- 3 — Advanced: Not expected at this level. Independent systems analysis is a
  later accountability.
- 4 — Expert: Not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a system (a piece of software they use daily
  counts) and list its main capabilities, to check they can structure that
  kind of description.
- A short task: show a simple screen or workflow and ask what a system needs
  to be able to do to support it.
- Ask a supervisor or referee whether the candidate has usefully supported
  systems analysis work when directed.

### Skill: Testing (business analysis)

**What the framework expects at this level**

A trainee business analyst is expected to understand the stages and purpose of
testing and have an awareness of tools and techniques used, and to work under
supervision to support the representation of business scenarios and to trace
requirements to develop functionality.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. "Understand" and "have an awareness of" describe
knowledge, and the practical work is explicitly supervised.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe why testing happens or what stages it typically
  goes through.
- 1 — Beginner: Can describe, in general terms, why requirements need to be
  tested and what a test scenario is.
- 2 — Intermediate: Has helped write or check a business scenario or test case
  against a requirement, under supervision.
- 3 — Advanced: Not expected at this level. Independently tracing requirements
  through testing is a later accountability.
- 4 — Expert: Not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to explain, in their own words, why a requirement needs a
  test scenario before it is considered done.
- A short task: give a simple requirement and ask the candidate to sketch one
  or two scenarios that would show whether it has been met.
- Ask a supervisor or referee whether the candidate has contributed
  test-related work reliably when directed.

trainee-level candidate who may simply not have had the opportunity to
practise a given skill yet. Weigh how central the skill is to the day-to-day
work of a trainee business analyst in your team, consider whether the gap is
