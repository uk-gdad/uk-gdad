# Service Owner - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This assessment is for an assessor — a line manager, a panel, or a reviewer —
judging whether a candidate or employee meets the baseline abilities expected
of a **service owner** in the product and delivery profession. It measures
demonstrated skill against the framework's own description of this role
level, for a hiring decision, a promotion into the role, or a periodic
capability check.

This is one input among several into that decision, never the whole of it. See
`## Advisory` below for why.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating on this
form must never be the sole basis for a hiring, promotion, pay, performance or
disciplinary decision. Before using it, consult your organisation's human
resources, people development, occupational psychology or legal compliance
colleagues.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` before you score anything.
This level is an accountable leader for the quality, performance, benefits
and outcomes of a whole service, bringing together a multidisciplinary team
and answering for governance, funding and culture — the evidence you need is
about that end-to-end accountability, not about one workstream done well.

**Score on evidence, not impression.** Every rating must trace to something
the person did, said, produced, or was observed doing. Ask for examples that
show ownership of outcomes across a whole service — a strategy the candidate
set and delivered, a funding case they won, a governance process they
answered for, a moment they were the escalation point when something went
operationally wrong. If you cannot point to evidence for a rating, record it
as unknown rather than guess.

**Be consistent.** Score every candidate for this role level the same way.
Where possible, have more than one assessor score independently before
comparing notes — this matters especially for a hiring or promotion decision.

**Stay fair.** See [Fair and lawful use](#fair-and-lawful-use) below. Never let
a rating be influenced by anything other than demonstrated, job-related
evidence. This is an unvalidated, AI-drafted instrument: used inconsistently —
different questions for different candidates, one assessor's impression taken
as final — it can produce unlawful adverse impact. Consistent, evidence-based
administration, ideally by more than one assessor, materially reduces that
risk.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

This is a **proficiency** scale, not a frequency scale: it rates how well the
person does the thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it or
how they feel about it.

**Worked example — Governance and assurance.** At 2, a candidate follows a
governance process someone else designed, preparing what is asked of them
for a service assessment. At 3, a candidate independently guides their own
team through a governance or assurance process and can explain why it is
proportionate. At 4 — the baseline for this role level — a candidate ensures
appropriate and proportionate governance and assurance is in place in the
first place and keeps improving it, guides teams through processes such as
service assessments so that standards are consistently met, and shares how
the team's governance works so stakeholders trust it to deliver value
sustainably. The distinction an assessor should look for is whether the
candidate designed or reshaped the governance model itself, or only operated
within one someone else set up.

## About this role level

The product and delivery profession's service owner role exists to be
accountable for the quality, performance, benefits and outcomes of a service.
A service owner provides clear end-to-end direction, prioritisation, risk
management and decision making for the teams delivering that service, in line
with departmental policy and user outcomes.

In this role, the framework says a service owner will:

- define success and quality measures for a service, driving continuous
  improvement towards these measures
- inform and influence stakeholders, getting consensus that enables work to
  progress
- ensure the delivery of change that realises the outcomes and benefits of
  their service

This role currently has one published level in the framework: Service owner.
There is no junior or senior variant published beneath or above it — a person
in this post is expected to arrive already able to lead across a
multidisciplinary team, rather than grow into the role from a lower step in
this ladder.

A **service owner** is an accountable leader that brings together a
multidisciplinary team to ensure delivery of a quality service. They
advocate for their service and teams, and develop an open and trust-based
culture. At this role level, the framework says a service owner will:

- ensure a strategy is in place that delivers the intended vision, outcomes
  and benefits
- prioritise areas of development, balancing user needs, business
  requirements and policy
- ensure appropriate governance and assurance is in place
- ensure appropriate funding is in place
- create the culture and environment for the team to do their best work

The framework names nine skills for this level: Adapting to delivery
methodologies, Applying user-centred insights, Financial ownership,
Governance and assurance, Leading performance and benefits, Life cycle
management, Operational management, Stakeholder relationship management, and
Strategic ownership.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Adapting to delivery methodologies

**What the framework expects at this level**

A service owner is expected to set and iterate standards for working within
different delivery methodologies, to actively promote collaboration and
agreement so the team delivers value at the right time, to experiment with
new and innovative ways of working to enhance delivery outcomes, and to
research new and evolving delivery methodologies and identify how their own
role would work within them.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Setting and iterating standards, and running
experiments to enhance delivery outcomes, describe strong independent
ownership of how the service delivers; the framework's wording for this
skill is about shaping standards within the service rather than teaching
delivery methodology to other services or the wider organisation, so the
baseline stops short of Expert.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe why one delivery methodology might suit a
  service better than another.
- 1 — Beginner: Follows a delivery methodology set up by someone else.
- 2 — Intermediate: Applies an established methodology to their own team
  with some support.
- 3 — Advanced: Sets and iterates standards for how their service works
  within a delivery methodology, promotes agreement across the team so value
  lands at the right time, and experiments with new ways of working.
- 4 — Expert: Not distinctly expected at this role level by the framework's
  own wording for this skill.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example where the candidate set or changed a delivery standard
  for their service, and why.
- Ask about an experiment in a new way of working they ran, and what they
  learned from it.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate has researched an evolving delivery
  methodology and identified how it would change their own role.

### Skill: Applying user-centred insights

**What the framework expects at this level**

A service owner is expected to advocate for a range of effective research
approaches across the organisation, to coach others in making decisions that
meet user needs across a range of channels, to advocate for continuous use of
user insights in teams, and to use user insights to make strategic decisions
that provide the best user experience.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Advocating "across the organisation" and coaching
"others" in making user-centred decisions describe mastery exercised to
guide people beyond the candidate's own team, which is the scale's own
definition of Expert.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe how user insight has shaped a decision they were
  involved in.
- 1 — Beginner: Reads user research findings prepared by someone else.
- 2 — Intermediate: Uses user insight to inform a decision within their own
  team.
- 3 — Advanced: Advocates for continuous use of user insight in their own
  team, and makes decisions across channels that visibly reflect it.
- 4 — Expert: Advocates for effective research approaches across the
  organisation, coaches others in using insight to meet user needs, and uses
  user insight to make strategic decisions about the service's direction.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of a strategic decision the candidate made or shaped
  using user insight, and the evidence behind it.
- Ask how they have coached someone else in making a user-centred decision.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate has advocated for research approaches
  beyond their own team.

### Skill: Financial ownership

**What the framework expects at this level**

A service owner is expected to ensure a business case is in place that
provides sustainable funding, to identify measurable benefits that represent
good value for money, to allocate money and make spending decisions across
competing priorities, to enhance and own relationships with suppliers to
increase quality and deliver good value outcomes, and to act as an escalation
point and work with stakeholders to manage financial and commercial risk.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Owning supplier relationships, allocating money across
competing priorities, and being the "escalation point" for financial and
commercial risk describe full ownership of the service's financial position,
exercised on behalf of the whole team rather than one workstream.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe how a service's funding is secured or how it is
  spent.
- 1 — Beginner: Tracks spend against a budget set by someone else.
- 2 — Intermediate: Contributes financial input into a business case with
  support.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently identifies measurable benefits and makes
  spending decisions within an agreed budget.
- 4 — Expert: Ensures a business case is in place that secures sustainable
  funding, allocates money across competing priorities, owns supplier
  relationships to secure good value, and is the escalation point when
  financial or commercial risk needs managing.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of a business case the candidate secured sustainable
  funding for, and the benefits it promised.
- Ask about a time they were the escalation point for a financial or
  commercial risk, and how they resolved it.
- Ask a referee about a supplier relationship the candidate owned and
  improved.

### Skill: Governance and assurance

**What the framework expects at this level**

A service owner is expected to ensure appropriate and proportionate
governance and assurance is in place and is continuously improved, aligned to
organisational and government needs, to guide teams through governance and
assurance processes such as service assessments so that standards are met,
and to share the team's governance and assurance processes so stakeholders
trust them to deliver value in a sustainable way.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "Ensure... governance and assurance is in place and is
continuously improved" describes ownership of the governance model itself,
not only operating within one someone else designed, and guiding teams
through it and building stakeholder trust in it are mastery exercised on
other people's behalf.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe why a service assessment or assurance process
  exists.
- 1 — Beginner: Prepares material for a governance process at someone else's
  direction.
- 2 — Intermediate: Follows an established governance process independently.
- 3 — Advanced: Guides their own team through a governance or assurance
  process, and can explain why it is proportionate to the service.
- 4 — Expert: Ensures proportionate governance and assurance is in place and
  keeps improving it, guides teams through processes such as service
  assessments so standards are consistently met, and shares how the
  governance works so stakeholders trust the service to deliver
  sustainably.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example where the candidate designed or reshaped a governance
  or assurance process for their service.
- Ask how they prepared a team for a service assessment and what the outcome
  was.
- Ask a referee whether stakeholders trust the candidate's governance
  approach without needing to check it closely.

### Skill: Leading performance and benefits

**What the framework expects at this level**

A service owner is expected to ensure appropriate measures are in place to
deliver valuable outcomes to users and agreed business benefits, to ensure
metrics are used to monitor, manage and prioritise delivery and inform
decision making, and to demonstrate the value of the service and build
understanding with stakeholders by ensuring effective data and insights are
shared.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Ensuring measures and metrics are in place and used
to prioritise delivery describes strong independent ownership of the
service's performance story; the framework's wording is about running this
well for the candidate's own service rather than teaching other services how
to measure benefits, so the baseline stops short of Expert.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe what metrics their service is measured against.
- 1 — Beginner: Reports a metric someone else defined.
- 2 — Intermediate: Uses an existing metric to inform a delivery decision.
- 3 — Advanced: Puts appropriate measures in place for user outcomes and
  business benefits, uses metrics to prioritise delivery, and demonstrates
  the service's value to stakeholders with data.
- 4 — Expert: Not distinctly expected at this role level by the framework's
  own wording for this skill.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of a measure the candidate put in place to track user
  outcomes or business benefits.
- Ask how a metric changed a delivery decision they made.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate has demonstrably built stakeholder
  understanding of a service's value using data.

### Skill: Life cycle management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A service owner is expected to work and consult with the right people at the
right time to move through the life cycle and deliver value, to use evidence
to decide when a team should continue, change direction or stop, to identify
tools and techniques required at different phases of the life cycle, and to
guide colleagues and stakeholders through different phases of the life
cycle.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Using evidence to decide whether a team should
continue, change direction or stop is a significant, independent judgement
call; the framework describes guiding colleagues and stakeholders through
this rather than coaching other services in life cycle practice more
broadly, so the baseline stops short of Expert.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe how a decision to stop or change direction on a
  service would be made.
- 1 — Beginner: Follows a life cycle plan set by someone else.
- 2 — Intermediate: Moves their own service through a phase with some
  support.
- 3 — Advanced: Uses evidence to decide whether a service should continue,
  change direction or stop, consults the right people at the right time, and
  guides colleagues and stakeholders through unfamiliar phases.
- 4 — Expert: Not distinctly expected at this role level by the framework's
  own wording for this skill.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of a continue, change-direction or stop decision the
  candidate made, and the evidence behind it.
- Ask how they guided a stakeholder unfamiliar with a life cycle phase
  through it.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate's life cycle judgement calls have held
  up over time.

### Skill: Operational management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A service owner is expected to ensure a scalable operating model is in place
to provide a resilient, high quality service, to coordinate teams from
operations, policy, digital and delivery to operate and iterate a service, to
ensure teams understand the importance of efficient service operations, to
identify and overcome operational constraints, and to act as the escalation
point for major operational issues.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Coordinating teams "from operations, policy, digital
and delivery" and being the "escalation point for major operational issues"
describe mastery exercised across a whole multidisciplinary operation, not
only the candidate's own delivery team.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe what a scalable operating model for their
  service looks like.
- 1 — Beginner: Follows an operational process set by someone else.
- 2 — Intermediate: Identifies an operational constraint within their own
  team.
- 3 — Advanced: Coordinates with one other function to overcome an
  operational constraint affecting the service.
- 4 — Expert: Ensures a scalable, resilient operating model is in place,
  coordinates operations, policy, digital and delivery teams together to
  operate and iterate the service, and is the escalation point for major
  operational issues.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example where the candidate coordinated operations, policy,
  digital and delivery teams to resolve an operational issue together.
- Ask about a major operational issue they were the escalation point for.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate has changed how a service scales or
  copes with demand.

### Skill: Stakeholder relationship management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A service owner is expected to direct the stakeholder relationship strategy
for their teams, to ensure stakeholders' objectives are set and support
teams to meet them, and to influence and negotiate with senior stakeholders
to resolve issues and enable progress.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "Direct the stakeholder relationship strategy" and
negotiating with "senior stakeholders" describe mastery exercised to set the
approach other people in the team follow, not only managing the candidate's
own relationships.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot name the service's key stakeholders or what each one
  needs.
- 1 — Beginner: Attends a stakeholder meeting arranged by someone else.
- 2 — Intermediate: Manages a straightforward stakeholder relationship
  independently.
- 3 — Advanced: Sets objectives with a stakeholder and manages a relationship
  through disagreement to a workable outcome.
- 4 — Expert: Directs the stakeholder relationship strategy for the whole
  team, ensures stakeholders' objectives are set and supported, and
  negotiates directly with senior stakeholders to resolve issues and enable
  progress.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of a stakeholder relationship strategy the candidate set
  for their team.
- Ask about a negotiation with a senior stakeholder that unblocked progress.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate is trusted to represent the service to
  senior stakeholders directly.

### Skill: Strategic ownership

**What the framework expects at this level**

A service owner is expected to support and coach others in creating and
implementing a successful long-term strategy and tactical approach that
others agree with, to influence and persuade stakeholders to support delivery
of the strategy, to support strategic decision making, and to ensure
strategic alignment across the organisation.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Coaching others in creating strategy, and ensuring
"strategic alignment across the organisation", describe mastery exercised
well beyond the candidate's own service.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe their service's long-term strategy or how it was
  set.
- 1 — Beginner: Delivers against a strategy set by someone else.
- 2 — Intermediate: Contributes ideas to a strategy discussion when asked.
- 3 — Advanced: Shapes their own service's tactical approach and persuades a
  stakeholder to support part of it.
- 4 — Expert: Coaches others in creating and implementing a long-term
  strategy that stakeholders agree with, persuades senior stakeholders to
  support its delivery, and ensures the service's strategy stays aligned
  with the wider organisation's.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of a long-term strategy the candidate coached someone
  else to create or implement.
- Ask about a time they persuaded a sceptical senior stakeholder to support a
  strategic direction.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate has kept a service's strategy aligned
  with wider organisational change.
