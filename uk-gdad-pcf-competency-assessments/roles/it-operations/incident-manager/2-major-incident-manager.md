# Major Incident Manager - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This assessment is for an assessor — a line manager, an interview panel
member, or anyone else evaluating a candidate or an employee — who needs to
judge whether a person has the baseline abilities the IT operations
profession's major incident manager role level requires. It measures
demonstrated skill against the UK GDAD Profession Capability Framework's own
description of this level, for a hiring decision, a promotion or progression
board, onboarding a new starter, or a periodic capability check. It is one
input into that decision, never the whole of it — see `## Advisory` before
you begin.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating on this
form must never be the sole basis for a hiring, promotion, pay, performance or
disciplinary decision. Before using it, consult your organisation's human
resources, people development, occupational psychology or legal compliance
colleagues.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you know what the
framework actually expects before you look at any evidence. Then gather what
exists for this person: a CV or portfolio, a structured interview, a work
sample or task, direct observation of them at work, or references from people
who have worked with them. At this level the framework expects someone to
manage significant outages and crises and to work closely with workplace
services, infrastructure, applications operators and communication managers,
and in some departments to act as head of service — so look for evidence of
leading through a genuinely high-stakes event, not just a routine incident.

**Score on evidence, not impression.** Every rating must trace to something
the person did, said, produced, or was observed doing. If you cannot point to
evidence for a rating, record it as unknown rather than guess — an honest gap
teaches you more than a guess dressed up as a score.

**Stay consistent.** Score every candidate for this role level the same way,
using the same evidence sources wherever you can. For a hiring or promotion
decision, have more than one assessor score independently, then compare notes,
rather than settling on a single view first.

**Fairness.** An unvalidated, AI-drafted instrument like this one, used
inconsistently, can produce unlawful adverse impact even when nobody intends
it. Consistent, evidence-based administration by more than one assessor
materially reduces that risk. Never let a rating be influenced by anything
other than demonstrated, job-related evidence.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point
proficiency scale — how well the person does the thing, on the evidence
available, not how often they do it.

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

**Worked example.** Take Skill: Incident management. At 2 — Intermediate, a
candidate diagnoses and resolves a familiar incident unassisted. At 3 —
Advanced, the baseline for this level, they lead the investigation and
resolution of an incident — the framework's own wording for this level —
co-ordinating other people involved rather than working alone. At 4 — Expert,
they lead the response to the most serious or complex incidents and are the
person others learn incident leadership from. Calibrate against evidence like
this before you start scoring.

## About this role level

IT operations role: Incident manager. Incident managers aim to restore normal
service operation as quickly as possible and minimise any adverse effects on
business operations, so that the best possible levels of service quality and
availability are returned and maintained.

The role has two levels, in order: incident manager and major incident
manager. This assessment covers major incident manager, the second of those.

Role level: Major incident manager. A major incident manager manages
significant outages and crises and reports issues to problem management. At
this level, the framework expects someone to work closely with workplace
services, infrastructure, applications operators and communication managers.
In some departments, this role may also be the head of service.

The framework names eleven skills for this level: Asset and configuration
management, Availability and capacity management, Change management,
Community collaboration, Continuity management, Incident management,
Ownership and initiative, Problem management, Service focus, Service
management framework knowledge, and User focus.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Asset and configuration management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A major incident manager tracks, logs and corrects information to protect
assets and components.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. The framework's wording for this skill is
unchanged from the level below, so the baseline stays at Intermediate too —
this level's step up shows in Incident management, Change management and
crisis leadership, not here.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot say what asset or component information needs protecting.
- 1 — Beginner: logs asset information when told exactly what to record.
- 2 — Intermediate: tracks, logs and corrects asset and component information
  accurately without prompting.
- 3 — Advanced: spots a gap in asset tracking before it puts a component at
  risk, and improves how the team records it.
- 4 — Expert: owns asset and component protection across a whole service.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe how they track and correct asset information
  day to day, with an example.
- Review an asset or component log they maintained.
- Ask a referee for an example of the candidate catching an inaccurate asset
  record before it caused a problem.

### Skill: Availability and capacity management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A major incident manager manages service components to ensure they meet
business needs and key performance indicators (KPIs).

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. This is a step up from explaining the processes at
the level below: managing service components against business needs and KPIs
is independent, accountable work.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot describe what a service component's KPI measures.
- 1 — Beginner: monitors a service component's KPI with a colleague
  interpreting what it means.
- 2 — Intermediate: monitors service components against KPIs and flags a
  breach when told to check for one.
- 3 — Advanced: manages service components proactively to ensure they meet
  business needs and KPIs, without being told to check.
- 4 — Expert: redesigns how a service's availability or capacity is managed
  to prevent recurring KPI breaches.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe how they managed a service component to keep
  it within its KPI.
- Review a capacity or availability report they own.
- Ask a referee for an example of the candidate acting proactively before a
  KPI breach happened.

### Skill: Change management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A major incident manager analyses and assesses the impact of change,
documents change requests, and actions changes from change requests.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. This is a clear step up from implementing change
requests under supervision at the level below: assessing impact and actioning
changes independently is strong, independent grasp of the skill.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot describe what assessing the impact of a change involves.
- 1 — Beginner: documents a change request when given the details to record.
- 2 — Intermediate: assesses the impact of a straightforward change with a
  colleague checking the assessment.
- 3 — Advanced: independently analyses and assesses the impact of a change,
  documents the request properly, and actions it.
- 4 — Expert: is consulted on the impact assessment for the highest-risk
  changes in the service.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to walk through a change they assessed, documented and
  actioned, including what they judged the impact to be and why.
- Review a change record showing their impact assessment.
- Ask a referee about a change the candidate actioned that had a wider impact
  than first expected, and how they handled it.

### Skill: Community collaboration

**What the framework expects at this level**

A major incident manager works collaboratively in a group, actively
networking with others, adapts feedback to ensure it is effective and
lasting, uses their topic to identify problems or issues in the team dynamic
and rectify them, and identifies issues through Agile "health checks" with the
team, helping to stimulate the right responses.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Rectifying team-dynamic issues and running structured
health checks, rather than simply contributing to others' work and
recognising issues, is independent facilitation and networking — a genuine
step up from the level below.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: works alone and does not engage with how the wider team is
  functioning.
- 1 — Beginner: contributes to a colleague's work when asked to.
- 2 — Intermediate: notices and raises a team issue, but relies on someone
  else to run a structured check on team dynamics.
- 3 — Advanced: actively networks across a group, adapts feedback so it lands
  well, and runs or contributes meaningfully to an Agile health check that
  changes how the team responds.
- 4 — Expert: is sought out to facilitate the most difficult team-dynamic
  conversations across a wider group.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a team-dynamic issue they identified and
  rectified.
- Ask a referee for an example of the candidate's feedback landing well
  because of how they adapted it.
- Ask about the candidate's role in an Agile health check or similar exercise
  and what changed as a result.

### Skill: Continuity management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A major incident manager manages the runbooks and the processes around
service continuity, and tests the runbooks to ensure that service
availability is maintained.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Owning and testing runbooks, rather than explaining
basic continuity principles as at the level below, is independent,
accountable management of continuity.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot describe what a runbook is for.
- 1 — Beginner: follows a runbook someone else manages.
- 2 — Intermediate: updates a runbook with a colleague reviewing the change.
- 3 — Advanced: manages the runbooks and continuity processes for their area,
  and tests them to confirm service availability is maintained.
- 4 — Expert: redesigns the continuity approach for a service based on what
  testing revealed.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a runbook they manage and how they last
  tested it.
- Review a runbook or continuity test record they own.
- Ask a referee for an example of a continuity test the candidate ran that
  found a real gap.

### Skill: Incident management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A major incident manager leads the investigation and resolution of incidents.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Leading the investigation and resolution, rather than
personally diagnosing and prioritising as at the level below, is
co-ordinating other people through an incident — clearly independent,
strong-grasp work, and the core of what a major incident manager does.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot organise an incident response beyond their own actions.
- 1 — Beginner: follows another manager's direction during an incident.
- 2 — Intermediate: directs a straightforward incident response with support
  available if it escalates.
- 3 — Advanced: leads the investigation and resolution of most incidents
  independently, co-ordinating others involved.
- 4 — Expert: leads the response to the most serious or complex incidents and
  crises, and is the person others learn incident leadership from.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a significant outage or crisis they led the
  investigation and resolution of, including how they directed others
  involved.
- Ask a referee to describe the candidate's leadership during a difficult
  incident.
- Review an incident report or post-incident review the candidate authored or
  led.

### Skill: Ownership and initiative

**What the framework expects at this level**

A major incident manager takes accountability for issues that occur and is
proactive in searching for potential problems, and achieves excellent user
outcomes.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Proactively searching for problems before they occur,
rather than only owning an issue once it exists, is a genuine step up from
the level below, matching independent, strong-grasp practice.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: only acts once an issue has already been raised.
- 1 — Beginner: owns an existing issue through to resolution when reminded to.
- 2 — Intermediate: owns issues through to resolution unprompted, but has not
  yet proactively surfaced a potential problem before it occurred.
- 3 — Advanced: takes accountability for issues that occur, proactively looks
  for potential problems, and consistently achieves a good outcome for users.
- 4 — Expert: builds a habit of proactive problem-searching into how the
  whole team works.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of a potential problem the candidate spotted and
  addressed before it became a major incident.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate takes accountability when something
  goes wrong, or shifts blame.
- Review evidence of a user outcome the candidate achieved that went beyond
  the minimum fix.

### Skill: Problem management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A major incident manager initiates and monitors actions to investigate
patterns and trends to resolve problems, effectively consults specialists
where required, determines the appropriate resolution and assists with its
implementation, and determines preventative measures.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. The framework's wording for this skill is
unchanged from the level below, so the baseline holds; this level's added
weight sits in Incident management and crisis leadership rather than a wider
Problem management expectation, and the role reports problem issues onward
rather than owning problem resolution end to end.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot distinguish a problem from an incident.
- 1 — Beginner: investigates a problem when told exactly what to look at.
- 2 — Intermediate: initiates and monitors a problem investigation, consults
  specialists appropriately, and determines a resolution unassisted.
- 3 — Advanced: identifies a preventative measure that stops a problem
  recurring, not just a one-off resolution.
- 4 — Expert: spots a pattern across problems that others have missed.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a problem investigation they initiated, who
  they consulted, and what resolution they determined.
- Review a problem record or root-cause note they contributed to.
- Ask a referee for an example of a preventative measure the candidate
  determined that stopped a recurring issue.

### Skill: Service focus

**What the framework expects at this level**

A major incident manager sees the bigger picture by taking groups of services
and investigating how to get the best of underlying services.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Taking groups of services together, rather than only
organising inputs into a coherent framework for their own area as at the
level below, is a genuine widening of scope that matches Advanced.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: only ever considers one service at a time.
- 1 — Beginner: helps analyse a group of services under a colleague's
  direction.
- 2 — Intermediate: takes several inputs and organises their own work into a
  coherent, working routine.
- 3 — Advanced: takes a group of related services and investigates how to get
  the best out of the underlying services that support them.
- 4 — Expert: redesigns how a group of services works together based on that
  investigation.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe an improvement they made that spanned more
  than one service.
- Review an analysis where the candidate looked at a group of services
  together.
- Ask a referee for an example of the candidate spotting an opportunity
  across services that a narrower view would have missed.

### Skill: Service management framework knowledge

**What the framework expects at this level**

A major incident manager shows they have a Level 3 service management
framework qualification.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. The framework's wording is unchanged from the
level below: the same qualification is expected, applied with working
knowledge rather than taught to others.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: holds no service management framework qualification and cannot
  describe the framework's basic concepts.
- 1 — Beginner: is working towards a Level 3 service management framework
  qualification.
- 2 — Intermediate: holds a Level 3 service management framework
  qualification and applies its concepts correctly in day-to-day work.
- 3 — Advanced: applies the framework's concepts to improve how their team
  works, beyond what the qualification alone requires.
- 4 — Expert: holds a higher-level qualification or is asked to explain the
  framework to others.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to show their Level 3 service management framework
  certificate or equivalent evidence.
- Ask the candidate to explain a framework concept, such as incident versus
  problem, in their own words.
- Ask a referee how the candidate applies the framework in practice.

### Skill: User focus

**What the framework expects at this level**

A major incident manager collaborates with user researchers and can represent
users internally, explains the difference between user needs and the desires
of the user, champions user research to focus on all users, prioritises and
defines approaches to understand the user story while guiding others in doing
so, and offers recommendations on the best tools and methods to use.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Championing user research and explaining the
difference between needs and desires is a genuine step up from engaging users
directly on individual incidents at the level below, matching independent,
strong-grasp practice.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: resolves the reported fault without asking about the user's
  wider impact.
- 1 — Beginner: engages users directly when directed to.
- 2 — Intermediate: engages the user or stakeholder directly to understand
  the business impact, and uses that to prioritise and resolve the incident.
- 3 — Advanced: champions user research, can explain the difference between
  what users ask for and what they need, and recommends tools or methods to
  use.
- 4 — Expert: represents users internally, prioritises and defines the
  approach to the user story, and guides colleagues through it.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of the candidate representing users' interests in a
  cross-functional discussion.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate is seen as championing user research
  internally.
- Review a recommendation the candidate made about which tools or methods to
  use for understanding user impact during incidents.
