# Principal Infrastructure Operations Engineer - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This assessment is for an assessor — a line manager, an interview panel
member, or anyone else evaluating a candidate or an employee — who needs to
judge whether a person has the baseline abilities the IT operations
profession's principal infrastructure operations engineer role level
requires. It measures demonstrated skill against the UK GDAD Profession
Capability Framework's own description of this level, for a hiring decision,
a promotion or progression board, onboarding a new starter, or a periodic
capability check. It is one input into that decision, never the whole of it
— see `## Advisory` before you begin.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating on this
form must never be the sole basis for a hiring, promotion, pay, performance or
disciplinary decision. Before using it, consult your organisation's human
resources, people development, occupational psychology or legal compliance
colleagues.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you know what the
framework actually expects before you look at any evidence. Then gather what
exists for this person: a CV or portfolio, a structured interview, a work
sample or task, direct observation of them at work, or references from people
who have worked with them. At this level the framework expects leadership and
direction of IT operations specialist teams, ownership of strategic and
tactical technology roadmaps, and ownership of operational supplier
relationships, so look for a track record over time — a roadmap that played
out, a supplier relationship managed through a difficult period — rather than
a single task.

**Score on evidence, not impression.** Every rating must trace to something
the person did, said, produced, or was observed doing. If you cannot point to
evidence for a rating, record it as unknown rather than guess — an honest gap
teaches you more than a guess dressed up as a score.

**Stay consistent.** Score every candidate for this role level the same way,
using the same evidence sources wherever you can. For a hiring or promotion
decision, have more than one assessor score independently, then compare notes,
rather than settling on a single view first.

**Fairness.** An unvalidated, AI-drafted instrument like this one, used
inconsistently, can produce unlawful adverse impact even when nobody intends
it. Consistent, evidence-based administration by more than one assessor
materially reduces that risk. Never let a rating be influenced by anything
other than demonstrated, job-related evidence.

**Handling sensitive material.** Infrastructure operations at this level
routinely touches live production infrastructure, supplier contracts and
regulatory compliance. When you record evidence in `## Assessor notes and
evidence log`, describe what the person did and how they reasoned about it —
not classified material, live vulnerability detail, or commercially sensitive
contract terms.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point
proficiency scale — how well the person does the thing, on the evidence
available, not how often they do it.

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

**Worked example.** Take Skill: Service focus. At 3 — Advanced, a candidate
improves a service framework so it works better for the wider team. At 4 —
Expert, the baseline for this level, they see the bigger picture by taking
groups of services and investigating how to get the best of the underlying
services that support them — the framework's own wording for this level, and
evidence of exactly the strategic, cross-service view a principal is expected
to hold. Calibrate against evidence like this before you start scoring.

## About this role level

IT operations role: Infrastructure operations engineer. An infrastructure
operations engineer supports, manages and maintains the core infrastructure
that underpins production services.

The role has five levels, in order: associate infrastructure operations
engineer, infrastructure operations engineer, senior infrastructure
operations engineer, lead infrastructure operations engineer, and principal
infrastructure operations engineer. This assessment covers principal
infrastructure operations engineer, the highest of those.

Role level: Principal infrastructure operations engineer. A principal
infrastructure operations engineer leads and directs IT operations specialist
teams in monitoring, operating and supporting technical solutions.

At this role level, the framework expects someone to:

- develop the strategic and tactical roadmaps for technologies and services,
  ensuring they are future-proofed and the organisation derives maximum value
  from investment in technologies
- own the operational relationships with suppliers, ensuring services and
  products are aligned to industry best practice and regulatory and
  contractual requirements

The framework names twelve skills for this level: Asset and configuration
management, Availability and capacity management, Change management, Coding
and scripting, Incident management, Ownership and initiative, Problem
management, Service focus, Service management framework knowledge, Technical
specialism, Testing, and User focus.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Asset and configuration management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal infrastructure operations engineer manages configuration items
and related information, and manages service compliance and risk.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Managing service compliance and risk, rather than
controlling or verifying individual assets as at the levels below, is
ownership of the whole governance picture — full mastery by the scale's
definition.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot describe what service compliance or risk means for asset
  and configuration management.
- 1 — Beginner: manages a single configuration item under a colleague's
  direction.
- 2 — Intermediate: manages configuration items and related information for
  their own area with some guidance.
- 3 — Advanced: manages configuration items and related information
  independently, and flags compliance or risk issues as they arise.
- 4 — Expert: owns service compliance and risk for asset and configuration
  management across the service, and sets how the wider team manages it.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe how they manage service compliance and risk
  for configuration items across a service, not a single item.
- Review a compliance or risk record they own.
- Ask a referee for an example of the candidate identifying and managing a
  configuration-related risk before it became a problem.

### Skill: Availability and capacity management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal infrastructure operations engineer ensures the correct
implementation of availability and capacity management standards and
procedures, identifies capacity issues and implements the required changes,
and initiates remedial action.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Ensuring correct implementation of standards across the
service and initiating remedial action, rather than managing individual
service components as at the levels below, is standard-setting, accountable
ownership — Expert on this scale.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot describe what a capacity management standard covers.
- 1 — Beginner: follows a capacity management standard someone else has set.
- 2 — Intermediate: applies an established capacity standard to their own
  service component.
- 3 — Advanced: identifies a capacity issue independently and implements a
  fix for their own area.
- 4 — Expert: ensures correct implementation of availability and capacity
  standards and procedures across the service, and initiates remedial action
  when they are not met.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a capacity or availability standard they
  ensured was correctly implemented across a service.
- Review a remedial action plan or capacity change the candidate initiated.
- Ask a referee for an example of the candidate identifying a capacity issue
  before it caused a service impact.

### Skill: Change management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal infrastructure operations engineer manages high-impact, complex
change requests and ensures that release policies, procedures and processes
are applied.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. The framework's wording is unchanged from the lead
level, so the baseline holds — full ownership of the highest-impact changes
and release policy is expected at both grades.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot describe what makes a change high-impact or complex.
- 1 — Beginner: contributes to part of a complex change under a colleague's
  direction.
- 2 — Intermediate: manages a moderately complex change with a senior
  colleague available to check progress.
- 3 — Advanced: manages a high-impact, complex change independently, and
  applies release policies correctly.
- 4 — Expert: manages the highest-impact, most complex changes in the
  service, and ensures the wider team applies release policy consistently.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a high-impact, complex change they managed
  end to end, including how they handled the risk.
- Review a change record or post-implementation review for a complex change
  they led.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate's management of complex changes has
  needed correction, and how release policy compliance was checked.

### Skill: Incident management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal infrastructure operations engineer leads the investigation and

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. The framework's wording is unchanged from the lead

**Behavioural indicators**

### Skill: Ownership and initiative

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal infrastructure operations engineer takes accountability for
issues that occur and is proactive in searching for potential problems, and
achieves excellent user outcomes.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. The framework's wording is unchanged from the lead
level; at principal level this proactive accountability is expected to extend
across the team's strategy and supplier relationships, which supports holding
the baseline at Expert rather than lowering it.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: only acts once an issue has already been raised.
- 1 — Beginner: owns an existing issue through to resolution when reminded to.
- 2 — Intermediate: owns issues through to resolution unprompted, but has not
  yet proactively surfaced a potential problem before it occurred.
- 3 — Advanced: takes accountability for issues that occur, proactively looks
  for potential problems, and consistently achieves a good outcome for users.
- 4 — Expert: builds a habit of proactive problem-searching and accountability
  into how the whole team works, and models it for other leads.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of a potential problem the candidate spotted and
  addressed before it became an incident, at a team or service level.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate takes accountability when something
  goes wrong, or shifts blame.
- Review evidence of a user outcome the candidate achieved that went beyond
  the minimum fix.

### Skill: Problem management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal infrastructure operations engineer ensures that the right actions
are taken to investigate, resolve and anticipate problems, and co-ordinates
the team to investigate problems, implement solutions and take preventive
measures.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Ensuring the right actions are taken and co-ordinating
the whole team, rather than personally determining a resolution as at the
levels below, is directing others through problem management — full mastery
on this scale.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot describe how problem management should be co-ordinated
  across a team.
- 1 — Beginner: contributes to a problem investigation someone else
  co-ordinates.
- 2 — Intermediate: co-ordinates a straightforward problem investigation with
  a colleague available to check the approach.
- 3 — Advanced: ensures the right actions are taken on most problems and
  co-ordinates the team's response independently.
- 4 — Expert: ensures the team consistently investigates, resolves and
  anticipates problems, and is the escalation point when co-ordination breaks
  down.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe how they co-ordinated a team through a
  significant problem investigation.
- Review a problem management process or escalation record they own.
- Ask a referee whether the team's problem management has improved under the
  candidate's co-ordination.

### Skill: Service focus

### Skill: Service management framework knowledge

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal infrastructure operations engineer shows they have an expert
certificate in the service management framework qualification.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. The framework names an expert-level certificate at this
level, in place of the Level 3 qualification held at every level below — the
framework's own words set the baseline here.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: holds no service management framework qualification.
- 1 — Beginner: holds the Level 3 qualification only, and is not yet working
  towards the expert certificate.
- 2 — Intermediate: is working towards the expert certificate in the service
  management framework.
- 3 — Advanced: holds the expert certificate but has not yet been asked to
  apply it to shape wider practice.
- 4 — Expert: holds the expert certificate in the service management
  framework and applies it to shape how the wider team or organisation
  practises.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to show their expert-level service management framework
  certificate or equivalent evidence.
- Ask them to describe how they have applied expert-level knowledge to shape
  practice beyond their own work.

### Skill: Technical specialism

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal infrastructure operations engineer understands the direction of
future technologies and delivers a model to support and maintain those future
technologies and any databases that coexist in the current environment.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Understanding where technology is heading and
delivering a support model for it, rather than setting today's standards as
at lead level, is forward-looking, strategic mastery.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 1 — Beginner: repeats a view on future technology direction that someone

- 2 — Intermediate: forms a view on a specific future technology with a
  colleague checking the reasoning.
- 3 — Advanced: forms an independent, well-reasoned view on the direction of
  future technologies relevant to the service.
- 4 — Expert: delivers a model that supports and maintains future
  technologies alongside the databases and systems that coexist with them
  today.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a technology direction they identified and
  how they planned to support it alongside existing systems.

### Skill: Testing

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal infrastructure operations engineer manages testing activities

preventative action when risks become unacceptable, and manages customer
relations.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Managing risk to the point of preventative action, and
managing customer relations around testing, is a wider, judgement-heavy remit

**Behavioural indicators**

### Skill: User focus

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal infrastructure operations engineer gives direction on which
tools or methods to use, demonstrates experience in meeting the needs of
users across a variety of channels, brings insight and expertise in how user
needs have changed over time to ensure they are met by the business, and
applies strategic thinking to provide the best service for the end user.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Bringing insight on how user needs have changed over
time and applying strategic thinking, on top of directing tools and methods,
is exactly the mastery-plus-guidance definition of Expert, and a clear step
beyond the lead level's championing role.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has no view on how user needs have changed over time.
- 1 — Beginner: follows direction on which tools or methods to use.
- 2 — Intermediate: meets user needs well through one channel with guidance.
- 3 — Advanced: champions user research and guides others through the user
  story.
- 4 — Expert: gives direction on tools and methods across a variety of
  channels, brings insight on how user needs have changed over time, and
  applies strategic thinking to keep the service meeting them.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe how user needs for this service have changed
  over time, and how they have responded strategically.
- Review a strategy or tooling recommendation the candidate directed.
- Ask a referee for an example of the candidate's strategic thinking on user
  focus changing the service's direction.
