# Senior Business Analyst - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This assessment is for an assessor — a line manager, a panel, or a reviewer —
judging whether a candidate or employee meets the baseline abilities expected
of a **senior business analyst** in the product and delivery profession. It
measures demonstrated skill against the framework's own description of this
role level, for a hiring decision, a promotion from business analyst, or a
periodic capability check.

This is one input among several into that decision, never the whole of it. See
`## Advisory` below for why.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating on this
form must never be the sole basis for a hiring, promotion, pay, performance or
disciplinary decision. Before using it, consult your organisation's human
resources, people development, occupational psychology or legal compliance
colleagues.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` before you score anything.
This level introduces leadership across large or complex projects, mentoring,
and community of practice management — the evidence you need is about a track
record of leading work and developing others, not just about the candidate's
own delivery.

**Score on evidence, not impression.** Every rating must trace to something
the person did, said, produced, or was observed doing. At this level, ask for
examples that show scale and leadership — a large or complex project, a
mentoring relationship, a best practice the candidate developed and others
adopted. A confident description of what "good" looks like is not the same as
having led it. If you cannot point to evidence for a rating, record it as
unknown rather than guess.

**Be consistent.** Score every candidate for this role level the same way.
Where possible, have more than one assessor score independently before
comparing notes — this matters especially for a hiring or promotion decision.

**Stay fair.** See [Fair and lawful use](#fair-and-lawful-use) below. Never let
a rating be influenced by anything other than demonstrated, job-related
evidence.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

This is a **proficiency** scale, not a frequency scale: it rates how well the
person does the thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it or
how they feel about it.

**Worked example — Business process improvement.** At 2, a candidate can
analyse and evaluate a process, propose an improvement and validate it, with
some support. At 3 — the baseline for this role level — a candidate leads
process analysis and evaluation to define performance problems and
opportunities, leads the design, test, implementation and assessment of
improvements, and manages the execution of business process tests and
usability evaluations, all with real independence and without needing someone
more senior to check the work. At 4, by contrast, a candidate sets the
direction for process improvement methods, tools and standards across an
organisation and coaches others in the discipline — evidence of that scope
sits above senior business analyst and points towards a lead business analyst
assessment instead.

## About this role level

The product and delivery profession's business analyst role exists to help
teams analyse and understand a business problem or opportunity: to research
how a business or business area works — its people, organisation, processes,
information, data and technology — identify areas for improvement, explore
feasible options, analyse the effects of change and define success measures.
Business analysts identify and elaborate user and business needs so that
services and business change can be effectively designed, developed and
tested; they make prioritisation and minimum viable product decisions using
analysis-led insights; they ensure new products and services meet business and
user needs and align with organisational goals; and they understand the
business and policy constraints a piece of work sits within, and assess the
implications.

The role has six levels: trainee business analyst, junior business analyst,
business analyst, senior business analyst, lead business analyst, and head of
business analysis. This assessment is for the fourth of those.

A **senior business analyst** has a good understanding of strategic arenas
and leads large or complex projects. At this role level, the framework says
the person will have functional or people management responsibilities, or a
combination of both, mentor others, develop best practice, own stakeholder
relationships, and manage community of practice activities within a business
area. This is a marked step up from a business analyst: the scale of the work
grows from small to medium projects to large or complex ones, and leadership
of other people — mentoring, developing best practice, managing a community
of practice — becomes an explicit accountability rather than an informal
extra.

The framework names nine skills for this level: Adapting to delivery
methodologies, Business modelling, Business process improvement, Context,
problem and option analysis, Defining and managing business needs, user needs
and requirements, Stakeholder relationship management, Digital and data
systems analysis, Testing (business analysis), and User experience analysis.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Adapting to delivery methodologies

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior business analyst is expected to determine the approach for their
role within the team's delivery methodology, to define and agree the scope of
delivery for their role within a multidisciplinary team, to negotiate and
agree delivery priorities with the team and relevant stakeholders, and to
clearly communicate the value of iterative delivery and realising benefits
early.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. "Determine", "negotiate" and "clearly communicate the
value" describe someone shaping how delivery happens, not just adapting to it —
a strong, independent grasp of the skill.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe how they would shape their role's scope within a
  delivery methodology.
- 1 — Beginner: Follows an agreed approach within a methodology but has not
  negotiated scope or priorities.
- 2 — Intermediate: Adapts their own working practice to a chosen methodology,
  with some support in negotiating scope with stakeholders.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently determines their approach, negotiates and agrees
  delivery priorities with the team and stakeholders, and can clearly
  communicate why iterative delivery matters to a sceptical stakeholder.
- 4 — Expert: Sets and iterates delivery methodology standards across an
  organisation, and coaches others in choosing and applying them — not
  expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example where the candidate negotiated delivery priorities with a
  stakeholder who wanted something different, and how it was resolved.
- Ask how they have communicated the value of iterative delivery to a
  stakeholder unfamiliar with or sceptical of it.
- Ask a referee how much the candidate shapes, rather than just follows, a
  team's delivery approach.

### Skill: Business modelling

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior business analyst is expected to lead on the modelling of business
processes, systems and data at varying levels of complexity across a project
or programme, and to manage the impact of proposed changes.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. "Lead on" and "manage the impact" across a project or
programme is a clear step up from independently producing a single model, and
sits below the enterprise-scale scope the framework describes at the next
level.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has never led modelling work for a project.
- 1 — Beginner: Contributes to modelling led by someone else.
- 2 — Intermediate: Produces business models independently for a bounded piece
  of work, with limited direction.
- 3 — Advanced: Leads the modelling of processes, systems and data across a
  project or programme, at varying levels of complexity, and manages how
  proposed changes ripple through what has been modelled.
- 4 — Expert: Not expected at this level. Determining scope and methodology
  for representing complex programmes across an organisation belongs to a lead
  business analyst or above.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of modelling work the candidate led across a whole
  project or programme, and how they scoped it.
- Ask how they managed the effect of a change to something already modelled.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate is the person others turn to for
  modelling leadership on a project.

### Skill: Business process improvement

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior business analyst is expected to lead process analysis and evaluation
to define business performance problems and opportunities, to lead analysis
and evaluation to design, test, implement and assess business process
improvements, and to manage the design, execution and assessment of business
process tests and usability evaluations.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Every verb here is "lead" or "manage" — independent
ownership of the whole improvement cycle, though not yet setting direction for
others' improvement work across an organisation.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has never led a business process improvement effort.
- 1 — Beginner: Contributes to improvement work led by someone more senior.
- 2 — Intermediate: Owns a single, moderately scoped improvement with some
  support.
- 3 — Advanced: Leads process analysis and evaluation to define problems and
  opportunities, leads design, test, implementation and assessment of
  improvements, and manages the execution of related usability evaluations.
- 4 — Expert: Not expected at this level. Leading identification of
  large-scale improvement opportunities and setting direction on methods,
  tools and standards belongs to a lead business analyst or above.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of a business process improvement the candidate led from
  problem definition through to assessed outcome.
- Ask how they managed a usability evaluation as part of that work.
- Ask a referee to describe the scale and independence of a process
  improvement effort the candidate led.

### Skill: Context, problem and option analysis

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior business analyst is expected to lead the approach to understanding
the context, the problem and root causes, to set goals for a piece of work
that align to the organisation's strategic context, and to lead the approach
to developing and evaluating multiple options, sharing and advocating for
recommendations to gain support.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. "Lead the approach", "set goals" and "advocating for
recommendations to gain support" describe someone driving analysis and
winning buy-in independently — well beyond formulating a recommendation for
their own piece of work.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot lead an analysis approach or set goals aligned to strategy.
- 1 — Beginner: Follows an analysis approach set by someone else.
- 2 — Intermediate: Plans and uses appropriate techniques independently for
  their own piece of work.
- 3 — Advanced: Leads the analysis approach for a project, sets goals aligned
  to the organisation's strategic context, and advocates for recommendations
  to build support among stakeholders.
- 4 — Expert: Not expected at this level. Advising on approaches to options
  analysis and guiding strategic plans across the organisation belongs to a
  lead business analyst or above.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example where the candidate set the analysis approach for a
  project and led others through it.
- Ask how they built support for a recommendation that faced resistance.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate's recommendations are typically acted
  on, and how much advocacy that took.

### Skill: Defining and managing business needs, user needs and requirements

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior business analyst is expected to advise on the approach to
requirements management within a project or programme, to define the most
appropriate requirements management life cycle methods and ensure the
requirement can be traced in the design, build, test, tender and evaluation
phases, and to co-ordinate and review the prioritisation of requirements and
engage in the negotiation of solutions to help meet programme objectives.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Advising on approach for a project or programme, and
negotiating solutions against programme objectives, is a genuine step up in
scope and independence from a business analyst's own task-based ownership.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot advise on or design a requirements management approach.
- 1 — Beginner: Manages requirements for their own piece of work with
  supervision.
- 2 — Intermediate: Independently manages requirements for their own piece of
  work, with limited supervision.
- 3 — Advanced: Advises on the requirements management approach for a project
  or programme, defines life cycle methods that trace requirements through
  design, build, test, tender and evaluation, and negotiates solutions to meet
  programme objectives.
- 4 — Expert: Not expected at this level. Directing requirements management
  approach and enabling others to select life cycle methods belongs to a lead
  business analyst or above.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example where the candidate advised a project or programme on its
  requirements management approach.
- Ask how they traced a requirement through design, build and test to ensure
  nothing was lost.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate has negotiated requirement trade-offs
  against wider programme objectives.

### Skill: Stakeholder relationship management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior business analyst is expected to work with the team to develop and
maintain an understanding of stakeholders, to work with the team to develop
and implement stakeholder communications strategies, to identify and resolve
issues, influence stakeholders and manage relationships effectively, and to
build long-term strategic relationships and communicate clearly and regularly
with stakeholders.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Owning stakeholder relationships is explicit in the
level description, and the skill's own wording — strategic relationships,
communications strategies, influencing and resolving issues — describes an
established, independent practice.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe a stakeholder communications strategy.
- 1 — Beginner: Manages individual stakeholder interactions with support.
- 2 — Intermediate: Independently manages day-to-day stakeholder
  relationships, building consensus and using evidence to explain decisions.
- 3 — Advanced: Develops and implements a stakeholder communications strategy
  with the team, resolves stakeholder issues by influencing rather than
  escalating, and builds long-term strategic relationships.
- 4 — Expert: Not expected at this level. Directing stakeholder relationship
  strategy across multiple teams belongs to a lead business analyst or above.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of a stakeholder communications strategy the candidate
  helped develop and implement.
- Ask about a strategic stakeholder relationship they have built over time,
  and what sustained it.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate resolves stakeholder issues through
  influence rather than needing a manager to intervene.

### Skill: Digital and data systems analysis

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior business analyst is expected to lead the identification and analysis
of IT system capabilities, developing models and system requirements, to use
systems analysis to support a deeper understanding of how processes and
systems work and to identify gaps, and to work with other digital
professionals to design and develop system and user interfaces, identify gaps
in functionality and assess the effect of proposed software changes on users
or the organisation.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. "Lead" and cross-disciplinary work with other digital
professionals on interfaces and functionality gaps describes a broader,
independent remit than a business analyst's own bounded systems analysis
work.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot lead systems analysis or work with other digital
  professionals on it.
- 1 — Beginner: Analyses a system's capabilities with support.
- 2 — Intermediate: Independently identifies and analyses IT system
  capabilities for a bounded piece of work, with minimal supervision.
- 3 — Advanced: Leads identification and analysis of IT system capabilities
  across a project, works with other digital professionals on interface
  design, and assesses the effect of proposed software changes on users or the
  organisation.
- 4 — Expert: Not expected at this level. Setting direction for systems
  analysis and enabling others to explore functionality gaps belongs to a lead
  business analyst or above.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example where the candidate led systems analysis work involving
  other digital professionals, such as developers or designers.
- Ask how they assessed the effect of a proposed software change on users or
  the organisation.
- Ask a referee how independently the candidate leads systems analysis work.

### Skill: Testing (business analysis)

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior business analyst is expected to recommend the value of testing and
apply the relevant tools and techniques to ensure test validity, to advise on
business scenarios and develop acceptance criteria to ensure requirements can
be traced to develop functionality, and to review prototypes, test plans and
test outcome reporting.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. The wording is unchanged from the business analyst
level, so the baseline stays the same — this skill's expectations plateau
here rather than continuing to climb every level, which the assessor should
treat as the framework's own statement rather than an oversight.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot develop acceptance criteria or review a test plan
  meaningfully.
- 1 — Beginner: Writes acceptance criteria with guidance.
- 2 — Intermediate: Independently identifies scenarios and develops
  acceptance criteria that trace requirements to functionality.
- 3 — Advanced: Recommends the value and approach to testing, applies the
  right tools and techniques to ensure test validity, and reviews prototypes,
  test plans and outcome reporting critically.
- 4 — Expert: Not expected by the framework at this level for this skill.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example where the candidate advised on the testing approach for
  a piece of work, not just wrote criteria for it.
- Ask them to review a sample test plan and identify gaps.
- Ask a referee how the candidate's involvement improved test validity or
  caught issues early.

### Skill: User experience analysis

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior business analyst is expected to advise on the approach to analysis,
prioritisation and validation of user experience needs, to recommend a range
of techniques to analyse the user experience and ensure that it meets
business and user needs, and to articulate and communicate how user
experience needs affect the design of a system in a project or programme of
work.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Advising on approach and recommending techniques,
rather than applying a single basic technique themselves, is a step up in
independence and influence from the business analyst level.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot advise on an approach to user experience analysis.
- 1 — Beginner: Applies a technique when told which one to use.
- 2 — Intermediate: Independently applies a basic technique and presents
  findings clearly.
- 3 — Advanced: Advises on the approach to analysing, prioritising and
  validating user experience needs, recommends a range of techniques, and
  articulates how those needs affect a system's design across a project or
  programme.
- 4 — Expert: Not expected at this level for this skill by the framework's
  wording.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example where the candidate advised a project on its approach to
  user experience analysis, not just carried out the analysis themselves.
- Ask how they have communicated the effect of a user experience need on
  system design to a technical audience.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate's recommendations on technique and
  approach have been adopted.
