# Associate Technical Architect - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This assessment is for the person doing the assessing — a line manager, a
hiring panel member, or a reviewer — judging whether a candidate or employee
possesses the baseline abilities the associate technical architect role
level requires. An associate technical architect supports technical
architects in putting forward designs as solutions to technology challenges,
usually under supervision. This is the entry level of a five-level ladder.

Work through the competency matrix below, one skill at a time, then complete
the overall determination. This assessment is one input among several into a
hiring, promotion or development decision, never the whole of it — see
`## Advisory`.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating on this
form must never be the sole basis for a hiring, promotion, pay, performance or
disciplinary decision. Before using it, consult your organisation's human
resources, people development, occupational psychology or legal compliance
colleagues.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` before you score anything, so
you are assessing against this level's own accountabilities — supported work
under supervision — and not against technical architect above it. Gather
whatever evidence exists first: a CV or portfolio, a structured interview, a
work sample or task, direct observation, or references. For this level, a
training exercise or a supervised real task is legitimate evidence; do not
expect the unsupervised track record a more senior level would show.

**Scoring on evidence, not impression.** Every rating must trace to something
the person did, said, produced, or was observed doing. If you cannot point to
evidence for a rating, record it as unknown rather than guess.

**Consistency.** Score every candidate for this role level the same way, and
where possible have a second assessor score independently before you compare
notes — this matters most for a hiring or promotion decision, and materially
reduces the risk of an unlawful adverse impact from an unvalidated,
AI-drafted instrument used inconsistently.

**Fairness.** Never let a rating be influenced by anything other than
demonstrated, job-related evidence — see
[Fair and lawful use](../../../../uk-gdad-pcf-competency-assessments/spec/index.md#fair-and-lawful-use)
in the project specification.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

This is a **proficiency** scale, not a frequency scale: it rates how well the
person does the thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it.

A worked example, using **Technical design throughout the life cycle**:

- A candidate rated **1 — Beginner** has produced a technical design for a
  training exercise or a small, well-understood real task, with a supervisor
  checking the result before it was used.
- A candidate rated **2 — Intermediate** has created a technical design of
  managed risk and complexity for a real piece of work, and adapted it during
  delivery when circumstances changed, with support available.
- A candidate rated **3 — Advanced** independently produces technical
  designs for real work — a bar this level is not yet expected to meet.

## About this role level

**The role: technical architect.** A technical architect provides technical
leadership and architectural design.

**This level: associate technical architect.** An associate technical
architect supports technical architects in putting forward designs as
solutions to technology challenges, usually under supervision.

**Where this level sits.** The technical architect role has five levels:

1. **Associate technical architect** — this level
2. Technical architect
3. Senior technical architect
4. Lead technical architect
5. Principal technical architect

The step up to technical architect is independence: this level's
close-supervision language ("work closely with developers when designing
appropriate solutions") gives way to structured analysis and design
consultation done unsupported.

**Accountabilities at this level.** The framework expects an associate
technical architect to:

- work closely with developers when designing appropriate solutions
- have an understanding of the overall strategy and how your work supports it

**The skills this assessment covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Architect for the whole context
2. Architecture communication
3. Community collaboration
4. Making architectural decisions
5. Strategy design
6. Technical design throughout the life cycle

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Architect for the whole context

**What the framework expects at this level**

Identify relevant information that can inform your architectural work, such
as strategies, roadmaps, policies and technical trends. Understand how your
work supports the team in enabling change.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. Identifying relevant information and understanding
how it connects to the team's change is awareness-level, not yet independent
judgement about what matters.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot say what information — a strategy, a roadmap, a policy —
  would be relevant to a piece of technical work.
- 1 — Beginner: Identifies relevant strategies, roadmaps or policies when
  pointed toward them, and can explain how their work supports the team's
  change.
- 2 — Intermediate: Finds relevant information independently for
  straightforward work, with some guidance on less obvious sources.
- 3 — Advanced: Not expected at this level.
- 4 — Expert: Not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Tell me about a piece of work you
  supported. What information did you look for, and where did you find it?"
- A work sample: give a short scenario and ask the candidate to list the
  kinds of information they would want before starting.

### Skill: Architecture communication

**What the framework expects at this level**

Show an awareness of different ways of creating architecture representations
for a limited audience, including technical and non-technical stakeholders.
Gather and explain information to be used in architecture representations.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. The framework's own word is "awareness," and the
audience is explicitly "limited" — a supported, close-supervision bar.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has not gathered or explained information for an architecture
  representation.
- 1 — Beginner: Aware of different ways to represent architecture, and

- 3 — Advanced: Not expected at this level.
- 4 — Expert: Not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- A work sample: ask the candidate to gather and summarise information for a
  simple architecture representation.

### Skill: Making architectural decisions

**What the framework expects at this level**

Describe the reasoning behind architectural design decisions. Gather
information to inform decisions. Understand architectural governance and
assurance relevant to your work.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. This level describes and gathers rather than makes
decisions — a supporting, close-supervision role in the decision.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot explain why a design decision was made on a piece of work
  they were involved in.
- 1 — Beginner: Describes the reasoning behind a decision made by someone
  else, and gathers information for a decision when asked.
- 2 — Intermediate: Gathers information and contributes reasoning to a
  decision with some independence, and understands the governance process
  relevant to their work.
- 3 — Advanced: Not expected at this level.
- 4 — Expert: Not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Describe a design decision you were
  involved in. What information did you gather?"
- A work sample: give a short scenario and ask what information the
  candidate would gather before a decision could be made.

### Skill: Technical design throughout the life cycle

**What the framework expects at this level**

Create technical designs characterised by managed levels of risk, impact, and
complexity. Provide guidance and support to teams using technical designs
throughout the life cycle. Adapt a technical design if needed during
delivery. Work with well-understood technology and identify appropriate
patterns.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. This bullet uses stronger, doing verbs than the
rest of this level's skill set — "create", "provide guidance", "adapt" — on
work that is already scoped to managed risk and well-understood technology,
which is a genuine Intermediate bar even at associate level.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has not produced a technical design used in real delivery.
- 1 — Beginner: Produces a technical design for managed-risk work with close
  supervision, and needs help adapting it when circumstances change.
- 2 — Intermediate: Independently creates a technical design for
  well-understood technology and managed risk, provides basic guidance to
  the team using it, and adapts it during delivery with occasional support.
- 3 — Advanced: Not expected at this level.
- 4 — Expert: Not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- A portfolio item: a technical design the candidate produced for real or
  training work.
- A structured interview question: "Describe a time your technical design
  had to change during delivery. What prompted the change, and what did
  you do?"
- A work sample: ask the candidate to sketch a technical design for a
  described, well-understood system.
