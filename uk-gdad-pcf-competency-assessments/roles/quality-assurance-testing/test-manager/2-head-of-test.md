# Head of Test - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This is a competency assessment for the quality assurance testing (QAT) role
of test manager, at the head of test role level, as defined by the UK
Government Digital and Data (GDAD) Profession Capability Framework. It is
written for the person doing the assessing — a line manager, a hiring panel,
or a reviewer — judging whether a candidate or employee possesses the
baseline abilities this role level requires.

Head of test is the second and top level in this role's ladder, above test
manager. Use this assessment to judge readiness for organisation-wide
ownership of QAT policy and strategy — for a hiring decision, a promotion
board, onboarding into the role, or a periodic capability check against the
framework.

This assessment is one input among several into that judgement, never the
whole of it. Read `## Advisory` below before you use it.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating on this
form must never be the sole basis for a hiring, promotion, pay, performance or
disciplinary decision. Before using it, consult your organisation's human
resources, people development, occupational psychology or legal compliance
colleagues.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you are assessing
against what this role level actually requires, not a general impression of
seniority. Then gather whatever evidence exists before you start scoring: a
CV or portfolio, a structured interview, a work sample or task, direct
observation, or references. For a role this senior, evidence often means a
track record rather than a single task — allow time to gather it properly.

**Scoring on evidence, not impression.** Every rating you give must trace to
something the person did, said, produced, or was observed doing. If you
cannot point to evidence for a rating, record it as unknown rather than guess
— an honest gap is more useful to the next stage of the process than a
confident guess dressed up as a score.

**Consistency.** Score every candidate for this role level the same way, using
the same evidence types where you can. Where possible, have more than one
assessor score independently and compare notes afterwards, especially for a
hiring or promotion decision — independent scoring catches where one
assessor's impression has drifted from the evidence.

**Fairness.** This is an unvalidated, AI-drafted instrument. Used
inconsistently, or scored on anything other than demonstrated, job-related
evidence, it can produce unlawful adverse impact against people with
protected characteristics. Consistent, evidence-based administration by more
than one assessor materially reduces that risk. See
[Fair and lawful use](#fair-and-lawful-use) in the project specification for
what this means in practice, and never let a rating be influenced by
anything other than demonstrated, job-related evidence.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix below is rated on the same five-point
scale. This is a **proficiency** scale — how well the person does the thing,
on the evidence available — not a frequency scale.

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

**Worked example — Test analysis.** A person rated 2 (Intermediate) analyses
risk for their own team's features independently, using one or two
techniques. A person rated 3 (Advanced) works with stakeholders across
several teams to decide which quality characteristics matter, and coaches a
colleague through their first risk-based analysis. A person rated 4 (Expert)
sets the standards and principles the whole organisation uses for test
analysis, and drives improvements across many teams through advocacy for
risk-based approaches — other teams cite that person's principles when
justifying their own approach to risk. The difference between 3 and 4 is
whether the person's judgement has become the organisation's reference point,
not just a strong individual practice.

## About this role level

A test manager takes ownership of the quality assurance and testing (QAT)
strategy as part of a broader risk-based approach. Head of test is the top
of this role's ladder: it takes ownership of establishing policy for the
test team and setting the testing strategy across the organisation.

This role has two levels, in order: test manager, which first carries full
ownership of a QAT strategy and its delivery, and **head of test** (this
level) above it, where that ownership becomes organisation-wide — setting
policy, not just delivering against it, with no more senior QAT role to
defer to.

At this role level, you will:

- own continuous improvement, researching new technology and new techniques
  to implement change
- make important decisions
- build the QAT community and provide organisational leadership
- potentially advocate test capabilities, both internally and outside
  government
- make sure that teams have the right capabilities

The framework names six skills at this level:

- Communicating between the technical and non-technical
- Designing and executing tests
- Managing, reporting and resolving defects
- Test analysis
- Test and quality planning
- Test engineering

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What the framework expects at this level**

Mediate between people and strengthen relationships, choosing the right way
to communicate for stakeholders at every level. Manage stakeholder
expectations and moderate difficult discussions about high-risk, complex
topics, including when timescales are tight. Speak on behalf of, and
represent, the testing community to large audiences inside and outside the
organisation.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. The skill's own wording is unchanged from test manager,
but head of test's own accountabilities — building the QAT community and
providing organisational leadership, with no more senior QAT role to defer
to — mean this skill has to be exercised at its fullest, not just
competently.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Avoids representing the QAT community to any audience beyond
  their immediate team, and cannot explain a contested quality decision to a
  senior non-technical audience without help preparing every point in
  advance.
- 1 — Beginner: Represents the QAT community in a low-stakes internal
  setting when supported by prepared material, but is not yet trusted to
  handle an unscripted question from a large or external audience.
- 2 — Intermediate: Speaks for the community at organisational events and
  forums on familiar topics, and mediates most cross-team disagreements
  about quality, but hands off the most contested, highest-profile disputes
  rather than resolving them.
- 3 — Advanced: Builds working relationships with stakeholders across the
  organisation proactively, resolves most contested cross-team quality
  disagreements without escalation, and represents the community
  externally on request.
- 4 — Expert: Is the person the organisation turns to when a quality
  dispute is too contested or too high-profile for anyone else to resolve,
  builds the QAT community's standing inside and outside government
  through sustained relationship-building, and other senior leaders cite
  the person's judgement when making their own decisions about risk.

**Evidence to gather**

- Structured interview: ask about a time they built or grew the QAT
  community's standing outside their own organisation.
- Work sample: a piece of external-facing material — a conference talk
  outline, a cross-government working group paper — they produced or led.
- Referee question: ask a senior stakeholder whether they have sought the
  candidate's view when a quality dispute was contested.
- Observed scenario: present a contested, high-profile scenario — two
  departments disagreeing about acceptable risk for a shared service — and
  ask how they would handle it.

### Skill: Designing and executing tests

**What the framework expects at this level**

Set standards and influence organisational decisions for test types,
techniques, design and execution. Coach others in test types, techniques,
design and execution. Advocate for continuous improvement and refinement of
test types and techniques. Make strategic decisions on new or improved test
types and techniques used in your area.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. The framework's own wording is the clearest
ceiling-level language in this role's skill set — setting organisational
standards and making strategic decisions — so full mastery is the baseline,
not an aspiration.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot say what test types or techniques the organisation
  should standardise on, and has no track record of improving how tests are
  designed or run beyond their own team.
- 1 — Beginner: Suggests a small improvement to test design or execution
  within their own team, but needs someone else to decide whether it should
  apply more widely.
- 2 — Intermediate: Improves test design or execution practice for their
  own team independently, and can explain the trade-offs of different
  techniques.
- 3 — Advanced: Coaches other test professionals in test types, techniques,
  design and execution, and successfully proposes a new or improved
  technique that another team adopts.
- 4 — Expert: Sets the standards for test types, techniques, design and
  execution used across the organisation, makes the strategic call on which
  new techniques the organisation invests in, and is the reference point
  other test managers escalate technique disputes to.

**Evidence to gather**

- Work sample: a test standards document or technique evaluation they
  authored that other teams now follow.
- Structured interview: ask about a strategic decision to adopt or retire a
  test type or technique across more than one team, and what evidence
  supported it.
- Referee question: ask a peer test manager whether the candidate's
  standards or decisions shaped their own team's testing approach.
- Document review: review an organisational test policy or standard
  attributed to the candidate.

### Skill: Managing, reporting and resolving defects

**What the framework expects at this level**

Lead and coach others in improving test and defect management processes.
Support others in assessing complex and challenging defects across the
organisation. Lead and coach others in using defect patterns and trends to
make tactical and strategic recommendations. Influence improvements to
quality processes, informed by defect patterns and trends.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. The skill's own wording is unchanged from test manager,
but as the top of the ladder with organisation-wide ownership of continuous
improvement and no more senior QAT role to escalate to, judgement on defect
trends has to be authoritative rather than advisory.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Treats defect data as someone else's job, and has no view on
  whether the organisation's defect management process is working.
- 1 — Beginner: Reviews defect trends when asked, but does not initiate
  action from them.
- 2 — Intermediate: Uses defect trends to improve their own area's
  practice, and occasionally flags a trend to a colleague elsewhere.
- 3 — Advanced: Proactively shares defect trend analysis with other teams
  and changes their own area's process as a result, without waiting to be
  asked.
- 4 — Expert: Owns the organisation's approach to using defect data to
  drive quality improvement, arbitrates the most contested defect
  assessments when no one else can, and has changed an organisational
  process on the strength of a defect trend they identified.

**Evidence to gather**

- Work sample: an organisational-level defect or quality trend analysis,
  and the process change it produced.
- Structured interview: ask about the most contested defect assessment
  they have been asked to arbitrate, and how they resolved it.
- Referee question: ask whether the candidate has changed an
  organisation-wide process based on defect evidence.

### Skill: Test analysis

**What the framework expects at this level**

Lead and guide multiple teams in test analysis, making sure it happens early
in the life cycle. Advocate for risk-based analysis to drive improvements
across many teams. Set standards and principles for test analysis across
the organisation.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. The framework's own wording explicitly assigns
organisation-wide standard-setting for this skill at this level.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has no view on whether test analysis is happening early enough
  across the organisation's delivery teams.
- 1 — Beginner: Analyses risk within one team when asked, and needs
  guidance to judge whether the approach is right for a wider audience.
- 2 — Intermediate: Leads test analysis in their own area independently,
  and can explain why earlier analysis reduces risk.
- 3 — Advanced: Guides more than one team in test analysis, ensures it
  happens early in more than one team's life cycle, and has improved
  another team's approach through advocacy.
- 4 — Expert: Sets the organisation's standards and principles for test
  analysis, drives improvements across many teams through advocacy for
  risk-based approaches, and is the person other teams cite when justifying
  why they analyse risk the way they do.

**Evidence to gather**

- Work sample: an organisational standard or principle for test analysis
  they authored.
- Structured interview: ask about a time they drove a change in test
  analysis practice across more than two teams.
- Referee question: ask a peer whether the organisation's approach to
  risk-based test analysis reflects the candidate's influence.
- Document review: review evidence of advocacy — a paper, a business case,
  or a delivery process change attributed to the candidate.

### Skill: Test and quality planning

**What the framework expects at this level**

Create and manage multiple quality testing plans, approaches and
strategies. Lead and guide multiple teams in adopting quality testing
strategy. Advocate for early quality testing involvement in organisational
delivery processes. Guide teams across an organisation in optimising
quality testing approaches, plans and strategies, using appropriate data.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. The skill's own wording is unchanged from test manager,
but head of test owns testing policy and strategy for the whole organisation
with no QAT role above to defer to, so the same activity has to be exercised
at full organisational authority, not under any oversight.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has not created or managed a quality testing plan spanning more
  than one team.
- 1 — Beginner: Contributes to a quality testing plan for their own area
  when asked.
- 2 — Intermediate: Creates and manages a quality testing plan for their
  own area independently, and updates it as circumstances change.
- 3 — Advanced: Creates and manages multiple quality testing plans across
  teams, and successfully advocates for quality testing to be involved
  earlier in at least one organisational delivery process.
- 4 — Expert: Sets the organisation's testing policy and strategy, uses
  data to guide teams across the whole organisation in optimising their
  approach, and is accountable for whether quality testing is embedded
  early in organisational delivery processes generally, not just in one
  instance.

**Evidence to gather**

- Work sample: an organisational test policy or strategy document they own.
- Structured interview: ask how they use data to decide where to focus
  quality testing effort across the organisation.
- Referee question: ask a senior stakeholder whether organisational
  delivery processes changed because of the candidate's advocacy.

### Skill: Test engineering

**What the framework expects at this level**

Explain why testing processes, environments and tools are important. Follow
test engineering practices and standards, with support. Support the
maintenance of automated tests and tools required for testing.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. The framework keeps the same modest, supported
wording for this skill even at the top of the ladder, so it is deliberately
not a hands-on specialism for this management track — the baseline sits one
point above test manager's, for independent judgement about when engineering
support is needed, but a point below this level's other skills.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot explain why the organisation's test engineering
  processes, environments or tools matter, and has no view on whether they
  need to change.
- 1 — Beginner: Explains why test engineering processes and tools matter
  when asked, but needs support to judge whether the organisation's current
  approach is adequate.
- 2 — Intermediate: Follows and supports test engineering practices and
  standards with some support, and can explain them to a non-technical
  audience.
- 3 — Advanced: Judges independently when the organisation's test
  engineering practices, environments or tools need investment or change,
  and supports the maintenance of automated tests and tooling without being
  asked.
- 4 — Expert: Sets the direction for test engineering investment across the
  organisation, and is the final point of escalation for engineering
  trade-off decisions.

**Evidence to gather**

- Structured interview: ask about a decision to invest in, or retire, a
  test engineering tool or environment across the organisation.
- Work sample: evidence of supporting or maintaining automated tests or
  tooling at an organisational level.
- Referee question: ask whether the candidate has judged correctly when
  test engineering practices needed to change.
