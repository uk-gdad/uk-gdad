# DevOps Engineer - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This is a competency assessment for the role of Development operations
(DevOps) engineer, at the DevOps engineer level. It is written for an
assessor — a line manager, an interview panel, or a reviewer — who needs to
judge whether a candidate or an employee possesses the baseline abilities this
role level requires, for a hiring decision, a promotion board, onboarding, or
a capability check against the framework.

This assessment is one input among several into that judgement, never the
whole of it. Read `## Advisory` below before you use any part of this
document.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has not
been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating on this form
must never be the sole basis for a hiring, promotion, pay, performance or
disciplinary decision. Before using it, consult your organisation's human
resources, people development, occupational psychology or legal compliance
colleagues.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` before you score anything, so
that every rating is anchored to what a DevOps engineer at this level is
actually expected to do. Gather whatever evidence exists before you start: a
CV or portfolio, a structured interview, a work sample or task, direct
observation of the person's day-to-day work, or references.

**Scoring on evidence, not impression.** Every rating in the competency matrix
must trace to something the person did, said, produced, or was observed
doing. If you cannot point to evidence for a rating, record it as unknown
rather than guess.

**Consistency.** Score every candidate for this role level the same way.
Where possible, have more than one assessor score independently and compare
notes afterwards, especially for a hiring or promotion decision.

**Fairness.** This is an unvalidated, AI-drafted instrument. Used
inconsistently, it can produce unlawful adverse impact — treating one
candidate less favourably than another for reasons that have nothing to do
with the job. Consistent, evidence-based administration by more than one
assessor materially reduces that risk. Never let a rating be influenced by
anything other than demonstrated, job-related evidence.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

This is a **proficiency** scale, not a frequency scale: it rates how well the
person does the thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it.

**Worked example — User focus.** At Beginner (1), a candidate could describe
being told what a user needed by someone else, without having gathered
evidence themselves. At Intermediate (2) — this level's baseline — they can
describe identifying and engaging with users or stakeholders themselves to
collect evidence of user needs, and using quantitative or qualitative data to
turn that into an outcome in their work. At Advanced (3), above this level's
baseline, they could describe championing user research across a team and
guiding others' approach to it, which is more influence than this level
expects.

## About this role level

Development operations (DevOps) engineers — commonly called "DevOps
engineers" — support the development and operation of software through tools,
environments and practices. The role is responsible for underpinning good
development processes, including managing tools and testing environments,
central code control, maintaining development standards, and writing software
that automates systems.

This assessment covers the **DevOps engineer** level, the third step of the
role's ladder — the point at which the role becomes fully independent, before
it splits into senior technical and management tracks. The full ladder, in
order, is: Apprentice DevOps engineer, Junior DevOps engineer, DevOps engineer
(this level), Senior DevOps engineer (technical and management track), Lead
DevOps engineer (technical and management), and Principal DevOps engineer
(technical and management).

A DevOps engineer delivers automated software components that form part of a
development operations toolchain. At this level, a person is expected to:

- transform technical requirements into DevOps processes
- build complex stories without additional support
- manage live test environments
- identify and resolve issues that are preventing delivery

This level names ten skills: Availability and capacity management,
Development process optimisation, Information security, Modern development
standards, Programming and build (software engineering), Prototyping, Service
support, Systems design, Systems integration, and User focus.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Availability and capacity management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A DevOps engineer is expected to manage service components to ensure they
meet business needs and key performance indicators (KPIs).

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. This is a clear step up from explaining the
process to actually managing service components, matching this level's
"build complex stories without additional support" accountability, though the
level below sat at "explain" so a person new to the level may still need some
guidance on business-specific KPIs.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot describe what a KPI relevant to service availability or
  capacity is for.
- 1 — Beginner: can explain what business needs and KPIs a service is
  managed against, without having managed one.
- 2 — Intermediate: manages the availability or capacity of a real service
  component to meet stated KPIs, with occasional guidance on interpreting the
  numbers.
- 3 — Advanced: manages this independently across more than one service, and
  proactively adjusts before a KPI is at risk.
- 4 — Expert: sets the KPIs themselves and coaches others on managing to
  them.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a service component they have managed against
  a specific KPI, and what they did when it was at risk.
- Ask to see a dashboard or report they have used to track a service's
  availability or capacity.

### Skill: Development process optimisation

**What the framework expects at this level**

A DevOps engineer is expected to explain the importance of developing process
efficiency and the common ways in which processes are optimised, to support
specific activities that improve development processes, and to identify
obvious deficiencies.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. "Support specific activities" and "identify
obvious deficiencies" are active, guided contributions rather than pure
explanation, matching this level's independence in delivery work.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot point to a deficiency in a development process even when
  shown one.
- 1 — Beginner: can explain why process efficiency matters, in general
  terms.
- 2 — Intermediate: has supported a specific activity to improve a
  development process, and can identify an obvious deficiency when they see
  one.
- 3 — Advanced: proposes and leads a small process improvement themselves,
  rather than only supporting someone else's.
- 4 — Expert: not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a deficiency they spotted in a development
  process, and what happened next.
- Ask about a specific activity they supported to improve how their team
  develops software.

- [ ] 1 — Beginner
- [ ] 2 — Intermediate

### Skill: Information security

**What the framework expects at this level**

A DevOps engineer is expected to design solutions and services with security
controls included, specifically engineered to mitigate security threats.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. "Design... with security controls included" is a
real step up from applying existing controls, but at this level it is
expected within their own solutions rather than across a wider service
estate.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: designs a solution without considering what security threats it
  might face.
- 1 — Beginner: can name relevant security threats for a solution but needs
  significant help choosing controls.
- 2 — Intermediate: designs a solution or service with appropriate security
  controls included, engineered against the threats that solution actually
  faces.
- 3 — Advanced: does this consistently across more complex solutions, and can
  justify each control chosen.
- 4 — Expert: reviews others' designs for security gaps and leads on setting
  security expectations for the team.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to walk through a solution they designed, and the
  security controls they built into it and why.
- Give a short design scenario and ask what threats they would consider and
  what controls they would include.

### Skill: Modern development standards

**What the framework expects at this level**

A DevOps engineer is expected to explain the most important principles of
modern development standards and how they apply to their work, and to apply
those principles under guidance.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. This wording is unchanged from the junior level,
so the baseline stays the same, though evidence at this level should show
standards applied to independent, complex work rather than simple tasks.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot name a development standard their work should follow.
- 1 — Beginner: can explain a standard but rarely applies it consistently.
- 2 — Intermediate: applies modern development standards to their own
  complex work, occasionally checking with others on edge cases.
- 3 — Advanced: applies standards without needing guidance, and helps a
  junior colleague apply them correctly.
- 4 — Expert: not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- Review a complex piece of work the candidate delivered, checking for
  consistent application of a named standard.
- Ask them to explain a standard they had to apply in an unusual situation,
  and how they adapted it.

### Skill: Programming and build (software engineering)

**What the framework expects at this level**

A DevOps engineer is expected to collaborate with others when necessary to
review specifications, and to use the agreed specifications to design, code,
test and document programs or scripts of medium-to-high complexity, using the
right standards and tools.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. "Medium-to-high complexity" and independent use of
agreed specifications match a strong, independent grasp of the skill, and
this level's "build complex stories without additional support"
accountability supports a baseline above Intermediate.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot build a working program or script from a specification
  without heavy support.
- 1 — Beginner: needs step-by-step direction to build from a specification.
- 2 — Intermediate: builds simple-to-medium complexity programs from a
  specification, with some guidance.
- 3 — Advanced: designs, codes, tests, corrects and documents programs or
  scripts of medium-to-high complexity independently, using the right
  standards and tools, and collaborates on specifications when needed.
- 4 — Expert: does this reliably at the highest complexity the role sees, and
  is sought out to review others' work.

**Evidence to gather**

- Set a realistic, medium-complexity coding task and observe the candidate's
  full process: specification review, build, test, and documentation.
- Ask to see a complex program or script the candidate built recently, and
  discuss the design decisions they made.

**Rate this skill**

- [ ] 0 — None
- [ ] 1 — Beginner
- [ ] 2 — Intermediate
- [ ] 3 — Advanced
- [ ] 4 — Expert

### Skill: Prototyping

**What the framework expects at this level**

A DevOps engineer is expected to approach prototyping as a team activity,
actively soliciting prototypes and testing with others, to establish design
patterns and iterate on them, and to use a variety of prototyping methods and
choose the most appropriate.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. This is a meaningful step up from the junior
level's understanding-only wording, involving active solicitation and method
choice, but still framed as team activity rather than leading it.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has never actively solicited a prototype or tested one with
  others.
- 1 — Beginner: has taken part in a prototyping activity someone else set up.
- 2 — Intermediate: actively solicits prototypes, tests them with others, and
  chooses a reasonable method for the situation, sometimes checking their
  choice with a colleague.
- 3 — Advanced: consistently chooses the most appropriate prototyping method
  for a given problem, and establishes design patterns that get reused.
- 4 — Expert: not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a prototype they set up, why they chose that
  method, and what they learned from testing it with others.
- Ask about a design pattern that came out of a prototyping exercise they led
  or contributed to.

**Rate this skill**

- [ ] 0 — None
- [ ] 1 — Beginner
- [ ] 2 — Intermediate

### Skill: Service support

**What the framework expects at this level**

A DevOps engineer is expected to identify, locate and fix service faults.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. This drops the junior level's "following agreed
procedures" language, implying independent diagnosis, but stops short of the
senior level's "complex" faults and advising others, so Intermediate is the
better fit than Advanced.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot locate the source of a straightforward service fault.
- 1 — Beginner: locates and fixes a straightforward fault with guidance.
- 2 — Intermediate: identifies, locates and fixes a service fault
  independently, for faults of typical difficulty.
- 3 — Advanced: does this for complex, less obvious faults and can advise a
  colleague on the approach.
- 4 — Expert: not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to walk through a service fault they diagnosed and fixed
  independently, from first symptom to resolution.
- Present a realistic fault scenario and ask how they would identify and
  locate the cause.

### Skill: Systems design

**What the framework expects at this level**

A DevOps engineer is expected to translate logical designs into physical
designs, produce detailed designs, effectively document all work using
required standards, methods and tools, including prototyping tools where
appropriate, design systems characterised by managed levels of risk,
manageable business and technical complexity, and meaningful impact, and to
work with well understood technology, identifying appropriate patterns.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. This is a substantial, independent design
responsibility — translating, producing, and documenting detailed designs —
consistent with a strong, independent grasp of the skill.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot translate a logical design into a workable physical
  design.
- 1 — Beginner: attempts this with significant support and correction
  needed.
- 2 — Intermediate: produces a physical design from a logical one for simple
  cases, with guidance.
- 3 — Advanced: independently translates logical designs into physical ones,
  produces and documents detailed designs to standard, and works confidently
  with well understood technology, choosing appropriate patterns.
- 4 — Expert: designs systems of higher risk or complexity than this level
  typically sees, and reviews others' design work.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to walk through a design they produced, from logical
  design through to documented physical design.
- Review a design document the candidate authored for completeness against
  the required standards, methods and tools.

### Skill: Systems integration

**What the framework expects at this level**

A DevOps engineer is expected to build and test simple interfaces between
systems, and to work on more complex integration as part of a wider team.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. Simple interfaces are built and tested
independently, while complex integration is done as part of a wider team, so
Intermediate — solid basics with some collaborative support on the harder
cases — fits better than Advanced.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot build or test a simple interface between two systems.
- 1 — Beginner: builds a simple interface with significant support.
- 2 — Intermediate: builds and tests simple interfaces independently, and
  contributes to more complex integration work as part of a wider team.
- 3 — Advanced: leads a complex integration effort within a wider team,
  rather than just contributing to it.
- 4 — Expert: not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a simple interface they built and tested
  between two systems.
- Ask about their role in a more complex integration effort involving a
  wider team.

### Skill: User focus

**What the framework expects at this level**

A DevOps engineer is expected to identify and engage with users or
stakeholders to collate user needs evidence, to understand and define
research that fits user needs, and to use quantitative and qualitative data
about users to turn user focus into outcomes.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. This is a real step up from the junior level's
awareness-only wording, involving independent engagement and use of data,
matching "knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance."

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: does not engage with users or stakeholders to inform their work.
- 1 — Beginner: engages with users when directed to, but does not yet define
  research themselves.
- 2 — Intermediate: identifies and engages with users or stakeholders
  themselves, collects evidence of user needs, and uses that data to shape an
  outcome, with occasional guidance.
- 3 — Advanced: does this independently and consistently, and can explain
  trade-offs between what different users want.
- 4 — Expert: not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a piece of work they shaped using evidence of
  user needs they gathered themselves.
- Ask how they turned quantitative or qualitative user data into a decision
  about what to build.
