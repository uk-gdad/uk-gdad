# Head of Digital Evaluation - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This assessment is for the person judging whether a candidate or an employee meets the
baseline a UK Government Digital and Data (GDAD) head of digital evaluation needs. It
is written for an assessor — a line manager, a panel member, or a reviewer — not for the
person being assessed.

A head of digital evaluation leads the digital evaluation community, acting as the
point of contact between the community and the rest of the organisation. This
assessment measures demonstrated capability against the framework's own description of
that level, across the seven skills the framework names for it. It is one input among
several into a hiring, promotion or development decision, never the whole of one — see
`## Advisory` for why.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for educational
purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has not been piloted, normed
or checked for adverse impact — and a rating on this form must never be the sole basis
for a hiring, promotion, pay, performance or disciplinary decision. Before using it,
consult your organisation's human resources, people development, occupational
psychology or legal compliance colleagues.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` before you score anything. Gather
whatever evidence exists: a CV or portfolio, a structured interview, a work sample or
task, direct observation, or references. This is the most senior level of the role —
expect evidence about setting strategy, shaping capability across the organisation, and
representing the community externally, not about running an individual evaluation.

**Scoring on evidence, not impression.** Every rating must trace to something the
person did, said, produced, or was observed doing. If you cannot point to evidence for a
rating, record it as unknown rather than guess.

**Consistency.** Score every candidate for this role level the same way, using the same
kinds of evidence where possible. For a hiring or promotion decision, have more than one
assessor score independently before comparing notes.

**Fairness.** Never let a rating be influenced by anything other than demonstrated,
job-related evidence. See [Fair and lawful use](../../../../uk-gdad-pcf-competency-assessments/spec/index.md#fair-and-lawful-use)
in the project specification for what this rules out.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point proficiency scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

This rates how well the person does the thing, on the evidence available — not how often
they do it. A worked example, using this level's skill of Evaluation planning and
strategy:

- **3 — Advanced**: they set the evaluation strategy for a single, complex product
  group unsupported.
- **4 — Expert**: they influenced the organisation's most senior stakeholders to secure
  resources for evaluation across several products at once, coached lead and senior
  digital evaluators on how to build their own evaluation strategies, and are the person
  the organisation turns to when evaluation plans need to meet quality standards under
  scrutiny.

A head of digital evaluation's baseline for this skill, below, is 4.

## About this role level

**The role: digital evaluator.** A digital evaluator assesses the design, implementation
and outcomes of a digital product or service, and whether it delivers value for the
public and society. Some are also members of a government analytical profession where
evaluation is a core skill. In this role, you work closely with delivery and policy
teams; develop theories of change for products and services that explain the links
between activities, outputs, intended outcomes and unintended effects; follow
established monitoring and evaluation methods and government guidance; produce
evaluation plans; design quantitative and qualitative research; collect and analyse
data; and communicate findings, including by publishing reports.

**The level: head of digital evaluation.** The framework describes this as the most
senior level of the role — leading the digital evaluation community and acting as the
point of contact between the community and the rest of the organisation. It is the
fifth of five levels in this role: junior digital evaluator, digital evaluator, senior
digital evaluator, lead digital evaluator, and head of digital evaluation. At this
level, you remove blockers and act as an escalation point for important issues; set the
digital evaluation strategy with input from lead and senior digital evaluators; define a
framework for quality assurance within evaluation; advocate for digital evaluation,
working with colleagues across government, other Government Digital and Data
communities and analytical professions; and manage the organisation's digital
evaluation capability through business planning, development and recruitment.

**The skills this level names**, in the summary's order: Communicating analysis and
insight; Data ethics and privacy; Evaluation delivery; Evaluation planning and strategy;
Monitoring and evaluation across the product life cycle; Product and service monitoring;
and Quality assurance of data and analysis. Four of these — Communicating analysis and
insight, Data ethics and privacy, Product and service monitoring, and Quality assurance
of data and analysis — carry text identical to the lead digital evaluator level below;
what genuinely distinguishes this level is Evaluation delivery, Evaluation planning and
strategy, and Monitoring and evaluation across the product life cycle, plus the
organisation-wide scope set out in this level's own accountability bullets above.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Communicating analysis and insight

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a head of digital evaluation to communicate relevant, compelling
stories using the most appropriate medium, and to make complex recommendations at both
a tactical and a strategic level. This wording is identical to the lead digital
evaluator level.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Choosing the medium and framing recommendations at the highest
altitude, for the most senior audiences the organisation has, is the essence of Expert.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not made a strategic recommendation from evaluation evidence.
- 1 — Beginner: has drafted part of a strategic communication with close direction.
- 2 — Intermediate: communicates tactical recommendations independently but needs
  support framing a strategic one.
- 3 — Advanced: independently communicates compelling stories and gives both tactical
  and strategic recommendations for a single area.
- 4 — Expert: is trusted to represent the whole evaluation community's findings and
  recommendations to the organisation's most senior stakeholders, and coaches lead and
  senior digital evaluators to do the same.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of representing evaluation findings to the organisation's most
  senior leadership, and what recommendation resulted.
- A structured interview question: "Tell me about a time you had to advocate for
  digital evaluation itself, not just present one evaluation's findings."
- Ask a referee whether the candidate has coached others across the community on
  communicating strategically.

### Skill: Data ethics and privacy

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a head of digital evaluation to oversee compliance with data ethics
standards and legislation across the organisation, develop, manage and continuously
improve an ethical framework within their area, develop a data ethics culture and
influence others to act on it, and ensure data ethics is applied appropriately when
formulating, implementing and evaluating organisational policies and programmes. This
wording is identical to the lead digital evaluator level.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Owning an organisation-wide ethical framework and shaping culture
through influence is expert, teach-and-guide-others work.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not owned a data ethics framework or compliance area.
- 1 — Beginner: has followed an ethical framework someone else owns.
- 2 — Intermediate: has contributed changes to an ethical framework with guidance.
- 3 — Advanced: independently oversees compliance with data ethics standards and
  legislation for a defined area.
- 4 — Expert: develops, manages and improves an organisation-wide ethical framework,
  has changed how colleagues act through influence, and ensures ethics is built into
  organisational policies and programmes from the start.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Tell me about an ethical framework you developed
  or improved at organisational level. What changed as a result?"
- Ask for an example of influencing how ethics was built into a policy or programme,
  not just checked afterwards.
- Ask a referee about the candidate's role in shaping the organisation's data ethics
  culture.

### Skill: Evaluation delivery

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a head of digital evaluation to provide guidance on evaluation
techniques for digital services to evaluators; to "assure the evaluation work of
digital evaluator teams define best practice and methodologies for evaluation and
ensure they are shared across teams" (quoted as published — the framework's own bullet
runs two expectations together with no separator: assuring the evaluation work of
digital evaluator teams, and separately, defining best practice and methodologies for
evaluation and ensuring they are shared across teams); and to coach people across the
organisation to use appropriate evaluation techniques.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Assuring other teams' work, defining best practice that is shared
across the organisation, and coaching people beyond the evaluation community itself are
all teach-and-guide-others, organisation-wide responsibilities.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not assured another team's evaluation work or coached anyone on
  technique.
- 1 — Beginner: has followed evaluation guidance someone else wrote.
- 2 — Intermediate: has provided evaluation guidance to one colleague, with support.
- 3 — Advanced: independently assures another team's evaluation work and provides
  guidance on technique.
- 4 — Expert: assures the evaluation work of digital evaluator teams across the
  organisation, defines best practice and methodologies that are shared across teams,
  and coaches people beyond the evaluation community — for example policy or delivery
  colleagues — to use appropriate evaluation techniques.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Tell me about a best practice or methodology
  document you defined that other teams then used."
- Ask for an example of assuring another evaluator team's work and what changed as a
  result.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate has coached someone outside the evaluation
  community on evaluation technique.

### Skill: Evaluation planning and strategy

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a head of digital evaluation to influence the organisation's senior
stakeholders to secure resources for evaluation projects, provide guidance on the
evaluation strategy for a group of digital products and services, ensure evaluation
plans are delivered effectively and meet quality standards, and coach teams on how to
create evaluation plans and strategies.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Influencing the organisation's most senior stakeholders and
coaching teams on how to build their own strategies, rather than building one strategy
alone, is Expert-level, organisation-wide work.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not influenced senior stakeholders to secure evaluation resources.
- 1 — Beginner: has helped present a resourcing case someone else built.
- 2 — Intermediate: has secured resources for a single evaluation, with support.
- 3 — Advanced: independently secures resources and sets strategy for a group of
  products or services.
- 4 — Expert: influences the organisation's most senior stakeholders on evaluation
  resourcing, ensures evaluation plans across the organisation meet quality standards,
  and coaches teams on building their own evaluation plans and strategies.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Tell me about influencing the organisation's most
  senior stakeholders on evaluation resourcing or strategy."
- Ask for an example of coaching a team to build their own evaluation strategy rather
  than building it for them.
- Ask a referee about the candidate's role in setting quality standards for evaluation
  plans across the organisation.

### Skill: Monitoring and evaluation across the product life cycle

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a head of digital evaluation to lead and champion best practice, and
identify improvements to current approaches for monitoring and evaluation through the
product life cycle; identify and raise evaluation or product risks during any stage of
the product life cycle; and coach, guide and support teams moving through the product
life cycle phases. This is different wording from the lead digital evaluator level
below, which was framed around applying techniques and recommending stop-or-proceed
decisions for a single evaluation rather than leading practice across the organisation.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Leading and championing best practice organisation-wide, and
coaching teams through life cycle phases, is teach-and-guide-others work at the
broadest scope this skill reaches.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not identified an improvement to how the organisation approaches
  monitoring and evaluation across the life cycle.
- 1 — Beginner: has flagged a life cycle risk to someone more senior.
- 2 — Intermediate: has raised a risk and suggested an improvement for one team's
  approach, with support.
- 3 — Advanced: independently identifies and raises risks, and improves the approach
  for their own team, across life cycle phases.
- 4 — Expert: leads and champions best practice for monitoring and evaluation across
  the organisation, identifies risks at any stage of any product's life cycle, and
  coaches multiple teams through their life cycle transitions.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Tell me about a best practice change you led for
  how the organisation monitors and evaluates products through their life cycle."
- Ask for an example of raising a product risk that other teams had missed.
- Ask a referee about the candidate's role coaching teams through a life cycle
  transition.

### Skill: Product and service monitoring

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a head of digital evaluation to get support from senior stakeholders
for the need to develop theories of change, provide guidance on how to develop theories
of change, and define best practice and methodologies for measuring indicators,
including economic outcomes, and communicating limitations. This wording is identical
to the lead digital evaluator level.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Defining methodology used by others and persuading senior
stakeholders of the case for a theory of change is organisation-facing, teach-and-guide
work.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not defined a methodology for others to follow.
- 1 — Beginner: has followed a theory of change methodology someone else defined.
- 2 — Intermediate: has provided guidance on developing a theory of change to one
  colleague, with support.
- 3 — Advanced: independently provides guidance on developing theories of change and
  measurement approaches to others.
- 4 — Expert: defines the best practice and methodology the organisation uses for
  measuring indicators, including economic outcomes, and has secured senior stakeholder
  backing for the need to develop theories of change where there wasn't appetite for one
  initially.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Tell me about a methodology you defined that the
  organisation, not just one team, now uses for measuring indicators or developing
  theories of change."
- Ask for an example of securing senior stakeholder backing for a theory of change.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate's guidance is used organisation-wide.

### Skill: Quality assurance of data and analysis

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a head of digital evaluation to set up tools and systems for
effectively assuring data and analysis across multiple teams, use appropriate
approaches for verifying and validating data and analysis, influence senior
stakeholders over data and analysis approaches, and coach and mentor others in assuring
data and analysis. This wording is identical to the lead digital evaluator level.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Setting up assurance systems across multiple teams and coaching
others in the practice is squarely the "teach or guide others" definition of Expert.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not set up an assurance tool or system used by more than one team.
- 1 — Beginner: has followed an assurance system someone else set up.
- 2 — Intermediate: has contributed to an assurance system across teams with guidance.
- 3 — Advanced: independently verifies and validates data and analysis for their own
  team.
- 4 — Expert: sets up tools and systems that assure data and analysis across multiple
  teams, influences senior stakeholders on data and analysis approaches, and defines
  the quality assurance framework the wider evaluation community uses.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Tell me about the quality assurance framework you
  defined for evaluation across the organisation."
- Ask for an example of influencing a senior stakeholder on a data or analysis
  approach.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate's assurance framework is used across multiple
  teams.

The baseline is normally met when every skill is rated at or above its baseline target.
As the most senior level of the role, every skill's target sits at Expert; four of the
seven skill blocks carry text identical to the lead digital evaluator level, so the
genuine step up from lead to head shows in scope and accountability — an escalation
point for the whole organisation, ownership of digital evaluation strategy — rather than
in different wording for those particular skills.

One or two skills below target is not automatically a fail, but weigh this level's
purpose carefully: it is the organisation's single point of accountability for the
digital evaluation community, so a shortfall in a skill central to that — Evaluation
planning and strategy, or Quality assurance of data and analysis — matters more than one
in a more peripheral area. Record the reasoning behind the determination, not only the
number.
