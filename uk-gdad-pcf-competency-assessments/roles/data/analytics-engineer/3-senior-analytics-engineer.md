# Senior Analytics Engineer - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This assessment is for an assessor — a line manager, a hiring panel member, or
a reviewer — judging whether a candidate or an employee meets the baseline
abilities required for the Data role of Analytics engineer, at the Senior
analytics engineer role level. It measures demonstrated skill against the UK
GDAD Profession Capability Framework's own description of this role level, for
a hiring decision, a promotion board, onboarding, or a capability check.

A senior analytics engineer oversees the development and testing of data
models, directs the work of other analytics engineers, coaches and mentors
them, and builds relationships with stakeholders within a defined area. This
assessment looks for independent, trusted delivery and the beginnings of
leading others, not the strategic, organisation-wide remit of the levels
above.

This assessment is one input among several into a decision, never the whole of
it. Read `## Advisory` before using it.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating on this
form must never be the sole basis for a hiring, promotion, pay, performance or
disciplinary decision. Before using it, consult your organisation's human
resources, people development, occupational psychology or legal compliance
colleagues.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, then gather whatever
evidence exists: a CV or portfolio, a structured interview, a work sample or
task (for example, reviewing a data model the candidate designed and asking
them to defend the choices), direct observation, or references — especially
references that can speak to how the candidate has coached or mentored more
junior colleagues.

**Score on evidence, not impression.** Every rating must trace to something
the person did, said, produced, or was observed doing. If you cannot point to
evidence for a rating, record it as unknown rather than guess.

**Consistency.** Score every candidate for this role level the same way. For a
hiring or promotion decision, have more than one assessor score independently
before comparing notes.

**Fairness.** Every rating and every piece of evidence you gather must be
about demonstrated, job-related behaviour — see
[Fair and lawful use](#fair-and-lawful-use) in the project specification.
Never let a rating be coloured by anything other than what the person actually
did, said, or produced.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix below is rated on the same five-point
scale. This is a **proficiency** scale — it rates how well the person does the
thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it or how they feel
about it.

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

**Worked example — Problem management.** The framework expects a senior
analytics engineer to "initiate and monitor actions to investigate patterns
and trends to resolve problems" and to "determine preventative measures". A
candidate who can only carry out an investigation someone else has scoped for
them is showing **1 — Beginner** or **2 — Intermediate**, below this level's
baseline. A candidate who, unprompted, spots a recurring issue across several
data loads, initiates an investigation, consults a specialist where needed,
and puts a preventative fix in place is showing **3 — Advanced** — the
baseline this level expects. A candidate who has additionally built the
team's whole approach to spotting these patterns before they recur is showing
evidence towards **4 — Expert**.

## About this role level

The Data profession's Analytics engineer role transforms data relevant to the
organisation into structures that enable analysis and decision-making. Across
the role, an analytics engineer works with subject matter experts to
understand organisational processes and translate these into data structures
optimised for analysis; works with data users to design and build data models
they can use for effective analysis and decision-making, using modelling
techniques such as Kimball or Inmon; supports data quality improvement;
develops standards for data transformation; creates and maintains data
documentation; refines requirements in response to feedback from users and
changes in the organisation; and provides ongoing support to users.

The role has five levels, in order: Trainee analytics engineer, Analytics
engineer, Senior analytics engineer, Lead analytics engineer, and Head of
analytics engineering. This assessment is for the third of them.

A **senior analytics engineer** oversees the development and testing of data
models, including directing the work of other analytics engineers. At this
role level, they will oversee tasks and support less senior analytics
engineers, build relationships with stakeholders within a defined area, coach
and mentor less senior analytics engineers, ensure documentation of data meets
standards, ensure issues and risks are resolved, oversee training and support
for users of data sets, and explore and develop new ways of working with data.

The framework names eight skills for this role level: Communicating between
the technical and non-technical, Data analysis and synthesis, Data
innovation, Data modelling, cleansing and enrichment, Metadata management,
Problem management, Programming and build (data and analytics engineering),
and Testing.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior analytics engineer is expected to communicate effectively with
technical and non-technical stakeholders, support and host discussions within
a multidisciplinary team with potentially difficult dynamics, be an advocate
for the team externally, and manage differing stakeholder perspectives. The
framework's own wording for this skill is unchanged from the level below.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. The skill's own bullets are the same as the level
below, but this level's accountabilities — overseeing tasks, building
stakeholder relationships within a defined area, coaching others — assume the
communicating and hosting is now done consistently and unsupervised, not just
attempted. The baseline moves up to reflect that surrounding context rather
than a change in the skill's own wording.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot adapt an explanation for a non-technical audience or
  moderate a disagreement between stakeholders.
- 1 — Beginner: Communicates with stakeholders on routine matters but needs
  support for anything contentious.
- 2 — Intermediate: Handles most stakeholder communication independently, but
  still escalates genuinely difficult discussions.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently hosts discussions with difficult dynamics,
  advocates for the team externally, and manages conflicting stakeholder
  expectations without needing to escalate.
- 4 — Expert: Is sought out across the organisation to mediate the most
  difficult stakeholder situations, and coaches others in doing the same.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a genuinely difficult stakeholder discussion
  they hosted, what made it difficult, and how they managed it.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate has represented the team's work
  externally, to people outside the immediate team.
- Present a short scenario with two stakeholders wanting incompatible things
  from the same data model, and ask how they would resolve it.

### Skill: Data analysis and synthesis

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior analytics engineer is expected to undertake data profiling and source
system analysis, and to present clear insights to colleagues to support the
end use of the data. The framework's own wording for this skill is unchanged
from the level below.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. As with communicating, the skill's own text does not
move, but this level's remit — overseeing others' work and exploring new ways
of working with data — implies the profiling and synthesis now needs to be
reliable enough for the senior to sign off on, not merely their own.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot profile a source system or present findings clearly.
- 1 — Beginner: Profiles simple sources and presents basic findings, with
  review needed before they go to stakeholders.
- 2 — Intermediate: Profiles moderately complex sources with some
  independence and presents usable findings.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently profiles complex or unfamiliar source systems,
  synthesises insight across multiple sources, and presents it clearly enough
  for stakeholders to act on without further explanation.
- 4 — Expert: Sets the team's standard for source analysis and coaches others
  to synthesise insight at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of a complex or unfamiliar source system the candidate
  has profiled, and what they found.
- Ask them to describe how they check that an insight is genuine before
  presenting it to stakeholders.
- Review a work sample, such as a profiling report or a findings summary, if
  one is available.

### Skill: Data innovation

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior analytics engineer is expected to understand the impact on the
organisation of emerging trends in data tools, analysis techniques and data
usage — a step up from simply noticing that new tools exist, into judging
their organisational impact.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. Understanding impact is more than awareness, but
the framework reserves identifying the right timing to adopt something (the
level above) and actively horizon-scanning for the organisation (two levels
above) for more senior roles, so the baseline sits at the middle of the
scale.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot say what effect a new tool or technique might have on the
  organisation, even when prompted.
- 1 — Beginner: Names emerging tools or trends but cannot judge their likely
  impact.
- 2 — Intermediate: Explains, with reasonable accuracy, what impact a named
  emerging trend would have on the team's or organisation's data work.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently assesses several emerging trends and advises on
  which are worth the organisation's attention.
- 4 — Expert: Horizon-scans proactively and introduces new ways of working
  based on that scanning.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate about an emerging data tool or technique and what impact
  it would have if the organisation adopted it.
- Ask whether they have advised a manager or team on whether to invest time in
  a new approach, and what they recommended.

**Rate this skill**

- [ ] 0 — None
- [ ] 1 — Beginner
- [ ] 2 — Intermediate

### Skill: Data modelling, cleansing and enrichment

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior analytics engineer is expected to build and review complex data
models ensuring adherence to standards, use data integration tools and
languages to integrate and store data while advising teams on best practice,
ensure data for analysis meets data quality standards and is interoperable
with other data sets, and work with other data professionals to improve
modelling and integration patterns and standards.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. "Build and review complex data models" and "advise
teams on best practice" describe someone trusted to design without
supervision and to guide others — a clear step up from the assigned, reviewed
production work of the level below, though the wider, cross-organisation
standard-setting role sits above this level.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot design a complex data model or judge whether one meets
  standards.
- 1 — Beginner: Builds simple models with support and cannot yet review
  others' work reliably.
- 2 — Intermediate: Builds moderately complex models independently but still
  needs support reviewing others' models against standards.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently builds and reviews complex data models, ensures
  they meet quality and interoperability standards, and advises other team
  members on best practice.
- 4 — Expert: Works across teams and disciplines to improve the
  organisation's modelling and integration standards themselves.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to walk through a complex data model they built or
  reviewed, and how they checked it met quality and interoperability
  standards.
- Ask about a time they advised another analytics engineer or a colleague on
  data modelling best practice.
- Review a modelling standard or a piece of documentation the candidate has
  written, if available.

### Skill: Metadata management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior analytics engineer is expected to design an appropriate metadata
repository, suggest changes to improve current metadata repositories,
understand a range of tools for storing and working with metadata, and advise
less experienced members of the team about metadata management.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Designing a repository and advising others describes
someone with strong, independent grasp of metadata practice who is starting to
shape it for others — Advanced fits, short of the organisation-wide governance
role expected at the top of the ladder.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot judge whether a metadata repository is fit for purpose.
- 1 — Beginner: Uses an existing repository correctly but could not design or
  improve one.
- 2 — Intermediate: Suggests small improvements to an existing repository
  with support.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently designs a metadata repository appropriate to a
  defined area, proposes improvements to existing repositories, and advises
  less experienced colleagues on metadata management.
- 4 — Expert: Sets metadata governance for the whole organisation.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a metadata repository they have designed or
  substantially improved, and why they made the choices they did.
- Ask about a time they advised a more junior colleague on metadata practice.

### Skill: Problem management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior analytics engineer is expected to initiate and monitor actions to
investigate patterns and trends to resolve problems, effectively consult
specialists where required, determine the appropriate resolution and assist
with its implementation, and determine preventative measures.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Initiating investigations, consulting specialists, and
determining (not just contributing to) the resolution and preventative
measures describe someone who owns problem management for their area, which is
Advanced independent practice.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Does not initiate investigation of a problem unless told to.
- 1 — Beginner: Investigates a problem when asked but needs support to decide
  the resolution.
- 2 — Intermediate: Investigates and proposes resolutions for moderately
  complex problems with some oversight.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently initiates and monitors investigations, consults
  specialists appropriately, determines the resolution, and puts preventative
  measures in place.
- 4 — Expert: Anticipates problems before they occur and builds the team's
  wider capability to do the same.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a problem they initiated the investigation
  of, without being asked, and what preventative measure they put in place
  afterwards.
- Ask about a time they consulted a specialist to resolve a problem, and how
  they judged that specialist input was needed.

### Skill: Programming and build (data and analytics engineering)

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior analytics engineer is expected to use agreed standards and tools to
design, code, test, correct and document moderate-to-complex programs and
scripts from agreed specifications and subsequent iterations, and to
collaborate with others to review specifications where appropriate.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. "Moderate-to-complex programs" built to agreed
standards, plus reviewing specifications collaboratively, describes solid
independent practice — a considerable step up from a trainee-level engineer's
simple, directed scripts, though the framework reserves setting standards and
taking overall technical responsibility for the level above this one.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot design or build a program from a specification without
  significant direction.
- 1 — Beginner: Builds simple programs from a clear specification, with
  substantial review needed.
- 2 — Intermediate: Builds moderately complex programs with some review, and
  documents them adequately.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently designs, builds, tests and documents
  moderate-to-complex programs to agreed standards, and collaborates on
  reviewing specifications before build starts.
- 4 — Expert: Sets programming standards and takes overall technical
  responsibility across a project.

**Evidence to gather**

- Review a program or script the candidate has built and ask them to explain
  the design choices and how it was tested.
- Ask about a time they reviewed a specification with colleagues before
  building against it, and what changed as a result.

### Skill: Testing

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior analytics engineer is expected to review requirements and
specifications and define test conditions, identify issues and risks
associated with the work, and analyse and report test activities and results.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Defining test conditions from requirements, and
analysing and reporting results, is independent test design and evaluation —
a clear step up from executing a script someone else wrote, which is what this
level's own predecessor was baselined against.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot define test conditions from a set of requirements.
- 1 — Beginner: Executes test scripts reliably but needs help defining test
  conditions.
- 2 — Intermediate: Defines test conditions for straightforward requirements
  with some support.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently reviews requirements and specifications, defines
  test conditions, identifies risks in the work, and reports results clearly
  enough to inform a decision.
- 4 — Expert: Sets testing standards across the team and reviews others' test
  design.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe how they would turn a set of requirements
  into test conditions for a new data model.
- Ask for an example of a risk they identified through testing that would
  otherwise have gone unnoticed.
- Review a test report or test plan the candidate has produced, if available.
