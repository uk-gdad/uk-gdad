# Chief digital and information officer - start here

Welcome. If you are working as, or working toward, a chief digital and
information officer role, this page is for you: what the role is about,
where to find everything else this repository has for it, and a pathway for
building the areas it names.

**One thing to know before you start.** The framework publishes this role and
its duties, but marks the role level as **not in use** and gives no
expectations against any of its named skills. That is the framework's own
position, not a gap in this page. So instead of a level-graded pathway, what
follows works from the role's own duties, grouped under the skill names the
framework does publish for it.

## Purpose

A chief digital and information officer shapes and enables delivery of the
organisation's strategy through digital, data and technology, building the
partnerships across the organisation that turn that strategy into outcomes
people actually feel — services that are easier to use, decisions made
faster, problems caught earlier. The role sits at the meeting point of
ambition and constraint: you are expected to be genuinely innovative while
keeping the organisation safe, secure and resilient, and to be a trusted
advisor to the board precisely because you hold both of those things at once
rather than picking one.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. There is no level above this role to
measure yourself against, so use an AI tool as a sounding board: describe a
real decision you're weighing and ask it to argue the case you haven't made
yet.

- [ ] **Shaping the direction of organisational strategy through digital, data and technology**

  Be a trusted advisor to the board on all matters of digital, data and
  technology — which only works if you're genuinely shaping strategy, not
  just reporting on delivery against a strategy others set.

  Ask AI: "What separates a technology leader who genuinely shapes
  organisational strategy from one who is only ever consulted on
  implementation after the strategic decisions are already made?"

- [ ] **Making strategic performance-led change**

  Lead innovative strategies that deliver the organisation's actual
  objectives, not just its technology roadmap — the two are not always the
  same thing, and it's your job to keep them aligned.

  Ask AI: "How would a technology leader tell whether a strategic change
  programme is actually improving organisational performance, versus just
  producing activity?"

- [ ] **Innovation in digital, data and technology**

  Identify and act on the changing landscape of digital, data and
  technology, and enable innovative approaches and emerging technologies
  while still ensuring inclusivity, security and ethical practice — none of
  which are optional extras bolted on afterwards.

  Ask AI: "How does a leader evaluate an emerging technology for genuine
  organisational value, rather than adopting it because it's attracting
  attention elsewhere?"

- [ ] **Leading digital products and services**

  Oversee the delivery, management and maintenance of digital solutions that
  are actually sustainable — meeting strategic goals and real user needs
  well after launch, not just at the point they ship.

  Ask AI: "What makes a digital service sustainable for years, rather than
  well-built at launch and quietly degrading afterwards?"

- [ ] **Optimising technology and data architecture**

  Ensure data and technology architecture decisions support scalability,
  reusability and interoperability across government, so that what one part
  of the organisation builds can genuinely be used by another.

  Ask AI: "What architectural decisions most affect whether a system can be
  reused or connected to elsewhere in government later, and how early do
  those decisions need to be made?"

- [ ] **Ensuring resilience of digital, data and technology**

  Ensure the organisation remains safe, secure and resilient to cyber
  threats and attacks — resilience meaning it keeps functioning under
  attack, not just that it was designed to prevent one.

  Ask AI: "What's the practical difference between a technology
  organisation that is 'secure' and one that is genuinely 'resilient', and
  why does the distinction matter at board level?"

- [ ] **Financial management for digital, data and technology**

  Negotiate and secure investment for digital, data and technology, and
  advise on investment priorities across competing demands so that limited
  funding produces the greatest benefit and outcome, not just the loudest
  business case.

  Ask AI: "How should a technology leader compare investment cases that
  promise very different kinds of benefit — cost saving, risk reduction, and
  user outcome — on some common basis?"

- [ ] **Capability building for digital, data and technology**

  Ensure the organisation anticipates and adapts to changes in the
  capability it needs, rather than discovering a skills gap only once a
  strategy already depends on it.

  Ask AI: "How would a technology leader spot a capability gap that will
  matter in two or three years, early enough to actually do something about
  it?"

Ask a peer in an equivalent role, in your sector or another part of
government, which of these has taken them longest to get right — that
conversation will teach you more than any general reading on technology
leadership.
