# Technical architect - start here

Welcome. If you are working toward the technical architect level, this
page is for you: what the role expects, where to find everything else this
repository has for it, and a pathway for building the skills it asks for.

## Purpose

A technical architect is responsible for the design and build of technical
architecture. You undertake structured analysis of technical issues,
translating that analysis into technical designs that describe a real
solution, get consulted about design and provide design patterns, identify
deeper issues that need fixing rather than just the obvious ones, and look
for opportunities to collaborate and reuse components, communicating with
both technical and non-technical stakeholders. Most named skills grow at
this level, because you're now trusted to own designs, not just contribute
to them.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. Each one is a requirement of this role
level. Where you want to go deeper, ask an AI tool a specific question
rather than searching for a generic guide — you'll get an answer shaped
around your own designs.

- [ ] **Make architectural decisions of managed risk, with support**

  Work with others to make architectural design decisions of managed risk
  and complexity, identify and address architectural risks relevant to
  your team or domain — business, data or security — and engage with
  architectural governance with support.

  Ask AI: "What's the difference between an architectural risk relevant to
  my own work and one that would need escalating to architectural
  governance?"

- [ ] **Support developing and implementing a strategy**

  Support developing a strategy or vision that aligns with organisational
  objectives, challenge requirements and assumptions to spot opportunities,
  support implementing it through a roadmap or plan, and use architectural
  principles and patterns when appropriate.

  Ask AI: "What does 'challenging requirements and assumptions' actually
  look like in a real strategy conversation, without it coming across as
  obstructive?"

- [ ] **Listen to stakeholders, and represent architecture clearly**

  Listen to the needs of technical and business stakeholders, create and
  use different architecture representations to reach agreement, and
  support discussions about architectural topics within a multidisciplinary
  team.

  Ask AI: "What makes an architecture representation genuinely clear to a
  non-technical stakeholder, rather than accurate but hard to follow?"

- [ ] **See beyond your own designs to the wider organisation**

  Align your work with other architects and technical professionals, track
  emerging issues, strategies, roadmaps, patterns and technologies to
  assess opportunities and risks, and identify how other teams contribute
  to delivering outcomes.

  Ask AI: "How would a technical architect notice an emerging issue that
  affects their designs, when their day-to-day focus is naturally on their
  own work?"

- [ ] **Contribute to a healthy, well-empowered team**

  Contribute to the work of others, help motivate and empower the team,
  help create the right environment for the team to work in, and recognise
  and deal with issues.

  Ask AI: "What are some early signs of an architecture team's dynamic
  going wrong, that a team member might notice before it's raised
  formally?"

- [ ] **Keep creating technical designs of managed risk**

  This skill's wording plateaus here, unchanged from associate level:
  creating technical designs of managed risk and complexity, guiding teams
  using them, and adapting designs during delivery.

  Ask AI: "Given this skill's description hasn't changed since associate
  level, what would genuinely technical-architect-level design judgement
  add beyond what's written here?"

Ask your manager or a more senior architect which of these your team relies on
most right now, and start there.
