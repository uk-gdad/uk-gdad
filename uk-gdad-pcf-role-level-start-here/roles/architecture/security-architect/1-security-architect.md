# Security architect - start here

Welcome. If you are working toward the security architect level, this page
is for you: what the role is about, where to find everything else this
repository has for it, and a pathway for building the skills it asks for.

## Purpose

A security architect designs and builds secure solutions. At this level,
you create and design security for a system or service, maintain security
documentation, and develop architecture patterns and security approaches
for new technologies. You recommend security controls that support real
business objectives, provide specialist advice across teams and
stakeholders, communicate widely, advise on important security-related
technologies and assess the risk of proposed changes, inspire and influence
others to actually apply security principles, and help review other
people's work.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. Each one is a requirement of this role
level, described plainly, with a way to go deeper by asking an AI tool a
specific question — the answers you get will be shaped around your own
organisation's systems, which a generic guide never is.

- [ ] **Design and review secure system architecture**

  Design and review system architectures through applying security
  patterns and principles, rather than bolting security on afterwards.

  Ask AI: "I'm new to security architecture. Explain, simply, what it
  means to design security in from the start, with an example of a
  pattern that does this."

- [ ] **Apply rigorous analysis to real problems**

  Apply your approach to real problems, considering all relevant
  information, with enough rigour that the full solution is designed and
  actually achieves the business outcome.

  Ask AI: "What does 'full rigour' look like in security analysis, as
  opposed to a quick check that misses relevant information?"

- [ ] **Advise on risk, and where it sits against tolerance**

  Work with risk owners to advise and give feedback, advise on risk impact
  and whether it's within tolerance, and describe different risk
  methodologies and how proportionality applies.

  Ask AI: "How would a security architect explain 'risk tolerance' to a
  risk owner who wants a simple yes-or-no answer about whether something
  is safe?"

- [ ] **Apply security understanding to real technical solutions**

  Interpret and apply an understanding of policy, process, business
  architecture, and legal and political implications to help develop
  technical solutions or controls.

  Ask AI: "How would a security architect translate a legal or policy
  requirement into a concrete technical control?"

- [ ] **Understand the effect of vulnerabilities on designs**

  Explain the effect of vulnerabilities on current and future designs, and
  share information on a range of systems, even while specialising in one.

  Ask AI: "What does it mean for a vulnerability to affect 'future
  designs', not just the system it's found in today?"

- [ ] **Advise on emerging security technology**

  Advise on developments to security properties in technology, and
  identify new technologies, designing their use in a business context.

  Ask AI: "How would a security architect judge whether an emerging
  security technology is genuinely relevant to their organisation, rather
  than interesting in the abstract?"

- [ ] **Communicate security and risk clearly, and manage expectations**

  Demonstrate a deep understanding of security concepts and apply them
  technically, translate and communicate security and risk implications
  accurately to both technical and non-technical stakeholders, respond
  successfully to challenges, and manage expectations while adapting to
  reach consensus.

  Ask AI: "How would a security architect explain a genuinely technical
  security risk to a non-technical stakeholder without losing the parts
  that actually matter?"

Ask your manager or a more senior architect which of these your team leans on
most right now, and start there.
