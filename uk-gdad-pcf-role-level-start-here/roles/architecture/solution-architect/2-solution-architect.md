# Solution architect - start here

Welcome. If you are working toward the solution architect level, this page
is for you: what the role expects, where to find everything else this
repository has for it, and a pathway for building the skills it asks for.

## Purpose

A solution architect is responsible for a single solution, usually working
independently where risk is low, while also supporting or contributing to
work led by more senior solution architects. You build relationships with
stakeholders across different business or technical areas, proactively
identify opportunities to improve the organisation, follow best practice for
solution design, use emerging technologies and approaches, and help your
team achieve its objectives. Almost every named skill grows at this level,
because owning even a low-risk solution asks more of you than supporting
someone else's did.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. Each one is a requirement of this role
level. Where you want to go deeper, ask an AI tool a specific question
rather than searching for a generic guide — you'll get an answer shaped
around your own solution.

- [ ] **Lead communication of complex architecture topics**

  Lead communicating complicated, complex or risky architecture topics
  with technical and non-technical stakeholders, communicate with senior
  stakeholders, adapt your message to your audience, advocate for your
  team, and manage expectations effectively.

  Ask AI: "How would a solution architect explain a genuinely complex
  design trade-off to a senior stakeholder without either oversimplifying
  it or losing them in detail?"

- [ ] **See beyond your own solution to the wider organisation**

  Align your work with other architects and technical professionals, track
  emerging issues, strategies, roadmaps, patterns and technologies to
  assess opportunities and risks, and identify how other teams contribute
  to delivering outcomes.

  Ask AI: "How would a solution architect notice an emerging issue that
  affects their solution, when their day-to-day focus is naturally on
  their own work?"

- [ ] **Contribute to a healthy, well-empowered team**

  Contribute to the work of others, help motivate and empower the team,
  help create the right environment for the team to work in, and recognise
  and deal with issues.

  Ask AI: "What are some early signs of an architecture team's dynamic
  going wrong, that a team member might notice before it's raised
  formally?"

- [ ] **Make architectural decisions of managed risk, with support**

  Work with others to make architectural design decisions of managed risk
  and complexity, identify and address architectural risks relevant to
  your team or domain — business, data or security — and engage with
  architectural governance with support.

  Ask AI: "What's the difference between an architectural risk relevant to
  my own solution and one that would need escalating to architectural
  governance?"

- [ ] **Frame problems of medium complexity, and give teams a shared view**

  Frame a problem of medium complexity, complication or risk so a solution
  can be created, produce architectural representations that give
  different teams a shared understanding, and describe options so the
  right delivery method can be chosen.

  Ask AI: "What makes an architectural representation genuinely useful for
  giving several teams a shared understanding, rather than just a diagram
  one person understands?"

- [ ] **Support developing and implementing a strategy**

  Support developing a strategy or vision that aligns with organisational
  objectives, challenge requirements and assumptions to spot opportunities,
  support implementing it through a roadmap or plan, and use architectural
  principles and patterns when appropriate.

  Ask AI: "What does 'challenging requirements and assumptions' actually
  look like in a real strategy conversation, without it coming across as
  obstructive?"

- [ ] **Understand the commercial context you work within**

  Understand commercial processes and the right internal contacts within a
  government department, and understand different sourcing strategies and
  when to apply them.

  Ask AI: "Explain, for someone moving into solution architecture, why
  understanding sourcing strategies matters for architectural decisions."

- [ ] **Keep creating technical designs of managed risk**

  This skill's wording plateaus here, unchanged from associate level:
  creating technical designs of managed risk and complexity, guiding teams
  using them, and adapting designs during delivery.

  Ask AI: "Given this skill's description hasn't changed since associate
  level, what would genuinely solution-architect-level technical design
  judgement add beyond what's written here?"

Ask your manager or a more senior architect which of these your team relies on
most right now, and start there.
