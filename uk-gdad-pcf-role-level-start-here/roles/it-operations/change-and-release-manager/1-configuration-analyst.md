# Configuration analyst - start here

Welcome. If you are starting out as a configuration analyst, this page is
for you: what the role is about, where to find everything else this
repository has for it, and a pathway for building the skills it asks for,
one step at a time.

## Purpose

Change and release managers lead the change advisory board, making sure
technical changes and releases are properly risk-assessed and scheduled,
and own the configuration management database along with the access,
security and configuration of change activities and release procedures. As
a configuration analyst, you provide administrative support to the change
and release manager: helping to maintain the integrity of the configuration
management database, and independently solving the smaller problems that
come up along the way.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. Each one is a requirement of this role
level, described plainly, with a way to go deeper by asking an AI tool a
specific question — the answers you get will be shaped around your own
organisation's configuration database, which a generic guide never is.

- [ ] **Keep configuration secure, and IT assets accounted for**

  Maintain secure configuration and accurate information, control IT
  assets in one or more areas, and verify the location and state of those
  assets.

  Ask AI: "I'm new to configuration management. Explain, simply, why
  keeping a configuration management database accurate matters beyond
  just record-keeping."

- [ ] **Implement changes under supervision**

  Implement change requests, and apply change control procedures under
  supervision.

  Ask AI: "Why do organisations use a formal change control procedure
  instead of just making a fix when it's needed?"

- [ ] **Identify and register incidents accurately**

  Identify and register incidents, gathering the information needed and
  allocating them to the right channel.

  Ask AI: "What information does a good incident record need to contain
  so the next person picking it up doesn't have to ask basic questions
  first?"

- [ ] **Investigate problems at the right level**

  Investigate problems in systems, processes and services with an
  understanding of what level a problem sits at — strategic, tactical or
  operational — and contribute to implementing remedies and preventative
  measures.

  Ask AI: "What's the difference between a strategic, a tactical and an
  operational problem, with an example of each?"

- [ ] **Understand problem resolution, and pass on what you can't solve**

  Show an awareness of problem resolution processes, and pass problems on
  to your team when you can't resolve them yourself.

  Ask AI: "How would a configuration analyst decide when to keep trying
  to fix something themselves versus passing it on to someone more
  experienced?"

- [ ] **Understand your team's dynamics and the value of feedback**

  Contribute to the work of others, help motivate and empower the team,
  help create the right environment for the team to work in, and
  recognise and deal with issues.

  Ask AI: "What does good team collaboration actually look like day to day
  on a small operations team, beyond just being friendly to colleagues?"

- [ ] **Understand the basics of IT continuity management**

  Explain the basic principles of IT standard continuity management
  processes and procedures.

  Ask AI: "Explain, for a beginner, what 'IT continuity management' means,
  and why it's treated separately from incident management."

- [ ] **Understand availability and capacity management**

  Explain availability and capacity management processes — why a service
  needs to stay up, and why "up" isn't enough if it can't handle the load
  it's given.

  Ask AI: "Explain, simply, what 'availability' and 'capacity' mean for an
  IT service, and why they're treated as separate concerns."

- [ ] **Build awareness of different products and services**

  Show an awareness of the different products and services your
  organisation runs, even before you're expected to manage change across
  all of them.

  Ask AI: "How would a configuration analyst build a working map of the
  products and services their organisation runs?"

- [ ] **Build service management framework knowledge**

  Work towards a Level 3 service management framework qualification, so
  your day-to-day practice is grounded in a recognised standard.

  Ask AI: "Explain, for a beginner, what a service management framework
  qualification actually covers, and why organisations value it."

- [ ] **Build a basic sense of who you're supporting**

  Show an awareness or understanding of user experience analysis and its
  principles, and explain the purpose of user stories and their focus on
  user needs.

  Ask AI: "Why does understanding a user story help a configuration
  analyst prioritise which change to schedule first?"

You do not need to master all of this before you start. Ask your manager or
a more senior colleague which of these your team leans on most, and start
there.
