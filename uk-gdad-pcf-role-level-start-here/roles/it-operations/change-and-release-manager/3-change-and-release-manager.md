# Change and release manager - start here

Welcome. If you are working toward the change and release manager level,
this page is for you: what the role expects, where to find everything else
this repository has for it, and a pathway for building the areas it names.

## Purpose

A change and release manager makes sure technical changes to IT services
are adequately assessed for impact, prioritised, scheduled, authorised and
implemented — in line with processes, using the right tools, chairing the
Change Advisory Board — the top of this profession's line. You provide an
escalation point, make decisions for all technical changes, and co-ordinate
releases and their interdependencies. Nearly every named skill grows at
this level, because owning every technical change across the organisation
asks for far more than analysing the impact of individual ones.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. There is no level above this one to
measure yourself against, so use an AI tool as a sounding board: describe a
real escalation or scheduling decision you're weighing and ask it to argue
the case you haven't made yet.

- [ ] **Own configuration management for the whole organisation**

  Manage configuration management for the organisation, ensuring value for
  the business and adherence to company policies, make sure changes to
  assets are recorded and controlled appropriately, and advocate for
  organisational commitment to asset control.

  Ask AI: "How would a change and release manager make the case for
  organisational commitment to asset control, to leaders who see it as
  pure overhead?"

- [ ] **Own change management processes, and keep optimising them**

  Implement new and current change management processes, and support
  change management by actively improving and optimising it.

  Ask AI: "How would a change and release manager tell that an existing
  change management process has stopped working well and needs
  optimising, rather than just feeling slow?"

- [ ] **Manage service components against business needs**

  Manage service components to make sure they meet business needs and key
  performance indicators.

  Ask AI: "What key performance indicators would a change and release
  manager typically be accountable for?"

- [ ] **Diagnose incidents, and find their resolution**

  Diagnose and prioritise incidents, investigate their causes, and find
  resolutions rather than just recording and routing them.

  Ask AI: "What's a structured way to diagnose the root cause of an
  incident during a release window, rather than fixing the first
  plausible symptom?"

- [ ] **Investigate patterns behind problems, and consult specialists**

  Initiate and monitor actions to investigate patterns and trends behind
  problems, consult specialists where needed, determine the right
  resolution and help implement it, and identify preventative measures.

  Ask AI: "What's the difference between fixing a problem that keeps
  recurring across releases and preventing the pattern behind it?"

- [ ] **Own an issue until it's genuinely resolved**

  Own an issue until a new owner has been found, or the problem has been
  mitigated or resolved — rather than passing it on the moment it gets
  difficult.

  Ask AI: "What does 'owning an issue' mean in practice for a change and
  release manager chairing the Change Advisory Board?"

- [ ] **Build genuine collaboration across your team**

  Work collaboratively, actively networking with others, adapt feedback so
  it's effective and lasting, use team health checks to spot dynamic
  issues, and help stimulate the right response to them.

  Ask AI: "What makes feedback 'effective and lasting' rather than
  something a colleague hears once and forgets?"

- [ ] **Build coherent frameworks from scattered inputs**

  Take inputs and establish coherent frameworks that work, rather than
  leaving information scattered across different sources.

  Ask AI: "What does 'establishing a coherent framework' mean in practice
  for someone coordinating releases across multiple services?"

- [ ] **Turn user evidence into decisions**

  Identify and engage with users or stakeholders to collate evidence of
  their needs, understand and define research that actually fits those
  needs, and use both quantitative and qualitative data to turn user
  focus into real outcomes.

  Ask AI: "How would a change and release manager use user evidence to
  decide when a release should be scheduled, rather than relying purely on
  technical readiness?"

- [ ] **Keep understanding the basics of IT continuity management**

  This skill's wording plateaus here, unchanged from the levels below:
  explaining the basic principles of IT continuity management processes.

  Ask AI: "Given this skill's description hasn't changed since earlier
  levels, what would genuinely top-level continuity management judgement
  add beyond what's written here?"

- [ ] **Keep building service management framework knowledge**

  This skill's wording also plateaus: holding a Level 3 service
  management framework qualification.

  Ask AI: "What would applying service management framework knowledge at
  organisational scale add, beyond holding the qualification?"

There is no resource list at this level that will tell you what to do next —
the accountability is yours. Ask peers in equivalent roles at other
organisations how they made their hardest recent call, and use it to
stress-test your own thinking rather than to copy their answer.
