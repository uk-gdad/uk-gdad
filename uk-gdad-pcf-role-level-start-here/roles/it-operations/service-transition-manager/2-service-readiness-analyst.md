# Service readiness analyst - start here

Welcome. If you are working toward the service readiness analyst level,
this page is for you: what the role expects, where to find everything
else this repository has for it, and a pathway for building the skills it
asks for.

## Purpose

IT service transition managers control the product life cycle of all
changes so beneficial changes can be made with minimum disruption to IT
services. As a service readiness analyst, you test the products and are
accountable for application acceptance and checking whether security
criteria have been met, flagging potential risks and escalating them to
the service transition manager. Availability and capacity management,
change management, community collaboration and ownership and initiative
all grow at this level, because being accountable for acceptance asks for
more than co-ordinating acceptance criteria under someone else's
direction.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. Each one is a requirement of this role
level, described plainly, with a way to go deeper by asking an AI tool a
specific question rather than searching for a generic guide.

- [ ] **Manage service components against business needs**

  Manage service components to make sure they meet business needs and key
  performance indicators.

  Ask AI: "What key performance indicators would a service readiness
  analyst typically be checking a product against before accepting it?"

- [ ] **Analyse the impact of change before it happens**

  Analyse and assess the impact of change, document change requests, and
  action changes from approved requests.

  Ask AI: "What should a service readiness analyst look for when
  assessing the potential impact of a change, beyond whether it
  technically works?"

- [ ] **Contribute to a well-functioning team**

  Contribute to the work of others, help motivate and empower the team,
  help create the right environment for the team to work in, and
  recognise and deal with issues.

  Ask AI: "What does good team collaboration actually look like day to
  day, beyond just being friendly to colleagues?"

- [ ] **Own an issue until it's genuinely resolved**

  Own an issue until a new owner has been found, or the problem has been
  mitigated or resolved — rather than passing it on the moment it gets
  difficult.

  Ask AI: "What does 'owning an issue' mean in practice for a service
  readiness analyst, when the actual fix might belong to someone else's
  team?"

- [ ] **Keep tracking and protecting assets and components**

  This skill's wording plateaus here, unchanged from service acceptance
  analyst level: tracking, logging and correcting information to protect
  assets and components.

  Ask AI: "Given this skill's description hasn't changed since the level
  below, what would genuinely service-readiness-level asset management
  judgement add beyond what's written here?"

- [ ] **Keep understanding the basics of IT continuity management**

  This skill also plateaus: explaining the basic principles of IT
  standard continuity management processes and procedures.

  Ask AI: "What would applying continuity management principles in a real
  test scenario look like, beyond explaining them?"

- [ ] **Keep identifying and registering incidents**

  This skill's wording plateaus too: identifying and registering
  incidents, gathering the required information, and allocating it to
  the appropriate channel.

  Ask AI: "Given this skill's description hasn't changed since the level
  below, what would genuinely service-readiness-level incident judgement
  add beyond what's written here?"

- [ ] **Keep investigating a problem, and knowing its level**

  This skill also plateaus: investigating problems in systems, processes
  and services, understanding their level, and contributing to remedies
  and preventative measures.

  Ask AI: "What would leading problem investigation across a testing cycle
  add, beyond investigating problems well yourself?"

- [ ] **Keep building coherent frameworks from scattered inputs**

  This skill's wording plateaus too: taking inputs and establishing
  coherent frameworks that work.

  Ask AI: "What does 'establishing a coherent framework' mean in practice
  for someone testing products across several releases?"

- [ ] **Keep building service management framework knowledge**

  This skill also plateaus: holding a Level 3 service management
  framework qualification.

  Ask AI: "What would deepening service management framework knowledge,
  beyond the base qualification, look like for a service readiness
  analyst?"

- [ ] **Keep turning user evidence into decisions**

  This skill's wording plateaus too: collating user needs evidence,
  defining fitting research, and using quantitative and qualitative data
  to turn user focus into outcomes.

  Ask AI: "How would a service readiness analyst use user evidence to
  decide whether a product is genuinely ready to accept?"

Ask a service transition manager which of these your team relies on most
right now, and start there.
