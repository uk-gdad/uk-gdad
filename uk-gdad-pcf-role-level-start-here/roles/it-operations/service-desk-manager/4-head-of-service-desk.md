# Head of service desk - start here

Welcome. If you are working toward the head of service desk level, this
page is for you: what the role expects, where to find everything else
this repository has for it, and a pathway for building the areas it names.

## Purpose

A head of service desk is responsible for managing a team that provides
first- and second-line technical support — the top of this role's line.
You ensure support for existing and emerging IT services, provide
technical advice to project teams, make sure the support team develops so
it can advise on future projects and current issues, and manage and
monitor service desk service level agreements and performance. IT service
reporting, problem management and service focus all grow at this level,
because managing a whole support team's agreements asks for more than
identifying emerging issues within it.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. There is no level above this one to
measure yourself against, so use an AI tool as a sounding board: describe a
real strategic decision you're weighing and ask it to argue the case you
haven't made yet.

- [ ] **Use prediction to see trends across processes**

  Use prediction methodologies to produce and maintain a holistic view of
  important trends across multiple processes, rather than reporting on
  business performance after the fact.

  Ask AI: "What's the difference between reporting on service desk trends
  after they've happened and using prediction methodologies to see them
  coming?"

- [ ] **Coordinate the team's response to problems, not just solve them**

  Ensure that the right actions are taken to investigate, resolve and
  anticipate problems, and co-ordinate the team to investigate problems,
  implement solutions and take preventive measures.

  Ask AI: "What does coordinating a team's response to a recurring service
  desk problem involve, beyond diagnosing and fixing it yourself?"

- [ ] **See the bigger picture across groups of services**

  See the bigger picture by taking groups of services and investigating
  how to get the best out of the underlying services that support them,
  rather than optimising one service at a time.

  Ask AI: "What would a head of service desk look for across a group of
  related services that wouldn't be visible looking at any one service in
  isolation?"

- [ ] **Give strategic direction on meeting user needs**

  Give direction on which tools or methods to use, demonstrate experience
  in meeting user needs across a variety of channels, bring insight into
  how those needs have changed over time, and apply strategic thinking to
  provide the best service for the end user.

  Ask AI: "How would a head of service desk decide that user needs have
  genuinely shifted, rather than one team simply having a noisy quarter?"

- [ ] **Keep tracking and protecting assets and components**

  This skill's wording plateaus here, unchanged from service desk manager
  level: tracking, logging and correcting information to protect assets
  and components.

  Ask AI: "Given this skill's description hasn't changed since the level
  below, what would genuinely head-of-level asset management judgement add
  beyond what's written here?"

- [ ] **Keep turning lessons learnt into continual service improvement**

  This skill also plateaus: translating lessons learnt into continual
  service improvement, overseeing the full range of customer service
  functions, and investigating automation.

  Ask AI: "What would sponsoring continual service improvement across the
  whole organisation add, beyond driving it well within one service
  desk?"

- [ ] **Keep taking accountability, and searching proactively for problems**

  This skill's wording plateaus too: taking accountability for issues,
  searching proactively for problems, and achieving excellent user
  outcomes.

  Ask AI: "What would organisation-wide accountability for user outcomes
  add, beyond taking accountability well for your own team's incidents?"

- [ ] **Keep building ITIL knowledge alongside your qualification**

  This skill also plateaus: holding a Level 3 service management
  framework qualification and demonstrating knowledge of ITIL's life
  cycle or capability elements.

  Ask AI: "What would deepening ITIL knowledge, beyond the base
  qualification, look like for a head of service desk?"

Ask a peer head of service desk, in your organisation or another part of
government, which of these has taken them longest to get right — that
conversation will teach you more than any general reading on technical
leadership.
