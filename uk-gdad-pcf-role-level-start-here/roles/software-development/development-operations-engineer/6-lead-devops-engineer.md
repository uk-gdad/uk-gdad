# Lead DevOps engineer - start here

Welcome. If you are working toward the lead DevOps engineer level, this page
is for you: what the role expects, where to find everything else this
repository has for it, and a pathway for building the skills it asks for.

There are two different roles at this level in the framework: a technical
one, covered here, and a management one. Both carry the same title and the
same named skills; if the day-to-day work you're aiming at is people
management rather than deep technical leadership, the
[lead DevOps engineer - management](https://uk-gdad.github.io/roles/software-development/development-operations-engineer/7-lead-devops-engineer-management/)
page describes the difference in practice.

## Purpose

A lead DevOps engineer leads one or a small number of related project teams
and contributes to the strategic direction of the wider effort. You act as a
technical product owner, developing medium to long term strategies for
product lines rather than delivering one piece of work at a time, provide
technical leadership through coaching and mentoring, and lead the sharing of
knowledge and good practice across teams. The shift from senior is scope:
your decisions and your standards now shape more than your own work or your
own team.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. Each one is a requirement of this role
level. At this level, an AI tool is most useful as a sounding board for a
strategic call — describe the trade-off you're weighing across teams and ask
what you might be missing.

- [ ] **Set development standards for complex, multi-team work**

  Identify and apply modern development standards to support complex
  projects and programmes, and lead others in applying them — the standard
  you set here is one other engineers are expected to follow.

  Ask AI: "How does a lead engineer introduce a development standard across
  several teams in a way that actually sticks, rather than being quietly
  ignored once attention moves elsewhere?"

- [ ] **Establish integration standards across a whole product life cycle**

  Establish standards and procedures across a service's product life cycle,
  including its development life cycle, and manage the resources that keep
  the systems integration function working effectively.

  Ask AI: "What does 'managing resources for systems integration' involve
  at a lead level, beyond the hands-on integration work itself?"

- [ ] **Lead on user focus across more than your own team**

  Collaborate with user researchers and represent users internally, explain
  the difference between user needs and user desires, champion user
  research so it covers all users, and guide others in understanding the
  user story.

  Ask AI: "How would a lead engineer champion user research consistently
  across several teams with different habits and priorities?"

- [ ] **Take on the harder faults, and advise others on how to approach them**

  Identify, locate and fix complex service faults, and advise others on
  different methodologies and types of service support.

  Ask AI: "What's a good way for a lead engineer to build a shared approach
  to service faults across teams, rather than each team handling them
  differently?"

- [ ] **Design systems of real complexity, and review other people's designs too**

  Design systems characterised by medium levels of risk, impact and
  business or technical complexity, select and apply the right design
  standards, and review the systems designs of others to check the
  technology choice and integration are sound.

  Ask AI: "What should a lead engineer look for when reviewing a design
  from a team they don't work in day to day?"

- [ ] **Keep contributing to process optimisation under guidance**

  This skill's wording here matches the earlier technical track rather than
  the more leadership-oriented version used one level below in the
  management line — work under guidance to identify process optimisation
  opportunities and contribute to implementing proposed solutions. That's
  the framework's own choice, not a gap in your practice; the leadership
  expected of you at this level shows up more clearly in the skills above.

  Ask AI: "Given that this skill is written at a more junior level than
  some of my other responsibilities, what would genuinely leading process
  optimisation look like if I chose to push beyond what's written here?"

- [ ] **Keep managing service components against business needs and KPIs**

  This skill's wording plateaus here too, matching the earlier technical
  track: manage service components to ensure they meet business needs and
  key performance indicators.

  Ask AI: "What would taking ownership of availability and capacity
  management, beyond just meeting the KPIs, look like for someone leading
  multiple teams?"

- [ ] **Keep building your programming and build practice**

  This skill's expectation is unchanged from the level below: collaborating
  on specifications, then designing, coding, testing and documenting
  programs of medium-to-high complexity to the right standards. Staying
  hands-on here is what keeps your technical leadership credible.

  Ask AI: "How does a lead engineer stay genuinely hands-on with code
  without it crowding out the leadership work only they can do?"

- [ ] **Keep treating prototyping as a team activity**

  Approach prototyping as something the team does together, establishing
  and iterating design patterns, and choosing the right method from several
  available.

  Ask AI: "How might a lead engineer encourage prototyping across several
  teams with different habits, without dictating one rigid method to all
  of them?"

- [ ] **Keep designing security in from the start**

  Design solutions and services with security controls included,
  specifically engineered to mitigate the relevant threats.

  Ask AI: "What security decisions made early tend to have the biggest
  consequences across a whole product line, rather than just one service?"

Ask a principal colleague which of these matters most in your organisation
right now, and start there.
