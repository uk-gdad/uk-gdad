# Principal DevOps engineer - management - start here

Welcome. If you are working toward the principal DevOps engineer (management)
level, this page is for you: what the role expects, where to find everything
else this repository has for it, and a pathway for building the skills it
asks for.

The framework carries two roles at this level, sharing one title and one set
of named skills: a technical one and this management one. If deep technical
practice, rather than strategic and people leadership, is where you want to
grow, the
[principal DevOps engineer (technical)](https://uk-gdad.github.io/roles/software-development/development-operations-engineer/8-principal-devops-engineer/)
page may fit better.

## Purpose

A principal DevOps engineer at this level still leads and plans development
across large or multiple teams, defining the strategic vision for delivery,
identifying and championing emerging technologies, and developing the
capability of the teams under them by recognising high performers, shaping
career paths and recruiting the right talent. Where this track's own wording
sharpens most is strategy: setting the strategy and resource allocation for
whole development programmes, and leading policy and strategy for service
and capacity management, rather than implementing within a strategy someone
else set.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. There is little level above this one, so
use an AI tool as a sounding board rather than a teacher: describe a real
strategic call you're weighing and ask it to argue the case you haven't made
yet.

- [ ] **Set strategy and resourcing for whole development programmes**

  Set the strategy and manage resource allocation for solution development
  programmes, and work with client functions to establish business
  requirements and identify, propose, initiate and lead those programmes.

  Ask AI: "How would a principal engineer in a strategic role decide which
  development programmes deserve investment when demand outstrips
  capacity?"

- [ ] **Lead policy and strategy for availability and capacity**

  Lead policy and strategy for service and capacity management, and lead
  the identification and implementation of the changes that strategy
  requires.

  Ask AI: "What makes an availability and capacity strategy something an
  organisation actually follows, rather than a document that sits unused?"

- [ ] **Quality-assure security across the organisation's solutions**

  Design, review and quality assure solutions and services to make sure
  they're specifically engineered to mitigate security threats, and lead
  others in using appropriate information security practices.

  Ask AI: "How does a strategic leader make quality-assuring security
  across many teams' work sustainable, rather than a bottleneck that slows
  everyone down?"

- [ ] **Think about prototyping and service design strategically**

  Use a variety of prototyping methods, share best practice and coach
  others in using them, and look at strategic service design end to end.

  Ask AI: "What changes about coaching teams on prototyping when you're
  responsible for strategy across many services, not delivering one?"

- [ ] **Set development standards for complex, multi-team work**

  Identify and apply modern development standards to support complex
  projects and programmes, and lead others in applying them.

  Ask AI: "How does a strategic leader get a development standard adopted
  across an organisation with genuinely different team cultures?"

- [ ] **Keep designing systems at a manageable level of complexity**

  This skill's wording plateaus here, unchanged since senior level: design
  systems characterised by medium levels of risk, impact and business or
  technical complexity, and review the designs of others.

  Ask AI: "Given this skill's description hasn't changed since senior
  level, what would genuinely strategic-level systems design judgement add
  beyond what's written here?"

- [ ] **Coordinate integration across systems**

  Define the integration build, co-ordinate build activities across
  systems, and understand how to support integration testing across the
  whole effort — the wording here is simpler than some of the
  technical-track descriptions at principal level, which is the
  framework's own choice; the strategic leadership expected of you shows up
  more clearly in the skills above.

  Ask AI: "Given this skill reads more simply than my other
  responsibilities, what would strategic-level oversight of systems
  integration add beyond what's written here?"

- [ ] **Find and fix service faults**

  Identify, locate and fix service faults — again, a simpler description
  than the equivalent technical-track skill at this level, reflecting where
  the framework expects this track's real depth to sit.

  Ask AI: "What role should a strategic leader play in service faults that
  they wouldn't be fixing personally?"

- [ ] **Keep building your programming and build practice**

  This skill's expectation is unchanged since the earlier levels:
  collaborating on specifications, then designing, coding, testing and
  documenting programs of medium-to-high complexity to the right standards.
  Staying grounded here is what keeps strategic decisions credible.

  Ask AI: "How does a strategic leader stay credible on technical decisions
  without doing hands-on development day to day?"

- [ ] **Keep leading on user focus**

  Collaborate with user researchers and represent users internally, explain
  the difference between user needs and user desires, champion user
  research so it covers all users, and guide others in understanding the
  user story.

  Ask AI: "How would a strategic leader make sure user focus stays central
  to programme-level decisions, not just individual delivery ones?"

Ask a peer principal engineer, in your organisation or another part of
government, which of these has taken them longest to get right — that
conversation will teach you more than any general reading on technical
leadership.
