# Lead DevOps engineer - management - start here

Welcome. If you are working toward the lead DevOps engineer (management)
level, this page is for you: what the role expects, where to find everything
else this repository has for it, and a pathway for building the skills it
asks for.

The framework carries two roles at this level, sharing one title and one set
of named skills: a technical one and this management one. If deep technical
leadership, rather than people and process leadership, is where you want to
grow, the
[lead DevOps engineer (technical)](https://uk-gdad.github.io/roles/software-development/development-operations-engineer/6-lead-devops-engineer/)
page may fit better — though the two tracks' named skills overlap closely
enough that reading both is worthwhile either way.

## Purpose

A lead DevOps engineer at this level still leads one or a small number of
related project teams and contributes to strategic direction, acting as a
technical product owner developing medium to long term strategies for
product lines. Where this track's own wording sharpens most is ownership:
analysing current processes and leading a team of experts to improve them,
and taking direct ownership of availability and capacity standards rather
than working within them. Providing technical leadership through coaching
and mentoring, and leading the sharing of knowledge and good practice, sit
alongside that ownership rather than instead of it.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. Each one is a requirement of this role
level. At this level, an AI tool is most useful as a sounding board for a
people or policy decision — describe the actual situation and ask what you
might be missing.

- [ ] **Lead a team of experts to improve how the organisation builds software**

  Analyse current development processes, identify and implement
  opportunities to optimise them, lead and develop a team of experts to
  deliver service improvements, and help set the policy and standards new
  changes get evaluated against.

  Ask AI: "How does a lead engineer sustain a process improvement effort
  over months, rather than it fading once the initial enthusiasm passes?"

- [ ] **Own availability and capacity standards across the teams you lead**

  Ensure the correct implementation of availability and capacity management
  standards and procedures, identify capacity issues before they bite, and
  initiate remedial action yourself.

  Ask AI: "What does owning availability and capacity standards look like
  when you're accountable for several teams rather than one service?"

- [ ] **Set development standards for complex, multi-team work**

  Identify and apply modern development standards to support complex
  projects and programmes, and lead others in applying them.

  Ask AI: "How does a lead in a management-focused role make sure
  development standards actually get followed, when they're not the one
  writing most of the code?"

- [ ] **Design systems of real complexity, and review other people's designs too**

  Design systems characterised by medium levels of risk, impact and
  business or technical complexity, select and apply the right design
  standards, and review the systems designs of others to check the
  technology choice and integration are sound.

  Ask AI: "What should a lead in a management-focused role look for when
  reviewing a design, given they may not write the code themselves day to
  day?"

- [ ] **Coordinate integration across systems**

  Define the integration build, co-ordinate build activities across
  systems, and understand how to support integration testing across the
  whole effort.

  Ask AI: "What coordination problems tend to appear when integration spans
  several teams with different priorities, and how would a lead resolve
  them?"

- [ ] **Find and fix service faults**

  Identify, locate and fix service faults — the wording here is simpler
  than some of the technical-track descriptions at this same level, which
  is the framework's own choice rather than a gap in what's expected of
  you; the leadership demanded of this level shows up more clearly in the
  skills around it.

  Ask AI: "Given this skill reads more simply than my other
  responsibilities, what would genuinely leading a team's approach to
  service faults look like if I pushed beyond what's written here?"

- [ ] **Lead on user focus across more than your own team**

  Collaborate with user researchers and represent users internally, explain
  the difference between user needs and user desires, champion user
  research so it covers all users, and guide others in understanding the
  user story.

  Ask AI: "How would a lead in a management-focused role champion user
  research consistently across teams they don't work in day to day?"

- [ ] **Keep building your programming and build practice**

  This skill's expectation is unchanged from the technical track at this
  level: collaborating on specifications, then designing, coding, testing
  and documenting programs of medium-to-high complexity to the right
  standards. Staying hands-on is what keeps your decisions grounded.

  Ask AI: "How can a lead in a management-focused role stay meaningfully
  hands-on with code without it crowding out the leadership work only they
  can do?"

- [ ] **Keep treating prototyping as a team activity**

  Approach prototyping as something the team does together, establishing
  and iterating design patterns, and choosing the right method from several
  available.

  Ask AI: "How might a lead encourage prototyping across teams with
  different habits, without dictating one method to all of them?"

- [ ] **Keep designing security in from the start**

  Design solutions and services with security controls included,
  specifically engineered to mitigate the relevant threats.

  Ask AI: "What security decisions made early tend to have the biggest
  consequences across a whole product line, and how would a lead make sure
  they get proper attention across teams?"

Ask a principal colleague which of these matters most in your organisation
right now, and start there.
