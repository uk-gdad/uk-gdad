# Senior developer - management - start here

Welcome. If you are working toward the senior developer (management) level,
this page is for you: what the role expects, where to find everything else
this repository has for it, and a pathway for building the skills it asks
for.

The framework carries two roles at this level, sharing one title and one set
of named skills: a technical one and this management one. Nine of the ten
named skills read identically between the two tracks — the one genuine
difference in the framework's own wording is availability and capacity
management, where this track leans toward owning the standard rather than
working within it. If deep technical delivery is where you want to grow, the
[senior developer (technical)](https://uk-gdad.github.io/roles/software-development/software-developer/4-senior-developer/)
page covers exactly the same ground either way.

## Purpose

A senior developer at this level still delivers and integrates software to
form a complete service, plans and leads development on sets of related
stories, and holds an understanding of the whole system well enough to teach
it to others — the same as the technical track. Where the framework's own
wording actually sharpens is ownership of availability and capacity: taking
direct responsibility for standards and remedial action, rather than
managing service components against KPIs someone else set. Coaching junior
colleagues and improving system robustness, resilience and stability sit
alongside that, exactly as they do on the technical track.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. Each one is a requirement of this role
level. At this level, an AI tool is often most useful for pressure-testing a
design or ownership decision — describe the actual trade-off you're weighing
and ask what you might be missing.

- [ ] **Own availability and capacity standards, not just meet them**

  Ensure the correct implementation of availability and capacity management
  standards and procedures, identify capacity issues before they bite, and
  initiate remedial action yourself rather than waiting to be asked — the
  one skill where this track's wording genuinely differs from the technical
  track at this level.

  Ask AI: "What's the difference between reacting well to a capacity issue
  and genuinely owning availability and capacity management as a standard
  others are held to?"

- [ ] **Take ownership of finding and fixing faults**

  Identify, locate and fix service faults yourself, the same expectation as
  on the technical track.

  Ask AI: "What diagnostic approach would help a senior developer locate
  the actual cause of a service fault efficiently, rather than guessing at
  fixes?"

- [ ] **Design systems of real complexity, and review other people's designs too**

  Design systems characterised by medium levels of risk, impact and
  business or technical complexity, select and apply the right design
  standards, and review the systems designs of others to check the
  technology choice and integration are sound.

  Ask AI: "What should a senior developer in a leadership track look for
  when reviewing someone else's system design, beyond a purely technical
  check?"

- [ ] **Coordinate integration across systems**

  Define the integration build, co-ordinate build activities across
  systems, and understand how to support integration testing across the
  whole effort rather than one interface at a time.

  Ask AI: "What coordination problems come up when integration spans
  several systems and several teams, that don't exist when it's a single
  interface?"

- [ ] **Keep contributing to process optimisation under guidance**

  This skill's wording is unchanged from the technical track at this level:
  working under guidance to identify process optimisation opportunities and
  contribute to implementing proposed solutions.

  Ask AI: "Given this skill reads more simply than my other
  responsibilities, what would genuinely leading process optimisation look
  like if I chose to push beyond what's written here?"

- [ ] **Keep designing security in from the start**

  This skill's wording is also unchanged: designing solutions and services
  with security controls included, specifically engineered to mitigate the
  relevant threats.

  Ask AI: "What security decisions at the design stage have the biggest
  long-term consequences if a team gets them wrong, and how would a leader
  make sure those decisions get proper attention?"

- [ ] **Apply development standards, and help others apply them too**

  Apply modern development standards yourself and support others in
  applying them.

  Ask AI: "What's an effective way to help a team consistently apply a
  development standard, rather than relying on individual discipline?"

- [ ] **Keep building your programming and build practice**

  This skill's expectation is unchanged: collaborating on specifications,
  then designing, coding, testing and documenting programs of
  medium-to-high complexity to the right standards. Staying hands-on here
  is what keeps your leadership decisions grounded in reality.

  Ask AI: "How can a senior developer in a management-leaning role stay
  genuinely hands-on with code, without it clashing with their leadership
  responsibilities?"

- [ ] **Keep treating prototyping as a team activity**

  Approach prototyping as something the team does together, establishing
  and iterating design patterns, and choosing the right method from several
  available.

  Ask AI: "How might a leader encourage a team to prototype more, when the
  pressure is usually to skip straight to building the real thing?"

- [ ] **Keep leading on user focus**

  Collaborate with user researchers and represent users internally, explain
  the difference between user needs and desires, champion research that
  covers all users, and guide others through the user story.

  Ask AI: "How does a technical lead champion genuinely inclusive user
  research, rather than research that only reaches the easiest users to
  reach?"

Ask your manager or a lead colleague which of these matters most in your
organisation right now, and start there.
