# Junior developer - start here

Welcome. If you are working toward the junior developer level, this page is
for you: what the role expects, where to find everything else this
repository has for it, and a pathway for building the skills it asks for.

## Purpose

A junior developer learns on the job by delivering software components — the
step up from apprentice is working under supervision to deliver real stories
in a multidisciplinary team, becoming proficient across different types of
testing, and starting to coach and mentor colleagues newer than you even
while you're still building your own practice. You're aware of security but
not yet responsible for it: that boundary is deliberate, and it's worth
understanding why rather than resenting it. Two new skills appear here —
systems design and systems integration — because delivering a real component
means understanding roughly where it sits in a bigger system.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. Each one is a requirement of this role
level. Where you want to go deeper, ask an AI tool a specific question
rather than searching for a generic guide — you'll get an answer shaped
around your own codebase and team.

- [ ] **Keep writing, testing and documenting programs under direction**

  This skill's wording plateaus here, unchanged from apprentice level:
  designing, coding, testing, correcting and documenting simple programs
  under the direction of others.

  Ask AI: "What's a realistic next step, beyond writing simple programs
  under direction, that a junior developer could actually practise?"

- [ ] **Contribute to designing a piece of a larger system**

  Assist, as part of a team, in the design of components of larger systems
  — you're not designing the whole system, but you are expected to
  understand enough of it to contribute meaningfully to your piece.

  Ask AI: "How does a junior developer contribute usefully to designing one
  component of a larger system, without needing to understand the entire
  system first?"

- [ ] **Understand how systems connect to each other**

  Explain the process and principles of integrating systems, and describe
  the challenges of designing, building and testing the interfaces between
  them.

  Ask AI: "Explain, simply, what 'integrating systems' means and why the
  interface between two systems is often where the hardest bugs hide."

- [ ] **Keep building a sense of who you're building for**

  This skill also plateaus here: showing an awareness of user experience
  analysis and its principles, and explaining the purpose of user stories.

  Ask AI: "What's a good next step, beyond understanding user stories, that
  a junior developer could actually practise?"

- [ ] **Start applying development standards, not just explaining them**

  Explain the most important principles of modern development standards
  and how they apply to your work, and apply those principles yourself
  under guidance.

  Ask AI: "What are the modern development standards most relevant to the
  kind of code a junior developer writes day to day?"

- [ ] **Learn when a prototype earns its keep**

  Understand when to use a specific prototyping technique or method, and be
  able to show your team the value of prototyping rather than jumping
  straight to a finished solution.

  Ask AI: "How would I decide whether a piece of work is worth prototyping
  first, versus just building it properly straight away?"

- [ ] **Fix faults by following procedure, and keep infrastructure maintained**

  Help fix service faults by following agreed procedures, and carry out
  maintenance tasks on the service support infrastructure that keeps
  everything running.

  Ask AI: "What's the difference between fixing a fault by following an
  agreed procedure and improvising a fix, and why does that difference
  matter at this level?"

- [ ] **Keep building your picture of availability and capacity**

  This skill's wording plateaus here too: explaining availability and
  capacity management processes.

  Ask AI: "What's a realistic next step, beyond explaining availability and
  capacity management, that a junior developer could actually practise?"

- [ ] **Move from explaining security to using it**

  Use information security practices and the security controls available
  to contribute to protecting solutions and services — the step up from
  apprentice is doing this, not just explaining it, even while the
  responsibility for security overall still sits with someone more senior.

  Ask AI: "What security practices would a junior developer be expected to
  actually apply day to day, as opposed to just being aware of?"

Ask your manager or a more senior colleague which of these your team relies on
most right now, and start there.
