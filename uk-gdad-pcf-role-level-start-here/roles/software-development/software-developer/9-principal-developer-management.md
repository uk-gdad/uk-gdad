# Principal developer - management - start here

Welcome. If you are working toward the principal developer (management)
level, this page is for you: what the role expects, where to find everything
else this repository has for it, and a pathway for building the skills it
asks for.

The framework carries two roles at this level, sharing one title and one set
of named skills: a technical one and this management one. If deep technical
practice, rather than strategic and people leadership, is where you want to
grow, the
[principal developer (technical)](https://uk-gdad.github.io/roles/software-development/software-developer/8-principal-developer/)
page may fit better.

## Purpose

A principal developer at this level still leads and plans development
across large or multiple teams, identifies and champions emerging
technologies, and develops the capability of the teams under them by
facilitating internal mobility, shaping career paths and recruiting the
right talent. Where this track's own wording sharpens most is strategy:
setting the strategy and resource allocation for whole development
programmes, and leading policy and standards for availability and capacity,
rather than implementing within a strategy someone else set.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. There is little level above this one, so
use an AI tool as a sounding board rather than a teacher: describe a real
strategic call you're weighing and ask it to argue the case you haven't made
yet.

- [ ] **Set strategy and resourcing for whole development programmes**

  Set the strategy and manage resource allocation for solution development
  programmes, and work with client functions to establish business
  requirements and identify, propose, initiate and lead those programmes.

  Ask AI: "How would a principal developer in a strategic role decide which
  development programmes deserve investment when demand outstrips
  capacity?"

- [ ] **Own availability and capacity standards across the organisation**

  Ensure the correct implementation of availability and capacity management
  standards and procedures, identify capacity issues early, and initiate
  remedial action.

  Ask AI: "What early warning signs of a capacity problem would a principal
  developer be expected to catch, that a less senior developer might miss?"

- [ ] **Shape system design practice at enterprise scale**

  Design systems characterised by high levels of risk, impact and business
  or technical complexity, control system design practice within an
  enterprise or industry architecture, influence industry-based models, and
  ensure adherence to relevant technical strategies and standards.

  Ask AI: "How does a strategic leader make sure enterprise-level system
  design standards actually get followed by teams under real delivery
  pressure?"

- [ ] **Quality-assure security across the organisation's solutions**

  Design, review and quality assure solutions and services to make sure
  they're specifically engineered to mitigate security threats, and lead
  others in using appropriate information security practices.

  Ask AI: "How does a strategic leader make quality-assuring security
  across many teams' work sustainable, rather than a bottleneck that slows
  everyone down?"

- [ ] **Think about prototyping and service design strategically**

  Use a variety of prototyping methods, share best practice and coach
  others in using them, and look at strategic service design end to end.

  Ask AI: "What changes about coaching teams on prototyping when you're
  responsible for strategy across many services, not delivering one?"

- [ ] **Keep setting development standards for complex, multi-team work**

  This skill's wording plateaus here, unchanged from the technical track:
  identifying and applying modern development standards to support complex
  projects and programmes, and leading others in applying them.

  Ask AI: "How does a strategic leader get a development standard adopted
  across an organisation with genuinely different team cultures?"

- [ ] **Keep finding and fixing the hardest service faults**

  This skill also plateaus: identifying, locating and fixing service
  faults.

  Ask AI: "What role should a strategic leader play in service faults that
  they wouldn't be fixing personally?"

- [ ] **Keep coordinating integration across systems**

  This skill's wording is unchanged: defining the integration build,
  co-ordinating build activities across systems, and supporting integration
  testing.

  Ask AI: "What does strategic-level oversight of systems integration add,
  beyond what's written in this skill?"

- [ ] **Keep leading on user focus**

  This skill's wording plateaus too: collaborating with user researchers,
  representing users internally, and guiding others through the user
  story.

  Ask AI: "How would a strategic leader make sure user focus stays central
  to programme-level decisions, not just individual delivery ones?"

- [ ] **Design and build to agreed specifications**

  The wording here is simpler than the equivalent technical-track skill at
  this level — collaborating on specifications, then designing, coding,
  testing and documenting programs of medium-to-high complexity. That's the
  framework's own choice; the strategic leadership expected of you shows up
  more clearly in the skills above.

  Ask AI: "How does a strategic leader stay credible on technical decisions
  without doing hands-on development day to day?"

Ask a peer principal developer, in your organisation or another part of
government, which of these has taken them longest to get right — that
conversation will teach you more than any general reading on technical
leadership.
