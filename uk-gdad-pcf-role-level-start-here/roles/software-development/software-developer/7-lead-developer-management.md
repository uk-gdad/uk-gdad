# Lead developer - management - start here

Welcome. If you are working toward the lead developer (management) level,
this page is for you: what the role expects, where to find everything else
this repository has for it, and a pathway for building the skills it asks
for.

The framework carries two roles at this level, sharing one title and one set
of named skills: a technical one and this management one. If deep technical
leadership, rather than people and process leadership, is where you want to
grow, the
[lead developer (technical)](https://uk-gdad.github.io/roles/software-development/software-developer/6-lead-developer/)
page may fit better — though the two tracks' named skills overlap closely
enough that reading both is worthwhile either way.

## Purpose

A lead developer at this level still leads development on one or a small
number of related project teams, guides how the team works, and provides
technical leadership through coaching and mentoring. Where this track's own
wording sharpens most is ownership: analysing current processes and leading
a team of experts to improve them, and taking direct ownership of
availability and capacity standards rather than working within them.
Contributing to the developer community and promoting knowledge sharing sit
alongside that ownership rather than instead of it.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. Each one is a requirement of this role
level. At this level, an AI tool is most useful as a sounding board for a
people or policy decision — describe the actual situation and ask what you
might be missing.

- [ ] **Lead a team of experts to improve how the organisation builds software**

  Analyse current development processes, identify and implement
  opportunities to optimise them, lead and develop a team of experts to
  deliver service improvements, and help set the policy and standards new
  changes get evaluated against.

  Ask AI: "How does a lead developer sustain a process improvement effort
  over months, rather than it fading once the initial enthusiasm passes?"

- [ ] **Own availability and capacity standards across the teams you lead**

  Ensure the correct implementation of availability and capacity management
  standards and procedures, identify capacity issues before they bite, and
  initiate remedial action yourself.

  Ask AI: "What does owning availability and capacity standards look like
  when you're accountable for several teams rather than one service?"

- [ ] **Set development standards for complex, multi-team work**

  Identify and apply modern development standards to support complex
  projects and programmes, and lead others in applying them.

  Ask AI: "How does a lead in a management-focused role make sure
  development standards actually get followed, when they're not the one
  writing most of the code?"

- [ ] **Keep finding and fixing service faults**

  This skill's wording plateaus here, unchanged from the technical track:
  identifying, locating and fixing service faults.

  Ask AI: "What role should a lead in a management-focused role play in
  service faults that they wouldn't be fixing personally?"

- [ ] **Keep designing systems of real complexity, and reviewing others' designs**

  This skill also plateaus: designing systems characterised by medium
  levels of risk, impact and business or technical complexity, and
  reviewing the systems designs of others.

  Ask AI: "What should a lead in a management-focused role look for when
  reviewing a design, given they may not write the code themselves day to
  day?"

- [ ] **Keep coordinating integration across systems**

  This skill's wording is unchanged too: defining the integration build,
  co-ordinating build activities across systems, and supporting integration
  testing.

  Ask AI: "What coordination problems tend to appear when integration spans
  several teams with different priorities, and how would a lead resolve
  them?"

- [ ] **Keep leading on user focus across more than your own team**

  This skill also plateaus: collaborating with user researchers,
  representing users internally, and guiding others through the user
  story.

  Ask AI: "How would a lead in a management-focused role champion user
  research consistently across teams they don't work in day to day?"

- [ ] **Keep designing security in from the start**

  This skill's wording is unchanged: designing solutions and services with
  security controls included, specifically engineered to mitigate the
  relevant threats.

  Ask AI: "What security decisions made early tend to have the biggest
  consequences across a whole product line, and how would a lead make sure
  they get proper attention across teams?"

- [ ] **Keep treating prototyping as a team activity**

  This skill's wording plateaus too: approaching prototyping as something
  the team does together, establishing and iterating design patterns.

  Ask AI: "How might a lead encourage prototyping across teams with
  different habits, without dictating one method to all of them?"

- [ ] **Design and build to agreed specifications**

  The wording here is simpler than the equivalent technical-track skill at
  this level — collaborating on specifications, then designing, coding,
  testing and documenting programs of medium-to-high complexity. That's the
  framework's own choice; the leadership demanded of this level shows up
  more clearly in the skills above.

  Ask AI: "Given this skill reads more simply than my other
  responsibilities, what would maintaining genuine technical credibility
  look like if I pushed beyond what's written here?"

Ask a principal colleague which of these matters most in your organisation
right now, and start there.
