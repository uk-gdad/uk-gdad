# Junior frontend developer - start here

Welcome. If you are working toward the junior frontend developer level, this
page is for you: what the role expects, where to find everything else this
repository has for it, and a pathway for building the skills it asks for.

## Purpose

A junior frontend developer learns on the job by delivering software
components — the step up from apprentice is working more independently, with
guidance from more senior developers, to identify user issues and needs
yourself rather than mostly shadowing. You're still building your
understanding of accessibility and your general knowledge, but you're now
expected to write clean code that follows web standards as a matter of
course. Two new skills appear at this level — systems integration and
strategic thinking — because delivering a real component means understanding
how it actually reaches a browser, and why the work fits into something
bigger than the ticket in front of you.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. Each one is a requirement of this role
level. Where you want to go deeper, ask an AI tool a specific question
rather than searching for a generic guide — you'll get an answer shaped
around your own codebase and team.

- [ ] **Build and document user interfaces from a reviewed specification**

  Collaborate with others to understand and review software requirements,
  then design, code, test, correct and document user interfaces using the
  right standards and tools, under the direction of others.

  Ask AI: "What should a junior developer actually check for when reviewing
  a requirement before starting to build against it?"

- [ ] **Understand how your code reaches a real browser**

  Show an awareness of how to build static assets from source code, and
  explain how browsers use and present those assets — including how
  different versions of an interface can be delivered for different
  browsers and devices.

  Ask AI: "Explain, simply, what happens between writing frontend source
  code and a user's browser actually displaying it."

- [ ] **Keep building a sense of who you're building for**

  This skill's wording stays the same as at apprentice level here: show an
  understanding of user experience analysis and its principles, and explain
  the purpose of user stories. The growth at this level is happening in the
  skills around it.

  Ask AI: "What's a good next step, beyond just understanding user stories,
  that a junior developer could actually practise?"

- [ ] **Move from awareness of accessibility to fixing it**

  Understand that users have different accessibility needs, and build user
  interfaces that meet accessibility standards, fixing accessibility issues
  under the direction of others.

  Ask AI: "What are the most common accessibility issues a junior frontend
  developer is likely to be asked to fix, and how would I recognise them in
  code?"

- [ ] **Start applying development standards, not just explaining them**

  Explain the most important principles of modern development standards and
  how they apply to your work, and apply those principles yourself under
  guidance.

  Ask AI: "What modern development standards are most relevant to the kind
  of frontend code a junior developer writes day to day?"

- [ ] **Learn when a prototype earns its keep**

  Understand when to use a specific prototyping technique or method, and be
  able to show your team the value of prototyping rather than jumping
  straight to a finished interface.

  Ask AI: "How would I decide whether a piece of interface work is worth
  prototyping first, versus just building it properly straight away?"

- [ ] **Keep building your web performance awareness**

  This skill's wording plateaus here, unchanged from apprentice level:
  describing the basic principles of web performance and helping improve it
  under supervision.

  Ask AI: "What's a realistic next step, beyond describing web performance
  principles, that a junior developer could actually practise?"

- [ ] **Start seeing how your work fits into a bigger strategy**

  Explain the strategic context of your work and why it matters, and
  support strategic planning in an administrative capacity — a new skill at
  this level, and the first sign you're expected to see beyond your own
  ticket.

  Ask AI: "How would a junior developer find out the strategic context
  behind a piece of work they've been asked to do, if nobody's explained it
  to them?"

- [ ] **Start representing your team, not just working within it**

  Take part in discussions within a multidisciplinary team, act as an
  advocate for your team externally, manage differing perspectives, and
  shape communications for the audience receiving them.

  Ask AI: "What does being 'an advocate for the team externally' actually
  look like for a junior developer, in a way that doesn't overstep?"

- [ ] **Help shape a healthy team environment**

  Contribute to the work of others, help motivate and empower the team,
  help create the right environment for the team to work in, and recognise
  and deal with issues as they come up.

  Ask AI: "What are some early warning signs of a team dynamic going wrong,
  that a junior developer might notice before anyone raises it formally?"

- [ ] **Keep following the team's agreed good practice**

  This skill's wording plateaus here too, unchanged from apprentice level:
  following the team's agreed good practice and understanding why feedback
  matters.

  Ask AI: "What would moving from following agreed good practice to helping
  shape it look like, even informally, at a junior level?"

Ask your manager or a more senior colleague which of these your team relies on
most right now, and start there.
