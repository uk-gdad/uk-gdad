# Senior service designer - start here

Welcome. If you are working toward the senior service designer level, this
page is for you: what the role expects, where to find everything else this
repository has for it, and a pathway for building the skills it asks for.

## Purpose

A senior service designer is an experienced designer who works with
minimal support and can influence and mentor others. You work with service
managers and programme directors to develop design concepts, potentially
take responsibility across complex services, help set direction and embed
good practice within teams, and make important decisions based on research
while understanding how that research affects others. Nearly every named
skill grows at this level, because minimal support means your own
judgement about how a whole service should behave now has to hold up
without someone senior checking it first.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. At this level, an AI tool is often
most useful for pressure-testing a service design decision — describe the
actual trade-off you're weighing and ask what you might be missing.

- [ ] **Explain complexity, and build consensus through hard questions**

  Clearly explain complex problems and ideas to teams or stakeholders
  outside design, document and communicate decisions, risks and unresolved
  issues, and build consensus around a design approach by asking difficult
  questions and challenging assumptions.

  Ask AI: "What kind of difficult question would genuinely test a service
  design approach's assumptions, rather than just sounding critical?"

- [ ] **Design ethical services, considering personal and social context**

  Advise teams on designing inclusive, accessible and environmentally
  sustainable content, design and deliver ethical services that consider
  users' personal and social context, and make sure designs meet
  accessibility standards.

  Ask AI: "What does considering a user's 'personal and social context'
  add to how a service journey is designed, beyond meeting accessibility
  standards technically?"

- [ ] **Use risk, opportunity and constraint to shape service design**

  Help a team understand how user-centred design helps it meet its goals,
  help teams align to their organisation's vision, use risks,
  opportunities and constraints in technology, systems and policy to
  shape design, and identify and create new design patterns and
  components.

  Ask AI: "How would a senior service designer use a policy constraint as
  an opportunity to improve a service, rather than treating it purely as
  a limitation?"

- [ ] **Advise others on running effective design sessions**

  Advise others on planning and running design sessions, adapt a session
  to make sure it achieves a useful outcome, effectively involve the right
  people, and work across team or profession boundaries such as with
  policy teams.

  Ask AI: "What would a senior service designer change mid-session if a
  workshop mapping a cross-government journey wasn't heading towards a
  useful outcome?"

- [ ] **Use complex evidence to develop and test service design ideas**

  Analyse and explain complex evidence such as large datasets, help your
  team use design hypotheses effectively, and use complex research and
  data to develop and test design ideas.

  Ask AI: "How would a senior service designer turn evidence from several
  different channels into a specific, testable hypothesis about a
  service?"

- [ ] **Help others iterate, and adapt complex services quickly**

  Help other designers apply iterative design principles and Agile
  methodologies, iterate and improve complex designs based on successive
  research, independently prototype complex ideas at an appropriate
  fidelity, and adapt designs quickly to changing requirements.

  Ask AI: "What would help a senior service designer decide how quickly a
  complex service genuinely needs to adapt to a changing policy
  requirement, versus waiting for more evidence?"

- [ ] **Keep leading design work, and supporting other designers**

  This skill's wording plateaus here, unchanged from service designer
  level: leading design work with support, communicating the value of
  user-centred design, and supporting other designers.

  Ask AI: "Given this skill's description hasn't changed since the level
  below, what would genuinely senior-level design leadership add beyond
  what's written here?"

Ask a lead service designer, or an equivalent principal colleague, which
of these matters most in your organisation right now, and start there.
