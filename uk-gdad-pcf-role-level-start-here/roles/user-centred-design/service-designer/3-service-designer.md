# Service designer - start here

Welcome. If you are working toward the service designer level, this page
is for you: what the role expects, where to find everything else this
repository has for it, and a pathway for building the skills it asks for.

## Purpose

A service designer is a confident, competent designer who can develop
designs based on evidence of user needs and organisational outcomes. You're
trusted to make good decisions, know when to ask for further guidance,
contribute to developing design concepts, and interpret evidence-based
research and incorporate it into your work. Designing strategically,
designing together and leading design all deepen at this level, because
you're now trusted with real judgement calls about how a whole service
should work, not just applying design skill to one part of it under
direction.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. Each one is a requirement of this role
level. Where you want to go deeper, ask an AI tool a specific question
rather than searching for a generic guide — you'll get an answer shaped
around your own service.

- [ ] **Use service design to manage risk and opportunity**

  Align your design work to both your team's goals and your
  organisation's strategic objectives, identify potential risks and use
  design to mitigate them, identify opportunities and use design to take
  advantage of them, and contribute to developing design patterns and
  components.

  Ask AI: "What's an example of a risk a service designer could mitigate
  through how a journey is structured across channels, rather than
  through policy or process?"

- [ ] **Plan and run design sessions with real people**

  Effectively plan and run a design session with your team, users or
  stakeholders, identify and engage the right people throughout the
  design process, and give and receive constructive feedback.

  Ask AI: "What makes a session testing an end-to-end journey actually
  productive, rather than just well attended?"

- [ ] **Lead design work, and support other designers**

  Lead and co-ordinate design work in your team with support, communicate
  the value of user-centred design to your team, support other designers,
  and work with digital and data leaders when needed.

  Ask AI: "What does 'supporting other designers' actually look like day
  to day for a service designer, beyond giving feedback when asked?"

- [ ] **Keep explaining service design problems and decisions clearly**

  This skill's wording plateaus here, unchanged from junior level: clearly
  explaining problems and design ideas, and explaining decisions using
  appropriate tools and methods.

  Ask AI: "Given this skill's description hasn't changed since the level
  below, what would genuinely more confident-level design communication
  add beyond what's written here?"

- [ ] **Keep designing inclusive services**

  This skill also plateaus: designing and delivering inclusive,
  accessible and sustainable content, and meeting standards such as
  accessibility regulations, with support.

  Ask AI: "What would taking full ownership of accessibility across a
  whole service journey, rather than meeting standards with support, look
  like at this level?"

- [ ] **Keep turning evidence into a tested service design idea**

  This skill's wording plateaus too: analysing and explaining evidence,
  and working with researchers or analysts to develop and test design
  ideas.

  Ask AI: "What would interpreting user research evidence independently,
  rather than working through it with a researcher each time, look like?"

- [ ] **Keep applying iterative design principles to real work**

  This skill also plateaus: applying iterative design and Agile
  methodologies, prototyping at different fidelities, and using and
  iterating design patterns.

  Ask AI: "Given this skill's description hasn't changed since the level
  below, what would genuinely more independent iterative design practice
  add beyond what's written here?"

Ask a senior service designer, or an equivalent lead colleague, which of
these matters most in your organisation right now, and start there.
