# Associate user researcher - start here

Welcome. If you are starting out as an associate user researcher, this
page is for you: what the role is about, where to find everything else this
repository has for it, and a pathway for building the skills it asks for,
one step at a time.

## Purpose

User researchers plan, design and carry out research activities with users
that help teams get a deep understanding of the people who use government
services — research that informs policy, proposition, service, content and
interaction design so services work well for users and achieve their policy
intent. As an associate, this is a trainee, entry-level role working with
more experienced researchers: you need an understanding of the role and to
show potential, with guidance and training to produce good work and
develop your skills.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. Each one is a requirement of this role
level, described plainly, with a way to go deeper by asking an AI tool a
specific question — the answers you get will be shaped around your own
organisation's users, which a generic guide never is.

- [ ] **Explain some basic user research methods**

  Explain some basic user research methods, even before you're expected to
  apply them independently.

  Ask AI: "I'm new to user research. Explain, simply, the difference
  between a user interview and usability testing, and when each is used."

- [ ] **Understand the importance of careful analysis**

  Show an awareness of the need for careful analysis of research data to
  produce clear findings, rather than jumping to conclusions from a first
  impression.

  Ask AI: "What does 'careful analysis' of research data actually look
  like, as opposed to summarising the first thing that stands out?"

- [ ] **Understand agile working and multidisciplinary roles**

  Describe Agile working and the roles in a multidisciplinary digital
  team.

  Ask AI: "Explain, for a beginner, how a user researcher's work fits into
  an Agile team alongside designers, developers and a product manager."

- [ ] **Build awareness of the diversity of service users**

  Demonstrate some awareness of the diversity of users of government
  services, and the need to make services usable and accessible for
  everyone.

  Ask AI: "What's an example of a group of users an associate researcher
  might overlook by default, and why does including them matter?"

- [ ] **Understand why a research plan and standards matter**

  Explain the importance of working to a user research plan, and of
  following standards for user research.

  Ask AI: "Explain, simply, what could go wrong if user research happens
  without a plan or agreed standards."

- [ ] **Learn who your stakeholders are, and why that matters**

  Describe who your stakeholders are and why managing relationships with
  them matters, and explain what they find important and why.

  Ask AI: "How would an associate user researcher work out who the real
  stakeholders are for a piece of research, beyond the obvious people in
  the room?"

- [ ] **Understand how services shape user behaviour**

  Describe how government services and technology affect how people
  behave, and explain how user-centred practices help build services that
  adapt to changing user behaviour and needs.

  Ask AI: "What's an example of a government service that changed how
  people behaved, in a way the original designers might not have
  expected?"

You do not need to master all of this before you start. Ask your manager or
a more senior researcher which of these your team leans on most, and start
there.
