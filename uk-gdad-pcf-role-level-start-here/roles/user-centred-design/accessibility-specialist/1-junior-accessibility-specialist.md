# Junior accessibility specialist - start here

Welcome. If you are starting out as a junior accessibility specialist, this
page is for you: what the role is about, where to find everything else this
repository has for it, and a pathway for building the skills it asks for,
one step at a time.

## Purpose

An accessibility specialist provides support, advice and guidance to other
roles across the Government Digital and Data profession on creating
accessible digital services — using knowledge of accessibility guidelines,
technical expertise and the voice of disabled users to make sure product
teams build services everyone can use. As a junior specialist, you're
learning on the job by assisting with accessibility testing: developing
your skills under supervision, understanding and using different types of
testing, staying aware of assistive technology testing without being
responsible for it yet, and engaging with the cross-government
accessibility community.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. Each one is a requirement of this role
level, described plainly, with a way to go deeper by asking an AI tool a
specific question — the answers you get will be shaped around your own
organisation's services, which a generic guide never is.

- [ ] **Build technical knowledge of accessibility standards and checks**

  Demonstrate knowledge of some accessibility standards and legislation
  and know where to find more, demonstrate basic knowledge of HTML, CSS
  and JavaScript and related accessibility issues, perform basic automated
  and manual checks and communicate findings and fixes, advocate for
  people affected by accessibility barriers, and demonstrate knowledge of
  using some assistive technology.

  Ask AI: "I'm new to accessibility work. Explain, simply, what an
  automated accessibility check can catch, and what it typically can't."

- [ ] **Execute test scripts, and understand how testing works**

  Correctly execute test scripts under supervision, and understand the
  role testing plays and how it works.

  Ask AI: "What is an accessibility test script actually checking for,
  and why does someone need to execute it correctly rather than just run
  it?"

- [ ] **Build a basic sense of who you're testing for**

  Show an awareness or understanding of user experience analysis and its
  principles, and explain the purpose of user stories and their focus on
  user needs.

  Ask AI: "Why do accessibility specialists care about user stories, not
  just technical compliance checklists?"

- [ ] **Understand what consultancy means for this role**

  Show an understanding of why consulting matters in this role and what
  it's for, work under guidance to identify what advice might be
  appropriate — usually by referring to previous work — and understand how
  to frame a problem that needs solving.

  Ask AI: "What's the difference between giving someone an accessibility
  fix and consulting with them properly, and why does a specialist need to
  do the second?"

- [ ] **Practise listening and joining stakeholder discussions**

  Listen to the needs of design and business stakeholders and interpret
  what they're telling you, and take part in discussions within a
  multidisciplinary team.

  Ask AI: "What should someone new to accessibility work focus on when
  joining a design discussion, so they contribute usefully rather than
  staying silent out of uncertainty?"

You do not need to master all of this before you start. Ask your manager or
a more senior specialist which of these your team leans on most, and start
there.
