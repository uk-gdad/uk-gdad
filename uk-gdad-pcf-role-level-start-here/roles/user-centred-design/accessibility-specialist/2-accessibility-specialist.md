# Accessibility specialist - start here

Welcome. If you are working toward the accessibility specialist level, this
page is for you: what the role expects, where to find everything else this
repository has for it, and a pathway for building the skills it asks for.

## Purpose

An accessibility specialist can support teams in creating accessible
services. You coach and mentor more junior colleagues, help provide
training materials and learning content, perform accessibility audits, and
engage with the cross-government accessibility community. Two new skills
appear here — governance and assurance, and leadership and guidance —
because supporting a whole team means feeding into how projects are
governed and making real judgement calls, not just checking pages against a
standard.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. Each one is a requirement of this role
level. Where you want to go deeper, ask an AI tool a specific question
rather than searching for a generic guide — you'll get an answer shaped
around your own organisation's services.

- [ ] **Communicate and audit against accessibility standards**

  Demonstrate knowledge of accessibility standards and legislation and
  communicate their requirements, advocate appropriate technical
  solutions, perform detailed audits and document findings clearly for
  others to work from, advocate for people affected by accessibility
  barriers across the department, provide introductory training, and
  demonstrate confidence using assistive technology.

  Ask AI: "What makes an accessibility audit finding genuinely actionable
  for a developer, rather than just a reference to a standard?"

- [ ] **Assess governance and project documents against accessibility standards**

  Analyse and provide feedback on governance and project documents, and on
  governance boards, according to a predefined framework, assessing them
  against accessibility standards.

  Ask AI: "What should an accessibility specialist look for when reviewing
  a project governance document, beyond whether accessibility is
  mentioned?"

- [ ] **Make decisions of managed risk, and resolve technical disputes**

  Contribute to best practice guidelines, understand the sustainability
  and consequences of your decisions, make decisions of managed risk and
  complexity, and resolve technical disputes between wider peers and
  indirect stakeholders.

  Ask AI: "How should an accessibility specialist resolve a disagreement
  between two colleagues who both have a reasonable case about an
  accessibility trade-off?"

- [ ] **Define test conditions, and analyse results**

  Review requirements and specifications to define test conditions,
  identify issues and risks associated with the work, and analyse and
  report on test activities and results.

  Ask AI: "What makes an accessibility test condition genuinely
  well-defined, as opposed to a vague check that something 'looks
  accessible'?"

- [ ] **Turn user evidence into decisions**

  Identify and engage with users or stakeholders to collate evidence of
  their needs, understand and define research that actually fits those
  needs, and use both quantitative and qualitative evidence to turn user
  focus into real outcomes.

  Ask AI: "How would an accessibility specialist gather enough evidence
  from disabled users to make a decision, without overstepping into a
  dedicated researcher's role?"

- [ ] **Advise as a specialist, not just apply guidance**

  Provide advice, guidance and recommendations based on your specialist
  knowledge and experience, propose methodologies and approaches to
  implementation, and frame problems so they're easy to understand and
  troubleshoot.

  Ask AI: "What's the difference between giving a team an answer and
  giving them consultancy, and when does an accessibility specialist need
  to do the second?"

- [ ] **Advocate for your team, and manage differing perspectives**

  Listen to the needs of design and business stakeholders, take part in
  multidisciplinary discussions, advocate for your team externally, manage
  differing perspectives, and shape communications for the audience.

  Ask AI: "How would an accessibility specialist advocate for their team
  externally without overstepping into speaking for decisions that aren't
  theirs to make?"

Ask your manager or a more senior colleague which of these your team relies on
most right now, and start there.
