# Associate product manager - start here

Welcome. If you are starting out as an associate product manager, this page
is for you: what the role is about, where to find everything else this
repository has for it, and a pathway for building the skills it asks for,
one step at a time. This is often an entry-level role, including for civil
servants moving into a digital role or on an emerging talent scheme, so
you're not expected to arrive knowing all of this already.

## Purpose

A product manager makes sure their products provide value and achieve the
right outcomes by balancing user and business needs. As an associate, you're
responsible for features, components or part of a product, contributing to
that value rather than owning the whole of it. You work with other product
managers to advocate for user needs across the organisation, manage a
smaller product with support or a subset of a larger one, and spend real
time learning product management approaches and methods rather than being
expected to already have them.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. Each one is a requirement of this role
level, described plainly, with a way to go deeper by asking an AI tool a
specific question — the answers you get will be shaped around your own
product and team, which a generic guide never is.

- [ ] **Create and prioritise goals that balance user and business needs**

  Create and prioritise actionable goals for your teams, balancing user
  needs and business perspectives, make or recommend evidence-based
  decisions, use a range of product management methods such as
  prioritisation and roadmapping, and help teams plan and deliver
  high-quality product increments.

  Ask AI: "I'm new to product management. Explain, simply, what balancing
  'user needs' against 'business needs' actually looks like when the two
  seem to pull in different directions."

- [ ] **Learn to gather and use user insight**

  Identify and engage users and stakeholders to understand their needs and
  trends, decide when you need more insight versus when you have enough to
  decide, work with others to define the problem and research goals, and
  use both quantitative and qualitative insight to improve outcomes.

  Ask AI: "How would an associate product manager judge when they have
  enough user insight to make a decision, rather than gathering research
  indefinitely?"

- [ ] **Understand why measuring outcomes matters**

  Explain why identifying and measuring product outcomes matters, explain
  the need for baseline data, and identify and explain the priority
  outcomes for your product or feature.

  Ask AI: "Explain, simply, why a product manager needs baseline data
  before they can claim an outcome has improved."

- [ ] **Learn what each phase of a product life cycle is for**

  Explain what each phase of the product or service life cycle is for, and
  how the needs of the product and the team's response change across
  phases.

  Ask AI: "Explain, simply, the different phases a digital product
  typically goes through, and what changes about a product manager's job
  in each one."

- [ ] **Describe the value of your work clearly**

  Clearly describe the value of your work, determine or estimate the
  benefits it's expected to deliver, and identify opportunities for reuse
  that save time and money.

  Ask AI: "What makes a description of a feature's value convincing to a
  stakeholder, rather than just a list of things it does?"

- [ ] **Learn to identify and communicate with stakeholders**

  Identify important stakeholders and communicate with them clearly and
  regularly, tailor communication to their needs, build and reach
  consensus, and use evidence to explain decisions.

  Ask AI: "How would an associate product manager work out who the real
  stakeholders are for their feature, beyond the obvious people in the
  room?"

- [ ] **Start understanding your organisation's strategy**

  Explain how you make decisions aligned to your organisation's strategy,
  and explain why having a strategy matters in the first place.

  Ask AI: "Explain, for a beginner, why a product needs a strategy beyond
  a list of features to build, and how a strategy actually guides day-to-
  day decisions."

- [ ] **Learn how product management fits with other roles**

  Describe how product management works alongside other roles in a
  multidisciplinary team, explain how user and business needs should be
  balanced in product development, and start attending product management
  community activities.

  Ask AI: "How does a product manager's role differ from a delivery
  manager's or a business analyst's, in a team where all three exist?"

- [ ] **Build a working knowledge of Agile and Lean practices**

  Demonstrate experience applying Agile principles, provide a clear, open
  and transparent framework for the team to deliver within, show awareness
  of Agile tools and start using them intelligently, and make the team's
  work visible.

  Ask AI: "What does 'making a team's work visible' actually look like for
  a product manager specifically, as distinct from a delivery manager's
  job?"

You do not need to master all of this before you start. Ask your manager or
a more senior product manager which of these your team leans on most, and
start there.
