# Associate delivery manager - start here

Welcome. If you are starting out as an associate delivery manager, this page
is for you: what the role is about, where to find everything else this
repository has for it, and a pathway for building the skills it asks for,
one step at a time.

## Purpose

A delivery manager is accountable for the delivery of products and services
— removing what gets in a team's way so the team itself can focus on the
work. As an associate, you're working on a small or mature Agile team,
learning on the job, and potentially working alongside a more experienced
delivery manager on a larger team: shadowing, providing support, or
delivering one element under guidance and mentorship. That's exactly the
right shape for this level — delivery management is learned by watching good
judgement in action, not from a checklist.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. Each one is a requirement of this role
level, described plainly, with a way to go deeper by asking an AI tool a
specific question — the answers you get will be shaped around your own
team's context, which a generic guide never is.

- [ ] **Build a working knowledge of Agile and Lean practices**

  Demonstrate experience applying Agile principles, provide a clear, open
  and transparent framework for the team to deliver within, show awareness
  of Agile tools and start using them intelligently, and make the team's
  work visible.

  Ask AI: "I'm new to delivery management. Explain, simply, what it means
  to make a team's work visible, and why that matters for how a team
  delivers."

- [ ] **Help the team find a process that actually works for them**

  Guide the team to focus on the output rather than the process itself,
  help them find a process that genuinely fits how they work, and support
  them in establishing it.

  Ask AI: "How would an associate delivery manager tell the difference
  between a process that's helping a team and one that's just process for
  its own sake?"

- [ ] **Start understanding team dynamics and why feedback matters**

  Explain the importance of team dynamics, collaboration and empowering
  delivery teams, and understand why feedback matters even before you're
  expected to give much of it yourself.

  Ask AI: "What does 'empowering a delivery team' actually mean in
  practice, as distinct from just leaving them alone?"

- [ ] **Learn why planning and forecasting matter**

  Understand the importance of planning and forecasting, and show an
  awareness of the different ways a plan can be developed.

  Ask AI: "Explain, simply, why delivery teams plan and forecast even when
  the work itself is uncertain, and what different planning approaches
  exist."

- [ ] **Start spotting and escalating risks and dependencies**

  Actively address internal risks and issues, and know when to escalate
  them, set a sustainable team cadence, track and communicate dependencies,
  and understand how they affect a team's work.

  Ask AI: "How would an associate delivery manager judge when a risk needs
  escalating, rather than just monitoring it themselves?"

- [ ] **Practise communicating between technical and non-technical people**

  Communicate effectively with technical and non-technical stakeholders,
  take part in discussions within a multidisciplinary team that has
  potentially difficult dynamics, and start advocating for the team
  externally.

  Ask AI: "What should someone new to delivery management focus on when
  joining a multidisciplinary team discussion, so they contribute usefully?"

- [ ] **Learn what each phase of a product or service life cycle is for**

  Explain what each phase of the product or service life cycle is for, and
  how the needs of the product or service — and how the team responds —
  change across phases.

  Ask AI: "Explain, simply, the different phases a digital product or
  service typically goes through, and what changes about a team's work in
  each one."

- [ ] **Start building financial awareness**

  Understand the financial impact of the work you do, and how costs and
  budgets are created in the first place.

  Ask AI: "Explain, for a beginner, how a delivery team's work translates
  into cost, and why understanding that matters even at associate level."

- [ ] **Get comfortable with the basics of commercial management**

  Act as a point of contact for contracted suppliers, understand the right
  internal contacts and processes within a government department, and
  understand how and when third parties should be brought into projects.

  Ask AI: "What should an associate delivery manager know about working
  with a contracted supplier, even before they're negotiating anything
  themselves?"

You do not need to master all of this before you start. Ask your manager or
whoever you're shadowing which of these your team leans on most, and start
there.
