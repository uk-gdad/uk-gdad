# Associate test engineer - start here

Welcome. If you are starting out as an associate test engineer, this page is
for you: what the role is about, where to find everything else this
repository has for it, and a pathway for building the skills it asks for, one
step at a time.

## Purpose

An associate test engineer is where testing stops being something you read
about and starts being something you do — under supervision, alongside people
who already do it well. You are not expected to arrive knowing how to plan a
test approach or judge a risk; you are expected to bring curiosity, ask good
questions, and get comfortable raising a defect and following it through. Every
script you write under guidance and every defect you help track is building
the judgement that later levels take for granted. That makes this one of the
most important levels in the whole framework: it is where good testing habits
either take root or don't.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. Each one is a requirement of this role
level, described plainly, with a way to go deeper by asking an AI tool a
specific question rather than by following a link — the answers you get will
be better tailored to your own team's tools and ways of working than anything
a generic page could offer.

- [ ] **Understand what "quality" means, and why it's testable**

  Before you can test anything, you need a working idea of the quality
  characteristics that matter — is the thing correct, is it usable, does it
  hold up under load — and why each one is worth checking for. At this level
  you are expected to describe these characteristics and explain why they
  matter, not yet to judge trade-offs between them.

  Ask AI: "I'm new to software testing. Explain the main quality
  characteristics testers look for, such as correctness, usability and
  reliability, with a simple example of each."

- [ ] **See why testing needs a plan, even a simple one**

  Testing without a plan is just poking at things and hoping. This level asks
  you to explain the value of a quality testing approach and how it changes
  with different ways of delivering software, and to follow a plan someone
  else has set, with support.

  Ask AI: "Explain, for someone new to testing, why teams write a test plan or
  test strategy before they start testing, and what can go wrong without one."

- [ ] **Learn to design and run a simple test**

  This is the core of the job at this level: given guidance, design, build and
  run simple tests that match what users actually need, with support from
  someone more experienced. You are also expected to be able to explain, in
  plain terms, why automating some of this work is valuable.

  Ask AI: "Walk me through how to design a simple test case for a feature,
  step by step, and explain what makes a test case good rather than just
  technically correct."

- [ ] **Learn to raise, describe and follow up a defect**

  Finding a problem is only half the job — reporting it clearly, with the
  right information, following a defect process, and retesting once it is
  fixed, is the other half. At this level you are expected to follow an
  existing process rather than design one.

  Ask AI: "What information should a good defect report include so a developer
  can act on it without having to ask me questions first?"

- [ ] **Get comfortable with the tools and environments testing relies on**

  Tests run somewhere, using something. You are not expected to build test
  environments or automation frameworks yet, but you are expected to
  understand why they matter and to support maintaining them, with help.

  Ask AI: "Explain, for a beginner, why testers care about test environments
  and automated test tooling, and what 'supporting the maintenance of
  automated tests' might involve day to day."

- [ ] **Practise explaining technical things to non-technical people**

  You will work with people who do not think in test cases and defect IDs.
  This level expects you to explain why translating technical concepts into
  plain language matters, and to recognise how different stakeholders need to
  be communicated with differently.

  Ask AI: "Give me an example of a technical testing concept, and show me two
  ways to explain it — one to a developer and one to someone with no technical
  background."

You do not need to master all of this before you start. Ask your manager or a
more senior test engineer which of these your team leans on most, and start
there.
