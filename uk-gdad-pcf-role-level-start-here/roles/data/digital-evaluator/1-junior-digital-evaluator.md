# Junior digital evaluator - start here

Welcome. If you are starting out as a junior digital evaluator, this page
is for you: what the role is about, where to find everything else this
repository has for it, and a pathway for building the skills it asks for,
one step at a time.

## Purpose

A digital evaluator assesses the design, implementation and outcomes of a
digital product or service, and whether it delivers real value for the
public and society — working closely with delivery and policy teams,
developing theories of change, following government monitoring and
evaluation guidance, and communicating findings, including through
published reports. As a junior evaluator, this is an entry-level role: you
have an understanding of the role and show potential, supporting more
experienced evaluators, developing your skills through training, and
receiving guidance on how to produce good work.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. Each one is a requirement of this role
level, described plainly, with a way to go deeper by asking an AI tool a
specific question — the answers you get will be shaped around your own
organisation's evaluation work, which a generic guide never is.

- [ ] **Evaluate interventions with support**

  Evaluate interventions with support, using techniques including process
  evaluation, impact evaluation, theory of change, experimentation and
  economic evaluation, collect and analyse quantitative and qualitative
  data with support, and use some analytical approaches such as
  descriptive and inferential analysis.

  Ask AI: "I'm new to digital evaluation. Explain, simply, the difference
  between process evaluation and impact evaluation, with an example of
  each."

- [ ] **Describe how to translate stakeholder needs into evaluation questions**

  Describe how you might work with a stakeholder to understand what they
  need to evaluate and why, describe a process for translating those needs
  into research questions, and describe some methods for monitoring and
  evaluating a product or service.

  Ask AI: "What questions would help a junior evaluator understand what a
  stakeholder actually needs to know from an evaluation, beyond what they
  first ask for?"

- [ ] **Understand what a theory of change is for**

  Describe what a theory of change is, identify some appropriate
  stakeholders to involve in developing one, describe common issues when
  measuring performance indicators, and explain the difference between
  analysing digital performance and broader monitoring against strategic
  objectives.

  Ask AI: "Explain, simply, what a 'theory of change' is and why teams
  build one before evaluating a product or service."

- [ ] **Describe how evaluation needs change across the product life cycle**

  Describe how evaluation needs and activities vary at different phases of
  the product life cycle.

  Ask AI: "How does what an evaluator needs to check change between a
  product's early discovery phase and after it's live?"

- [ ] **Understand basic data quality issues, and how to check them**

  Describe some basic data issues and how to check that data and analysis
  look right, explain what it means for data to be fit for purpose in
  context, describe some methods for preparing data, and describe quality
  assurance processes.

  Ask AI: "What are common signs that a dataset might not be fit for
  purpose, that a junior evaluator should learn to spot?"

- [ ] **Understand why clearly communicating findings matters**

  Explain why it's important to communicate findings from analysis
  clearly, and explain some methods for doing that effectively.

  Ask AI: "What separates a clearly communicated evaluation finding from a
  technically accurate but confusing one?"

- [ ] **Understand why data ethics and privacy matter**

  Explain the importance of using data ethics and privacy in your work,
  and identify the right channels to discuss ethical issues, with support.

  Ask AI: "What's an example of a data ethics concern a junior digital
  evaluator might notice in their day-to-day work, and who should they
  raise it with?"

You do not need to master all of this before you start. Ask your manager or
a more senior evaluator which of these your team leans on most, and start
there.
