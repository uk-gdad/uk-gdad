# Associate performance analyst - start here

Welcome. If you are starting out as an associate performance analyst, this
page is for you: what the role is about, where to find everything else this
repository has for it, and a pathway for building the skills it asks for,
one step at a time. This is an entry-level role that asks for numeracy and
some experience in analysis or data handling, so you're not expected to
arrive knowing all of this already.

## Purpose

A performance analyst conducts analysis: developing performance measurement
frameworks — key performance indicators, goals, user needs and benefits —
and analysing how a service or product performs against them, adapting the
approach as things change. As an associate, you're learning to use
analytical tools and produce standardised reports and charts, starting on
projects with limited scope under guidance, replicating existing analysis
techniques before progressing to greater independence, building an
understanding of the wider analytics context, staying responsible for the
accuracy and quality of your data and analysis, and engaging with the
performance analysis community.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. Each one is a requirement of this role
level, described plainly, with a way to go deeper by asking an AI tool a
specific question — the answers you get will be shaped around your own
organisation's data, which a generic guide never is.

- [ ] **Apply core techniques for analysis and insight**

  Understand and work with colleagues to apply core techniques for
  analysis and insight, and present clear insights that colleagues can
  understand and use.

  Ask AI: "I'm new to performance analysis. Explain, simply, what makes an
  insight actually 'clear' to a colleague who wasn't involved in producing
  it."

- [ ] **Understand why performance measurement matters**

  Explain the importance of performance measurement and how it connects
  to business objectives.

  Ask AI: "Explain, for a beginner, what a key performance indicator
  actually measures and why it needs to connect to a real business
  objective."

- [ ] **Understand how performance measures vary across the life cycle**

  Describe the importance of performance measures, how they relate to
  business objectives, and how they vary at different stages of the
  product life cycle.

  Ask AI: "How would the performance measures for a brand new product
  differ from the ones used once it's been live for a year?"

- [ ] **Understand basic data quality issues, and how to check them**

  Describe some basic data issues and how to check that data and analysis
  look right, explain what it means for data to be fit for purpose, and
  describe some methods for preparing data and quality assurance
  processes.

  Ask AI: "What are common signs that a dataset might not be fit for
  purpose, that an associate performance analyst should learn to spot?"

- [ ] **Build awareness of analytical tools and digital technology**

  Show an awareness of analytical tools and technologies, using some of
  them, and show an awareness of digital technology and its impact.

  Ask AI: "What analytical tools and technologies would an associate
  performance analyst most benefit from getting comfortable with early
  on?"

- [ ] **Understand that everything is focused on the user**

  Show an awareness that everything you do is focused on the user of the
  product or service, even work that looks purely about numbers.

  Ask AI: "How does keeping the end user in mind change how an associate
  performance analyst interprets a set of numbers?"

- [ ] **Communicate analysis clearly, with support**

  Choose the right medium to communicate findings, shape communications
  for the audience, present analysis clearly for both specialist and
  non-specialist audiences with support, and give tactical recommendations.

  Ask AI: "What makes a chart or report genuinely clear to a
  non-specialist audience, rather than just accurate?"

You do not need to master all of this before you start. Ask your manager or
a more senior colleague which of these your team leans on most, and start
there.
