Welcome. If you are working toward the service desk analyst level, this
page is for you: what the role is about, where to find everything else
this repository has for it, and a pathway for building the skills it asks
for.

## Purpose

A service desk manager is responsible for managing the first- and
second-line technical support for all departmental IT applications and
services across sites, including end-user computing. As a service desk
analyst, you interact with customers directly: taking calls, trying to
resolve their queries, and knowing when to escalate an issue to your
manager rather than struggling on alone.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. Each one is a foundation for this role,
described plainly, with a way to go deeper by asking an AI tool a specific
question rather than searching for a generic guide.

- [ ] **Track and protect assets and components**

  Track, log and correct information to protect assets and components,
  keeping records accurate as things change.

  Ask AI: "Why does keeping asset information accurate matter for
  resolving a customer's call quickly?"

- [ ] **Resolve requests, and empathise with the user**

  Resolve user requests to at least the agreed service level agreement,
  empathise with the end user, and use customer-focused metrics to reach
  the desired outcome.

  Ask AI: "What's a simple way for a beginner to genuinely empathise with
  a frustrated caller, rather than just following a script?"

- [ ] **Produce reports, and interpret data for others**

  Produce relevant reports in the right format and agreed timeframe, work
  with stakeholders on changes to reporting processes, and interpret a
  data set to communicate it to others.

  Ask AI: "What makes a report about service desk calls genuinely useful
  to a stakeholder, rather than just a list of numbers?"

- [ ] **Own an issue until it's genuinely resolved**

  Own an issue until a new owner has been found, or the problem has been
  mitigated or resolved — rather than passing it on the moment it gets
  difficult.

  Ask AI: "What does 'owning an issue' mean in practice for a service desk
  analyst, when the actual fix might belong to someone else's team?"

- [ ] **Build awareness of the products and services you support**

  Show an awareness of different products and services, so you can place
  a call in context rather than treating each one in isolation.

  Ask AI: "Why would knowing about a product or service beyond the one
  call in front of you help a service desk analyst resolve it faster?"

- [ ] **Understand user experience analysis**

  Show an awareness or understanding of user experience analysis and its
  principles.

  Ask AI: "Explain, for a beginner, what 'user experience analysis' means
  for someone whose job is answering calls, not designing software."

- [ ] **Explain user stories, and why they focus on user needs**

  Explain the purpose of user stories and the focus on user needs.

  Ask AI: "Why do service desk teams write user stories at all, when most
  of the work is answering the phone and logging tickets?"

Ask your manager or a more senior colleague which of these your team relies on
most right now, and start there.
