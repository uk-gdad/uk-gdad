# Promoting this project

Where to tell people about the UK GDAD PCF, in the order worth working through.
The audience is the people the documents are written for: civil servants and
wider public sector staff in digital and data roles, the human resources and
people development colleagues who send them a skills gap form, and the people
deciding whether to join the profession at all.

Checked against the published sources in August 2026. Addresses and invite
links go stale; confirm before sending anything that matters.

## The constraint

The richest channels — cross-government Slack, and the mailing lists of the 28
cross-government communities — admit only people with a `.gov.uk` address or
another qualified public sector email. Contractors and commercial suppliers are
told to obtain one before joining.

Without one, those doors are shut. The way through them is a civil servant who
finds the project useful and posts it themselves, so **finding that person is
the highest-value thing in this document**. Everything in
[section 5](#5-gated-the-richest-rooms) waits on it, and
[section 2](#2-communities-open-to-anyone) is where you are most likely to meet
them.

## 1. Tell the framework's owners first

Before broadcasting anywhere. This project restates their published framework
under the Open Government Licence, and they will hear about it eventually.
Hearing it from you, with the attribution and the unofficial banner already in
place, is the difference between a collaborator and a complaint. They may
simply link to it.

| Who | Address | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Framework team, DSIT | `digitaldatacapabilityframework@dsit.gov.uk` | Owns the capability framework and answers questions about using it |
| GDD Profession Capability team, DSIT | `gdd-profession-capability-team@dsit.gov.uk` | The wider profession team, if the first does not reply |
| CDDO communications | `CDDO-Comms@digital.cabinet-office.gov.uk` | Runs profession comms and [the Digital People blog](https://digitalpeople.blog.gov.uk/); a route to a guest post rather than a link |

## 2. Communities open to anyone

No government email needed. This is what can be done without an advocate.

**[One Team Government](https://www.oneteamgov.uk/take-action)** — a global
public sector reform community, around 2,500 people on Slack, joined through an
open invite link. Invite links expire, so take a current one from that page.
Introduce yourself before posting anything.

**[UKGovCamp](https://www.ukgovcamp.com/)** — the annual unconference, plus a
year-round Slack at `ukgovcamp.slack.com` and organisers at
`organisers@ukgovcamp.com`. Pitching a session is stronger than posting a link,
because it puts you in a room of exactly this audience. Also on Bluesky as
`@ukgovcamp.bsky.social`.

**[LocalGov Digital](https://localgov.digital/)** — over 3,000 digital
practitioners in and around local government. The framework is used well beyond
Whitehall, and this audience is less well served by the official material.

**[Cross-Government Software Engineering Community](https://www.uk-x-gov-software-community.org.uk/)**
— a public site, a mailing list, a
[GitHub organisation](https://github.com/uk-x-gov-software-community/), and a
Lean Coffee on the fourth Thursday of each month. An open-source project is
on topic here in a way it is not everywhere.

**[Government Digital and Data Hub](https://governmentdigitalanddatahub.campaign.gov.uk/)**
— the cross-public-sector hub for digital and data careers, learning, and
community, which is precisely this audience. Newsletter signup is public; no
submission route is published, so ask through section 1.

**[x-govuk](https://x-govuk.org/)** — the community-run home for
cross-government open-source tooling and writing. Sympathetic to this kind of
side project, and its authors are well connected inside government.

## 3. Press and newsletters

A named editor beats a contact form.

**[UKAuthority](https://www.ukauthority.com/about/contact-us/)** — independent
public sector digital news, which asks for news that involves or affects public
sector organisations and the digital and data profession. That is this
project's exact description. Managing editor `mark@ukauthority.co.uk`,
publisher `helen@ukauthority.co.uk`.

**[PublicTechnology](https://www.publictechnology.net/)** — a daily newsletter
on public sector IT and digital, whose social account states that its direct
messages are open for news tips. Approach through `@PublicTech` rather than
hunting for an inbox.

**[Civil Service World](https://www.civilserviceworld.com/)** — read across the
whole service rather than the digital professions alone. A longer shot, but
"someone rebuilt the capability framework as 1,845 open documents" is a story.

## 4. Social, honestly ranked

**LinkedIn is the one that works.** It is where UK government digital talks
shop. Post from your own account, describing the problem the project solves,
and tag the profession page rather than expecting it to post for you:
`linkedin.com/company/government-digital-and-data` and
`linkedin.com/company/government-digital-service`.

**Bluesky and Mastodon are worth the time.** A large part of this community
moved and stayed. Smaller reach, a far higher proportion of the right people,
and links are not suppressed. The hashtags `#GovDesign` and `#GovContent` are
still in use.

**X reaches journalists, not the profession.** `@GDSTeam` and `@UKGOVdigijobs`
are one-way channels for their own announcements and will not amplify an
unofficial project. `@PublicTech` and `@UKAuthority` are worth following for
the press route above.

**Instagram and Facebook are dead ends here.** The Government Digital Service
runs `@gdsteam` on Instagram, but it is employer branding aimed at recruits,
not a route for a third-party tool, and there is no comparable Facebook
presence for this audience. Time is better spent on section 2.

## 5. Gated: the richest rooms

Every entry needs a `.gov.uk` address. Give an advocate the channel name and
two sentences and it costs them a minute.

**[Cross-government Slack](https://ukgovernmentdigital.slack.com/)** — the
single richest room, at `ukgovernmentdigital.slack.com`. This project spans all
eight professions, so it is on topic in many channels — `#software-development`,
`#design`, `#content`, `#user-research`, `#data-science`, `#weeknotes` and
others. Post once, in the few most relevant, not everywhere.

**[The 28 community mailing lists](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/communities)**
— every cross-government community in the Service Manual has one, and each
community page gives its own address. Four that are published:

| Community | Address |
| --- | --- |
| Design | `join-digital-service-designers@digital.cabinet-office.gov.uk` |
| User research | `join-digital-user-research@digital.cabinet-office.gov.uk` |
| Performance analysis | `performance-analysts@digital.cabinet-office.gov.uk` |
| Form building | `x-gov-forms@digital.cabinet-office.gov.uk` |

**Technical architecture** — an invitation-only email group for people working
on technology in central government, at
`gov-topic-technical-architecture-managers@digital.cabinet-office.gov.uk`. It
covers the seven architecture roles this project documents.

**[Cross-government data science community](https://datasciencecampus.ons.gov.uk/capability/cross-government-and-public-sector-data-science-community/)**
— run by the ONS Data Science Campus, with its own Slack at
`govdatascience.slack.com`, a newsletter, and a Knowledge Hub group. It covers
the nine data roles, and `government.data.science.community@ons.gov.uk` accepts
questions from outside.

## 6. Reddit

`r/TheCivilService` is the substantial UK civil service subreddit and the one
worth trying. `r/ukjobs` and `r/DevelopmentUK` are weaker fits.

**Read the rules before posting.** They could not be checked when this was
written, because Reddit blocks automated fetching, so treat everything here as
unverified. Expect strict self-promotion rules.

What passes on a subreddit like this is an account with real history in the
community, posting a genuinely free thing with no signup, no advertising, and
no tracking, described by what it does rather than as a launch. What gets
removed is a first-post link drop. If the account has no history there, this
channel is not worth forcing.

## What to say

**Lead with the thing, not the repository.** Almost nobody wants a link to a
git repository. They want the page for *their* role level: what the framework
expects, what to learn next, a continuing professional development checklist,
practice assessment material, and a skills gap form they can fill in and take
to a development conversation.

**Say three things every time.** It is unofficial and not a government service.
The role summaries are adapted from the framework under the Open Government
Licence. The derived documents are AI-assisted with human review. All three are
already on every page, so saying them up front costs nothing and buys trust.

**Name a concrete hook.** "Every role level has a skills gap form you can fill
in on screen and export" is a reason to click. "A community reference for the
GDAD PCF" is not.

## Norms

- **Everything on cross-government Slack is subject to Freedom of Information**,
  public or private, group or direct. Anyone posting on your behalf should know
  that.
- **Post once per community, then stay.** A link dropped into eight channels in
  an afternoon reads as spam. The same link, posted once by someone who answers
  questions afterwards, reads as a contribution.
- **Attribution is not optional.** The Open Government Licence requires it
  wherever the role summaries are republished, including in a social post that
  quotes one.
- **Never imply endorsement.** The site carries an unofficial banner for this
  reason, and the wording of any post should match it.
