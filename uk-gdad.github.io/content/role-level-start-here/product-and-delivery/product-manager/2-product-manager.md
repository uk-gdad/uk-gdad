# Product manager - start here

Welcome. If you are working toward the product manager level, this page is
for you: what the role expects, where to find everything else this
repository has for it, and a pathway for building the skills it asks for.
This can be an entry-level role for people moving into product management
from other digital roles, so don't expect every skill to feel familiar on
day one.

## Purpose

A product manager is responsible for product quality and value, working
with the team to identify, implement and improve strategies and tactics —
and might recruit and manage people in the product profession, making sure
people with the right skills are hired, supported and developed. You lead a
product through discovery, development, delivery and continuous
improvement to retirement, support lead and senior product managers, and
prioritise work while being able to measure outcomes. Every named skill
grows at this level, because you now own a whole product's trajectory, not
just a feature within one.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. Each one is a requirement of this role
level. Where you want to go deeper, ask an AI tool a specific question
rather than searching for a generic guide — you'll get an answer shaped
around your own product.

- [ ] **Investigate what adds value now, and what might in future**

  Work with your team to investigate and validate what adds value now and
  what could add value later, create a prioritised roadmap to discuss
  strategy with stakeholders, manage scope and deadlines under
  uncertainty, recommend a range of product management methods, and work
  in the open, sharing your work as you go.

  Ask AI: "How would a product manager validate that something will add
  value in future, when they can't test it directly yet?"

- [ ] **Lead a product through its life cycle, with some support**

  Manage the delivery of products and services that add value through
  different life cycle phases, with some support, identify whether and how
  a product should move to the next phase, and ensure it meets the right
  service standards.

  Ask AI: "What would tell a product manager their product is genuinely
  ready to move to the next life cycle phase, rather than just due to move
  by the calendar?"

- [ ] **Define outcomes, and be open about how you decide**

  Work with others to define the key performance indicators that assess
  performance, value and outcomes, analyse and use trusted data to make
  decisions and drive improvement, be open about your decision making, and
  explain success metrics to stakeholders.

  Ask AI: "What makes a key performance indicator genuinely useful for a
  product, as opposed to something that's easy to measure but doesn't
  reflect real success?"

- [ ] **Represent users in internal discussions**

  Use user insights to represent users in internal discussions, advocate
  for research with all types of user, use user needs to agree work and
  determine priorities with the team, and recommend the right tools to get
  appropriate insight.

  Ask AI: "How would a product manager represent users convincingly in a
  discussion where no user researcher is present?"

- [ ] **Weigh effort against value when prioritising**

  Estimate the value of each roadmap item, compare effort to value when
  deciding priorities, judge when reuse is appropriate, decide how and
  when to escalate risk to value, discuss estimates with stakeholders, and
  analyse the benefit of investment against anticipated outcomes.

  Ask AI: "How would a product manager compare two roadmap items with very
  different kinds of value — one that saves money, one that improves user
  experience — on some common basis?"

- [ ] **Own stakeholder understanding across your team**

  Work with your team to develop and maintain a shared understanding of
  stakeholders, develop and implement communications strategies, identify
  and resolve issues, influence stakeholders, and build long-term
  relationships.

  Ask AI: "What does a genuine stakeholder communications strategy contain
  for a product, beyond a list of who to update and how often?"

- [ ] **Develop and iterate your product's strategy**

  Engage and work with users, stakeholders and others to develop a
  strategy and vision, make sure it sets out the value, benefits and
  outcomes that justify investment, and review and iterate it regularly
  rather than treating it as fixed.

  Ask AI: "How often should a product strategy genuinely be revisited, and
  what would prompt a product manager to revise it sooner?"

- [ ] **Advocate for your product, and protect your team**

  Advocate for your product and a product-led approach, seek opportunities
  for reuse and learning, protect the team from unnecessary distractions
  or requests to change direction, consult and mediate with stakeholders,
  and participate in product management community activities.

  Ask AI: "What does 'protecting the team from unnecessary distractions'
  actually look like in practice, without cutting them off from useful
  stakeholder input?"

- [ ] **Choose and adapt Agile and Lean approaches deliberately**

  Compare and select the most appropriate delivery methods and processes,
  recognise and adapt when something isn't working, use a blended approach
  where it fits, and help teams measure, evaluate and visualise their
  outcomes.

  Ask AI: "How would a product manager decide a delivery approach isn't
  working, rather than assuming it just needs more time?"

Ask your manager or a more senior colleague which of these your team relies on
most right now, and start there.
