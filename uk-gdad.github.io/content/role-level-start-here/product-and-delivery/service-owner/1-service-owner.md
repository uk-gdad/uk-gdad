# Service owner - start here

Welcome. If you are working toward the service owner role, this page is for
you: what the role is about, where to find everything else this repository
has for it, and a pathway for building the areas it names. The framework
publishes this as a single level rather than a ladder, and its named skills
read at a genuinely senior register throughout — this is an accountable
leadership role, not an entry point into product and delivery.

## Purpose

A service owner is accountable for the quality, performance, benefits and
outcomes of a service, providing clear end-to-end direction, prioritisation,
risk management and decision making for teams in line with department,
policy and user outcomes. You define success and quality measures that drive
continuous improvement, inform and influence stakeholders to build the
consensus that lets work progress, and ensure the delivery of change that
actually realises your service's outcomes and benefits. As a leader, you
bring together a multidisciplinary team, advocate for your service and
teams, and build an open, trust-based culture — making sure a strategy,
appropriate governance and appropriate funding are all genuinely in place,
not just written down somewhere.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. At this level, an AI tool is most
useful as a sounding board for a genuine trade-off — describe the actual
decision you're weighing and ask what you might be missing.

- [ ] **Coach others in building and delivering strategy**

  Support and coach others in creating and implementing a long-term
  strategy and tactical approach that others genuinely agree with,
  influence and persuade stakeholders to support its delivery, support
  strategic decision making, and ensure strategic alignment across the
  organisation.

  Ask AI: "What's the difference between coaching someone to build their
  own strategy for a service and just handing them one you've already
  decided?"

- [ ] **Lead on performance and benefits, not just delivery**

  Make sure the right measures are in place to deliver valuable outcomes
  and agreed business benefits, use metrics to monitor, manage and
  prioritise delivery, and demonstrate the service's value by sharing
  effective data and insight with stakeholders.

  Ask AI: "What's the difference between a metric that looks good and one
  that genuinely demonstrates a service's value to a sceptical
  stakeholder?"

- [ ] **Own the financial sustainability of your service**

  Make sure a business case is in place for sustainable funding, identify
  measurable benefits that represent good value for money, allocate money
  across competing priorities, own supplier relationships to increase
  quality and value, and act as an escalation point for financial and
  commercial risk.

  Ask AI: "How would a service owner justify sustainable, ongoing funding
  for a service, rather than funding tied to a one-off project?"

- [ ] **Make sure governance is proportionate, and trusted**

  Ensure governance and assurance is appropriate and proportionate,
  continuously improved and aligned to organisational and government
  needs, guide teams through processes such as service assessments, and
  share your governance approach so stakeholders trust it to deliver value
  sustainably.

  Ask AI: "What does 'proportionate governance' actually mean for a
  service, as opposed to governance that's either too heavy or too light
  for the risk involved?"

- [ ] **Coordinate operations across disciplines**

  Make sure a scalable operating model is in place for a resilient,
  high-quality service, coordinate teams from operations, policy, digital
  and delivery to run and iterate it, make sure teams understand why
  efficient operations matter, identify and overcome operational
  constraints, and act as the escalation point for major operational
  issues.

  Ask AI: "What does coordinating operations, policy, digital and delivery
  teams together actually involve, when each of them reports somewhere
  different?"

- [ ] **Guide your service through the entire life cycle**

  Work and consult with the right people at the right time to move
  through the life cycle and deliver value, use evidence to decide whether
  a team should continue, change direction or stop, identify the tools and
  techniques each phase needs, and guide colleagues and stakeholders
  through it.

  Ask AI: "What evidence would genuinely justify stopping a service, as
  opposed to evidence that just makes stopping feel justified?"

- [ ] **Direct stakeholder strategy, and negotiate at senior level**

  Direct the stakeholder relationship strategy for your teams, make sure
  stakeholder objectives are set and supported, and influence and
  negotiate with senior stakeholders to resolve issues and enable
  progress.

  Ask AI: "What negotiation approach works best with a senior stakeholder
  who has the authority to block a service decision but no obligation to
  compromise?"

- [ ] **Champion user-centred practice across the organisation**

  Advocate for a range of effective research approaches across the
  organisation, coach others in making decisions that meet user needs
  across every channel, advocate for the continuous use of user insight,
  and use it to make strategic decisions about user experience.

  Ask AI: "How would a service owner make user-centred practice something
  every team involved in the service does by habit, rather than something
  they're reminded to do?"

- [ ] **Set and evolve delivery methodology standards for your service**

  Set and iterate standards for working within different delivery
  methodologies, promote collaboration so the team delivers value at the
  right time, experiment with new and innovative ways of working, and
  research emerging methodologies to work out how your service would fit
  within them.

  Ask AI: "How would a service owner evaluate whether a new delivery
  methodology is worth experimenting with for their service, rather than
  adopting it because it's fashionable?"

Ask a peer service owner, in your organisation or another part of
government, which of these has taken them longest to get right — that
conversation will teach you more than any general reading on service
ownership.
