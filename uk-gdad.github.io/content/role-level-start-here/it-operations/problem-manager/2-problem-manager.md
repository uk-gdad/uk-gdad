# Problem manager - start here

Welcome. If you are working toward the problem manager level, this page is
for you: what the role expects, where to find everything else this
repository has for it, and a pathway for building the areas it names.

## Purpose

A problem manager picks up feeds from numerous sources, such as the service
desk, with the aim of putting in place continual improvements that stop
incidents such as regular service outages recurring — resolving emerging
and recurring problems, performing root cause analysis, and working
closely with major incident managers. Most named skills grow at this
level, because owning continual improvement across the sources feeding
into it asks for more than maintaining a system that's already running.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. There is no level above this one to
measure yourself against, so use an AI tool as a sounding board: describe a
real problem-management decision you're weighing and ask it to argue the
case you haven't made yet.

- [ ] **Analyse the impact of change before it happens**

  Analyse and assess the impact of change, document change requests, and
  action changes from approved requests.

  Ask AI: "What should a problem manager look for when assessing the
  potential impact of a change made in response to a recurring problem,
  beyond whether it technically works?"

- [ ] **Build genuine collaboration across your team**

  Work collaboratively, actively networking with others, adapt feedback so
  it's effective and lasting, use team health checks to spot dynamic
  issues, and help stimulate the right response to them.

  Ask AI: "What makes feedback 'effective and lasting' rather than
  something a colleague hears once and forgets?"

- [ ] **Manage runbooks, and test service continuity**

  Manage the runbooks and processes around service continuity, and test
  those runbooks to make sure service availability is genuinely
  maintained, not just documented.

  Ask AI: "What's the difference between a runbook that reads well and one
  that's actually been tested to work during a real outage?"

- [ ] **Lead the investigation and resolution of incidents**

  Lead the investigation and resolution of incidents, rather than
  diagnosing and resolving them individually.

  Ask AI: "What does leading an incident investigation involve, that
  diagnosing and resolving one individually doesn't?"

- [ ] **Take accountability, and search proactively for problems**

  Take accountability for issues that occur, be proactive in searching for
  potential problems before they surface, and achieve excellent user
  outcomes.

  Ask AI: "What does 'proactively searching for potential problems' look
  like in practice, rather than waiting for the next recurring incident to
  reveal one?"

- [ ] **Coordinate the team's response to problems, not just solve them**

  Ensure that the right actions are taken to investigate, resolve and
  anticipate problems, and co-ordinate the team to investigate problems,
  implement solutions and take preventive measures.

  Ask AI: "What does coordinating a team's response to a recurring problem
  involve, beyond diagnosing and fixing it yourself?"

- [ ] **See the bigger picture across groups of services**

  Take groups of services and investigate how to get the best out of the
  underlying services that support them, rather than optimising one
  service at a time.

  Ask AI: "What would a problem manager look for across a group of
  related services that wouldn't be visible looking at any one service in
  isolation?"

- [ ] **Keep tracking and protecting assets and components**

  This skill's wording plateaus here, unchanged from problem analyst
  level: tracking, logging and correcting information to protect assets
  and components.

  Ask AI: "Given this skill's description hasn't changed since the level
  below, what would genuinely problem-manager-level asset management
  judgement add beyond what's written here?"

- [ ] **Keep managing service components against business needs**

  This skill also plateaus: managing service components to make sure they
  meet business needs and key performance indicators.

  Ask AI: "What would setting availability and capacity standards across a
  team add, beyond meeting them well yourself?"

- [ ] **Keep building service management framework knowledge**

  This skill's wording plateaus too: holding a Level 3 service management
  framework qualification.

  Ask AI: "What would deepening service management framework knowledge,
  beyond the base qualification, look like for a problem manager?"

- [ ] **Keep turning user evidence into decisions**

  This skill also plateaus: collating user needs evidence, defining
  fitting research, and using quantitative and qualitative data to turn
  user focus into outcomes.

  Ask AI: "How would a problem manager balance user needs against
  technical urgency when deciding which recurring problem to tackle
  first?"

There is no resource list at this level that will tell you what to do next —
the accountability is yours. Ask peers in equivalent roles at other
organisations how they made their hardest recent call, and use it to
stress-test your own thinking rather than to copy their answer.
