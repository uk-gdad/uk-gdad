# IT service analyst - start here

Welcome. If you are working toward the IT service analyst level, this
page is for you: what the role is about, where to find everything else
this repository has for it, and a pathway for building the skills it asks
for.

## Purpose

IT service managers are responsible for managing the service delivery of
information and communications technology services, working with teams
from IT service operations. As an IT service analyst, you provide
frontline technical support to end users on issues relating to hardware,
software and peripherals, responding to, documenting and resolving
service desk incident tickets in a timely manner according to a service
level agreement — diagnosing complex problems, performing root cause
analysis, and developing checklists so the next analyst does not have to
start from nothing.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. Each one is a foundation for this role,
described plainly, with a way to go deeper by asking an AI tool a specific
question rather than searching for a generic guide.

- [ ] **Track and protect assets and components**

  Track, log and correct information to protect assets and components,
  keeping records accurate as things change.

  Ask AI: "Why does keeping asset information accurate matter for
  responding to service desk tickets quickly?"

- [ ] **Investigate problems, and contribute to solutions**

  Investigate problems and opportunities in existing processes, and
  contribute to recommending solutions.

  Ask AI: "What's the difference between fixing one broken ticket and
  spotting a process problem behind several tickets that look similar?"

- [ ] **Produce reports, and interpret data for others**

  Produce relevant reports in the right format and agreed timeframe, work
  with stakeholders on changes to reporting processes, and interpret a
  data set to communicate it to others.

  Ask AI: "What makes a report about service desk tickets genuinely useful
  to a stakeholder, rather than just a list of numbers?"

- [ ] **Own an issue until it's genuinely resolved**

  Own an issue until a new owner has been found, or the problem has been
  mitigated or resolved — rather than passing it on the moment it gets
  difficult.

  Ask AI: "What does 'owning an issue' mean in practice for a service
  analyst, when the actual fix belongs to someone else's team?"

- [ ] **Build awareness of the products and services you support**

  Show an awareness of different products and services, so you can place
  a ticket in context rather than treating each one in isolation.

  Ask AI: "Why would knowing about a product or service beyond the one
  ticket in front of you help a service analyst resolve it faster?"

- [ ] **Build service management framework knowledge**

  Work towards a Level 3 service management framework qualification, so
  your day-to-day practice is grounded in a recognised standard.

  Ask AI: "Explain, for a beginner, what a service management framework
  qualification actually covers, and why organisations value it."

- [ ] **Identify stakeholders, and communicate with them clearly**

  Identify important stakeholders and communicate with them clearly and
  regularly, tailoring communication to their needs, building and
  reaching consensus, and using evidence to explain decisions made.

  Ask AI: "What's a simple way for a beginner to work out who the
  important stakeholders are for a service desk ticket?"

- [ ] **Understand strategic context, and contribute to policy**

  Work within a strategic context, communicate how your activities meet
  strategic goals, and contribute to the development of strategy and
  policies.

  Ask AI: "Explain, simply, what it means to work 'within a strategic
  context' for someone whose day-to-day work is resolving individual
  tickets?"

- [ ] **Turn user evidence into decisions**

  Identify and engage with users or stakeholders to collate evidence of
  their needs, understand and define research that actually fits those
  needs, and use both quantitative and qualitative data to turn user
  focus into real outcomes.

  Ask AI: "How would an IT service analyst use user evidence to decide how
  to prioritise several competing tickets?"

Ask your manager or a more senior colleague which of these your team relies on
most right now, and start there.
