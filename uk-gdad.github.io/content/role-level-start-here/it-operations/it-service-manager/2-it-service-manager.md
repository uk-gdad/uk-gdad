# IT service manager - start here

Welcome. If you are working toward the IT service manager level, this
page is for you: what the role expects, where to find everything else this
repository has for it, and a pathway for building the skills it asks for.

## Purpose

An IT service manager is responsible for defining service level agreements
and operational level agreements across all relevant business functions,
managing a fully functioning multi-sourced service support team with a
customer-centric approach across multiple locations, and making sure the
service catalogue, reporting and continual service improvement are all in
place. Asset and configuration management, business analysis, service
focus and service management framework knowledge all grow at this level,
because defining and managing agreements across a team asks for more than
supporting frontline tickets under one.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. Each one is a requirement of this role
level, described plainly, with a way to go deeper by asking an AI tool a
specific question rather than searching for a generic guide.

- [ ] **Maintain secure configuration, and track IT assets**

  Maintain secure configuration and accurate information, control IT
  assets in one or more areas, and verify their location and state.

  Ask AI: "What's the difference between tracking asset information as an
  analyst and being responsible for controlling IT assets across an
  area?"

- [ ] **Work with stakeholders on objectives and benefits**

  Investigate problems and opportunities in existing processes, contribute
  to recommending solutions, and work with stakeholders to identify
  objectives and potential benefits.

  Ask AI: "What does 'identifying potential benefits' with a stakeholder
  add, beyond recommending a solution to a process problem?"

- [ ] **See the bigger picture across groups of services**

  See the bigger picture by taking groups of services and investigating
  how to get the best out of the underlying services that support them,
  rather than being aware of individual products in isolation.

  Ask AI: "What would an IT service manager look for across a group of
  related services that wouldn't be visible looking at any one service in
  isolation?"

- [ ] **Build ITIL knowledge alongside your qualification**

  Hold a Level 3 service management framework qualification, and
  demonstrate knowledge of the life cycle or capability elements of ITIL
  (Information Technology Infrastructure Library).

  Ask AI: "Explain, for someone who already has a service management
  qualification, what ITIL's life cycle and capability elements add on
  top of it."

- [ ] **Keep producing reports, and interpreting data for others**

  This skill's wording plateaus here, unchanged from IT service analyst
  level: producing relevant reports, discussing reporting process changes
  with stakeholders, and interpreting data.

  Ask AI: "What would setting reporting standards across a support team
  add, beyond producing good reports yourself?"

- [ ] **Keep owning an issue until it's genuinely resolved**

  This skill also plateaus: owning an issue until a new owner is found or
  it's mitigated or resolved.

  Ask AI: "What would taking accountability for an issue, rather than
  just owning it until resolved, look like at the next level?"

- [ ] **Keep identifying stakeholders, and communicating with them**

  This skill's wording plateaus too: identifying important stakeholders,
  tailoring communication to their needs, building and reaching consensus,
  and using evidence to explain decisions.

  Ask AI: "What would negotiating and influencing stakeholders, rather
  than communicating well with them, look like at the next level?"

- [ ] **Keep working within a strategic context**

  This skill also plateaus: working within a strategic context,
  communicating how activities meet strategic goals, and contributing to
  strategy and policy development.

  Ask AI: "What would defining strategy and policy, rather than
  contributing to it, look like for an IT service manager?"

- [ ] **Keep turning user evidence into decisions**

  This skill's wording plateaus too: collating user needs evidence,
  defining fitting research, and using quantitative and qualitative data
  to turn user focus into outcomes.

  Ask AI: "What would leading user-focused decisions across a whole
  service, rather than one team's tickets, look like at this level?"

Ask a senior IT service manager which of these your team relies on most
right now, and start there.
