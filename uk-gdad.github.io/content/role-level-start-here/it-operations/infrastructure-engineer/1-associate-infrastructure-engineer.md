# Associate infrastructure engineer - start here

Welcome. If you are working toward the associate infrastructure engineer
level, this page is for you: what the role is about, where to find
everything else this repository has for it, and a pathway for building the
skills it asks for.

## Purpose

An infrastructure engineer designs, builds, manages and supports the
infrastructure services that underpin all internal user services and
services to the public. As a trainee, you work in an established team and
support infrastructure engineers in their daily work, under their
direction — the foundations of computing, storage, networking, physical
infrastructure and cloud computing, and how a team resolves the problems
that arise across them.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. Each one is a foundation for this role,
described plainly, with a way to go deeper by asking an AI tool a specific
question rather than searching for a generic guide.

- [ ] **Support the infrastructure that underpins everything else**

  Support infrastructure solutions and services, including computing,
  storage, networking, physical infrastructure, software, and
  commercial-off-the-shelf and open source packages and solutions.

  Ask AI: "I'm new to infrastructure engineering. Explain, simply, what
  'infrastructure' actually includes, and how the pieces fit together to
  support a public-facing service."

- [ ] **Understand virtual and cloud computing**

  Support virtual and cloud computing, including Infrastructure as a
  Service, Platform as a Service and Software as a Service.

  Ask AI: "Explain, for a beginner, the difference between Infrastructure as
  a Service, Platform as a Service and Software as a Service, with an
  example of each."

- [ ] **Explain information security controls**

  Explain information security and the security controls available to
  protect solutions and services.

  Ask AI: "What are the main categories of security control used to protect
  IT infrastructure, and how would a beginner start to recognise them in a
  real system?"

- [ ] **Explain why modern development standards matter**

  Explain the importance of using modern development standards.

  Ask AI: "Why do infrastructure teams care about modern development
  standards, when most of the work isn't writing application code?"

- [ ] **Assist with designing components of larger systems**

  Assist as part of a team in the design of components of larger systems.

  Ask AI: "What does it mean to design a 'component' of a larger system,
  rather than the whole system, and why is that a sensible way to split the
  work?"

- [ ] **Understand systems integration, and its challenges**

  Explain the process and principles of integrating systems, and describe
  the challenges of designing, building and testing interfaces between
  systems.

  Ask AI: "Explain, simply, what 'systems integration' means, and why
  testing the interface between two systems is often harder than testing
  either system alone."

- [ ] **Build basic troubleshooting capability**

  Show an awareness of different technology capabilities, and demonstrate
  basic troubleshooting capability.

  Ask AI: "What's a simple, structured way for a beginner to start
  troubleshooting an infrastructure problem, rather than guessing at
  fixes?"

- [ ] **Investigate problems, and know their level**

  Investigate problems in systems, processes and services, understanding
  the level of a problem — for example, strategic, tactical or
  operational — and contribute to remedies and preventative measures.

  Ask AI: "What's the difference between a strategic, a tactical and an
  operational problem in IT infrastructure, and why does knowing which is
  which change how you respond?"

- [ ] **Show awareness of problem resolution, and pass problems on**

  Show an awareness of problem resolution processes, and pass problems on
  to your team when they're beyond what you can resolve yourself.

  Ask AI: "How does a trainee know when to keep trying to solve a problem
  and when to hand it to someone more experienced?"

Ask your manager or a more senior colleague which of these your team relies on
most right now, and start there.
