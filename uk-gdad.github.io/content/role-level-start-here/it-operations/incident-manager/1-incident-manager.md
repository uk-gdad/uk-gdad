# Incident manager - start here

Welcome. If you are working toward the incident manager level, this page is
for you: what the role is about, where to find everything else this
repository has for it, and a pathway for building the skills it asks for.

## Purpose

Incident managers aim to restore normal service operation as quickly as
possible, minimising any adverse effects on business operations, so the
best possible levels of service quality and availability are returned and
maintained. At this level, you oversee incidents and restore normal
operations quickly, with the least impact on the business or the user:
working on-site to maintain hardware and fix technical problems, providing
first-time resolution by troubleshooting and diagnosing or escalating
faults to major incident managers and problem managers, and coaching
apprentices while sharing knowledge with your team. In some departments
your responsibilities may mirror those of an end user computing engineer.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. Each one is a requirement of this role
level, described plainly, with a way to go deeper by asking an AI tool a
specific question — the answers you get will be shaped around your own
organisation's incidents, which a generic guide never is.

- [ ] **Diagnose incidents, and find their resolution**

  Diagnose and prioritise incidents, investigate their causes, and find
  resolutions rather than just recording and routing them.

  Ask AI: "I'm new to incident management. Explain, simply, a structured
  way to diagnose the root cause of an incident, rather than fixing the
  first plausible symptom."

- [ ] **Implement changes under supervision**

  Implement change requests, and apply change control procedures under
  supervision.

  Ask AI: "Why do organisations use a formal change control procedure
  instead of just making a fix when it's needed?"

- [ ] **Understand the basics of IT continuity management**

  Explain the basic principles of IT standard continuity management
  processes and procedures.

  Ask AI: "Explain, for a beginner, what 'IT continuity management'
  means, and why it's treated separately from incident management."

- [ ] **Understand availability and capacity management**

  Explain availability and capacity management processes — why a service
  needs to stay up, and why "up" isn't enough if it can't handle the load
  it's given.

  Ask AI: "Explain, simply, what 'availability' and 'capacity' mean for an
  IT service, and why they're treated as separate concerns."

- [ ] **Own an issue until it's genuinely resolved**

  Own an issue until a new owner has been found, or the problem has been
  mitigated or resolved — rather than passing it on the moment it gets
  difficult.

  Ask AI: "What does 'owning an issue' mean in practice for an incident
  manager, when the actual fix belongs to someone else's team?"

- [ ] **Turn user evidence into decisions**

  Identify and engage with users or stakeholders to collate evidence of
  their needs, understand and define research that actually fits those
  needs, and use both quantitative and qualitative data to turn user
  focus into real outcomes.

  Ask AI: "How would an incident manager use user evidence to decide how
  to prioritise several competing incidents?"

- [ ] **Build coherent frameworks from scattered inputs**

  Take inputs and establish coherent frameworks that work, rather than
  leaving incident information scattered across different sources.

  Ask AI: "What does 'establishing a coherent framework' mean in practice
  for someone managing incidents across several services?"

- [ ] **Understand your team's dynamics and the value of feedback**

  Contribute to the work of others, help motivate and empower the team,
  help create the right environment for the team to work in, and
  recognise and deal with issues.

  Ask AI: "What does good team collaboration actually look like day to
  day during an incident response, beyond just being friendly to
  colleagues?"

- [ ] **Investigate patterns behind problems, and consult specialists**

  Initiate and monitor actions to investigate patterns and trends behind
  problems, consult specialists where needed, determine the right
  resolution and help implement it, and identify preventative measures.

  Ask AI: "What's the difference between fixing a problem and preventing
  the same class of problem from recurring, and how would an incident
  manager approach the second?"

- [ ] **Track and protect assets and components**

  Track, log and correct information to protect assets and components,
  keeping records accurate as things change.

  Ask AI: "Why does keeping asset information accurate matter for
  responding to incidents quickly?"

- [ ] **Build service management framework knowledge**

  Work towards a Level 3 service management framework qualification, so
  your day-to-day practice is grounded in a recognised standard.

  Ask AI: "Explain, for a beginner, what a service management framework
  qualification actually covers, and why organisations value it."

Ask your manager or a more senior colleague which of these your team relies on
most right now, and start there.
