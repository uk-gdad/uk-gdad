# Operational control manager - start here

Welcome. If you are working toward the operational control manager level,
this page is for you: what the role expects, where to find everything else
this repository has for it, and a pathway for building the skills it asks
for.

## Purpose

An operational control manager is accountable for day-to-day IT
operations, acting as the escalation point for incidents and problems. You
take responsibility for effective change management processes that keep
services available, using proactive and preventative measures, stay on
call 24/7 or as business requirements demand, and are allowed to address
issues across the whole of IT operations. Most named skills grow at this
level, because being the escalation point asks for real ownership, not
just competent handling of what reaches you.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. At this level, an AI tool is often
most useful for pressure-testing an escalation decision — describe the
actual situation and ask what you might be missing.

- [ ] **Lead the investigation and resolution of incidents**

  Lead the investigation and resolution of incidents, rather than
  diagnosing and resolving them individually.

  Ask AI: "What does leading an incident investigation involve, that
  diagnosing and resolving one individually doesn't?"

- [ ] **Manage complex change, and enforce release discipline**

  Manage high-impact, complex change requests, and make sure release
  policies, procedures and processes are actually applied.

  Ask AI: "What makes a change request genuinely high-impact, and how
  should that change how an operational control manager handles it
  compared with a routine one?"

- [ ] **Manage runbooks, and test service continuity**

  Manage the runbooks and processes around service continuity, and test
  those runbooks to make sure service availability is genuinely
  maintained, not just documented.

  Ask AI: "What's the difference between a runbook that reads well and one
  that's actually been tested to work under real conditions?"

- [ ] **Take accountability, and search proactively for problems**

  Take accountability for issues that occur, be proactive in searching for
  potential problems before they surface, and achieve excellent user
  outcomes.

  Ask AI: "What does 'proactively searching for potential problems' look
  like in practice, rather than waiting for an incident to reveal one?"

- [ ] **Lead on user focus, not just gather it**

  Collaborate with user researchers and represent users internally,
  explain the difference between user needs and desires, champion user
  research so it covers all users, and guide others in understanding the
  user story.

  Ask AI: "What's the practical difference between what users need and
  what users say they want, and how would an operational control manager
  explain that difference to a colleague who's confusing the two?"

- [ ] **See the bigger picture across groups of services**

  Take groups of services and investigate how to get the best out of the
  underlying services that support them, rather than optimising one
  service at a time.

  Ask AI: "What would an operational control manager look for across a
  group of related services that wouldn't be visible looking at any one
  service in isolation?"

- [ ] **Build genuine collaboration across your team**

  Work collaboratively, actively networking with others, adapt feedback so
  it's effective and lasting, use team health checks to spot dynamic
  issues, and help stimulate the right response to them.

  Ask AI: "What makes feedback 'effective and lasting' rather than
  something a colleague hears once and forgets?"

- [ ] **Build ITIL knowledge alongside your qualification**

  Hold a Level 3 service management framework qualification, and
  demonstrate knowledge of the life cycle or capability elements of ITIL
  (Information Technology Infrastructure Library).

  Ask AI: "Explain, for someone who already has a service management
  qualification, what ITIL's life cycle and capability elements add on
  top of it."

- [ ] **Keep configuration secure, and IT assets accounted for**

  This skill's wording plateaus here, unchanged from senior operations
  analyst level: maintaining secure configuration, controlling IT assets,
  and verifying their location and state.

  Ask AI: "Given this skill's description hasn't changed since the level
  below, what would genuinely operational-control-level asset management
  judgement add beyond what's written here?"

- [ ] **Keep managing service components against business needs**

  This skill also plateaus: managing service components to meet business
  needs and key performance indicators.

  Ask AI: "What would owning availability and capacity across IT
  operations add, beyond managing components well for one service?"

- [ ] **Keep investigating patterns behind problems**

  This skill's wording plateaus too: investigating patterns and trends,
  consulting specialists, and determining preventative measures.

  Ask AI: "What would leading problem management strategy across IT
  operations add, beyond investigating patterns well yourself?"

Ask a head of command and control which of these matters most in your
organisation right now, and start there.
