# Business relationship manager - start here

Welcome. If you are working toward the business relationship manager
level, this page is for you: what the role is about, where to find
everything else this repository has for it, and a pathway for building the
skills it asks for.

## Purpose

A business relationship manager — sometimes known as a "business partner" —
establishes and maintains meaningful relationships with colleagues to
deliver positive impact across the organisation: bringing together
different functions to ensure value, working strategically across teams so
digital and data investment pays off, exploring the perspectives and
ambitions of teams to shape a pipeline of opportunities, and setting the
conditions for a culture of genuine connection. At this level, you're
responsible for ensuring consistency in engagement across the business:
preparing documentation and getting support to progress new ideas and
investments, using the right systems to maintain a view of activity and
business knowledge, and supporting continuous improvement that matures your
team's capability.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. Each one is a requirement of this role
level, described plainly, with a way to go deeper by asking an AI tool a
specific question — the answers you get will be shaped around your own
organisation, which a generic guide never is.

- [ ] **Investigate problems and opportunities, and recommend solutions**

  Investigate problems and opportunities in existing processes, contribute
  to recommending solutions, and work with stakeholders to identify
  objectives and potential benefits.

  Ask AI: "I'm new to business relationship management. Explain, simply,
  the difference between investigating a problem and investigating an
  opportunity, and why a business relationship manager needs to do both."

- [ ] **Negotiate with stakeholders, and remove blockers**

  Negotiate with and influence stakeholders, manage relationships
  effectively, deal with challenging situations and remove blockers, and
  build long-term strategic relationships that support mutual needs while
  focusing on users.

  Ask AI: "How would a business relationship manager negotiate with a
  stakeholder who wants something that doesn't serve the wider
  organisation's interests?"

- [ ] **Connect your work to strategic goals, and contribute to strategy**

  Work within a strategic context and communicate how your activities meet
  strategic goals, and start contributing to developing strategy and
  policy rather than only implementing it.

  Ask AI: "What would contributing to strategy development actually
  involve for a business relationship manager, as distinct from a policy
  team's role?"

- [ ] **Turn user evidence into decisions**

  Identify and engage with users or stakeholders to collate evidence of
  their needs, understand and define research that actually fits those
  needs, and use both quantitative and qualitative data to turn user
  focus into real outcomes.

  Ask AI: "How would a business relationship manager gather enough
  evidence from users to make the case for an investment, without
  overstepping into a dedicated researcher's role?"

- [ ] **Own an issue until it's genuinely resolved**

  Own an issue until a new owner has been found, or the problem has been
  mitigated or resolved — rather than passing it on the moment it gets
  difficult.

  Ask AI: "What does 'owning an issue' mean in practice for a business
  relationship manager, when the actual fix belongs to someone else's
  team?"

- [ ] **Build coherent frameworks from scattered inputs**

  Take inputs and establish coherent frameworks that work, rather than
  leaving information scattered across different sources.

  Ask AI: "What does 'establishing a coherent framework' mean in practice
  for someone managing relationships across several teams?"

- [ ] **Build service management framework knowledge**

  Work towards a Level 3 service management framework qualification, so
  your day-to-day practice is grounded in a recognised standard.

  Ask AI: "Explain, for a beginner, what a service management framework
  qualification actually covers, and why it matters for someone managing
  business relationships rather than delivering technical services
  directly."

Ask your manager or a more senior colleague which of these your team relies on
most right now, and start there.
