Welcome. If you are working toward the content strategist role, this page
is for you: what the role is about, where to find everything else this
repository has for it, and a pathway for building the areas it names. The
framework publishes this as a single level rather than a ladder, and its
named skills read at a genuinely expert register throughout — this is not
a role most people arrive at without content design experience already
behind them.

## Purpose

A content strategist takes an overview of content and how it's produced,
designing governance, workflows, and taxonomies in a user-centred context
and working with senior stakeholders, user researchers, and content,
service and interaction designers. As an expert practitioner in content
strategy and taxonomy design, you develop governance, processes and
workflows that deliver strategy and policy intent, manage relationships and
interdependencies with other content roles, shape and structure content
architecture on user-centred design principles, and work with lead content
designers to organise and manage teams towards outcomes.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. At this level, an AI tool is most
useful as a sounding board for a governance or architecture decision —
describe the actual trade-off you're weighing and ask what you might be
missing.

- [ ] **Assure content quality across teams, and coach junior colleagues**

  Show a deep understanding of end-to-end journeys and how content is
  affected within them, identify where fixes are needed, take
  responsibility for assuring content quality from junior colleagues,
  coach and guide them, ensure content is regularly reviewed, and deliver
  through others by directing and critiquing their work.

  Ask AI: "What should a content strategist look for when assuring
  content quality across several teams at once, rather than reviewing one
  piece at a time?"

- [ ] **Lead strategy design, and direct its evaluation**

  Lead the design and implementation of strategy, directing the
  evaluation of strategies and policies to make sure business requirements
  are being met.

  Ask AI: "How would a content strategist evaluate whether a content
  strategy is genuinely meeting business requirements, rather than just
  being followed?"

- [ ] **Direct stakeholder strategy, and negotiate at senior level**

  Direct the stakeholder relationship strategy for your teams, make sure
  stakeholder objectives are set and supported, and influence and
  negotiate with senior stakeholders to resolve issues and enable
  progress.

  Ask AI: "What negotiation approach works best with a senior stakeholder
  who has the authority to block a content governance decision but no
  obligation to compromise?"

- [ ] **Set direction on user needs across channels**

  Give direction on which tools or methods to use, demonstrate experience
  meeting user needs across a variety of channels, bring insight into how
  user needs have changed over time, and apply strategic thinking to
  provide the best service for the end user.

  Ask AI: "How would a content strategist use insight about how user needs
  have changed to influence organisational content architecture?"

- [ ] **Help teams choose the best delivery approach**

  Identify and compare the best processes or delivery methods, including
  measuring and evaluating outcomes, help the team decide the best
  approach, and help teams manage and visualise outcomes while adhering to
  agreed scope and priorities.

  Ask AI: "How would a content strategist compare two plausible delivery
  approaches for a piece of governance work, beyond just picking the
  familiar one?"

- [ ] **Understand when prototyping earns its place**

  Understand when to use a specific prototyping technique or method, and
  show your team the value of prototyping.

  Ask AI: "When would prototyping a governance workflow or taxonomy be
  worth the time, rather than documenting the design directly?"

Ask a peer content strategist, in your organisation or another part of
government, which of these has taken them longest to get right — that
conversation will teach you more than any general reading on content
strategy.
