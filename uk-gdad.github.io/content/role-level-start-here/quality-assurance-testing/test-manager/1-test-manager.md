# Test manager - start here

Welcome. If you are working toward the test manager level, this page is for
you: what the role expects, where to find everything else this repository has
for it, and a pathway for building the skills it asks for.

## Purpose

A test manager owns the quality assurance and testing strategy for their area
as part of a broader risk-based approach — and, at this level, owns delivery
too: setting the strategy and leading its implementation. That means taking
responsibility for test improvement and optimisation, for the people doing the
work — talent, succession planning, supplier management — and for the
day-to-day decisions that keep testing aligned with the realities of the
delivery environment it sits in, not an idealised one. You may also find
yourself promoting and advocating for test capability, inside your
organisation and occasionally beyond it. The shift from a senior technical
role is real: your decisions now shape what other people are able to do, more
than what you do yourself.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. Each one is a requirement of this role
level. At this level, an AI tool is most useful for pressure-testing a
decision you're about to make about people or strategy — describe the actual
situation and ask what you might be missing.

- [ ] **Own quality testing strategy, not just quality testing**

  Create and manage multiple quality testing plans, approaches and
  strategies, lead and guide multiple teams in adopting them, advocate for
  quality testing to be involved early in delivery, and guide teams in
  optimising their approach using real data rather than habit.

  Ask AI: "What separates a test strategy that actually gets used from one
  that sits in a document nobody reads after the first week?"

- [ ] **Judge risk and value across a whole area of delivery**

  Lead work with stakeholders across teams to determine which functional and
  non-functional quality characteristics genuinely add value, judge when an
  approach needs to change based on effort and risk, ensure test needs are
  addressed early, and use multiple techniques to analyse complex,
  ambiguous information.

  Ask AI: "How would a test manager decide where to focus limited testing
  effort across several competing pieces of delivery work?"

- [ ] **Set the standards other people design and run tests against**

  Influence and guide which test types and techniques get used to catch risk
  early, lead others through designing, building and running tests,
  contribute to and implement standards, and keep improving them through a
  structured process rather than one-off exceptions.

  Ask AI: "What makes a testing standard something teams actually follow,
  versus something they quietly work around?"

- [ ] **Turn defect and risk patterns into decisions**

  Lead and coach others in improving test and defect management processes,
  support colleagues assessing complex and challenging defects, use defect
  patterns and trends to make tactical and strategic recommendations, and
  influence wider quality process improvements as a result.

  Ask AI: "What's the difference between fixing a defect and fixing the
  process that let a whole class of defects like it through?"

- [ ] **Know where your own engineering depth needs to stop**

  This skill stays at an explanatory level: understanding why testing
  processes, environments and tools matter, and supporting the maintenance
  of what already exists, rather than building the frameworks yourself —
  that depth belongs to the engineers you manage or work alongside.

  Ask AI: "As a test manager, what do I need to understand about test tooling
  and automation well enough to make good decisions, without needing to be
  able to build it myself?"

- [ ] **Represent test capability to people who don't report to you**

  Mediate between people and strengthen relationships, adopting the right
  communication method for stakeholders at every level, manage expectations
  and moderate difficult discussions about high-risk, complex topics under
  time pressure, and speak on behalf of, and represent, your community to
  large audiences inside and outside the organisation.

  Ask AI: "How should a test manager prepare to advocate for testing
  capability to senior stakeholders who see it mainly as a cost?"

Beyond the six named skills, this level is also about people: talent,
succession planning and supplier management. Ask a peer manager, inside or
outside your organisation, how they built those skills — that conversation
will teach you more than any general reading on management theory.
