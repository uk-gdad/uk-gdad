# Quality assurance test analyst - start here

Welcome. If you are working toward the quality assurance test analyst level,
this page is for you: what the role expects, where to find everything else
this repository has for it, and a pathway for building the skills it asks
for.

## Purpose

A quality assurance test analyst designs and executes functional and
non-functional tests, applying more than one testing technique to decide how
best to mitigate risk. The step up from associate is that you now apply test
approaches and plans based on risk yourself, rather than following one you've
been handed: you decide what to test given an agreed approach, make sure test
coverage genuinely reflects business risk rather than just covering what's
easiest to check, and follow standards that catch problems early instead of
at the end. That shift — from following an approach to choosing and adapting
one — is what the rest of this level's skills build on.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. Each one is a requirement of this role
level. Where you want to go deeper, ask an AI tool a specific question rather
than searching for a generic guide — you'll get an answer shaped around the
artefacts and risks in front of you.

- [ ] **Decide what to test, and why, based on risk**

  Work with stakeholders to determine which functional and non-functional
  quality characteristics genuinely add value, decide what to test following
  an agreed approach, and analyse the information available to identify
  where risk actually sits — including advocating for the data, access and
  environments you need to do it properly.

  Ask AI: "Given a feature I describe to you, help me work out which quality
  characteristics matter most and where the real testing risk is likely to
  be, not just the obvious places."

- [ ] **Create or adapt a testing approach, not just follow one**

  Create or adapt a quality testing approach based on risk, with some
  support, and follow — and help improve — an existing testing strategy
  rather than treating it as fixed.

  Ask AI: "How would I adapt a general test approach to fit a piece of work
  that's higher risk than usual, and what would I change first?"

- [ ] **Broaden how you design and run tests**

  Set up your own test environments with some support, select suitable test
  types and techniques, design, build, maintain and run tests that reflect
  real user needs, try exploratory testing, and look for new techniques
  worth trying.

  Ask AI: "Explain what exploratory testing is and how to run a session that
  finds genuinely useful problems rather than just wandering around the
  system."

- [ ] **Help build the defect process, don't just follow it**

  Collaborate on creating a defect management process, with support, and
  start critically assessing dependencies, defects and risks rather than
  just recording them, contributing to mitigation plans and communicating
  risk and impact clearly to stakeholders.

  Ask AI: "How should a tester decide whether a defect is high enough risk to
  block a release, and how would they explain that judgement to someone who
  disagrees?"

- [ ] **Know the tools and environments testing depends on**

  At this level the expectation stays practical rather than technical: be
  able to explain why testing processes, environments and tools matter,
  follow test engineering practices and standards with support, and help
  maintain the automated tests and tooling already in place.

  Ask AI: "What should a test analyst, as opposed to a test engineer,
  understand about test tooling and environments — and where does that
  understanding stop being their job?"

- [ ] **Grow into hosting harder conversations**

  Communicate effectively with technical and non-technical stakeholders,
  support and host discussions in a team with real disagreement in it, act
  as an advocate for your team externally, and manage stakeholders who see
  the same issue differently.

  Ask AI: "Give me a scenario where a developer and a business stakeholder
  disagree about test coverage, and suggest how a test analyst might help
  that conversation reach a decision."

Ask your manager or a more senior colleague which of these your team relies on
most right now, and start there.
