# Lead quality assurance test analyst - start here

Welcome. If you are working toward the lead quality assurance test analyst
level, this page is for you: what the role expects, where to find everything
else this repository has for it, and a pathway for building the skills it
asks for.

## Purpose

A lead quality assurance test analyst sets the strategy for test analysis and
shapes practice across a broad area of the organisation — developing,
monitoring and evaluating the standards and methods other analysts work to,
and making the strategic calls that improve test analysis more widely. You
are expected to set standards across the whole test analysis community, lead
and guide teams beyond your own, define and implement test strategies, mentor
and coach other analysts, and negotiate and escalate the risks and defects
that are too significant for any one team to own alone. Where a senior
analyst makes a defined area stronger, a lead analyst makes the practice
itself better, for people they may never work with directly.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. Each one is a requirement of this role
level. At this level, use an AI tool as a sounding board for decisions that
will outlast the project in front of you — ask it to argue the other side of
a standard you're about to set, not to set it for you.

- [ ] **Set standards and principles for the whole test analysis community**

  Lead and guide multiple teams across the organisation in test analysis,
  ensuring it happens early in the life cycle, advocate for risk-based
  analysis to drive improvement everywhere it applies, and set the standards
  and principles other analysts will be measured against.

  Ask AI: "What makes a test analysis standard something people actually
  follow, rather than something that gets written once and ignored?"

- [ ] **Own quality testing strategy across the organisation**

  Create and manage multiple quality testing plans, approaches and
  strategies at once, lead and guide multiple teams in adopting them,
  advocate for quality testing to be involved early in delivery rather than
  bolted on, and guide teams in optimising their approach using real data.

  Ask AI: "How would a lead decide when it's worth imposing one testing
  strategy across many teams, versus letting local variation stand?"

- [ ] **Make the strategic calls on test types and techniques**

  Set standards and influence organisational decisions on test types,
  techniques, design and execution; coach others in them; advocate for their
  continuous improvement; and make the strategic decisions about new or
  improved approaches used in your area.

  Ask AI: "How should a lead weigh the cost of standardising a testing
  technique across an organisation against the value of local flexibility?"

- [ ] **Turn defect data into organisational-level recommendations**

  Lead and coach others in improving test and defect management processes,
  support colleagues assessing the most complex defects the organisation
  faces, and use defect patterns and trends to make both tactical and
  strategic recommendations that influence wider quality processes.

  Ask AI: "What's the difference between a tactical fix and a strategic
  recommendation arising from the same defect pattern, and how would a lead
  decide which a given pattern deserves?"

- [ ] **Be clear about where analysis ends and engineering begins**

  This skill stays at an explanatory level here too: you are expected to
  explain why testing processes, environments and tools matter and to help
  maintain existing tooling. The deep engineering work — building and
  extending frameworks — sits with the test engineer track, and knowing
  that boundary well is part of leading analysis effectively alongside it.

  Ask AI: "How should a lead test analyst and a lead test engineer divide
  responsibility for testing tools and frameworks, and where do their
  remits need to overlap deliberately?"

- [ ] **Represent test analysis to people who don't report to you**

  Mediate between people and strengthen relationships across teams, manage
  stakeholder expectations and moderate difficult discussions about
  high-risk, complex topics even under tight timescales, and speak on behalf
  of, and represent, the community to large audiences inside and outside the
  organisation.

  Ask AI: "How would a lead prepare to represent their team's quality
  position to a large, mostly non-technical audience, including handling
  pushback on a decision they didn't personally make?"

Ask peers in other leadership roles which of these is hardest for people at
this level to get right — that conversation is often more useful than any
single resource.
