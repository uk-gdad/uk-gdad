# Associate network architect - start here

Welcome. If you are starting out as an associate network architect, this
page is for you: what the role is about, where to find everything else this
repository has for it, and a pathway for building the skills it asks for,
one step at a time.

## Purpose

A network architect is responsible for network designs and specifications,
including cloud networks. As an associate, you work with more experienced
network architects on designs and specifications, with guidance, rather
than owning them yourself. You stay aware of new technology solutions and
help produce network design policy, draft recommendations for network
support, create and maintain network plans, make sure designs meet service
level agreements, and build an understanding of one or two network
technologies — such as local or wide area networks, wifi, data centre
networks, software-defined networks, telephony, mobile infrastructure or
network security — rather than being expected to know them all yet.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. Each one is a requirement of this role
level, described plainly, with a way to go deeper by asking an AI tool a
specific question — the answers you get will be shaped around your own
organisation's network, which a generic guide never is.

- [ ] **Build, configure and support infrastructure**

  Build, configure, administer and support infrastructure solutions and
  services — networking and physical infrastructure, off-the-shelf
  software, open source packages, or virtual and cloud computing.

  Ask AI: "I'm new to network architecture. Explain, simply, the
  difference between building infrastructure and administering it day to
  day."

- [ ] **Turn logical network designs into physical ones**

  Translate logical designs into physical designs, produce detailed
  designs documented to the required standard, and design systems with
  managed levels of risk and complexity using technology you already
  understand well.

  Ask AI: "What's the practical difference between a logical network
  design and a physical one, and what does 'managed risk' mean when
  choosing between design options?"

- [ ] **Understand how systems connect to each other**

  Explain the process and principles of integrating systems, and describe
  the challenges of designing, building and testing the interfaces between
  them.

  Ask AI: "Explain, simply, what 'integrating systems' means for a network
  architect, and why the interface between two systems is often where the
  hardest problems hide."

- [ ] **Troubleshoot problems across technology capabilities**

  Troubleshoot and identify problems across different technology
  capabilities, rather than assuming an issue sits in just one part of the
  network.

  Ask AI: "What's a structured approach to troubleshooting a network
  problem that could plausibly sit in more than one layer of the
  infrastructure?"

- [ ] **Understand why governance and assurance exist**

  Explain why governance and assurance matter, and follow the relevant
  processes under supervision.

  Ask AI: "Explain, for a beginner, what 'governance and assurance' means
  for network design, with a concrete example of what it actually checks."

You do not need to master all of this before you start. Ask your manager or
a more senior architect which of these your team leans on most, and start
there.
