# Senior accessibility specialist - start here

Welcome. If you are working toward the senior accessibility specialist
level, this page is for you: what the role expects, where to find
everything else this repository has for it, and a pathway for building the
skills it asks for.

## Purpose

A senior accessibility specialist works with people across a range of role
levels to embed accessibility into the output of teams. You coach and
mentor junior colleagues, input into accessibility strategy with business
areas or teams, create and deliver training, engage with teams at every
level and capability, potentially manage or lead individuals or sub-teams,
and engage with the cross-government accessibility community. Every named
skill grows at this level, because embedding accessibility across a team's
output asks more than performing an audit well.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. Each one is a requirement of this role
level. At this level, an AI tool is often most useful for pressure-testing
a technical recommendation before you take it to a team — describe the
actual trade-off and ask what you might be missing.

- [ ] **Master accessibility technology, including edge cases**

  Demonstrate excellent knowledge of accessibility standards and
  legislation including edge cases, communicate requirements to a range of
  stakeholders, share deep knowledge of technologies such as ARIA
  (Accessible Rich Internet Applications), perform detailed audits with
  solutions others can work from, understand how usability and
  accessibility interact, advocate removing barriers everywhere, and use a
  range of assistive technologies confidently.

  Ask AI: "What's an accessibility edge case that a good general
  understanding of standards wouldn't catch, and how would a senior
  specialist learn to spot it?"

- [ ] **Challenge teams, and reduce accessibility risk through relationships**

  Challenge teams, make full use of existing arrangements, and build
  strong relationships to reduce or remove the risk of not meeting
  accessibility standards.

  Ask AI: "How would a senior accessibility specialist challenge a team
  about an accessibility gap without damaging the working relationship?"

- [ ] **Build a motivated team, and choose feedback deliberately**

  Make decisions of medium risk and complexity, build consensus between
  services or independent stakeholders, identify and rectify team dynamic
  issues, choose the right type of feedback for the moment so decisions
  stick, bring people together to form a motivated team, and help create
  the right environment.

  Ask AI: "What different types of feedback should a senior specialist
  have in their toolkit, and how would they choose between them in a
  specific moment?"

- [ ] **Manage testing risk, and customer relationships**

  Manage testing activities within development or integration work, manage
  risks and take preventative action when they become unacceptable, and
  manage customer relations.

  Ask AI: "What would tell a senior accessibility specialist that a
  testing risk has become unacceptable and needs preventative action, not
  just monitoring?"

- [ ] **Lead on user focus, not just gather it**

  Collaborate with user researchers and represent users internally,
  explain the difference between user needs and desires, champion user
  research so it covers all users, and guide others in understanding the
  user story.

  Ask AI: "What's the practical difference between what disabled users
  need and what they say they want, and how would a senior specialist
  explain that difference to a colleague who's confusing the two?"

- [ ] **Lead guidance, not just give advice**

  Provide advice based on significant specialist knowledge and experience,
  and lead the definition of guidance that informs how the organisation
  approaches delivery or uses digital and data skills.

  Ask AI: "What's the difference between advising one team well and
  setting guidance the whole organisation follows on accessibility?"

- [ ] **Manage stakeholder expectations, not just communicate information**

  Work collaboratively and build relationships with others, identify
  issues through team health checks, manage stakeholder expectations, and
  host or moderate difficult discussions with the team or senior
  stakeholders.

  Ask AI: "What does 'managing stakeholder expectations' actually involve
  day to day for an accessibility specialist, beyond just answering their
  questions?"

Ask a head of accessibility, or an equivalent principal colleague, which of
these matters most in your organisation right now, and start there.
