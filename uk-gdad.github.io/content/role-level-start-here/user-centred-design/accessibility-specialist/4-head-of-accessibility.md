# Head of accessibility - start here

Welcome. If you are working toward the head of accessibility level, this
page is for you: what the role expects, where to find everything else this
repository has for it, and a pathway for building the areas it names.

## Purpose

A head of accessibility is an expert practitioner who can create an
accessibility vision or strategy for an organisation — the top of this
profession's line. You define and assure best practice while influencing,
leading and mentoring others, influence organisational strategy and
priorities working with counterparts across government, make sure the
right conditions exist for accessibility specialists to work effectively,
develop departmental capability through internal mobility, career paths and
recruitment, own the overall approach to accessibility risk — including how
it's communicated and how legal compliance risk is managed — and engage
with the cross-government accessibility community. Leadership and
guidance jumps sharply in scope at this level, from team dynamics to
resolving conflict at the highest level across an organisation, which is
the clearest sign of what separates this level from senior.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. There is no level above this one to
measure yourself against, so use an AI tool as a sounding board: describe a
real organisational decision you're weighing and ask it to argue the case
you haven't made yet.

- [ ] **Lead organisational change and resolve the hardest conflicts**

  Change organisational structures towards sustainable designs, lead
  strategy for the whole organisation, make and justify decisions of high
  risk and complexity, build consensus between highly independent
  stakeholders, solve and unblock issues at the highest level, and coach
  the organisation on team dynamics and conflict resolution.

  Ask AI: "What separates a head of accessibility who genuinely resolves
  organisational conflict from one who just mediates a truce that
  reappears later?"

- [ ] **Build accessibility into governance across the organisation**

  Analyse governance and assurance systems and add appropriate measures to
  make sure accessibility is built into every project, programme and
  change activity.

  Ask AI: "How would a head of accessibility get accessibility genuinely
  embedded into an organisation's governance systems, rather than
  bolted on as a separate checklist?"

- [ ] **Represent the community to the largest audiences**

  Mediate between people and mend relationships, communicate with
  stakeholders at every level, manage expectations and moderate difficult
  discussions about high-risk, complex topics under real time pressure,
  and speak on behalf of the community to large audiences inside and
  outside government.

  Ask AI: "How would a head of accessibility prepare to represent their
  community to a large, mostly non-technical audience?"

- [ ] **Set the strategic direction for test planning**

  Manage planning system and acceptance tests, co-ordinating functional
  and non-functional specifications, provide authoritative advice on test
  planning, and identify process improvements contributing to best
  practice.

  Ask AI: "What does authoritative, organisation-wide test planning advice
  look like, as opposed to planning tests well for one team?"

- [ ] **Bring strategic direction to user focus**

  Give direction on which tools or methods to use, demonstrate experience
  meeting user needs across a variety of channels, bring insight into how
  user needs have changed over time, and apply strategic thinking to
  provide the best service for the end user.

  Ask AI: "How would a head of accessibility use insight about how
  disabled users' needs have changed over time to influence organisational
  strategy?"

- [ ] **Keep communicating and auditing against accessibility standards**

  This skill's wording here matches the accessibility specialist level
  rather than the more advanced version used at senior level — knowledge
  of standards, technical solutions, detailed audits and training. That's
  the framework's own choice, not a gap in your practice; the leadership
  expected of you at this level shows up clearly in the skills above.

  Ask AI: "Given this skill reads more simply than my other
  responsibilities, what would genuinely head-of-role technical judgement
  on accessibility standards add if I pushed beyond what's written here?"

There is no resource list at this level that will tell you what to do next —
the accountability is yours. Ask peers in equivalent roles at other
organisations how they made their hardest recent call, and use it to
stress-test your own thinking rather than to copy their answer.
