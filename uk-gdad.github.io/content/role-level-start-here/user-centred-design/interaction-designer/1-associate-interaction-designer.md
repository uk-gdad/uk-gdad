# Associate interaction designer - start here

Welcome. If you are starting out as an associate interaction designer,
this page is for you: what the role is about, where to find everything
else this repository has for it, and a pathway for building the skills it
asks for, one step at a time.

## Purpose

An interaction designer works out the best way to let users interact with
services, both in terms of the overall flow through a journey and at the
level of individual design elements — a button, a form field, a moment of
feedback. As an associate, this is a trainee, entry-level position, working
under supervision: what matters most is aptitude, potential and
understanding the role, not finished expertise.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. Each one is a requirement of this role
level, described plainly, with a way to go deeper by asking an AI tool a
specific question — the answers you get will be shaped around your own
service's flows, which a generic guide never is.

- [ ] **Understand iterative design, and prototyping fidelity**

  Describe the basics of iterative design methodologies such as Agile,
  explain how iteration supports user-centred design, and describe some
  ways to prototype ideas at different levels of realism, or "fidelities",
  and when to use them.

  Ask AI: "I'm new to interaction design. Explain, simply, the difference
  between a rough wireframe and a clickable high-fidelity prototype, and
  when a designer would use each."

- [ ] **Understand how evidence informs interaction decisions**

  Explain how research and analytics inform user-centred design, frame
  ideas as design hypotheses to be tested, and identify common sources and
  types of data that inform design.

  Ask AI: "Explain, for a beginner, how a piece of analytics data — like
  where users drop off in a journey — might turn into a testable design
  hypothesis."

- [ ] **Understand why explaining interaction decisions matters**

  Explain the importance of articulating design decisions to others, and
  describe some methods for doing that.

  Ask AI: "What makes an explanation of why a form is laid out a certain
  way genuinely convincing, rather than just a description of the
  layout?"

- [ ] **Understand what inclusive interaction design means**

  Explain the importance of making government content and services
  inclusive, accessible and environmentally sustainable, explain different
  access needs users can have, and describe how privilege and power
  influence design and delivery.

  Ask AI: "What's an example of an access need that specifically affects
  how someone interacts with a service, beyond how it looks?"

- [ ] **Understand why aligning interaction design to strategy matters**

  Explain why aligning to an organisation's strategy matters, describe how
  your work aligns to your team's wider goals, and explain the value of
  using design patterns and components.

  Ask AI: "How would an associate interaction designer find out the
  strategic context behind a specific flow they've been asked to design,
  if nobody's explained it to them?"

- [ ] **Understand how to bring perspectives together in design**

  Explain different techniques for bringing together perspectives from
  multiple people to inform a design, and explain why constructive
  feedback matters in the design process.

  Ask AI: "What makes feedback on an interaction flow 'constructive'
  rather than just a list of things someone dislikes?"

- [ ] **Understand the role of leadership in design**

  Explain the role of user-centred design in an organisation, describe
  typical leadership responsibilities in design, and identify the leaders
  designers need to work with.

  Ask AI: "Why does user-centred design typically need someone to
  advocate for it at a leadership level within an organisation?"

You do not need to master all of this before you start. Ask your manager or
a more senior designer which of these your team leans on most, and start
there.
