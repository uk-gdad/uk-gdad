# Junior interaction designer - start here

Welcome. If you are working toward the junior interaction designer level,
this page is for you: what the role expects, where to find everything else
this repository has for it, and a pathway for building the skills it asks
for.

## Purpose

A junior interaction designer is typically a graduate with a relevant
degree, or someone with some relevant work experience, or both. You explain
design decisions, work collaboratively, take responsibility as part of a
service, work independently once a more senior designer has given
direction, and independently identify user issues and important needs.
Design communication, designing for everyone, evidence-based design and
iterative design all deepen at this level, because you're now producing
real interaction work, not describing concepts.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. Each one is a requirement of this role
level. Where you want to go deeper, ask an AI tool a specific question
rather than searching for a generic guide — you'll get an answer shaped
around your own service's flows.

- [ ] **Apply iterative design principles to real interaction work**

  Apply iterative design principles and Agile methodologies to your work,
  iterate and improve a design based on successive rounds of research,
  prototype ideas at different fidelities, use a range of design tools and
  techniques, and use and iterate design patterns and components.

  Ask AI: "How would a junior interaction designer decide how many rounds
  of testing a flow genuinely needs, rather than shipping the first
  version that seems to work?"

- [ ] **Turn evidence into a tested interaction idea**

  Analyse, synthesise and clearly explain evidence relevant to users or a
  service, such as web analytics data, and work with researchers or
  analysts to use evidence to develop and test a design idea.

  Ask AI: "How would a junior interaction designer turn a drop-off point
  in analytics data into a specific, testable change to a flow?"

- [ ] **Explain interaction problems and decisions clearly**

  Clearly explain problems and design ideas to others, and clearly
  explain design decisions using appropriate tools and methods.

  Ask AI: "What makes an explanation of why a flow works a certain way
  clear to a non-designer, rather than clear only to another designer?"

- [ ] **Design inclusive interactions, and meet accessibility standards**

  Work with others to design and deliver inclusive, accessible and
  environmentally sustainable content or services, and make sure a design
  meets appropriate standards such as accessibility regulations, with
  support.

  Ask AI: "What accessibility considerations matter most for interaction
  design specifically, as opposed to visual design?"

- [ ] **Keep understanding why aligning to strategy matters**

  This skill's wording plateaus here, unchanged from associate level:
  explaining why aligning to an organisation's strategy matters, and the
  value of using design patterns and components.

  Ask AI: "Given this skill's description hasn't changed since the level
  below, what would genuinely applying strategic alignment, rather than
  just explaining its importance, look like at this level?"

- [ ] **Keep understanding how to bring perspectives together**

  This skill also plateaus: explaining techniques for bringing together
  multiple perspectives, and the importance of constructive feedback.

  Ask AI: "What would actually running a session that brings multiple
  perspectives together, rather than just explaining the technique, look
  like for a junior interaction designer?"

- [ ] **Keep understanding the role of leadership in design**

  This skill's wording plateaus too: explaining the role of user-centred
  design, describing leadership responsibilities, and identifying the
  leaders designers work with.

  Ask AI: "What would a junior designer's first step into supporting
  design leadership within their team actually look like?"

Ask your manager or a more senior colleague which of these your team relies on
most right now, and start there.
