# Content designer - start here

Welcome. If you are working toward the content designer level, this page
is for you: what the role expects, where to find everything else this
repository has for it, and a pathway for building the skills it asks for.

## Purpose

A content designer is responsible for creating, evaluating, updating and
reviewing content at every stage of the end-to-end user journey. You're
comfortable using evidence, data and research to make content decisions,
build relationships across government to focus on user needs and influence
stakeholders, contribute to and use style guides and content patterns, and
engage with the cross-government content community. Every named skill grows
at this level, because you're now producing real content independently,
not working under close direction.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. Each one is a requirement of this role
level. Where you want to go deeper, ask an AI tool a specific question
rather than searching for a generic guide — you'll get an answer shaped
around your own organisation's content.

- [ ] **Write content that meets user needs, and publish it well**

  Design content to meet user needs and make complex language and
  processes easy to understand, present information in the right format,
  write in plain, accessible language, understand accessibility
  requirements for assistive technology, use evidence to review and
  improve content, apply content standards consistently, and work
  autonomously.

  Ask AI: "What does 'plain language' actually mean in practice, beyond
  just using shorter sentences?"

- [ ] **Apply an Agile mindset to real, fast-moving work**

  Understand and apply an Agile mindset, work in a fast-paced, iterative
  environment, appreciate why Agile delivery matters for government
  digital projects, take risks and learn from mistakes, keep situational
  awareness of the team's work, and use the right tools to plan your own.

  Ask AI: "What does 'taking risks and learning from mistakes' look like
  responsibly for a content designer, as opposed to publishing something
  half-finished?"

- [ ] **Turn user research into content decisions**

  Identify tasks that give insight into a problem, formulate hypotheses
  from data and research, understand the range of users who access your
  content and their needs, translate user stories into content, and use
  quantitative and qualitative data to turn insight into outcomes.

  Ask AI: "How would a content designer turn a user research finding into
  a specific content change, rather than a general observation?"

- [ ] **Choose and use prototyping techniques deliberately**

  Understand different prototyping techniques from paper sketches to
  coded prototypes, use them to visualise content in context, choose the
  best option for different audiences, and show the team the value of
  prototyping.

  Ask AI: "How would a content designer decide between a paper sketch and
  a coded prototype for a specific piece of content work?"

- [ ] **Build stakeholder relationships, and reach consensus**

  Identify important stakeholders and communicate with them clearly and
  regularly, tailor communication to their needs, build and reach
  consensus, and use evidence to explain decisions.

  Ask AI: "How would a content designer build genuine consensus with a
  stakeholder who disagrees with a content decision, rather than just
  getting their sign-off?"

- [ ] **Contribute to content strategy, and focus on outcomes**

  Contribute to content strategies and policies, create content patterns
  or standards, support content improvement projects, and focus
  effectively on outcomes rather than solutions and activities.

  Ask AI: "What's the difference between focusing on a content solution
  and focusing on the outcome it's meant to achieve, with a concrete
  example?"

Ask your manager or a more senior colleague which of these your team relies on
most right now, and start there.
