# Associate content designer - start here

Welcome. If you are starting out as an associate content designer, this
page is for you: what the role is about, where to find everything else this
repository has for it, and a pathway for building the skills it asks for,
one step at a time.

## Purpose

Content designers make things easier for people to understand and use,
whether that's a single piece of content or the end-to-end journey of a
service — helping users complete their goal and government deliver its
policy intent. You make sure the right content shows up in the right place
and format, starting from discovery and working closely with user
researchers, service designers and interaction designers. As an associate,
this is a trainee, entry-level position, working under supervision: what
matters most is aptitude, potential and understanding the role, performing
basic standard checks and copy edits.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. Each one is a requirement of this role
level, described plainly, with a way to go deeper by asking an AI tool a
specific question — the answers you get will be shaped around your own
organisation's content, which a generic guide never is.

- [ ] **Build basic editorial and style standards**

  Demonstrate basic but decent editorial standards, show an awareness of
  style standards, and work well under supervision.

  Ask AI: "I'm new to content design. Explain, simply, what an editorial
  standard is and why consistency in style matters for users."

- [ ] **Learn what prototyping is for**

  Show an awareness of prototyping and explain why and when to use it, and
  understand how to work in an open, collaborative way — for example, by
  working in a pair with someone more experienced.

  Ask AI: "Explain what a content prototype is, why teams build one before
  the final content, and what pair working actually looks like day to
  day."

- [ ] **Build a basic sense of who you're writing for**

  Show an awareness or understanding of user experience analysis and its
  principles, and see the purpose of user stories and their focus on user
  needs.

  Ask AI: "Why do content designers write for a specific user story rather
  than just writing generally good copy?"

- [ ] **Learn who your stakeholders are, and why that matters**

  Understand who your stakeholders are, what evidence is relevant to
  them, and why managing relationships with them matters.

  Ask AI: "How would an associate content designer work out who the real
  stakeholders are for a piece of content, beyond the obvious people in
  the room?"

- [ ] **Build a working knowledge of Agile practices**

  Demonstrate experience working in Agile teams, show awareness of Agile
  tools and how to use them, adapt and respond to feedback, and see beyond
  the process itself.

  Ask AI: "I'm new to Agile working. Explain, simply, what an Agile
  approach means for a content designer specifically, as distinct from
  a developer."

- [ ] **Start connecting your work to the wider strategy**

  Show an awareness of the strategic context of your work and why it
  matters, and support strategic planning in an administrative capacity.

  Ask AI: "How would an associate content designer find out the strategic
  context behind a piece of content, if nobody's explained it to them?"

You do not need to master all of this before you start. Ask your manager or
a more senior content designer which of these your team leans on most, and
start there.
