# Lead content designer - start here

Welcome. If you are working toward the lead content designer level, this
page is for you: what the role expects, where to find everything else this
repository has for it, and a pathway for building the areas it names.

## Purpose

A lead content designer is an expert practitioner and leader who directs a
team of content designers, assuring content quality across whole teams and
making sure content aligns to strategy and objectives while meeting user
needs. You work closely with service managers, programme directors and
senior stakeholders to resource teams, resolve problems and develop future
projects, and you promote the content design discipline, engaging with the
cross-government community and staying current with industry change.
Agile working, stakeholder relationship management, user focus and
user-centred content design all sharpen at this level, while content
concepts and prototyping settles back to a more foundational description —
the framework's own signal that your depth here now shows up in leading
teams, not in your own hands-on prototyping.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. At this level, an AI tool is most
useful as a sounding board for a team or capability decision — describe
the actual situation and ask what you might be missing.

- [ ] **Direct content life cycle management across teams**

  Direct the approach to content life cycle management so content is
  regularly reviewed by teams, oversee teams' work to make sure the right
  content gets produced, demonstrate extensive experience across multiple
  channels, encourage teams to evaluate their processes, take
  accountability for high-quality output, and identify capability gaps to
  help teams grow.

  Ask AI: "How would a lead content designer identify a content design
  capability gap in their team before it becomes visible in poor output?"

- [ ] **Manage strategic stakeholder relationships, and arbitrate blockers**

  Manage long-term strategic relationships, identifying where connections
  need making or nurturing, direct the strategic approach to stakeholder
  relationships, act as an escalation point when relationships become
  challenging for junior team members, influence senior stakeholders, and
  facilitate discussions across high-risk, complex areas under
  constrained timelines.

  Ask AI: "How would a lead content designer step in when a junior
  colleague's stakeholder relationship has broken down, without
  undermining that colleague's authority going forward?"

- [ ] **Direct solutions across complex, evolving user journeys**

  Give direction on which tools or methods teams should use, demonstrate
  extensive experience meeting user needs across channels, understand
  complex user journeys and direct solutions within them, and bring
  insight into how user needs have changed over time to keep them met.

  Ask AI: "How would a lead content designer judge that a user journey has
  become too complex for its current content approach and needs
  redirecting?"

- [ ] **Lead Agile and Lean practice, and assess team capability**

  Coach and lead teams in Agile and Lean practices, determining the right
  approach for the team or project, evaluate and iterate the approach
  through a project's life, think of innovative ways of working, act as a
  recognised expert and advocate, and regularly assess team capability.

  Ask AI: "How would a lead content designer decide a team's current way
  of working needs to change partway through a project, rather than
  pushing through with what's not working?"

- [ ] **Keep leading strategic content improvement projects**

  This skill's wording plateaus here, unchanged from senior level: helping
  lead strategy design and implementation, and directing improvement
  projects focused on the highest-priority areas.

  Ask AI: "Given this skill's description hasn't changed since senior
  level, what would genuinely lead-level strategic judgement add beyond
  what's written here?"

- [ ] **Understand what prototyping is for**

  This skill's wording here matches the earlier, more foundational
  levels of this role rather than the more elaborate version used at
  content designer and senior level — showing awareness of prototyping and
  working collaboratively. That's the framework's own choice, not a gap in
  your practice; the depth expected of you at this level shows up in
  leading teams, not in your own hands-on prototyping.

  Ask AI: "Given this skill reads more simply than my other
  responsibilities, what would genuinely lead-level prototyping judgement
  add if I pushed beyond what's written here?"

Ask a head of content design, or an equivalent principal colleague, which
of these matters most in your organisation right now, and start there.
