# Associate graphic designer - start here

Welcome. If you are starting out as an associate graphic designer, this
page is for you: what the role is about, where to find everything else this
repository has for it, and a pathway for building the skills it asks for,
one step at a time.

## Purpose

A graphic designer creates the graphic elements that underpin interaction
and service design — using layout, spacing, colour, type and iconography so
content is legible and readable, and so users see and understand
interactions. As an associate, this is a trainee, entry-level position,
working under supervision: what matters most is aptitude, potential and
understanding the role, not finished expertise.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. Each one is a requirement of this role
level, described plainly, with a way to go deeper by asking an AI tool a
specific question — the answers you get will be shaped around your own
organisation's services, which a generic guide never is.

- [ ] **Understand iterative design, and prototyping fidelity**

  Describe the basics of iterative design methodologies such as Agile,
  explain how iteration supports user-centred design, and describe some
  ways to prototype ideas at different levels of realism, or "fidelities",
  and when to use them.

  Ask AI: "I'm new to graphic design. Explain, simply, what a low-fidelity
  versus a high-fidelity prototype is, and when a designer would choose
  each."

- [ ] **Understand how evidence informs user-centred design**

  Explain how research and analytics inform user-centred design, frame
  ideas as design hypotheses to be tested, and identify common sources and
  types of data that inform design.

  Ask AI: "Explain, for a beginner, what a 'design hypothesis' is and how
  it differs from just having a design idea."

- [ ] **Understand why explaining design decisions matters**

  Explain the importance of articulating design decisions to others, and
  describe some methods for doing that.

  Ask AI: "What makes an explanation of a design decision genuinely
  convincing, rather than just a description of what the design looks
  like?"

- [ ] **Understand what inclusive design means, and why it matters**

  Explain the importance of making government content and services
  inclusive, accessible and environmentally sustainable, explain different
  access needs users can have, and describe how privilege and power
  influence design and delivery.

  Ask AI: "What's an example of an access need a graphic designer should
  consider that goes beyond colour contrast and font size?"

- [ ] **Understand why aligning to strategy matters**

  Explain why aligning to an organisation's strategy matters, describe how
  your work aligns to your team's wider goals, and explain the value of
  using design patterns and components.

  Ask AI: "How would an associate graphic designer find out the strategic
  context behind a piece of design work, if nobody's explained it to
  them?"

- [ ] **Understand how to bring perspectives together in design**

  Explain different techniques for bringing together perspectives from
  multiple people to inform a design, and explain why constructive
  feedback matters in the design process.

  Ask AI: "What makes feedback on a design 'constructive' rather than just
  critical?"

- [ ] **Understand the role of leadership in design**

  Explain the role of user-centred design in an organisation, describe
  typical leadership responsibilities in design, and identify the leaders
  designers need to work with.

  Ask AI: "Why does user-centred design typically need someone to advocate
  for it at a leadership level within an organisation?"

You do not need to master all of this before you start. Ask your manager or
a more senior designer which of these your team leans on most, and start
there.
