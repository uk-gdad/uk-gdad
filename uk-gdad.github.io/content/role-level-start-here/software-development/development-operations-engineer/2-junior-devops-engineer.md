# Junior DevOps engineer - start here

Welcome. If you are working toward the junior DevOps engineer level, this
page is for you: what the role expects, where to find everything else this
repository has for it, and a pathway for building the skills it asks for.

## Purpose

A junior DevOps engineer learns on the job by building software components —
the step up from apprentice is that you're now assisting in building a
complex story yourself, still under supervision, still involved in
implementation rather than planning, but genuinely producing things rather
than mostly watching. Three new skills appear at this level — systems design,
systems integration and user focus — because building a real component means
understanding roughly where it sits in a bigger system and who it's actually
for, not just how to make it run.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. Each one is a requirement of this role
level. Where you want to go deeper, ask an AI tool a specific question rather
than searching for a generic guide — you'll get an answer shaped around your
own codebase and team.

- [ ] **Design, code and document a simple program under direction**

  You are expected to design, code, test, correct and document simple
  programs or scripts under the direction of others — the full small cycle,
  not just the coding part.

  Ask AI: "Walk me through the full cycle of writing a simple script:
  designing it, coding it, testing it, and documenting it, so I don't skip
  any of those steps under time pressure."

- [ ] **Contribute to designing a piece of a larger system**

  Assist, as part of a team, in the design of components of larger systems —
  you're not designing the whole system, but you are expected to understand
  enough of it to contribute meaningfully to your piece.

  Ask AI: "How does a junior engineer contribute usefully to designing one
  component of a larger system, without needing to understand the entire
  system first?"

- [ ] **Understand how systems connect to each other**

  Explain the process and principles of integrating systems, and describe
  the challenges of designing, building and testing the interfaces between
  them.

  Ask AI: "Explain, simply, what 'integrating systems' means and why the
  interface between two systems is often where the hardest bugs hide."

- [ ] **Build a basic sense of who you're building for**

  Show an awareness of user experience analysis and its principles, and
  explain the purpose of user stories and why they focus on user needs
  rather than technical convenience.

  Ask AI: "Why do teams write user stories instead of just listing technical
  requirements, and what does a good one actually contain?"

- [ ] **Start applying development standards, not just explaining them**

  Explain the most important principles of modern development standards and
  how they apply to your work, and apply those principles yourself under
  guidance.

  Ask AI: "What are the modern development standards most relevant to the
  kind of code a junior DevOps engineer writes day to day?"

- [ ] **Learn when a prototype earns its keep**

  Understand when to use a specific prototyping technique or method, and be
  able to show your team the value of prototyping rather than jumping
  straight to a finished solution.

  Ask AI: "How would I decide whether a piece of work is worth prototyping
  first, versus just building it properly straight away?"

- [ ] **Fix faults by following procedure, and keep infrastructure maintained**

  Help fix service faults by following agreed procedures, and carry out
  maintenance tasks on the service support infrastructure that keeps
  everything running.

  Ask AI: "What's the difference between fixing a fault by following an
  agreed procedure and improvising a fix, and why does that difference
  matter at this level?"

- [ ] **Keep building your picture of availability and capacity**

  This skill stays at an explanatory level here, the same as at apprentice:
  explain availability and capacity management processes. The growth at
  this level is happening in the skills above, not here yet.

  Ask AI: "What's a realistic next step, beyond just explaining availability
  and capacity management, that a junior engineer could actually practise?"

- [ ] **Move from explaining security to using it**

  Use information security practices and the security controls available
  to contribute to protecting solutions and services — the step up from
  apprentice is doing this, not just explaining it.

  Ask AI: "What security practices would a junior DevOps engineer be
  expected to actually apply day to day, as opposed to just being aware of?"

Ask your manager or a more senior colleague which of these your team relies on
most right now, and start there.
