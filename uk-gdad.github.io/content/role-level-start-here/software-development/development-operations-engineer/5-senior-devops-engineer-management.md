# Senior DevOps engineer - management - start here

Welcome. If you are working toward the senior DevOps engineer (management)
level, this page is for you: what the role expects, where to find everything
else this repository has for it, and a pathway for building the skills it
asks for.

The framework carries two roles at this level, sharing one title and one set
of named skills: a technical one and this management one. The duties and
most of the named skills read identically between them; where they genuinely
differ is in two skills — development process optimisation and availability
and capacity management — where this track leans toward leading a team and
setting policy rather than doing the technical work directly. If deep
technical delivery, rather than people and process leadership, is where you
want to grow, the
[senior DevOps engineer (technical)](https://uk-gdad.github.io/roles/software-development/development-operations-engineer/4-senior-devops-engineer/)
page may fit better.

## Purpose

A senior DevOps engineer at this level still delivers and integrates
software to form a complete DevOps toolchain and is responsible for planning
and designing large groups of stories, the same as the technical track — but
where that distinction actually shows up in the framework's own wording is
leadership: analysing current processes and leading a team of experts to
improve them, and taking ownership of availability and capacity standards
rather than just monitoring against them. Coaching and mentoring junior
colleagues, and ensuring deployment strategies are repeatable, scalable and
highly available, sit alongside that leadership work rather than instead of
it.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. Each one is a requirement of this role
level. At this level, an AI tool is often most useful for pressure-testing a
people or policy decision — describe the actual situation and ask what you
might be missing.

- [ ] **Lead a team of experts to improve how the organisation builds software**

  Analyse current development processes, identify and implement
  opportunities to optimise them, lead and develop a team of experts to
  deliver service improvements, and help set the policy and standards new
  changes get evaluated against.

  Ask AI: "How does leading a team through process improvement differ from
  contributing individual optimisation ideas, and what changes about how
  you spend your time?"

- [ ] **Own availability and capacity standards, not just meet them**

  Ensure the correct implementation of availability and capacity management
  standards and procedures, identify capacity issues before they bite, and
  initiate remedial action yourself rather than waiting to be asked.

  Ask AI: "What's the difference between reacting well to a capacity issue
  and genuinely owning availability and capacity management as a standard
  others are held to?"

- [ ] **Design systems of real complexity, and review other people's designs too**

  Design systems characterised by medium levels of risk, impact and
  business or technical complexity, select and apply the right design
  standards, and review the systems designs of others to check the
  technology choice and integration are sound.

  Ask AI: "What should a senior engineer in a leadership track look for
  when reviewing someone else's system design, beyond a purely technical
  check?"

- [ ] **Coordinate integration across systems**

  Define the integration build, co-ordinate build activities across
  systems, and understand how to support integration testing across the
  whole effort rather than one interface at a time.

  Ask AI: "What coordination problems come up when integration spans
  several systems and several teams, that don't exist when it's a single
  interface?"

- [ ] **Take on the harder faults, and advise others on how to approach them**

  Identify, locate and fix complex service faults, and advise others on
  different methodologies and types of service support.

  Ask AI: "How would a senior engineer decide when to step in personally on
  a complex fault versus coaching someone else through fixing it?"

- [ ] **Apply development standards, and help others apply them too**

  Apply modern development standards yourself and support others in
  applying them.

  Ask AI: "What's an effective way to help a team consistently apply a
  development standard, rather than relying on individual discipline?"

- [ ] **Lead on user focus, not just gather it**

  Collaborate with user researchers and represent users internally, explain
  the difference between user needs and user desires, champion user
  research so it covers all users, and guide others in understanding the
  user story.

  Ask AI: "How would a technical lead champion genuinely inclusive user
  research, rather than research that only reaches the easiest users to
  reach?"

- [ ] **Keep building your programming and build practice**

  This skill's expectation is unchanged from the technical track at this
  level: collaborating on specifications, then designing, coding, testing
  and documenting programs of medium-to-high complexity to the right
  standards. Staying hands-on here is what keeps your leadership decisions
  grounded in reality.

  Ask AI: "How can a senior engineer in a management-leaning role stay
  genuinely hands-on with code, without it clashing with their leadership
  responsibilities?"

- [ ] **Keep treating prototyping as a team activity**

  Approach prototyping as something the team does together, establishing
  and iterating design patterns, and choosing the right method from
  several available.

  Ask AI: "How might a leader encourage a team to prototype more, when the
  pressure is usually to skip straight to building the real thing?"

- [ ] **Keep designing security in from the start**

  Design solutions and services with security controls included,
  specifically engineered to mitigate the relevant threats.

  Ask AI: "What security decisions at the design stage have the biggest
  long-term consequences if a team gets them wrong, and how would a leader
  make sure those decisions get proper attention?"

Ask your manager or a lead colleague which of these matters most in your
organisation right now, and start there.
