# Senior DevOps engineer - start here

Welcome. If you are working toward the senior DevOps engineer level, this
page is for you: what the role expects, where to find everything else this
repository has for it, and a pathway for building the skills it asks for.

There are two different roles at this level in the framework: a technical
one, covered here, and a management one. Both carry the same title and the
same named skills; if the day-to-day work you're aiming at is people
management rather than deep technical delivery, the
[senior DevOps engineer - management](https://uk-gdad.github.io/roles/software-development/development-operations-engineer/5-senior-devops-engineer-management/)
page describes the difference in practice.

## Purpose

A senior DevOps engineer delivers and integrates software to form a complete
DevOps toolchain, and is responsible for planning and designing large groups
of stories — real technical ownership, not just larger tasks. You're expected
to transform technical requirements into an effective toolchain that enables
delivery, coach and mentor more junior colleagues, make sure deployment
strategies are repeatable, scalable and highly available, and bring deep
technical knowledge to bear on the complex problems delivery teams can't
solve alone. The coaching accountability matters as much as the technical
one here: this is the level where explaining your reasoning to someone else
becomes part of the job, not a favour you do on the side.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. Each one is a requirement of this role
level. At this level, an AI tool is often most useful for pressure-testing a
design decision — describe the actual trade-off you're weighing and ask what
you might be missing.

- [ ] **Design systems of real complexity, and review other people's designs too**

  Design systems characterised by medium levels of risk, impact and
  business or technical complexity, select and apply the right design
  standards, methods and tools, and review the systems designs of others to
  check the technology choice, resource use and integration are all sound.

  Ask AI: "What should a senior engineer look for when reviewing someone
  else's system design, beyond just checking it will technically work?"

- [ ] **Coordinate integration across systems, not just build one interface**

  Define the integration build, co-ordinate build activities across
  systems, and understand how to support integration testing across the
  whole thing rather than one interface at a time.

  Ask AI: "What does coordinating integration across several systems
  actually involve day to day, compared with building and testing one
  interface?"

- [ ] **Take on the harder faults, and advise others on how to approach them**

  Identify, locate and fix complex service faults, and advise others on
  different methodologies and types of service support — the shift here is
  from doing the fix to also shaping how the team approaches fixing things.

  Ask AI: "What separates a genuinely complex service fault from a merely
  time-consuming one, and how would a senior engineer decide how to
  approach each differently?"

- [ ] **Apply development standards, and help others apply them too**

  Apply modern development standards yourself and support others in
  applying them — the step up from the level below is the second half of
  that sentence: this is now partly about other people's practice, not just
  your own.

  Ask AI: "What's an effective way for a senior engineer to help a
  colleague apply a development standard they're struggling with, without
  just doing it for them?"

- [ ] **Lead on user focus, not just gather it**

  Collaborate with user researchers and represent users internally, explain
  the difference between user needs and user desires, champion user
  research so it covers all users, and guide others in understanding the
  user story rather than working it out alone.

  Ask AI: "What's the practical difference between what users need and what
  users say they want, and how would a senior engineer explain that
  difference to a colleague who's confusing the two?"

- [ ] **Manage service components against business needs and KPIs**

  This skill's expectation stays the same as at the level below — manage
  service components to ensure they meet business needs and key
  performance indicators — so the growth you're building at this level is
  mostly happening in the skills around it.

  Ask AI: "What would taking availability and capacity management from
  'meeting the KPIs' to genuinely owning them look like at a senior level?"

- [ ] **Contribute to optimising the development process**

  Work under guidance to identify process optimisation opportunities and
  contribute to implementing the solutions proposed — a level below the
  process leadership expected further up, but a real contribution rather
  than just following the process as given.

  Ask AI: "What's a good way for a senior engineer to spot a process
  optimisation opportunity worth raising, even while still working under
  guidance on how to act on it?"

- [ ] **Keep building your programming and build practice**

  This skill's wording plateaus here, unchanged from the level below:
  collaborating on specifications, then designing, coding, testing and
  documenting programs of medium-to-high complexity to the right standards.
  It's worth being deliberately good at this rather than assuming it looks
  after itself.

  Ask AI: "What separates genuinely strong code review and documentation
  habits from merely adequate ones at a senior level?"

- [ ] **Keep treating prototyping as a team activity**

  This skill also plateaus in wording here: approaching prototyping as
  something the team does together, establishing and iterating design
  patterns, and choosing the right method from several.

  Ask AI: "What might a senior engineer do differently in a prototyping
  session compared with a more junior one, even if the described skill
  looks the same on paper?"

- [ ] **Keep designing security in from the start**

  This skill's wording also plateaus here: designing solutions and services
  with security controls included, specifically engineered to mitigate the
  relevant threats.

  Ask AI: "What security decisions at the design stage have the biggest
  long-term consequences if they're got wrong at a senior level?"

Ask your manager or a lead colleague which of these matters most in your
organisation right now, and start there.
