# Apprentice frontend developer - start here

Welcome. If you are starting out as an apprentice frontend developer, this
page is for you: what the role is about, where to find everything else this
repository has for it, and a pathway for building the skills it asks for, one
step at a time.

## Purpose

A frontend developer designs, builds and improves website software that
meets user needs — writing clean, accessible code with a progressive
enhancement approach, open by default so others can reuse it, built on an
understanding of the three fundamental frontend technologies: HTML, CSS and
JavaScript. As an apprentice, you're attending certified training and
building that understanding on the job, spending a substantial portion of
your time shadowing others and working alongside developers on code and
tests rather than writing much alone yet. That's exactly right for where you
are: the habits you absorb watching good frontend code get written are worth
more right now than rushing to write it solo.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. Each one is a requirement of this role
level, described plainly, with a way to go deeper by asking an AI tool a
specific question — the answers you get will be shaped around your own
team's codebase, which a generic tutorial never is.

- [ ] **Design and build a simple user interface under direction**

  Design, code, test, correct and document simple user interfaces using the
  right standards and tools, under the direction of others — the whole
  small cycle, not just typing the code.

  Ask AI: "Walk me through the full cycle of building a simple user
  interface element: designing it, coding it, testing it, and documenting
  it, so I don't skip a step under time pressure."

- [ ] **Understand why development standards exist**

  Explain the importance of using modern development standards, even though
  applying them under guidance comes at the next level.

  Ask AI: "Explain, for a complete beginner, what 'modern development
  standards' means for frontend code specifically, and why teams enforce
  them."

- [ ] **Learn what prototyping is for**

  Explain what prototyping is, and why and when to use it, and start
  understanding how to work in an open, collaborative way — for example, by
  working in a pair with someone more experienced.

  Ask AI: "Explain what a prototype is in frontend development, why teams
  build one before the real interface, and what pair working actually looks
  like day to day."

- [ ] **Start building your understanding of accessibility**

  Understand that users have different accessibility needs, and help fix
  accessibility issues under the direction of others.

  Ask AI: "Explain, for a beginner, what web accessibility means in
  practice, with a couple of concrete examples of accessibility needs a
  frontend developer should keep in mind."

- [ ] **Build a basic sense of who you're building for**

  Show an understanding of user experience analysis and its principles, and
  explain the purpose of user stories and why they focus on user needs
  rather than technical convenience.

  Ask AI: "Why do frontend teams write user stories instead of just listing
  what a page needs to display, and what does a good one contain?"

- [ ] **Learn why performance matters, and help improve it**

  Describe the basic principles of web performance, explain why it matters,
  and help improve it under supervision.

  Ask AI: "Explain, simply, why a slow-loading webpage is a real problem
  for users, not just an inconvenience, and what usually causes it."

- [ ] **Practise listening to stakeholders and joining team discussions**

  Listen to the needs of design and business stakeholders and interpret
  what they're telling you, and take part in discussions within a
  multidisciplinary team.

  Ask AI: "What should someone new to a team focus on when joining a
  multidisciplinary discussion, so they contribute usefully rather than
  staying silent out of uncertainty?"

- [ ] **Understand how your team works together**

  Understand the work of others and why team dynamics, collaboration and
  feedback matter, even before you're expected to shape any of them
  yourself.

  Ask AI: "What does good team collaboration actually look like day to day
  on a small software team, beyond just being friendly to colleagues?"

- [ ] **Follow the team's agreed good practice**

  Follow the team's agreed good practice, show an awareness of why team
  dynamics and collaboration matter, and understand why feedback matters —
  the foundation the leadership and guidance skill builds on at later
  levels.

  Ask AI: "Why do teams write down 'agreed good practice' rather than just
  relying on everyone doing what feels right, and what happens when someone
  skips it?"

You do not need to master all of this before you start. Ask your manager or
whoever you're shadowing which of these your team leans on most, and start
there.
