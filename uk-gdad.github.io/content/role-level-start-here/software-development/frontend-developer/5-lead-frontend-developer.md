# Lead frontend developer - start here

Welcome. If you are working toward the lead frontend developer level, this
page is for you: what the role expects, where to find everything else this
repository has for it, and a pathway for building the skills it asks for.

## Purpose

A lead frontend developer is an expert practitioner who influences and
mentors others, setting direction and assuring the quality of development
across teams rather than just within one. You lead multiple or highly
complex services, contribute technical leadership, coaching and mentoring to
the wider community, promote knowledge sharing and the adoption of good
practice, and stay proficient across a wide enough range of technical
systems to identify the right technology and approach for a given problem.
The scale of what "leadership and guidance" means jumps sharply at this
level — organisational structures, cross-organisation consensus, conflict at
the highest level — which is the clearest sign of what separates lead from
senior.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. Each one is a requirement of this role
level. At this level, an AI tool is most useful as a sounding board for a
strategic or organisational decision — describe the trade-off you're weighing
across teams and ask what you might be missing.

- [ ] **Lead organisational change and resolve the hardest conflicts**

  Change organisational structures towards more sustainable designs, lead
  the strategy for the whole organisation, make and justify decisions of
  high risk, impact and complexity, build consensus between highly
  independent stakeholders or organisations, solve and unblock issues
  between teams or departments at the highest level, and coach the
  organisation on team dynamics and conflict resolution.

  Ask AI: "What separates a lead who genuinely resolves cross-team conflict
  from one who just mediates a truce that reappears later?"

- [ ] **Represent the community to audiences far beyond your team**

  Mediate between people and mend relationships, communicate with
  stakeholders at every level, manage expectations and host or moderate
  discussions about high-risk, complex topics under real time pressure, and
  speak on behalf of, and represent, the community to large audiences
  inside and outside government.

  Ask AI: "How would a lead developer prepare to represent their community
  to a large, mostly non-technical audience, including handling pushback on
  a decision they didn't personally make?"

- [ ] **Advocate for accessibility beyond your own organisation**

  Show a strong understanding of accessibility and advocate for users with
  accessibility needs, champion accessibility to internal and external
  stakeholders, offer design solutions rather than just feedback, and
  recommend the best tools and methods for accessibility testing.

  Ask AI: "How would a lead developer make the case for accessibility to an
  external stakeholder who has no obligation to listen?"

- [ ] **Set the technical standards other developers are held to**

  Advise on the right way to apply standards and methods to ensure
  compliance, provide technical advice to stakeholders, set team-based
  standards for programming tools and techniques, and use specifications to
  build, test and document user interfaces of high complexity.

  Ask AI: "What makes a technical standard something developers actually
  follow, rather than something imposed that gets quietly worked around?"

- [ ] **Lead the design and implementation of strategy**

  Lead the design and implementation of strategy, directing the evaluation
  of strategies and policies to make sure business requirements are being
  met.

  Ask AI: "What's the difference between contributing to a strategy and
  genuinely leading its design and implementation?"

- [ ] **Get modern development standards adopted across complex programmes**

  Identify and apply modern development standards to support complex
  projects and programmes, and lead others in applying them.

  Ask AI: "How does a lead developer get a development standard adopted
  across programmes with genuinely different technical constraints?"

- [ ] **Keep shaping team dynamics**

  This skill's wording plateaus here, unchanged from earlier levels:
  networking within the team, adapting feedback so it lasts, and using team
  health checks to spot and address issues.

  Ask AI: "What would shaping team dynamics look like for someone leading
  across several teams, rather than working inside just one?"

- [ ] **Keep treating prototyping as a team activity**

  This skill also plateaus: approaching prototyping as a team activity,
  establishing and iterating design patterns, and choosing the right method
  from several available.

  Ask AI: "How might a lead developer encourage prototyping across several
  teams with different habits, without dictating one method to all?"

- [ ] **Keep owning integration end to end**

  This skill plateaus too: defining the static assets build, co-ordinating
  all aspects of integration, taking responsibility for interface tests,
  and configuring and testing the network-facing part of the system.

  Ask AI: "What would end-to-end ownership of integration look like across
  several services at once, rather than one?"

- [ ] **Keep representing user needs internally**

  This skill also plateaus: collaborating with user researchers and
  interaction designers, explaining the difference between user needs and
  desires, and championing research that covers all users.

  Ask AI: "How would a lead developer make sure user focus stays consistent
  across several teams with different research habits?"

- [ ] **Keep guiding on web performance**

  This skill plateaus as well: identifying and resolving performance
  issues, collecting data to monitor them, and guiding others on the best
  approaches to use.

  Ask AI: "What would leading web performance practice across several
  services look like, rather than fixing issues in one?"

Ask a head of frontend development, or an equivalent principal colleague,
which of these has taken them longest to get right — that conversation will
teach you more than any general reading on technical leadership.
