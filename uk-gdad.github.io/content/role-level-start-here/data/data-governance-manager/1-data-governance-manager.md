# Data governance manager - start here

Welcome. If you are working toward the data governance manager level, this
page is for you: what the role is about, where to find everything else this
repository has for it, and a pathway for building the skills it asks for.

## Purpose

A data governance manager plans, monitors and enforces the management of
data assets — cataloguing and identifying the data essential to the
organisation's operations, assigning clear ownership, building governance
into routine processes, managing risk around data, and advocating for
better data use and literacy. At this level, you define, implement and
manage data governance frameworks, policies and standards: designing
practical governance solutions, spotting opportunities to improve data
quality and trustworthiness, establishing wider data management
competencies such as metadata management and data modelling, and working to
minimise data risk while increasing data literacy across the organisation.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. Each one is a requirement of this role
level, described plainly, with a way to go deeper by asking an AI tool a
specific question — the answers you get will be shaped around your own
organisation's data, which a generic guide never is.

- [ ] **Build a data governance framework end to end**

  Demonstrate up-to-date knowledge of data governance theory and
  methodology, develop an end-to-end governance framework, and work
  effectively with technical teams and other stakeholders to implement it
  and ensure compliance.

  Ask AI: "I'm new to data governance. Explain, simply, what an 'end-to-end
  data governance framework' actually contains, from policy down to daily
  practice."

- [ ] **Apply governance consistently across the data life cycle**

  Understand and apply data governance over a data life cycle, making sure
  best practice is followed at each phase.

  Ask AI: "What does 'best practice' for data governance actually look
  like differently at the start of a data life cycle versus near the end
  of it?"

- [ ] **Apply and develop data standards**

  Use data policies, processes and standards effectively, work with
  subject matter experts to develop standards that protect data, monitor
  compliance within your team, and analyse the impact when a standard is
  breached.

  Ask AI: "What would a data governance manager look for when analysing
  the impact of a standards breach, beyond just noting that it happened?"

- [ ] **Measure data maturity, and describe the benefits of improving it**

  Explain the different data maturity models available, measure maturity
  and implement best practice, and describe the benefits of potential
  improvements to stakeholders.

  Ask AI: "How would a data governance manager measure an organisation's
  data maturity in a way that leads to a real improvement plan, rather
  than just a score?"

- [ ] **Follow governance, and support data management with some support**

  Follow organisational data governance, including policies on access,
  sharing, dissemination and protection, participate in or deliver data
  management across services or products, and use the right tools and
  methods with some support.

  Ask AI: "What data governance policies would a data governance manager
  most need to apply day to day, beyond knowing they exist?"

- [ ] **Raise awareness of data literacy across the organisation**

  Raise awareness of the need for data literacy, and support training and
  development that helps data consumers understand what they can and
  cannot do with data.

  Ask AI: "What does 'data literacy' actually mean for a non-specialist
  employee, and what's a realistic way to help them build it?"

- [ ] **Understand the organisational impact of emerging data trends**

  Understand the impact on the organisation of emerging trends in data
  tools, analysis techniques and data usage — a step up from simply being
  aware of them.

  Ask AI: "How would a data governance manager judge whether an emerging
  data trend is genuinely relevant to their organisation, rather than
  interesting in the abstract?"

- [ ] **Advise risk owners, and describe risk proportionality**

  Work with risk owners to advise and give feedback, advise on risk impact
  and whether it's within tolerance, and describe different risk
  methodologies and how proportionality applies.

  Ask AI: "How would a data governance manager explain 'risk tolerance' to
  a risk owner who wants a simple yes-or-no answer about whether something
  is safe?"

- [ ] **Connect your work to the wider strategy**

  Work within a strategic context and communicate how your activities meet
  strategic goals, and start contributing to developing strategy and
  policy rather than only implementing it.

  Ask AI: "How would a data governance manager find out the strategic
  context behind a governance requirement, if nobody's explained it to
  them?"

- [ ] **Learn to identify and communicate with stakeholders**

  Identify important stakeholders and communicate with them clearly and
  regularly, tailor communication to their needs, build and reach
  consensus, and use evidence to explain decisions.

  Ask AI: "How would a data governance manager work out who the real
  stakeholders are for a governance decision, beyond the obvious people in
  the room?"

- [ ] **Communicate analysis clearly, with support**

  Choose the right medium to communicate findings, shape communications
  for the audience, present analysis clearly for both specialist and
  non-specialist audiences with support, and give tactical recommendations.

  Ask AI: "What makes a data visualisation genuinely clear to a
  non-specialist audience, rather than just accurate?"

- [ ] **Communicate effectively across a multidisciplinary team**

  Communicate effectively with technical and non-technical stakeholders,
  support discussions within a multidisciplinary team that may have
  difficult dynamics, advocate for your team externally, and manage
  differing stakeholder perspectives.

  Ask AI: "What should a data governance manager focus on when explaining
  a technical governance decision to a non-technical stakeholder, so it
  actually lands?"

- [ ] **Apply data ethics and privacy principles, and share risks**

  Apply fundamental principles of data ethics and privacy in your work
  under supervision, and share ethics and privacy risks through the
  appropriate channels.

  Ask AI: "What's an example of a data ethics or privacy risk a data
  governance manager might spot, and how would they raise it?"

Ask your manager or a more senior colleague which of these your team relies on
most right now, and start there.
