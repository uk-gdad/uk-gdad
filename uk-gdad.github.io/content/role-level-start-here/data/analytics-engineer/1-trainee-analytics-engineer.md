# Trainee analytics engineer - start here

Welcome. If you are starting out as a trainee analytics engineer, this page
is for you: what the role is about, where to find everything else this
repository has for it, and a pathway for building the skills it asks for,
one step at a time.

## Purpose

An analytics engineer transforms data relevant to the organisation into
structures that enable analysis and decision-making — working with subject
matter experts to translate real organisational processes into data
structures, and with data users to build models they can actually use. As a
trainee, you're attending training and developing skills on the job: spend
time shadowing other analytics engineers, build your knowledge of the
organisation, learn to manage data and use the applications and tools your
team relies on, handle simple queries and document requirements, and
contribute to data documentation and user training.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. Each one is a requirement of this role
level, described plainly, with a way to go deeper by asking an AI tool a
specific question — the answers you get will be shaped around your own
organisation's data, which a generic guide never is.

- [ ] **Build awareness of data models and modelling patterns**

  Show an awareness of different data models and tools and when they could
  be used, and show an awareness of industry-recognised data modelling
  patterns and standards.

  Ask AI: "I'm new to analytics engineering. Explain, simply, what a data
  model is and why different modelling approaches suit different
  situations."

- [ ] **Interpret data to find key insights**

  Interpret data to find the insights that actually matter, rather than
  just producing a correct but unremarkable summary.

  Ask AI: "What separates a genuinely useful insight from data from a
  technically correct but unremarkable observation?"

- [ ] **Understand what metadata is, and maintain it under direction**

  Explain what metadata is, and maintain the information stored in
  metadata repositories under the direction of others.

  Ask AI: "Explain, for a beginner, what metadata actually is and why
  keeping it accurate matters for people using the data later."

- [ ] **Understand testing, and execute scripts under supervision**

  Correctly execute test scripts under supervision, and understand the
  role testing plays and how it works.

  Ask AI: "What is a test script actually checking for in a data pipeline,
  and why does someone need to execute it correctly rather than just run
  it?"

- [ ] **Investigate problems at the right level**

  Investigate problems in systems, processes and services with an
  understanding of what level a problem sits at — strategic, tactical or
  operational — and contribute to implementing remedies and preventative
  measures.

  Ask AI: "What's the difference between a strategic, a tactical and an
  operational data problem, with an example of each?"

- [ ] **Stay aware of innovation in data tools and uses**

  Show an awareness of opportunities for innovation with new tools and
  uses of data, without needing to champion their adoption yet.

  Ask AI: "How would a trainee analytics engineer stay aware of emerging
  data tools and techniques without it becoming a full-time pursuit
  alongside their actual job?"

- [ ] **Communicate technical concepts in plain language**

  Explain why it's important to communicate technical concepts in
  non-technical language, and explain the different ways internal and
  external stakeholders need to be communicated with.

  Ask AI: "Give me a technical data concept, and show me two ways to
  explain it — one for a data specialist and one for someone with no
  technical background."

You do not need to master all of this before you start. Ask your manager or
a more senior colleague which of these your team leans on most, and start
there.
