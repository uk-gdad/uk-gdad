# Chief data officer - start here

Welcome. If you are working as, or working toward, a chief data officer role,
this page is for you: what the role is about, where to find everything else
this repository has for it, and a pathway for building the areas it names.

**One thing to know before you start.** The framework publishes this role and
its duties, but marks the role level as **not in use** and gives no expectations
against any of its named skills. That is the framework's own position, not a
gap in this page. So instead of a level-graded pathway, what follows works
from the role's own duties, grouped under the skill names the framework does
publish for it.

## Purpose

A chief data officer makes sure their organisation can actually use data to
make better decisions, policies and services — and builds the environment
where data protection and privacy sit alongside access, sharing and
innovation, rather than in permanent tension with them. That is a genuinely
hard balance: too much caution and data never gets used for anything; too
little and trust breaks the moment something goes wrong. Everything below —
strategy, governance, architecture, risk, innovation and capability — is in
service of holding that balance across an entire organisation, not just a
team.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. There is no level above a chief data
officer to measure yourself against, so use an AI tool as a sounding board:
describe a real decision you're weighing and ask it to argue the case you
haven't made yet.

- [ ] **Strategic data planning**

  Define a vision and strategy for data that lets the organisation achieve
  its objectives and aligns with the wider government data strategy, and
  work with senior leaders to align priorities and secure their support for
  it — a strategy nobody senior has bought into is just a document.

  Ask AI: "What makes a government data strategy something senior leaders
  actually act on, rather than a document they sign off and forget?"

- [ ] **Data governance leadership**

  Lead the organisation in actually implementing the data strategy, not just
  publishing it, and ensure the organisation uses data ethically and
  complies with data standards and regulations as routine practice rather
  than a periodic audit exercise.

  Ask AI: "How does a chief data officer build data governance that people
  follow because it helps them, rather than governance that gets treated as
  a compliance obstacle?"

- [ ] **Data architecture**

  Determine what data and data technology the organisation actually needs
  and how to source it, make sure data can be easily used, reused and shared
  by the people who need it, and keep raising the quality of data used
  across the organisation.

  Ask AI: "What does 'data that's easy to reuse and share' actually require
  architecturally, beyond just storing it somewhere central?"

- [ ] **Data risk management**

  Work with cyber security so that data stays secure while remaining
  accessible and controllable — protection and usability are not
  opposites here, they are both requirements, and trading one off against
  the other completely is usually the wrong call.

  Ask AI: "Give me an example of a data access decision where security and
  usability genuinely pull in different directions, and how a chief data
  officer might resolve it without just picking one side."

- [ ] **Innovation in digital, data and technology**

  Create an environment where data protection and privacy are balanced with
  access, sharing and innovation, so that new uses of data can be explored
  safely rather than either blocked by caution or waved through without
  scrutiny.

  Ask AI: "How does an organisation encourage genuine innovation with data
  without loosening the protections that keep that data trustworthy?"

- [ ] **Capability building for digital, data and technology**

  Increase data literacy across the organisation by coaching it, not just
  training a specialist team, and make sure the organisation has people with
  the level of data skills it actually needs, now and as needs change.

  Ask AI: "What does raising data literacy across an entire organisation look
  like in practice, beyond running a one-off training course?"

Ask a peer chief data officer, in your sector or another part of government,
which of these has taken them longest to get right — that conversation will
teach you more than any general reading on data leadership.
