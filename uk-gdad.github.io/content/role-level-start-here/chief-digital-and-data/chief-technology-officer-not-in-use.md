# Chief technology officer - start here

Welcome. If you are working as, or working toward, a chief technology officer
role, this page is for you: what the role is about, where to find everything
else this repository has for it, and a pathway for building the areas it
names.

**One thing to know before you start.** The framework publishes this role and
its duties, but marks the role level as **not in use** and gives no
expectations against any of its named skills. That is the framework's own
position, not a gap in this page. So instead of a level-graded pathway, what
follows works from the role's own duties, grouped under the skill names the
framework does publish for it.

## Purpose

A chief technology officer is the organisation's technology strategist,
making the decisions about technical direction that other senior leaders
build their own plans around. That means treating technology as a genuine
part of the organisation's strategy rather than a delivery function that
implements decisions made elsewhere, and holding two things in tension at
once: infrastructure reliable enough that nobody thinks about it, and enough
appetite for new technology that the organisation doesn't fall behind. Get
the balance wrong in either direction — too cautious or too eager — and it
shows up everywhere, not just in technology.

## Learning pathway

Work through these roughly in order. There is no level above this role to
measure yourself against, so use an AI tool as a sounding board: describe a
real decision you're weighing and ask it to argue the case you haven't made
yet.

- [ ] **Strategic technology planning**

  Create a strategy and vision for technology that aligns with the
  organisation's broader strategy, lead the organisation in actually
  implementing it, and advocate for technology as a critical part of that
  broader strategy rather than a supporting function to it.

  Ask AI: "How does a technology leader make sure their technology strategy
  genuinely serves the organisation's broader goals, rather than becoming a
  separate roadmap that only technologists read?"

- [ ] **Technology evaluation**

  Evaluate the organisation's existing range of technologies and work out
  which ones it actually needs — which means being honest about what to
  retire or consolidate, not just what to add.

  Ask AI: "What's a sound way to evaluate whether an existing technology is
  still earning its place in an organisation, rather than staying just
  because replacing it is disruptive?"

- [ ] **Financial decision making for technology**

  Evaluating technology always has a cost side: weighing what's worth
  investing in against what isn't, and working within appropriate levels of
  risk rather than either avoiding all risk or accepting it without
  scrutiny.

  Ask AI: "How should a technology leader compare the cost of a new
  technology investment against the risk of not making it, in a way a board
  without a technical background can follow?"

- [ ] **Technology architecture**

  Put in place a technology architecture that gives reliable, permanent
  infrastructure, and make sure systems and services stay continuously
  operational and resistant to attacks and disasters, not just resilient on
  paper.

  Ask AI: "What's the difference between infrastructure that's reliable
  under normal conditions and infrastructure that's genuinely resilient
  under attack or disaster, and how does an architecture account for both?"

- [ ] **Innovation in digital, data and technology**

  Enable the organisation to be innovative and adopt new technologies that
  offer genuine value — value to the organisation's actual objectives, not
  novelty for its own sake.

  Ask AI: "How does a technology leader tell the difference between a new
  technology worth adopting and one that's just fashionable right now?"

- [ ] **Capability building for digital, data and technology**

  Make sure people have the tools, technical skills and working environment
  they need, and create working practices and processes that genuinely
  support effective delivery rather than existing for their own sake.

  Ask AI: "What working practices most affect whether a technology team can
  actually deliver well, beyond having the right individual technical
  skills?"

Ask a peer chief technology officer, in your sector or another part of
government, which of these has taken them longest to get right — that
conversation will teach you more than any general reading on technology
leadership.
