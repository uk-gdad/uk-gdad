# Head of command and control - upskill resources

This page focuses on learning and growth resources for the work role of head of command and control.

- The resources include links to online courses, blogs, research articles, videos, and books.

- These help with job upskilling, work training, professional development, and career advancement.

- These are intended to be good starting points, not a comprehensive catalogue.

At this role level you own the strategy roadmap, integration and capability of the team, and you ensure the tools are fit for purpose and meet business requirements. The resources below emphasise operations strategy, tooling and platform judgement, availability and capacity standards, service compliance and risk, and building a capability rather than staffing a rota.

We welcome constructive feedback to improve this page and these lists.

- GitHub issues and/or pull requests are welcome.

- The resources are found via AI searches then go through human-in-the-loop proofing.

## Research articles

- [Alert Fatigue in Security Operations Centres: Research Challenges and Opportunities](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3723158): An ACM Computing Surveys review, useful for judging vendor claims about alert reduction.

- [Artificial Intelligence for IT Operations (AIOps) Workshop White Paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.06054): The AIOps landscape from resource management to failure prediction, which is the market you are buying tools in.

- [A Survey of Time Series Anomaly Detection Methods in the AIOps Domain](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.00393): What anomaly detection actually does and where it fails, which is what a tooling evaluation should probe.

- [AIOps for log anomaly detection in the era of LLMs: A systematic literature review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2667305325001346): Where log anomaly detection has genuinely reached, as opposed to where it is marketed.

- [Predicting operators reliability for control room alarm management using knowledge-based Bayesian networks](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0951832026000773): Operator reliability under alarm load, which is a design constraint on your roadmap.

- [Capacity and availability management by quantitative project management in the IT service industry](https://www.emerald.com/ajq/article-abstract/13/2/163/60850/Capacity-and-availability-management-by): A quantitative approach to capacity and availability tracking from baselined data.

- [Effectiveness of IT Service Management Capability: Value Co-Creation and Value Facilitation Mechanisms](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/333777833_Effectiveness_of_IT_Service_Management_Capability_Value_Co-Creation_and_Value_Facilitation_Mechanisms): A study of 256 organisations on how service management capability creates value.

- [Measuring IT Service Management Capability: Scale Development and Empirical Validation](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/299616426_Measuring_IT_Service_Management_Capability_Scale_Development_and_Empirical_Validation): A validated capability measure, better than a self-assessed maturity score for a roadmap baseline.

- [Cyber Incident Response and Planning: A Flexible Approach](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1361372321000099): An incident response maturity model developed against practice requirements.

- [Enhancing reliability in AI inference services: An empirical study on real production incidents](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.07424): An analysis of 156 high-impact production incidents with implications for platform governance.

## Professional development tips

- Hold an expert-level certificate in your service management framework, since it is expected at this role level
- Judge tools against the work your team actually does, and pilot before committing to an integration
- Set availability and capacity standards explicitly, then check that they are implemented rather than assumed
- Make service compliance and risk visible on a schedule rather than after an incident forces it
- Build capability deliberately, including succession, because a control centre that depends on individuals is fragile
- Take groups of services together when looking for improvement, since the biggest wins are usually at the seams
- Keep enough operational contact to tell an honest status report from a comfortable one
