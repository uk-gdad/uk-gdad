# Operations analyst - upskill resources

This page focuses on learning and growth resources for the work role of operations analyst.

- The resources include links to online courses, blogs, research articles, videos, and books.

- These help with job upskilling, work training, professional development, and career advancement.

- These are intended to be good starting points, not a comprehensive catalogue.

At this role level you provide administrative support to the command and control centre manager and alert support staff when needed. The resources below emphasise the monitoring tools you will live inside, registering incidents accurately, tracking assets and components, and the availability and capacity concepts behind the numbers on your screens.

We welcome constructive feedback to improve this page and these lists.

- GitHub issues and/or pull requests are welcome.

- The resources are found via AI searches then go through human-in-the-loop proofing.

## Coursera courses

- [Technical Support Fundamentals](https://www.coursera.org/learn/technical-support-fundamentals): Computer hardware, networks, operating systems and troubleshooting, which is what the alerts you triage are actually about.

- [ITSM Foundations: Optimizing IT Service Management](https://www.coursera.org/learn/itsm-foundations-optimizing-it-service-management): Core ITSM principles including how incidents, problems and requests move through a service management platform.

- [Foundations of Site Reliability Engineering Training](https://www.coursera.org/learn/foundations-of-site-reliability-engineering-training): Core reliability principles and how modern operations teams measure whether a service is healthy.

## Research articles

- [A Survey of Time Series Anomaly Detection Methods in the AIOps Domain](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.00393): How monitored KPIs become time series and how anomalies in them are detected. Background for what your tooling is doing.

- [Artificial Intelligence for IT Operations (AIOps) Workshop White Paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.06054): The AIOps landscape from resource management to failure prediction and remediation.

- [Alert Fatigue in Security Operations Centres: Research Challenges and Opportunities](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3723158): An ACM Computing Surveys treatment of alert fatigue. The security context differs but the human problem is identical.

- [Quantifying the effectiveness of an alarm management system through human factors studies](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0098135414000945): Evaluates alarm systems by how operators actually interact with them, not by their specifications.

- [Monitor Alarm Fatigue: An Integrative Review](https://array.aami.org/doi/full/10.2345/0899-8205-46.4.268): A review from clinical monitoring, where alarm fatigue has been studied longest and most rigorously.

- [Implementation and impacts of IT Service Management in the IT function](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0268401223000099): A systematic review of empirical ITSM research from 2012 to 2021.

- [Ensuring Efficient IT Service Management to Increase Information Systems Availability](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/337847919_Ensuring_Efficient_IT_Service_Management_to_Increase_Information_Systems_Availability): Connects service management practice to measured availability.

- [Improving the Software Problem Management Process: A Case Study](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/11908562_5): A case study of the difficulties in a real problem management process.

- [An IT Service Management Literature Review: Challenges, Benefits, Opportunities and Implementation Practices](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/349857851_An_IT_Service_Management_Literature_Review_Challenges_Benefits_Opportunities_and_Implementation_Practices): Benefits, challenges and practices across 47 studies.

- [Continual Process Improvement for ITIL Service Operations: A Lean Perspective](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10580530.2019.1587576): A five-step improvement framework for service operations.

## Professional development tips

- Work towards a Level 3 service management framework qualification, since it is expected at this role level
- Learn what each dashboard actually measures, because watching a metric you cannot explain is not monitoring
- Register incidents with enough context that the responder does not have to come back to you
- Track and correct asset information as you go, since accuracy decays fastest under time pressure
- Notice which alerts never turn out to matter, and raise them, because tolerating noise is how fatigue starts
- Learn the escalation paths before you need one at three in the morning
- Ask to sit in on incident reviews, since seeing the outcome changes what you record at the start
