# Operational control manager - upskill resources

This page focuses on learning and growth resources for the work role of operational control manager.

- The resources include links to online courses, blogs, research articles, videos, and books.

- These help with job upskilling, work training, professional development, and career advancement.

- These are intended to be good starting points, not a comprehensive catalogue.

At this role level you are accountable for day-to-day IT operations and act as the escalation point for incidents and problems. You manage the runbooks and the processes around service continuity, test them, and are on call according to business requirements. The resources below emphasise leading incident investigation, runbook and continuity discipline, high-impact change, and the team dynamics that make a control centre sustainable.

We welcome constructive feedback to improve this page and these lists.

## Research articles

- [Alert Fatigue in Security Operations Centres: Research Challenges and Opportunities](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3723158): An ACM Computing Surveys review of alert fatigue and what has been tried against it.

- [Predicting operators reliability for control room alarm management using knowledge-based Bayesian networks](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0951832026000773): Operator reliability under alarm load, which is a staffing and design question you own.

- [Quantifying the effectiveness of an alarm management system through human factors studies](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0098135414000945): Evaluating alarm systems by how operators actually use them.

- [Monitor Alarm Fatigue: An Integrative Review](https://array.aami.org/doi/full/10.2345/0899-8205-46.4.268): A review from clinical monitoring, the domain where alarm fatigue has been studied most rigorously.

- [A Comprehensive Survey on Root Cause Analysis in (Micro) Services](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.00803v1): Root cause analysis techniques across metrics, traces, and logs.

- [Improved Root Cause Analysis Supporting Resilient Production Systems](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0278612522001273): The 14 challenges and 17 enablers of root cause analysis, most of which are addressable by a manager.

- [Cyber Incident Response and Planning: A Flexible Approach](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1361372321000099): Develops an incident response maturity model aligned to practice requirements.

- [Enhancing reliability in AI inference services: An empirical study on real production incidents](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.07424): An analysis of 156 high-impact production incidents with lessons that generalise.

- [Minimizing Incident Response Time Using Quantum Computing](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11219-023-09632-6): Speculative in method, but a useful framing of response time as an optimisation problem at scale.

- [Continual Process Improvement for ITIL Service Operations: A Lean Perspective](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10580530.2019.1587576): A five-step improvement framework you can run with your team.

## Professional development tips

- Test the runbooks on a schedule, because an untested runbook is a document rather than a control
- Demonstrate knowledge of the ITIL life cycle or capability elements, since it is expected at this role level
- Lead investigations by protecting the space for evidence rather than by supplying the conclusion
- Track alert volume per shift as a health metric for the team, not only for the systems
- Use agile health checks to surface team problems, then act on what they show
- Look across groups of services for the compounding availability wins, since single-service tuning runs out
- Make sure being on call is genuinely sustainable, including for you, since fatigue at the escalation point is a systemic risk
