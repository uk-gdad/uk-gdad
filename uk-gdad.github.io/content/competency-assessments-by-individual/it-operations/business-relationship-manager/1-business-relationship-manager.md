# Business Relationship Manager - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This is for you: a chance to rate your own current strengths against the
baseline the UK GDAD Profession Capability Framework sets for this role
level, using the framework's own words for what it covers.

A business relationship manager, sometimes known as ‘business partner’, establishes and maintains meaningful relationships with colleagues to deliver positive impact across the organisation. A business relationship manager is responsible for ensuring consistency in engagement across the business.

Work through the competency matrix below, one skill at a time, then fill in
the overall determination at the end. Treat this as a mirror, not a test —
there's no assessor watching, so being honest with yourself only costs you a
slightly less flattering picture today in exchange for a far more useful one.
Read `## Advisory` before you begin.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating you
give yourself here must never be treated as equivalent to a formal
assessment for a hiring, promotion, pay or performance decision. Use it for
your own reflection, and talk to your manager, a mentor, or your
organisation's people development team about what you find.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you're rating
yourself against what this level actually expects rather than the level
above or below it. Then think back over recent, real examples of your work —
not how you'd like to have handled something, but what you actually did.

**Rate on evidence, not impression.** For every skill, try to recall a
specific moment that supports the rating you give yourself. If you can't
think of one, that's useful information too — it might mean the rating
should be lower, or just that you haven't had the chance to show this skill
yet, which is worth noting separately.

**Be consistent with yourself.** Try to apply the same standard across every
skill, rather than being generous where you feel confident and harsh where
you feel unsure. Coming back to this after a few days, with fresh examples in
mind, often sharpens a rating more than sitting with it in one go.

**Be kind, and be honest.** This only works if you can look at the result
without flinching. A low rating here is not a verdict on you — it's a
starting point. Everyone doing this role, at every level, has skills they are
still building.

## Rating scale

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

This is a proficiency scale, not a frequency scale — it rates how well someone does the thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it.

**Worked example — Stakeholder relationship management (IT operations).** Someone who has never had to negotiate with or influence a stakeholder sits at 0 or 1. Someone who can describe negotiating with a stakeholder, influencing a decision, and dealing with a challenging situation to remove a blocker sits at 2 — this level's own baseline. Someone who additionally shows they build long-term strategic relationships and communicate with stakeholders regularly, balancing mutual needs with a clear focus on user needs, is showing evidence closer to level 3.

## About this role level

The business relationship manager establishes and maintains meaningful relationships with colleagues to deliver positive impact across the organisation. Across the whole role, a business relationship manager brings together different functions to ensure value across the organisation, works strategically across teams to ensure digital and data investment adds value, explores the perspectives, challenges and ambitions of teams to shape a pipeline of opportunities, acts as a point of focus for colleagues who influence strategic direction, and sets the conditions for a culture of creating meaningful connections. The role runs across three levels: business relationship manager, senior business relationship manager, and lead business relationship manager.

The business relationship manager level is the entry level of this role. The framework describes it as being responsible for ensuring consistency in engagement across the business. At this level, you prepare documentation and obtain support to progress new ideas, opportunities and investments, use relevant systems to maintain a view of activity and business-related knowledge, and support continuous improvement activities that mature the capability of the team.

The framework names six skills at this level: business analysis (IT operations) (investigating problems and opportunities in existing processes, and contributing to recommending solutions, and working with stakeholders to identify objectives and potential benefits), ownership and initiative (owning an issue until a new owner has been found or the problem has been mitigated or resolved), service focus (taking inputs and establishing coherent frameworks that work), service management framework knowledge (holding a Level 3 service management framework qualification), stakeholder relationship management (IT operations) (negotiating with and influencing stakeholders and managing relationships effectively, influencing decisions, dealing with challenging situations and removing blockers, and building long-term strategic relationships while communicating clearly and regularly, supporting mutual needs and commitments while focusing on user needs), strategic thinking (working within a strategic context, communicating how activities meet strategic goals, and contributing to the development of strategy and policies), and user focus (identifying and engaging with users or stakeholders to collate user needs evidence, understanding and defining research that fits user needs, and using quantitative and qualitative data about users to turn user focus into outcomes).

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Business analysis (IT operations)

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a business relationship manager to investigate problems and opportunities in existing processes, contributing to recommending solutions, and to work with stakeholders to identify objectives and potential benefits.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. "Contribute to recommending" and "work with stakeholders to identify" describe active, guided participation, which sits above Beginner but below the independent leadership language used at the levels above.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe how they would investigate a business problem or opportunity.
- 1 — Beginner: Investigates a problem with close direction, and contributes little to the eventual recommendation.
- 2 — Intermediate: Investigates problems and opportunities largely independently, contributes meaningfully to recommendations, and works with stakeholders to establish objectives and benefits.
- 3 — Advanced: Leads investigative work into problems and opportunities, and produces recommendations stakeholders act on.
- 4 — Expert: Absorbs large amounts of conflicting information across the organisation and produces solutions others could not have reached alone.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe a business problem or opportunity you investigated, including how you identified objectives and potential benefits with stakeholders.
- Give a short written scenario describing a process with an obvious inefficiency, and ask what you would investigate first and why.
- Ask a referee how much of your analysis work needed rework before it was usable.

### Skill: Ownership and initiative

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a business relationship manager to own an issue until a new owner has been found or the problem has been mitigated or resolved.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. Holding an issue through to a proper resolution or handover is solid, independent working practice for someone in a stakeholder-facing role from day one.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Drops issues once they become someone else's to chase.
- 1 — Beginner: Holds an issue for a short time but needs reminding to follow it through.
- 2 — Intermediate: Consistently holds an issue until it is mitigated, resolved, or properly handed to a new owner.
- 3 — Advanced: Proactively chases stalled issues across organisational boundaries to keep them moving.
- 4 — Expert: Is someone colleagues rely on to make sure nothing important falls through the cracks.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe an issue you owned across more than one team, and how you knew when it was properly closed.
- Ask a referee whether issues you owns tend to get properly closed out or forgotten.

### Skill: Service focus

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a business relationship manager to take inputs and establish coherent frameworks that work.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. Turning a set of inputs into a working framework, for their own area of engagement, is applied independent work rather than a beginner exercise.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe how their engagement work fits into a wider service.
- 1 — Beginner: Follows an existing engagement framework without adapting it.
- 2 — Intermediate: Takes a set of inputs — stakeholder needs, organisational priorities, existing tools — and puts together a working approach for their own engagement area.
- 3 — Advanced: Builds coherent engagement frameworks that colleagues elsewhere in the function adopt too.
- 4 — Expert: Designs engagement frameworks that span the whole function.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe a time you pulled together several inputs into a coherent way of engaging with a business area.
- Think about how you'd critique a fragmented engagement process and suggest a more coherent alternative.

### Skill: Service management framework knowledge

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a business relationship manager to show they have a Level 3 service management framework qualification.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. A named Level 3 qualification maps to solid, certified working knowledge — Intermediate on this scale — rather than beginner exposure or expert-level certification.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Holds no service management framework qualification and cannot explain basic service management terms.
- 1 — Beginner: Is working towards a Level 3 qualification, or holds an entry-level award only.
- 2 — Intermediate: Holds a Level 3 service management framework qualification and applies its concepts correctly in engagement work.
- 3 — Advanced: Holds Level 3 and demonstrates working knowledge beyond what this level formally asks for.
- 4 — Expert: Holds an expert-level service management qualification and is asked to explain framework concepts to others.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd provide or describe your service management framework qualification and certificate level.
- Think about how you'd explain one framework concept relevant to your engagement work.

### Skill: Stakeholder relationship management (IT operations)

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a business relationship manager to negotiate with and influence stakeholders and manage relationships effectively, to influence decisions, deal with challenging situations and remove blockers, and to build long-term strategic relationships, communicating clearly and regularly with stakeholders while supporting mutual needs and commitments and focusing on user needs.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. This is the core skill of the role, and the framework's own wording describes real influence and relationship-building at this entry level — more than a beginner's exposure, but the wider strategic scope named at more senior levels is not yet expected.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has never negotiated with a stakeholder or attempted to influence a decision.
- 1 — Beginner: Manages a straightforward stakeholder relationship with support, but struggles when a situation becomes challenging.
- 2 — Intermediate: Negotiates with and influences stakeholders effectively, deals with challenging situations and removes blockers, and sustains a relationship with regular, clear communication.
- 3 — Advanced: Builds and sustains long-term strategic relationships across a wider set of stakeholders, arbitrating between competing needs.
- 4 — Expert: Is trusted with the organisation's most difficult stakeholder relationships and coaches others on how to manage them.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe a stakeholder relationship that became difficult and how you worked through it.
- Ask for an example of a blocker you removed by influencing a decision, and what you said or did.
- Ask a referee how you are regarded by the stakeholders you engage with.

### Skill: Strategic thinking

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a business relationship manager to work within a strategic context and communicate how activities meet strategic goals, and to contribute to the development of strategy and policies.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. "Contribute to the development" is participation rather than ownership of strategy, which fits Intermediate at the entry level of this role.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot connect their own work to the organisation's wider strategic goals.
- 1 — Beginner: Repeats a stated strategic goal but cannot explain how their own work supports it.
- 2 — Intermediate: Clearly explains how their day-to-day activities meet strategic goals, and contributes useful input when strategy or policy is being developed.
- 3 — Advanced: Defines strategies and policies, and guides others in how to work within the strategic context.
- 4 — Expert: Sets the strategic direction others in the function follow.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd explain how a piece of your recent work connects to a wider organisational strategic goal.

### Skill: User focus

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a business relationship manager to identify and engage with users or stakeholders to collate user needs evidence, to understand and define research that fits user needs, and to use quantitative and qualitative data about users to turn user focus into outcomes.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. Actively collating and using user evidence to inform outcomes is applied independent practice, matching Intermediate.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Does not engage with users or use any user evidence in their work.
- 1 — Beginner: Collects user feedback when asked, but does not yet connect it to decisions.
- 2 — Intermediate: Actively collates user needs evidence from stakeholders and uses it to inform routine decisions.
- 3 — Advanced: Defines what research would answer a given user needs question, and combines quantitative and qualitative data to shape outcomes.
- 4 — Expert: Is sought out by others for how to turn user evidence into a concrete change.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask you for an example of user or stakeholder feedback you collected and how it changed an outcome.
- Think about how you'd describe how you would find out what a business area actually needs from a proposed investment.
