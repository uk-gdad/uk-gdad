# Senior DevOps Engineer (management) - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This is for you: a chance to rate your own current strengths against the
baseline the UK GDAD Profession Capability Framework sets for this role
level, using the framework's own words for what it covers.

Commonly referred to as ‘DevOps engineers’, development operations engineers support the development and operation of software through tools, environments and practices. A senior DevOps engineer delivers and integrates software to form a complete DevOps toolchain. They are responsible for planning and designing large groups of stories.

Work through the competency matrix below, one skill at a time, then fill in
the overall determination at the end. Treat this as a mirror, not a test —
there's no assessor watching, so being honest with yourself only costs you a
slightly less flattering picture today in exchange for a far more useful one.
Read `## Advisory` before you begin.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating you
give yourself here must never be treated as equivalent to a formal
assessment for a hiring, promotion, pay or performance decision. Use it for
your own reflection, and talk to your manager, a mentor, or your
organisation's people development team about what you find.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you're rating
yourself against what this level actually expects rather than the level
above or below it. Then think back over recent, real examples of your work —
not how you'd like to have handled something, but what you actually did.

**Rate on evidence, not impression.** For every skill, try to recall a
specific moment that supports the rating you give yourself. If you can't
think of one, that's useful information too — it might mean the rating
should be lower, or just that you haven't had the chance to show this skill
yet, which is worth noting separately.

**Be consistent with yourself.** Try to apply the same standard across every
skill, rather than being generous where you feel confident and harsh where
you feel unsure. Coming back to this after a few days, with fresh examples in
mind, often sharpens a rating more than sitting with it in one go.

**Be kind, and be honest.** This only works if you can look at the result
without flinching. A low rating here is not a verdict on you — it's a
starting point. Everyone doing this role, at every level, has skills they are
still building.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

This is a **proficiency** scale, not a frequency scale: it rates how well someone does the thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it.

**Worked example — Development process optimisation.** At Intermediate (2), someone could describe analysing a process and spotting one improvement
opportunity, without yet leading a team through delivering it. At Advanced
(3) — this level's baseline — they can describe leading and developing a
team of experts to deliver a service improvement, and helping set policy or
standards for how changes are implemented, not just proposing the change
themselves. At Expert (4), above this level's baseline, they would be setting
optimisation strategy across more than one team.

## About this role level

Development operations (DevOps) engineers — commonly called "DevOps
engineers" — support the development and operation of software through tools,
environments and practices. The role is responsible for underpinning good
development processes, including managing tools and testing environments,
central code control, maintaining development standards, and writing software
that automates systems.

This assessment covers the **Senior DevOps engineer** level, **management
track** — the fourth step of the role's ladder, where the role splits into a
technical and a management track. The full ladder, in order, is: Apprentice
DevOps engineer, Junior DevOps engineer, DevOps engineer, Senior DevOps
engineer (a separate technical-track document, and this level, management
track), Lead DevOps engineer (technical and management), and Principal
DevOps engineer (technical and management).

A senior DevOps engineer delivers and integrates software to form a complete
DevOps toolchain, and is responsible for planning and designing large groups
of stories. At this level, a person is expected to:

- transform technical requirements into an effective DevOps toolchain to
  enable product delivery
- provide coaching and mentoring to more junior colleagues
- ensure that deployment strategies for products are repeatable, scalable and
  highly available
- have deep technical knowledge, providing support to delivery teams and
  solving complex problems

These accountabilities are shared with the technical track. What
distinguishes the management track is how several of this level's skills are
framed: policy-setting, resourcing and leading a team of experts, rather than
personal technical delivery alone. This level names ten skills: Availability
and capacity management, Development process optimisation, Information
security, Modern development standards, Programming and build (software
engineering), Prototyping, Service support, Systems design, Systems
integration, and User focus.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Availability and capacity management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior DevOps engineer on the management track is expected to ensure the
correct implementation of availability and capacity management standards and
procedures, to identify capacity issues and implement the required changes,
and to initiate remedial action.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. "Ensure the correct implementation of... standards
and procedures" and "initiate remedial action" describe governing how the
skill is delivered, not only doing it themselves — a management
responsibility that needs a strong, independent grasp of the underlying
practice to discharge well.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot describe what standards or procedures govern availability
  and capacity management in their area.
- 1 — Beginner: knows the standards exist but has not checked whether they
  are being followed.
- 2 — Intermediate: checks that standards and procedures are being followed
  in one part of the service, with guidance on what "correct implementation"
  looks like.
- 3 — Advanced: ensures standards and procedures are correctly implemented
  across the services they are responsible for, identifies capacity issues
  before they bite, and initiates remedial action rather than waiting to be
  told.
- 4 — Expert: sets or revises the standards and procedures themselves, across
  more than one team.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe a time you identified a capacity issue and
  initiated remedial action, including who you needed to involve.
- Ask how you check that availability and capacity procedures are actually
  being followed, rather than assuming you are.

### Skill: Development process optimisation

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior DevOps engineer on the management track is expected to analyse
current processes, identify and implement opportunities to optimise them,
lead and develop a team of experts to deliver service improvements, and help
evaluate and establish requirements for the implementation of changes by
setting policy and standards.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. "Lead and develop a team of experts" and "setting
policy and standards" are unambiguously management responsibilities, well
beyond the technical track's guided-contribution wording for this same skill
at this level, and support a strong, independent baseline.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not led a team through a process improvement.
- 1 — Beginner: contributes to a process improvement led by someone else.
- 2 — Intermediate: leads a small, well-defined process improvement with
  guidance.
- 3 — Advanced: analyses current processes, leads and develops a team of
  experts to deliver improvements, and helps set the policy or standards a
  change is implemented against.
- 4 — Expert: sets development process strategy across more than one team.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe a service improvement you led a team of
  experts to deliver, including how you developed the team's capability
  along the way.
- Ask about a policy or standard you helped establish for how process
  changes get implemented.

### Skill: Information security

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior DevOps engineer on the management track is expected to design
solutions and services with security controls included, specifically
engineered to mitigate security threats.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. The wording is shared with the technical track, but a
manager at this level is also accountable for how consistently security
practice is applied across the people they lead, so evidence should show both
personal design skill and oversight of others' practice.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: designs a solution without considering security threats, and
  does not check whether their team does either.
- 1 — Beginner: names relevant threats but needs help choosing controls.
- 2 — Intermediate: includes appropriate controls in their own designs, with
  some guidance on complex cases.
- 3 — Advanced: consistently designs solutions with appropriate security
  controls, and sets the expectation that the team they lead does the same.
- 4 — Expert: is accountable for security practice across more than one
  team's solutions.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe how you make sure the people you manage
  build security controls into your designs, not just how you do it
  yourself.
- Ask about a time you caught a security gap in a design produced by
  someone you were responsible for.

### Skill: Modern development standards

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior DevOps engineer on the management track is expected to apply modern
development standards and support others in applying them.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. "Support others in applying them" is a people
responsibility that fits naturally with a management track, and matches this
level's coaching and mentoring accountability.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: does not apply standards consistently, and does not support
  others in doing so.
- 1 — Beginner: applies standards to their own work with reminders.
- 2 — Intermediate: applies standards consistently to their own work.
- 3 — Advanced: applies standards consistently and actively builds the
  capability of the people they manage to apply them, for example through
  structured feedback rather than one-off corrections.
- 4 — Expert: sets standards for a wider group and measures how well they are
  embedded.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask you how you have built a team's capability to apply a
  development standard consistently, not just corrected individual mistakes.
- Ask for an example of coaching a colleague through a standard you were
  applying incorrectly.

### Skill: Programming and build (software engineering)

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior DevOps engineer on the management track is expected to collaborate
with others when necessary to review specifications, and to use the agreed
specifications to design, code, test and document programs or scripts of
medium-to-high complexity, using the right standards and tools.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. This wording matches the technical track's, and a
manager at this level still needs a strong, independent grasp of the craft
they lead others in, to make credible resourcing and quality decisions.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot independently build from an agreed specification.
- 1 — Beginner: needs significant support even on medium-complexity work.
- 2 — Intermediate: builds medium-complexity programs with occasional
  guidance.
- 3 — Advanced: designs, codes, tests, corrects and documents programs or
  scripts of medium-to-high complexity independently, and uses that
  credibility to make sound resourcing and quality decisions for their team.
- 4 — Expert: is the technical benchmark their team is measured against.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask to see a medium-to-high complexity program or script you
  built recently, and how your hands-on skill informs how you resource
  similar work for your team.
- Ask about a resourcing or quality decision you made that depended on your own technical judgement.

### Skill: Prototyping

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior DevOps engineer on the management track is expected to approach
prototyping as a team activity, actively soliciting prototypes and testing
with others, to establish design patterns and iterate them, and to use a
variety of prototyping methods and choosing the most appropriate.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. The wording matches the technical track's, and a
strong, independent grasp fits this level's overall seniority, with the
management angle showing in how someone resources and organises the
team's prototyping activity rather than only doing it themselves.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not organised or led a team prototyping activity.
- 1 — Beginner: takes part in prototyping activities others organise.
- 2 — Intermediate: organises prototyping activities with occasional
  guidance on method choice.
- 3 — Advanced: consistently chooses appropriate prototyping methods,
  establishes reusable design patterns, and organises the team's time and
  effort around prototyping activity that matters.
- 4 — Expert: not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask you how you decide, as a manager, when to allocate team time
  to prototyping versus building the real thing.
- Ask about a reusable design pattern that came from a prototyping exercise
  you organised.

### Skill: Service support

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior DevOps engineer on the management track is expected to identify,
locate and fix service faults.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. Worth noting plainly, not smoothing over: this
level's own wording for this skill is simpler than the technical track's
("complex service faults; advise others..."), so the baseline sits at
Intermediate — solid personal practice — while someone's broader
management accountability is better evidenced through how they resource and
oversee the team's fault response rather than through this skill's wording
alone.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot locate the source of a typical service fault.
- 1 — Beginner: locates and fixes a straightforward fault with guidance.
- 2 — Intermediate: identifies, locates and fixes a service fault
  independently, for faults of typical difficulty.
- 3 — Advanced: does this for less obvious faults and organises how their
  team responds to a wider incident.
- 4 — Expert: not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd walk through a service fault you diagnosed and fixed
  independently.
- Ask how you organise your team's response when a service fault affects
  more than one component.

### Skill: Systems design

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior DevOps engineer on the management track is expected to design
systems characterised by medium levels of risk, impact, and business or
technical complexity, to select appropriate design standards, methods and
tools and ensure they are applied effectively, and to review the systems
designs of others to ensure the selection of appropriate technology, efficient
use of resources and integration of multiple systems and technology.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Reviewing others' designs and ensuring standards are
applied effectively across a team are management responsibilities that
naturally sit with this track, on top of independent design ability.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot design a system of medium risk or complexity
  independently.
- 1 — Beginner: designs simple systems with heavy support.
- 2 — Intermediate: designs medium-complexity systems with some guidance,
  and does not yet review others' work.
- 3 — Advanced: independently designs systems of medium risk, impact and
  complexity, ensures design standards are applied effectively across their
  team, and reviews others' designs for good technology and resource choices.
- 4 — Expert: sets design standards across more than one team.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe how you ensure design standards are applied
  effectively across the people you manage, not only in your own work.
- Ask about a time reviewing a colleague's design led to a better choice of
  technology or resource use.

### Skill: Systems integration

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior DevOps engineer on the management track is expected to define the
integration build, co-ordinate build activities across systems, and
understand how to undertake and support integration testing activities.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Defining and co-ordinating a build across systems is
inherently a co-ordination and resourcing responsibility, which fits this
track, and matches this level's toolchain-ownership accountability.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot define what an integration build needs to cover.
- 1 — Beginner: contributes to an integration build defined by someone else.
- 2 — Intermediate: defines a straightforward integration build with

  properly supported.
- 4 — Expert: not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe an integration build you defined and how
  you co-ordinated the different people and systems involved.

### Skill: User focus

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior DevOps engineer on the management track is expected to collaborate
with user researchers and represent users internally, to explain the
difference between user needs and the desires of the user, to champion user
research to focus on all users, to prioritise and define approaches to
understand the user story while guiding others in doing so, and to offer
recommendations on the best tools and methods to use.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Championing research and guiding others is naturally
a leadership responsibility, and matches this level's coaching and mentoring
accountability.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: does not represent user needs in their team's work or decisions.
- 1 — Beginner: uses user evidence when given it, but does not champion
  research.
- 2 — Intermediate: engages with user researchers and uses evidence, with
  some guidance on prioritising approaches.
- 3 — Advanced: represents users internally, champions research across the
  team they manage, and guides others on the right approach to understanding
  a user story.
- 4 — Expert: not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe how you have championed user research
  within a team you managed, including a time it changed a decision.
- Ask about a time you guided someone on the right approach to understanding
  a user story.
