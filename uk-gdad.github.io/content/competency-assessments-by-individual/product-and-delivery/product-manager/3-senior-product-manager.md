# Senior Product Manager - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This is for you: a chance to rate your own current strengths against the
baseline the UK GDAD Profession Capability Framework sets for this role
level, using the framework's own words for what it covers.

A product manager makes sure that their products provide value and achieve the right outcomes by balancing user and business needs. A senior product manager can work on a group of products that share a value proposition, or a single product that is complex, high risk or sensitive. They are responsible for product quality and value, working with teams to identify, implement and improve strategies and tactics for their products. They might also be involved in parts of programme management activity.

Work through the competency matrix below, one skill at a time, then fill in
the overall determination at the end. Treat this as a mirror, not a test —
there's no assessor watching, so being honest with yourself only costs you a
slightly less flattering picture today in exchange for a far more useful one.
Read `## Advisory` before you begin.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating you
give yourself here must never be treated as equivalent to a formal
assessment for a hiring, promotion, pay or performance decision. Use it for
your own reflection, and talk to your manager, a mentor, or your
organisation's people development team about what you find.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you're rating
yourself against what this level actually expects rather than the level
above or below it. Then think back over recent, real examples of your work —
not how you'd like to have handled something, but what you actually did.

**Rate on evidence, not impression.** For every skill, try to recall a
specific moment that supports the rating you give yourself. If you can't
think of one, that's useful information too — it might mean the rating
should be lower, or just that you haven't had the chance to show this skill
yet, which is worth noting separately.

**Be consistent with yourself.** Try to apply the same standard across every
skill, rather than being generous where you feel confident and harsh where
you feel unsure. Coming back to this after a few days, with fresh examples in
mind, often sharpens a rating more than sitting with it in one go.

**Be kind, and be honest.** This only works if you can look at the result
without flinching. A low rating here is not a verdict on you — it's a
starting point. Everyone doing this role, at every level, has skills they are
still building.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point
**proficiency** scale — how well someone does the thing, on the evidence
available, not how often they do it or how they feel about it.

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

Take **Product management**, one of the skills this role level names. Someone
at Intermediate (2) plans prioritised work across two teams with guidance, and
can explain how dependencies between them were handled when asked. Someone at
Advanced (3) independently sets strategy and vision for a group of products
and plans across multiple teams, minimising dependencies and risk, without
needing that plan checked by someone more senior. Someone at Expert (4) does
all of that and also coaches or mentors other product managers in the
discipline, visibly growing someone else's capability rather than only
delivering their own products well. The gap between 3 and 4 here is not about
scale of delivery — it is whether someone is actively developing other
product managers, not only doing the work themselves.

## About this role level

A **product manager** makes sure that their products provide value and
achieve the right outcomes by balancing user and business needs. In this
role, whatever the level, a product manager works with multidisciplinary
teams, bringing together their views, user needs and business needs to frame
the problem; works with multidisciplinary teams to deliver the right
outcomes for users and the organisation; makes decisions on priorities for
their teams; and ensures teams comply with appropriate standards.

The role has five levels, in order: 1. Associate product manager;
2. Product manager; **3. Senior product manager — you are assessing this
level**; 4. Lead product manager; 5. Head of product.

A **senior product manager** can work on a group of products that share a
value proposition, or on a single product that is complex, high risk or
sensitive. They are responsible for product quality and value, working with
teams to identify, implement and improve strategies and tactics for their
products, and they might also be involved in parts of programme management
activity.

At this role level, a person is expected to lead products through the
different phases of the product life cycle, from discovery through
development, delivery and continuous improvement, to retirement; to make
decisions based on the overall quality and value of their products; to
manage associate product managers and product managers; and to be involved
in recruiting associate product managers, product managers and contractors.

The skills this level names, in the order the summary lists them, are:

- Agile and Lean practices
- Applying user-centred insights
- Creating value for money
- Life cycle management
- Managing product outcomes
- Product leadership
- Product management
- Stakeholder relationship management
- Strategic ownership

For orientation before you reach the matrix below: three of these skills —
**Agile and Lean practices**, **Applying user-centred insights**, and
**Stakeholder relationship management** — carry exactly the same published
wording at this level as they do at product manager. That is the framework as
published, not an oversight in this document. The distinction the framework
draws for those three skills sits in this level's broader accountabilities —
managing people, and working on higher-risk or more complex products — not in
different wording, and the baselines below reflect that.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Agile and Lean practices

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior product manager compares and selects the most appropriate delivery
methods and processes for the work in front of them, recognises and adapts
when something is not working while encouraging experimentation, blends
approaches depending on context rather than following one method rigidly,
helps teams measure, evaluate and visualise outcomes, and encourages
reflective practice so ways of working keep improving.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. The published wording is unchanged from product
manager, but a senior product manager works across a group of products or a
single complex, high-risk product, and manages other product managers — so
the baseline for independently choosing a method and coaching someone else
through a change is higher than it is at product manager.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has not worked within an agile, lean or other iterative delivery
  approach; cannot describe how any team they have led plans or reviews its
  work.
- 1 — Beginner: Uses the method a team already has in place, but needs
  support to judge whether it still suits a more complex or higher-risk
  product.
- 2 — Intermediate: Chooses a delivery approach for their own team with some
  guidance, and runs a retrospective that produces one real change.
- 3 — Advanced: Blends or changes delivery approaches across more than one
  team without needing sign-off from someone more senior, and coaches
  another product manager through an experiment that was not working for
  their team.
- 4 — Expert: Sets the delivery approach used across a group of related
  products, and is someone other senior product managers consult when a
  method choice is contested or high-stakes.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe a time you changed the delivery approach
  for more than one team, and why.
- Ask a referee whether you coached another product manager
  through an agile or lean problem.
- Structured interview question: "Tell me about a delivery method choice you
  made for a complex or high-risk product. What made it the right choice
  for that context?"

### Skill: Applying user-centred insights

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior product manager uses user insights to represent users in internal
discussions, advocates for research to be completed with all types of user of
the product or service, uses user needs to agree the work that needs to be
done and to determine priorities with the team, and works with others to
understand and recommend tools for getting the right user insight.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. The wording is identical to product manager, but at
this level the product may be complex, high risk or sensitive, so the
baseline is that user insight independently shapes strategic decisions, not
only day-to-day prioritisation.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot cite user research when discussing the strategy or
  direction of a complex or high-risk product.
- 1 — Beginner: Uses research findings that already exist, but needs support
  to judge what is missing for a higher-risk product.
- 2 — Intermediate: Brings insight into prioritisation discussions, and asks
  for research to be extended to a group of users that was missed.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently commissions research to fill a gap that
  matters for a complex or sensitive product, changes strategic direction on
  the strength of it, and helps other product managers judge what insight
  their own products need.
- 4 — Expert: Builds how insight reaches decision making across a group of
  products, and is someone consulted when a decision on a high-risk
  product needs defending against a lack of evidence.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of a strategic decision on a complex or high-risk
  product that changed because of user research.
- Ask a referee whether you have advised other product managers on
  getting insight for hard-to-reach users.
- Look back at a research plan or evidence base you built for a sensitive
  product, keeping the description free of confidential detail.

### Skill: Creating value for money

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior product manager advocates for user needs, ensuring user value is
balanced against cost and value for the organisation; determines the benefit
of a product and persuades others it is the right product to use; writes or
contributes to business cases; communicates business-value propositions; and
helps others make value-based decisions, influencing the direction of
development.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. This wording moves from estimating and deciding for
your own roadmap to persuading others and writing business cases, and a
senior product manager is expected to influence and help other people make
value-based decisions across teams — that is mastery, not only independent
working.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot explain the value case for their product beyond its
  immediate roadmap.
- 1 — Beginner: Can describe a value case someone else wrote, but needs
  support to persuade a sceptical stakeholder.
- 2 — Intermediate: Contributes sections to a business case with guidance,
  and communicates a value proposition to a receptive audience.
- 3 — Advanced: Writes a business case largely unaided, and persuades a
  stakeholder who started sceptical that a product is the right one to
  invest in.
- 4 — Expert: Is trusted to write or lead business cases that secure
  significant investment, and actively coaches other product managers to
  make and communicate value-based decisions rather than doing it for them.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for a business case or investment paper you wrote or led,
  and the outcome.
- Structured interview question: "Tell me about a time you had to persuade
  a sceptical senior stakeholder that your product was the right
  investment. What worked?"
- Ask a referee whether you helped other product managers make a
  value-based decision, with an example.

### Skill: Life cycle management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior product manager works and consults with the right people at the
right time to move through the life cycle and deliver value, uses evidence to
decide when a team should continue, change direction or stop, identifies the
tools and techniques a phase of the life cycle requires, and guides
colleagues and stakeholders through different phases of the life cycle.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Unlike product manager, this wording drops "with some
support" and adds guiding colleagues and stakeholders through the life cycle,
so the baseline is independently running phase decisions, including the
harder call to stop.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe how they would decide whether a team should
  continue, change direction or stop.
- 1 — Beginner: Can describe the phases of a life cycle, but needs support
  to decide when a team should change direction.
- 2 — Intermediate: Moves a product through a phase transition with some
  guidance, and identifies the tools a phase needs.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently decides, on evidence, that a team should
  change direction or stop, and guides colleagues or stakeholders through
  what that means, without a more senior person making the call for them.
- 4 — Expert: Is someone consulted on the hardest stop-or-continue
  decisions across a group of products, and their evidence-based calls set
  the pattern others follow.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask about a time you recommended stopping or changing direction on a
  product, and the evidence behind it.
- Ask a referee how you guided stakeholders through a difficult
  phase decision.
- Look back at a stage-gate paper, or a stop-or-continue recommendation, you
  authored.

### Skill: Managing product outcomes

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior product manager leads product teams to create effective methods for
measuring product outcomes, advocates for data-driven decision making, helps
others prioritise to make better choices, uses data to drive continuous
improvement that increases quality and user experience, and identifies
opportunities to share and connect product data.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. The wording moves from defining and using metrics for
your own product to leading teams to build measurement methods and helping
others prioritise, so the baseline includes leading that work for people
other than yourself.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe a method for measuring outcomes beyond a single
  metric someone else set.
- 1 — Beginner: Uses outcome metrics that already exist, but needs support
  to help another product manager improve theirs.
- 2 — Intermediate: Contributes to building a measurement method for a
  team, and uses data to suggest one improvement.
- 3 — Advanced: Leads a team to build its own outcome-measurement method,
  helps another product manager prioritise using their data, and identifies
  a genuine opportunity to connect data across products.
- 4 — Expert: Sets the standard for outcome measurement across a product
  area, and is someone other product managers ask to help interpret
  contested or ambiguous data.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask about a measurement method you led a team to build, and
  what it changed.
- Ask a referee about a time you helped another product manager
  prioritise using data.
- Look back at a dashboard, outcomes framework or cross-product data-sharing
  proposal you led.

### Skill: Product leadership

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior product manager seeks opportunities for reuse and learning across
their organisation, represents product management in their area in
conversations with senior stakeholders, protects teams from unnecessary
distractions and requests to change direction, coaches and manages others in
product management, and leads some product management community activities,
participating in cross-government communities.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. This wording adds representing product management to
senior stakeholders and coaching and managing others, which product manager
does not carry, so the baseline moves from advocating for one product to
leading and protecting more than one team.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Does not speak for product management with senior stakeholders,
  and has no part in coaching or managing anyone in the discipline.
- 1 — Beginner: Represents their own product to senior stakeholders when
  asked, but needs support to coach another product manager.
- 2 — Intermediate: Protects more than one team from an unnecessary
  distraction, and gives another product manager occasional informal
  coaching.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently represents product management to senior
  stakeholders across their area, formally manages or coaches other product
  managers, and leads a community activity rather than only attending one.
- 4 — Expert: Is a recognised voice for product management at a senior level
  across the organisation, and their coaching visibly develops other product
  managers into more senior roles.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of representing product management to a senior
  stakeholder, and the outcome.
- Ask a referee about your line management or coaching of
  another product manager.
- Ask about your leadership of a product management community activity,
  guild or cross-government forum.

### Skill: Product management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior product manager sets the strategy and vision for their products,
collaborating with others; creates a prioritised roadmap for their products
and uses it to discuss product strategy at all levels; plans prioritised work
across multiple teams, minimising dependencies and risks; supports multiple
teams in delivering quality outcomes in the right way; and coaches and
mentors others in product management.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. This moves from creating one roadmap and recommending
methods to setting strategy across products, planning across multiple teams
and coaching others in the discipline, which is the mastery this scale
reserves for teaching and guiding others, not only delivering well yourself.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has no strategy, roadmap or planning artefact that spans more
  than one team.
- 1 — Beginner: Manages a roadmap for one team, and needs significant
  support to plan across more than one.
- 2 — Intermediate: Plans prioritised work across two teams with guidance,
  and can explain how dependencies were handled.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently sets strategy and vision for a group of
  products, plans across multiple teams while minimising dependencies and
  risk, and supports those teams to deliver quality outcomes.
- 4 — Expert: Does all of the above, and actively coaches or mentors other
  product managers in the discipline, visibly growing their capability
  rather than only delivering their own products well.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe a strategy and roadmap that spanned
  multiple teams, and how dependencies were managed.
- Ask a referee for an example of you coaching or mentoring
  another product manager, and what changed for that person.
- Look back at a cross-team plan, strategy document or product review you led.

### Skill: Stakeholder relationship management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior product manager works with the team to develop and maintain an
understanding of stakeholders, works with the team to develop and implement
stakeholder communications strategies, identifies and resolves issues while
influencing stakeholders and managing relationships effectively, and builds
long-term strategic relationships, communicating clearly and regularly with
stakeholders.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. The wording is unchanged from product manager, but at
this level stakeholder relationships often sit around a complex, high-risk or
sensitive product and may include recruitment and programme-level contacts,
so the baseline for handling them independently, including the hardest ones,
is higher.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot name the stakeholders around a complex or sensitive
  product, or describe what tension exists between them.
- 1 — Beginner: Maintains relationships with straightforward stakeholders
  but needs support with a difficult or senior one.
- 2 — Intermediate: Manages a stakeholder map for a complex product with
  some support, and resolves a routine disagreement without escalation.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently builds and sustains a relationship with a
  senior or difficult stakeholder on a high-risk or sensitive product, and
  resolves a genuine conflict of interest between two stakeholders without
  it needing to be escalated above them.
- 4 — Expert: Is trusted with the organisation's most senior or sensitive
  stakeholder relationships across a group of products, and coaches other
  product managers in handling theirs.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of managing a stakeholder relationship on a complex,
  high-risk or sensitive product, keeping the description free of
  confidential detail.
- Ask a referee about a genuine conflict of interest you resolved
  between stakeholders.
- Look back at a stakeholder communications strategy for a higher-risk product you authored.

### Skill: Strategic ownership

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior product manager ensures that the vision and strategy deliver the
intended benefits and policy intent, using evidence to challenge this when
needed; works with stakeholders to get agreement on the strategic approach,
building trust and adapting based on feedback; and proactively reviews and
iterates the strategy based on user and organisation insights.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. This wording adds challenging the strategy against
policy intent with evidence and building stakeholder agreement and trust,
which is a step up from contributing to and reviewing a strategy, so the
baseline is independently owning and defending it.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe the intended benefits or policy intent behind
  their product's strategy.
- 1 — Beginner: Can repeat the strategy and its intended benefits, but needs
  support to challenge it when evidence suggests it is wrong.
- 2 — Intermediate: Reviews the strategy against its benefits with guidance,
  and gets stakeholder agreement on straightforward points.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently uses evidence to challenge the strategy or
  policy intent when it is not working, builds genuine stakeholder trust and
  agreement on the strategic approach, and iterates the strategy
  proactively rather than waiting to be asked.
- 4 — Expert: Owns strategic direction across a group of related products,
  and their evidence-based challenge to policy intent changes decisions at a
  senior level.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask about a time you used evidence to challenge a strategy or
  its assumed benefits, and what happened.
- Ask a referee about you building stakeholder trust and
  agreement on a strategic approach.
- Look back at a strategy document showing evidence of iteration based on
  insight.
