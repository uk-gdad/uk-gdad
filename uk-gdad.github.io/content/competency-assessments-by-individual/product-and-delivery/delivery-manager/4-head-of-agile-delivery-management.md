# Head of (Agile) Delivery Management - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This is for you: a chance to rate your own current strengths against the
baseline the UK GDAD Profession Capability Framework sets for this role
level, using the framework's own words for what it covers.

A delivery manager is accountable for the delivery of products and services. A head of (Agile) delivery management is an experienced practitioner who exemplifies what good looks like across the delivery roles.

Work through the competency matrix below, one skill at a time, then fill in
the overall determination at the end. Treat this as a mirror, not a test —
there's no assessor watching, so being honest with yourself only costs you a
slightly less flattering picture today in exchange for a far more useful one.
Read `## Advisory` before you begin.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating you
give yourself here must never be treated as equivalent to a formal
assessment for a hiring, promotion, pay or performance decision. Use it for
your own reflection, and talk to your manager, a mentor, or your
organisation's people development team about what you find.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you're rating
yourself against what this level actually expects rather than the level
above or below it. Then think back over recent, real examples of your work —
not how you'd like to have handled something, but what you actually did.

**Rate on evidence, not impression.** For every skill, try to recall a
specific moment that supports the rating you give yourself. If you can't
think of one, that's useful information too — it might mean the rating
should be lower, or just that you haven't had the chance to show this skill
yet, which is worth noting separately.

**Be consistent with yourself.** Try to apply the same standard across every
skill, rather than being generous where you feel confident and harsh where
you feel unsure. Coming back to this after a few days, with fresh examples in
mind, often sharpens a rating more than sitting with it in one go.

**Be kind, and be honest.** This only works if you can look at the result
without flinching. A low rating here is not a verdict on you — it's a
starting point. Everyone doing this role, at every level, has skills they are
still building.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

This is a **proficiency** scale, not a frequency scale: it rates how well someone does the thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it or
how they feel about it.

**Worked example — Team dynamics and collaboration.** At 3, someone
identifies and rectifies dynamics issues within their own teams and uses a
structured tool such as a health check to diagnose them. At 4 — the baseline
for this role level — someone changes organisational structures into
fixable, sustainable designs, leads strategy for an entire organisation,
makes and justifies decisions with high levels of risk, impact and
complexity, builds consensus between organisations or highly independent
stakeholders, solves and unblocks issues at the highest level within teams or
departments, and coaches a whole organisation on team dynamics and conflict
resolution. The framework's wording for this skill jumps sharply from the
senior delivery manager level, and you should look for evidence at
that same scale — organisational, not team-level.

## About this role level

The product and delivery profession's delivery manager role exists to make a
team accountable for the delivery of products and services. A delivery
manager builds and maintains an effective team, removes obstacles, keeps the
team focused on what matters most, and helps the team improve how it works.

The role has four levels: associate delivery manager, delivery manager,
senior delivery manager, and head of (Agile) delivery management. This
assessment is for the fourth and most senior of those.

A **head of (Agile) delivery management** is an experienced practitioner who
exemplifies what good looks like across the delivery roles. At this role
level, the framework says someone will represent and champion the role
within their department, across government and in industry; lead the
community of practice for this role and build capability and excellence in
Agile, Lean practice; be responsible for the recruitment of the right people
to the right teams; support professional development and continuous
improvement of their community; work with other heads of roles to promote
effective cross-functional delivery; be a skilled team leader who can
confidently communicate the value of the role to digital and non-digital
stakeholders; and be credible and influential across departments. This is a
step beyond senior delivery manager in kind, not just in degree: the job is
now defined by leading and building the whole delivery management discipline
across an organisation and beyond it, rather than by delivering — or even
escalating — any single, however complex, piece of work.

The framework names the same nine skills for this level as for senior
delivery manager: Agile and Lean practices, Commercial management,
Communicating between the technical and non-technical, Financial management,
Life cycle management, Maintaining delivery momentum, Making a process work,
Planning, and Team dynamics and collaboration. Where the framework's own
wording for a skill is identical between the two levels, this assessment says
so rather than inventing a difference the framework does not state, and
grounds the baseline and indicators in this level's distinct
accountability — representing, leading and building the discipline — rather
than in different skill wording.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Agile and Lean practices

**What the framework expects at this level**

A head of (Agile) delivery management is expected to coach and lead teams in
Agile and Lean good practices, to create and tailor the right approach for a
team, challenging, evaluating and iterating the approach through the life
cycle, to experiment with new and innovative ways of working to improve
delivery across the organisation, and to act as a recognised expert and
advocate for Agile and Lean approaches.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. The framework's wording is unchanged from the senior
delivery manager level; at this level "across the organisation" reach and
recognised advocacy are exercised in service of building capability and
excellence for the whole delivery management community, which is this
level's own defining accountability.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has never coached another delivery manager or team in Agile or
  Lean practice.
- 1 — Beginner: Applies an Agile approach chosen by someone else.
- 2 — Intermediate: Independently applies Agile principles within their own
  team.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently compares and selects the most appropriate
  method for a team's context.
- 4 — Expert: Coaches and leads teams across the delivery management
  community, sets and evolves Agile and Lean good practice as part of
  building the community's capability, and is recognised — across the
  organisation and, credibly, beyond it — as an expert advocate.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of how you have built the delivery management
  community's capability in Agile and Lean practice, not just coached one
  team.
- Ask about an innovation in ways of working that spread across the
  organisation because of your advocacy.
- Ask a referee whether you are recognised as an expert beyond your own department, for example across government.

### Skill: Commercial management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A head of (Agile) delivery management is expected to act as the escalation
point and resolve large or high risk commercial management issues, and to
coach others in appropriate commercial management.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "Act as the escalation point" for the largest or
highest-risk commercial issues, and coaching others, matches the scale's own
definition of expert practice — resolving what nobody more junior could, and
building other people's capability to do the same.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has never resolved a large or high-risk commercial management
  issue.
- 1 — Beginner: Acts as a point of contact for a straightforward supplier
  relationship.
- 2 — Intermediate: Manages supplier relationships independently.
- 3 — Advanced: Takes responsibility for a complex supplier relationship and
  negotiates it independently.
- 4 — Expert: Is the escalation point for large or high-risk commercial
  management issues across the delivery function, resolves them, and coaches
  other delivery managers in commercial management practice.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of a large or high-risk commercial issue you were escalated to resolve.
- Ask how you have coached another delivery manager in commercial
  management.
- Ask a referee whether you are genuinely the organisation's
  escalation point for the most serious commercial issues affecting delivery.

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What the framework expects at this level**

A head of (Agile) delivery management is expected to mediate between people
and strengthen relationships, adopting the appropriate communication method
with stakeholders at all levels, to manage stakeholder expectations and
moderate difficult discussions about high risk and complex topics even within
constrained timescales, and to speak on behalf of, and represent the
community to, large audiences inside and outside the organisation.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. The framework's wording is unchanged from the senior
delivery manager level; this level's own accountability to "confidently
communicate the value of the role to digital and non-digital stakeholders"
and be "credible and influential across departments" is what representing
the community to large audiences concretely means here.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Struggles to explain a technical concept to a non-technical
  audience or vice versa.
- 1 — Beginner: Communicates simple, low-stakes information clearly.
- 2 — Intermediate: Supports discussions within a multidisciplinary team.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently hosts a difficult discussion involving senior
  stakeholders on their own team's work.
- 4 — Expert: Mediates and strengthens relationships across stakeholders at
  every level, moderates high-risk discussions under time pressure, and
  represents delivery management to large, senior audiences inside and
  outside the organisation — including, credibly, other government
  departments or industry.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example where you represented the delivery management
  discipline to an audience outside your own department.
- Ask about a high-risk, time-constrained discussion you moderated at
  organisational level.
- Ask a referee whether you are trusted to speak on behalf of the
  discipline to the most senior or external audiences.

### Skill: Financial management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A head of (Agile) delivery management is expected to negotiate, influence or
set budgets in complex environments, and to write or input into business
cases and communicate business-value propositions.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. The framework's wording is unchanged from the senior

### Skill: Life cycle management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A head of (Agile) delivery management is expected to lead and coach teams
through different phases of the life cycle, to ensure effective support for
the product or service and its users, to predict, prevent, mitigate and
manage potential risks at different phases of the life cycle, and to
contribute to the assessment of other teams, providing guidance and support.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "Lead and coach teams", predicting and managing risk
proactively across the life cycle, and contributing to the assessment of
other teams describes mastery exercised on behalf of multiple teams — the
capability-building role this level's own description names.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe how life cycle risks should be predicted or
  managed proactively.
- 1 — Beginner: Manages delivery through a phase with significant support.
- 2 — Intermediate: Manages delivery through a phase with some support.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently manages a product or service through the life
  cycle and adapts approach as new information emerges.
- 4 — Expert: Leads and coaches multiple teams through life cycle phases,
  predicts and manages risk proactively rather than reactively, and
  contributes to formally assessing other teams' life cycle management,
  providing guidance and support.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example where you coached more than one team through a
  life cycle phase.
- Ask how you have predicted and mitigated a life cycle risk before it
  became a live issue.
- Ask a referee whether you have formally assessed and supported
  another team's life cycle management.

### Skill: Maintaining delivery momentum

**What the framework expects at this level**

A head of (Agile) delivery management is expected to optimise the delivery
flow of teams, to actively address the most complicated risks, issues and
dependencies including where ownership exists outside the team or no clear
ownership exists, and to identify innovative ways to unblock issues.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. The framework's wording is unchanged from the senior
delivery manager level; at this level it is exercised across the whole
delivery function someone leads, which is this level's own defining
scope.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe what affects delivery flow across more than one
  team.
- 1 — Beginner: Tracks a dependency or risk within their own team, with
  support.
- 2 — Intermediate: Tracks and manages risks and dependencies within their
  own team independently.
- 3 — Advanced: Facilitates their own team's delivery flow and addresses
  risks owned outside the team.
- 4 — Expert: Optimises delivery flow across the whole function or
  department, resolves the most complicated, ownerless risks and issues, and
  is someone others bring genuinely novel blockers to.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of a department-wide delivery flow issue you
  resolved.
- Ask about the most difficult, ownerless risk or issue you have unblocked.
- Ask a referee whether other heads of role bring your hardest delivery
  problems to you.

### Skill: Making a process work

**What the framework expects at this level**

A head of (Agile) delivery management is expected to identify and challenge
organisational processes of increasing complexity and those that are
unnecessarily complicated, to add value and coach the organisation to inspect
and adapt processes, and to guide teams through the implementation of a new
process.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. The framework's wording is unchanged from the senior
delivery manager level, so the baseline holds steady — this level's growth in
accountability shows up more clearly in the skills tied directly to
representing and building the community than here.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot identify a process that is getting in the way of a team's
  output.
- 1 — Beginner: Supports a team in following an established process.
- 2 — Intermediate: Helps a team adapt its own process, with some support.
- 3 — Advanced: Identifies and challenges an unnecessarily complicated
  organisational process, coaches the organisation to inspect and adapt it,
  and guides teams through implementing a new one across the department.
- 4 — Expert: Not distinctly expected at this level by the framework's own
  wording for this skill.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example where you challenged a department-wide process
  and helped change it.
- Ask how you guided teams through adopting a genuinely new process at
  scale.
- Ask a referee whether your process challenges have led to

### Skill: Planning

**What the framework expects at this level**

A head of (Agile) delivery management is expected to lead a continual
planning process in a very complex environment, to plan beyond product
delivery, to identify dependencies in plans across services and co-ordinate
delivery, and to coach other teams as the central point of expertise.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. The framework's wording is unchanged from the senior
delivery manager level; being "the central point of expertise" that coaches
other teams matches this level's own community-building accountability
directly.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe a dependency that spans more than one service.
- 1 — Beginner: Contributes to a plan with support.
- 2 — Intermediate: Manages complex dependencies for their own team's plan.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently prioritises high-value work using data and
  removes blockers affecting their own team's plan.
- 4 — Expert: Leads a continual planning process across a very complex,
  department-wide environment, co-ordinates dependencies across services, and
  is the organisation's central point of planning expertise that other heads
  of role and teams turn to.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example where you led planning across a genuinely
  department-wide or cross-organisational environment.
- Ask how other teams have used you as a central point of planning

### Skill: Team dynamics and collaboration

**What the framework expects at this level**

A head of (Agile) delivery management is expected to change organisational
structures to fixable and sustainable designs, to lead on strategy for an
entire organisation, joining up business needs with innovative analysis, to
make and justify decisions characterised by high levels of risk, impact and
complexity, to effectively build consensus between organisations — private or
public — or highly independent and diverse stakeholders, to solve and unblock
issues within teams or departments at the highest level, to understand the
psychology of a team and have strong mediation skills, and to coach an
organisation on team dynamics and conflict resolution.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. This skill's wording changes sharply from the senior
delivery manager level — organisational structure, entire-organisation
strategy, and coaching a whole organisation on team dynamics — describing
mastery exercised at the highest scale the framework names for any level in
this role.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has never changed an organisational structure or led strategy at
  organisational level.
- 1 — Beginner: Identifies a team dynamics issue when pointed out.
- 2 — Intermediate: Notices and raises team dynamics issues independently.
- 3 — Advanced: Identifies and rectifies dynamics issues within their own
  teams, using structured tools such as health checks.
- 4 — Expert: Changes organisational structures into sustainable designs,
  leads strategy for an entire organisation, makes and justifies decisions
  with high risk, impact and complexity, builds consensus between
  organisations or highly independent stakeholders, solves the hardest
  team or department-level issues, and coaches a whole organisation on team
  dynamics and conflict resolution.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example where you changed an organisational structure,
  and the reasoning behind the new design.
- Ask about a decision you made or justified that carried high risk, impact
  and complexity.
- Ask a referee whether you have built consensus between genuinely
  independent or competing organisations or stakeholder groups.
