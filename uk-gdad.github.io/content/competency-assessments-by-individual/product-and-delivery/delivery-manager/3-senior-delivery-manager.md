# Senior Delivery Manager - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This is for you: a chance to rate your own current strengths against the
baseline the UK GDAD Profession Capability Framework sets for this role
level, using the framework's own words for what it covers.

A delivery manager is accountable for the delivery of products and services. A senior delivery manager is accountable for the effective delivery of complex, high risk products and services. The role is similar to a delivery manager role, but senior delivery managers have more experience across a range of products and services, throughout the entire product life cycle, and have greater responsibility and accountability as the main point of escalation.

Work through the competency matrix below, one skill at a time, then fill in
the overall determination at the end. Treat this as a mirror, not a test —
there's no assessor watching, so being honest with yourself only costs you a
slightly less flattering picture today in exchange for a far more useful one.
Read `## Advisory` before you begin.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating you
give yourself here must never be treated as equivalent to a formal
assessment for a hiring, promotion, pay or performance decision. Use it for
your own reflection, and talk to your manager, a mentor, or your
organisation's people development team about what you find.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you're rating
yourself against what this level actually expects rather than the level
above or below it. Then think back over recent, real examples of your work —
not how you'd like to have handled something, but what you actually did.

**Rate on evidence, not impression.** For every skill, try to recall a
specific moment that supports the rating you give yourself. If you can't
think of one, that's useful information too — it might mean the rating
should be lower, or just that you haven't had the chance to show this skill
yet, which is worth noting separately.

**Be consistent with yourself.** Try to apply the same standard across every
skill, rather than being generous where you feel confident and harsh where
you feel unsure. Coming back to this after a few days, with fresh examples in
mind, often sharpens a rating more than sitting with it in one go.

**Be kind, and be honest.** This only works if you can look at the result
without flinching. A low rating here is not a verdict on you — it's a
starting point. Everyone doing this role, at every level, has skills they are
still building.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

This is a **proficiency** scale, not a frequency scale: it rates how well someone does the thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it or
how they feel about it.

**Worked example — Agile and Lean practices.** At 3, someone independently
compares and selects the most appropriate delivery method for a team's
context. At 4 — the baseline for this role level — someone coaches and
leads teams in Agile and Lean good practice, creates and tailors the right
approach for a team while challenging and iterating it through the whole
life cycle, experiments with new ways of working across the organisation, and
is recognised as an expert advocate for the approach. The distinction you should look for is whether someone's influence reaches beyond
their own team — do other delivery managers or teams change how they work
because of this person's coaching or advocacy?

## About this role level

The product and delivery profession's delivery manager role exists to make a
team accountable for the delivery of products and services. A delivery
manager builds and maintains an effective team, removes obstacles, keeps the
team focused on what matters most, and helps the team improve how it works.

The role has four levels: associate delivery manager, delivery manager,
senior delivery manager, and head of (Agile) delivery management. This
assessment is for the third of those.

A **senior delivery manager** is accountable for the effective delivery of
complex, high risk products and services. The framework describes the role as
similar to a delivery manager role, but says senior delivery managers have
more experience across a range of products and services throughout the entire
product life cycle, and have greater responsibility and accountability as the
main point of escalation. At this role level, the framework says someone
will have strong communication skills and engage senior stakeholders, and
will coach and mentor delivery managers. This is a step up from delivery
manager in the risk and complexity of the work, in breadth of experience
across the life cycle, and in being an escalation point and coach for other
delivery managers rather than only leading their own team.

The framework names nine skills for this level: Agile and Lean practices,
Commercial management, Communicating between the technical and non-technical,
Financial management, Life cycle management, Maintaining delivery momentum,
Making a process work, Planning, and Team dynamics and collaboration.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Agile and Lean practices

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior delivery manager is expected to coach and lead teams in Agile and
Lean good practices, to create and tailor the right approach for a team,
challenging, evaluating and iterating the approach through the life cycle, to
experiment with new and innovative ways of working to improve delivery across
the organisation, and to act as a recognised expert and advocate for Agile
and Lean approaches.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "Coach and lead teams", "act as a recognised expert and
advocate" and improving delivery "across the organisation" are, by the
scale's own definition, expert-level behaviour — mastery exercised to guide
other people and teams, not just someone's own delivery.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has never coached another delivery manager or team in Agile or
  Lean practice.
- 1 — Beginner: Applies an Agile approach chosen by someone else.
- 2 — Intermediate: Independently applies Agile principles within their own
  team.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently compares and selects the most appropriate
  method for their own team's context.
- 4 — Expert: Coaches and leads more than one team in Agile and Lean good
  practice, tailors and iterates the approach across a whole life cycle, and
  is recognised across the organisation as an expert advocate whose
  experiments with new ways of working change how other teams deliver.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example where you coached another delivery manager or
  team, and what changed as a result.
- Ask about an experiment with a new way of working you led that spread
  beyond your own team.
- Ask a referee whether you are recognised across the organisation as
  an expert in Agile and Lean practice.

### Skill: Commercial management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior delivery manager is expected to take responsibility for complex
relationships with contracted suppliers, to identify appropriate contractual
frameworks and identify appropriate suppliers, to negotiate with contracted
suppliers, and to get good value out of contracts and suppliers.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. "Take responsibility for complex relationships" and
"negotiate" describe independent commercial judgement exercised with real
consequence, a clear step up from acting as a day-to-day point of contact —
though the framework does not describe this level as setting commercial
standards for others, so the baseline stops short of Expert.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe what makes a supplier relationship complex or how
  to negotiate one.
- 1 — Beginner: Acts as a point of contact for a supplier under guidance.
- 2 — Intermediate: Manages straightforward supplier relationships
  independently.
- 3 — Advanced: Takes responsibility for a complex supplier relationship,
  identifies the appropriate contractual framework and suppliers, negotiates
  directly, and can demonstrate the value achieved from a contract.
- 4 — Expert: Not distinctly expected at this level by the framework's own
  wording for this skill.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of a complex supplier relationship you took
  responsibility for, and how you negotiated it.
- Ask how you chose an appropriate contractual framework for a piece of
  work.
- Ask a referee whether you have demonstrably got good value from a
  supplier relationship you managed.

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior delivery manager is expected to mediate between people and
strengthen relationships, adopting the appropriate communication method with
stakeholders at all levels, to manage stakeholder expectations and moderate
difficult discussions about high risk and complex topics even within
constrained timescales, and to speak on behalf of, and represent the
community to, large audiences inside and outside the organisation.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Representing "the community" to large audiences inside
and outside the organisation, and moderating high-risk discussions under
time pressure, describes mastery exercised on behalf of a whole discipline,
not just a single team's stakeholders.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Struggles to explain a technical concept to a non-technical
  audience or vice versa.
- 1 — Beginner: Communicates simple, low-stakes information clearly.
- 2 — Intermediate: Supports discussions within a multidisciplinary team.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently hosts a difficult discussion involving senior
  stakeholders on their own team's work.
- 4 — Expert: Mediates and strengthens relationships across stakeholders at
  every level, moderates high-risk, complex discussions under time pressure,
  and represents the delivery community to large audiences inside and outside
  the organisation.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example where you represented the delivery community
  to an audience outside the organisation, or a large internal one.
- Ask about a high-risk, time-constrained discussion you moderated.
- Ask a referee whether you are trusted to speak on behalf of the
  delivery function to senior or external audiences.

### Skill: Financial management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior delivery manager is expected to negotiate, influence or set budgets
in complex environments, and to write or input into business cases and
communicate business-value propositions.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. "Negotiate, influence or set budgets in complex
environments" and writing business cases describes independent financial
judgement exercised with real weight, a clear step up from monitoring and
reporting on a budget someone else set.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe what goes into a business case or budget
  negotiation.
- 1 — Beginner: Monitors and reports on a budget set by someone else.
- 2 — Intermediate: Balances cost against value in day-to-day delivery
  decisions.
- 3 — Advanced: Negotiates, influences or sets a budget in a complex
  environment, and writes or contributes meaningfully to a business case,
  communicating the business-value proposition clearly.
- 4 — Expert: Not distinctly expected at this level by the framework's own
  wording for this skill.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of a budget you negotiated or set in a complex
  environment.
- Ask to see, or hear about, a business case you wrote or significantly
  contributed to.
- Ask a referee whether your business-value arguments have
  persuaded senior decision-makers.

### Skill: Life cycle management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior delivery manager is expected to work and consult with the right
people at the right time to move through the life cycle and deliver value, to
use evidence to decide when a team should continue, change direction or stop,
to identify tools and techniques required at different phases of the life
cycle, and to guide colleagues and stakeholders through different phases of
the life cycle.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "Use evidence to decide when a team should continue,
change direction or stop" is one of the most consequential judgements the
framework describes for this role, and "guide colleagues and stakeholders"
through the life cycle means exercising that judgement on others' behalf, not
only someone's own team's.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe how a decision to stop or change direction on a
  product would be made.
- 1 — Beginner: Manages delivery through a phase with significant support.
- 2 — Intermediate: Manages delivery through a phase with some support and
  identifies readiness for the next phase.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently manages a product or service through the life
  cycle and adapts approach as new information emerges.
- 4 — Expert: Uses evidence to make or strongly influence a genuine
  continue/change direction/stop decision, consults the right people at the
  right time to move value through the life cycle, and guides other
  colleagues and stakeholders through unfamiliar phases.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of a continue, change-direction or stop decision you made or strongly influenced, and the evidence behind it.
- Ask how you have guided a colleague or stakeholder unfamiliar with a life
  cycle phase through it.
- Ask a referee whether your life cycle judgement calls have held
  up over time.

### Skill: Maintaining delivery momentum

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior delivery manager is expected to optimise the delivery flow of teams,
to actively address the most complicated risks, issues and dependencies
including where ownership exists outside the team or no clear ownership
exists, and to identify innovative ways to unblock issues.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "Optimise the delivery flow of teams" (plural) and
handling risks with "no clear ownership" describes mastery exercised across
more than one team, on the hardest class of problem the framework names for
this skill.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe what affects delivery flow across more than one
  team.
- 1 — Beginner: Tracks a dependency or risk within their own team, with
  support.
- 2 — Intermediate: Tracks and manages risks and dependencies within their
  own team independently.
- 3 — Advanced: Facilitates their own team's delivery flow and pace, and
  addresses risks owned outside the team.
- 4 — Expert: Optimises delivery flow across multiple teams, actively resolves
  the most complicated risks and issues — including ones with no clear
  owner — and finds genuinely innovative ways to unblock them.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of a risk or issue with no clear owner that you resolved.
- Ask how you optimised delivery flow across more than one team.
- Ask a referee for an example of an innovative unblock you found
  that others had not.

### Skill: Making a process work

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior delivery manager is expected to identify and challenge organisational
processes of increasing complexity and those that are unnecessarily
complicated, to add value and coach the organisation to inspect and adapt
processes, and to guide teams through the implementation of a new process.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. The framework's wording for this skill is unchanged
from the delivery manager level, so the baseline holds steady even though the
role level around it has grown in scope and risk.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot identify a process that is getting in the way of a team's
  output.
- 1 — Beginner: Supports a team in following an established process.
- 2 — Intermediate: Helps a team adapt its own process, with some support.
- 3 — Advanced: Identifies and challenges an unnecessarily complicated
  organisational process, coaches the organisation to inspect and adapt it,
  and guides teams through implementing a new one, across more than one
  team.
- 4 — Expert: Not distinctly expected at this level by the framework's own
  wording for this skill.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example where you challenged a process across more
  than one team and helped change it.
- Ask how you guided a team through adopting a genuinely new process under
  real complexity or risk.
- Ask a referee whether your process challenges have led to
  organisation-wide change.

### Skill: Planning

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior delivery manager is expected to lead a continual planning process in
a very complex environment, to plan beyond product delivery, to identify
dependencies in plans across services and co-ordinate delivery, and to coach
other teams as the central point of expertise.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "Lead... in a very complex environment", planning
"beyond product delivery", and being "the central point of expertise" for
coaching other teams describes mastery exercised well beyond a single team's
plan.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe a dependency that spans more than one service.
- 1 — Beginner: Contributes to a plan with support.
- 2 — Intermediate: Manages complex dependencies for their own team's plan.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently prioritises high-value work using data and
  removes blockers affecting their own team's plan.
- 4 — Expert: Leads a continual planning process across a very complex
  environment, plans beyond a single product's delivery, co-ordinates
  dependencies across services, and coaches other teams as the organisation's
  central point of planning expertise.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example where you led planning across a very complex
  environment spanning more than one product or service.
- Ask how you coached another team's planning as the point of expertise.
- Ask a referee whether other teams turn to you for planning
  advice.

### Skill: Team dynamics and collaboration

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior delivery manager is expected to identify problems or issues in the
team dynamic and rectify them, to identify issues through Agile "health
checks" with the team and help stimulate the right responses, to engage in
varying types of feedback, choosing the right type at the appropriate time and
ensuring the discussion and decision stick, and to accelerate the team
development cycle.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Identifying, rectifying and using structured tools
such as health checks to diagnose team dynamics describes a strong,
independent grasp of the skill applied within someone's own teams,
though the framework does not yet describe this level as coaching the whole
organisation on team dynamics.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe what an Agile health check is or what it reveals.
- 1 — Beginner: Recognises a team dynamics issue when it is pointed out.
- 2 — Intermediate: Notices and raises a team dynamics issue independently.
- 3 — Advanced: Identifies and rectifies dynamics issues, uses a structured
  tool such as a health check to diagnose them, chooses the right type of
  feedback for the moment so it actually sticks, and accelerates a team's
  development.
- 4 — Expert: Not expected at this level. Changing organisational structures
  and coaching whole organisations on team dynamics and conflict resolution
  belongs to a head of (Agile) delivery management.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example where you ran or used a health check to
  diagnose and fix a dynamics issue.
- Ask about a time you chose a particular type of feedback deliberately, and
  why.
- Ask a referee whether a team's development visibly accelerated under your leadership.
