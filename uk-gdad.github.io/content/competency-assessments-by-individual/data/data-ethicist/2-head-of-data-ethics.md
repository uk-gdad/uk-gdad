# Data Ethicist (Head of Data Ethics) - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This is for you: a chance to rate your own current strengths against the
baseline the UK GDAD Profession Capability Framework sets for this role
level, using the framework's own words for what it covers.

A data ethicist assesses the societal effect of technology and data, and produces recommendations for other data professionals. This involves thinking about fairness, accountability, the law, moral dilemmas and risks in the creation of technology and data products and policies. The head of data ethics leads the development and implementation of organisational data ethics policies.

Work through the competency matrix below, one skill at a time, then fill in
the overall determination at the end. Treat this as a mirror, not a test —
there's no assessor watching, so being honest with yourself only costs you a
slightly less flattering picture today in exchange for a far more useful one.
Read `## Advisory` before you begin.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating you
give yourself here must never be treated as equivalent to a formal
assessment for a hiring, promotion, pay or performance decision. Use it for
your own reflection, and talk to your manager, a mentor, or your
organisation's people development team about what you find.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you're rating
yourself against what this level actually expects rather than the level
above or below it. Then think back over recent, real examples of your work —
not how you'd like to have handled something, but what you actually did.

**Rate on evidence, not impression.** For every skill, try to recall a
specific moment that supports the rating you give yourself. If you can't
think of one, that's useful information too — it might mean the rating
should be lower, or just that you haven't had the chance to show this skill
yet, which is worth noting separately.

**Be consistent with yourself.** Try to apply the same standard across every
skill, rather than being generous where you feel confident and harsh where
you feel unsure. Coming back to this after a few days, with fresh examples in
mind, often sharpens a rating more than sitting with it in one go.

**Be kind, and be honest.** This only works if you can look at the result
without flinching. A low rating here is not a verdict on you — it's a
starting point. Everyone doing this role, at every level, has skills they are
still building.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

This is a **proficiency** scale, not a frequency scale: it rates how well someone does the thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it.

**Worked example — Data ethics and privacy.** At 1 — Beginner, someone
might describe checking a single project's data handling against a checklist
someone else wrote, without being able to say why each check mattered. At 2 —
Intermediate, they might describe applying an existing ethical framework
consistently across several projects, but referring anything ambiguous to a
more senior colleague. At 4 — Expert, they would describe having written or
substantially revised an organisation's ethical framework themselves,
explained why it applied across a specific policy or programme that had not
previously been assessed for data ethics, and changed how the organisation
worked as a result — evidence that would justify a top rating on this skill at
this level.

## About this role level

The data profession's **data ethicist** role covers people who assess the
societal effect of technology and data, and produce recommendations for other
data professionals. This involves thinking about fairness, accountability, the
law, moral dilemmas and risks in the creation of technology and data products
and policies. In this role, a data ethicist provides research and expertise on
data ethics, enables others to implement data ethics best practice in their
work — for instance by providing training, advising data science teams, or
demonstrating how to apply ethical principles through examples and case
studies — communicates effectively to explain and raise awareness of data
ethics issues, and listens to, convenes, advises and mediates between various
parts of the organisation, and helps people to ask questions, express concerns
and discuss ethical dilemmas.

The role has two levels:

1. Data ethics lead
2. **Head of data ethics** — this level

The **head of data ethics** is the top of this role's ladder. They lead the
development and implementation of organisational data ethics policies — someone the organisation looks to for data ethics judgement at the highest
level, and someone who sets the standard the data ethics lead and the wider
organisation work to.

This role level names ten skills: Analysis and synthesis (data ethics),
Applied social sciences, Communicating between the technical and
non-technical, Communication (data ethics), Data ethics and privacy, Empathy
and inclusivity, Managing decisions and risks, Problem management, Product
ownership (data ethics), and Stakeholder relationship management.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Analysis and synthesis (data ethics)

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a head of data ethics to expertly draw together,
analyse and evaluate qualitative and quantitative data; quickly read and
interpret complex documents from a range of sources and distil what is
relevant; turn research data into clear findings that inform data ethics
decisions for the entire organisation; build capacity in analysis and
synthesis, involving others to increase consensus and challenge assumptions;
help teams define project outcomes and ethical considerations and integrate
ethical diagnostics and assessment; and help the organisation continually
assure and improve its practices so it generates clear and valuable data
ethics findings.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. The framework's own wording — "expertly", "the entire
organisation", "build capacity ... involve others" — describes mastery
exercised at organisational scale and passed on to others, which is the
definition of this level as the top of the role's ladder.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot draw a clear finding from a mixed set of qualitative and
  quantitative evidence without help.
- 1 — Beginner: Summarises a single source accurately, but struggles to
  combine several sources into one coherent finding.
- 2 — Intermediate: Synthesises evidence from a few sources into a finding
  that supports a decision on one project, with some guidance on structure.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently synthesises complex, mixed evidence into
  findings that hold up under challenge from technical and non-technical
  colleagues alike.
- 4 — Expert: Produces synthesis that shapes an organisation-wide data ethics
  decision, and has visibly built other people's capacity to do the same —
  for example by running a workshop on distilling findings, or reviewing and
  improving a colleague's draft analysis.

**Evidence to gather**

- Give yourself a short, mixed bundle of evidence (a survey summary, a
  technical note, a stakeholder email) and try to produce a one-page
  finding from it.
- Ask for an example of research you turned into an organisation-wide data
  ethics decision, and what happened as a result.
- Ask a referee whether you have visibly built other people's
  analysis and synthesis skills, and how.

### Skill: Applied social sciences

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a head of data ethics to explain complex concepts
across the social sciences, such as anthropology, economics, sociology,
philosophy, psychology or race theory; apply various social science theories
to the strategic oversight of data projects, products and policies; apply
those theories to evaluate and challenge assumptions made in data science
projects; identify and use best practice in data ethics; and work with
academics and external researchers to publish research on applied digital
ethics.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "Strategic oversight" and publishing research with
external academics are activities that sit only at the top of this role's
ladder, where someone is expected to shape the field, not just apply it.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot connect a social science concept to a specific data
  project's ethical question.
- 1 — Beginner: Names a relevant social science concept when prompted, but
  cannot yet apply it to a live project.
- 2 — Intermediate: Applies a familiar social science framework to challenge
  an assumption in a data science project, with support.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently applies social science theory to evaluate
  several projects, and explains the reasoning clearly to non-specialists.
- 4 — Expert: Has used social science theory to shape strategic oversight of
  a data programme or policy, and has co-published or contributed to research
  with academics or external researchers on applied digital ethics.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe a project where a social science concept
  changed the direction of a data ethics decision.
- Look back at any published or co-authored research, articles, or conference
  contributions on applied digital ethics.
- Ask about your working relationships with academics or external
  researchers, and what came out of you.

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a head of data ethics to mediate between people and
strengthen relationships, adopting the appropriate communication method with
stakeholders at all levels; manage stakeholder expectations and moderate
difficult discussions about high-risk and complex topics, even within
constrained timescales; and speak on behalf of, and represent the community
to, large audiences inside and outside the organisation.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Representing the community to large audiences outside
the organisation, and moderating high-risk discussions under time pressure,
are external-facing, high-stakes activities that only the top of this role's
ladder is asked to carry.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Struggles to explain a technical data ethics issue to a
  non-technical stakeholder without jargon.
- 1 — Beginner: Explains a straightforward issue clearly one-to-one, with
  preparation.
- 2 — Intermediate: Moderates a small internal discussion between technical
  and non-technical colleagues on a moderately contentious topic.
- 3 — Advanced: Moderates a high-risk or complex discussion under time
  pressure, and keeps stakeholders at different levels aligned.
- 4 — Expert: Represents the organisation's data ethics position to a large
  external audience — a conference, a public consultation, a cross-government
  forum — and is trusted to speak for the organisation without a script.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of moderating a difficult, high-risk discussion between
  technical and non-technical stakeholders under a tight deadline.
- Ask about a time you represented your organisation, or a community of
  practice, to an external audience.
- Ask a referee how you are regarded by stakeholders at very
  different levels of seniority or technical fluency.

### Skill: Communication (data ethics)

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a head of data ethics to expertly translate technical
concepts to non-technical audiences so they are understood by all;
demonstrate a good understanding of how technology and data products and
services are built; understand technical jargon and have sufficient knowledge
to hold meaningful conversations with data science experts on issues such as
minimising bias in data, or gathering, collecting, cleansing, triangulating
and reusing data; and effectively support data scientists and engineers in
implementing data ethics.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. The framework's own word for this level's ability is
"expertly", and the skill spans both directions — holding a meaningful
technical conversation with data scientists and translating it for everyone
else — which the level's own text treats as full mastery, not a developing
skill.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot follow a data science team's discussion of a technical
  bias issue well enough to comment on it.
- 1 — Beginner: Follows a technical explanation with help, but struggles to
  restate it for a non-technical audience.
- 2 — Intermediate: Translates a familiar technical concept accurately for a
  non-technical audience, with review.
- 3 — Advanced: Holds a substantive conversation with data scientists on
  technical issues such as data cleansing or bias, and translates it
  independently for others.
- 4 — Expert: Is sought out by data scientists and engineers to help them
  implement data ethics directly in their work, because they can operate
  fluently on both sides of the conversation.

**Evidence to gather**

- Give yourself a short technical description of a data pipeline issue
  (for example, a biased sampling method) and try to explain it to a
  non-technical panel member.
- Ask for an example of supporting a data science or engineering team to
  implement a specific data ethics change in your work.
- Ask a referee from a technical team whether you found your
  input genuinely useful, or too high-level to act on.

### Skill: Data ethics and privacy

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a head of data ethics to oversee compliance with data
ethics standards and legislation across the organisation; develop, manage and
continuously improve an ethical framework within their area; develop a data
ethics culture, influencing others to act on it; and ensure data ethics is
applied appropriately when formulating, implementing and evaluating
organisational policies and programmes.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. This is the accountability that defines the level
itself — leading "the development and implementation of organisational data
ethics policies" — and the bullets ask for organisation-wide oversight and
culture-building, which is full mastery exercised at the top of the role's
ladder.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe how a specific policy or programme was assessed
  against a data ethics framework.
- 1 — Beginner: Applies an existing checklist to one project, with close
  supervision.
- 2 — Intermediate: Applies an established ethical framework consistently
  across several projects, escalating anything ambiguous.
- 3 — Advanced: Manages and improves part of an ethical framework
  independently, and ensures compliance with data ethics standards and
  legislation within a defined area.
- 4 — Expert: Has written or substantially reshaped an organisation's data
  ethics framework, and can point to a specific organisational policy or
  programme that changed because of it.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of influencing a colleague or team to change behaviour

### Skill: Empathy and inclusivity

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a head of data ethics to incorporate a wide variety of
views from underrepresented groups into product and policy work, using
in-depth consulting and outreach strategies; be involved in the wider
organisational diversity and inclusion plan; draw on a multidisciplinary
background and personal experience to understand the consequences of data
systems on a diverse range of stakeholders; and demonstrate a thorough
understanding of social issues, and the types of bias and discrimination
different groups can face, using this knowledge to inform their data ethics
work.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "In-depth consulting and outreach strategies" and
involvement in the organisation's own diversity and inclusion plan describe a
strategic, organisation-wide practice, which sits at the top of this role's
ladder.

Assessors: rate this skill entirely on someone's demonstrated
professional practice — how they design outreach, run inclusive consultation,
and translate what they learn into data ethics work. Never ask about, infer,
or record someone's own protected characteristics, personal identity, or
lived experience; the skill measures a working method, not who someone
is.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe a specific method for hearing from groups a
  project would otherwise miss.
- 1 — Beginner: Follows an existing outreach process to gather views from an
  underrepresented group, with support.
- 2 — Intermediate: Designs a simple consultation activity that reaches
  beyond the usual stakeholders, for one project.
- 3 — Advanced: Runs in-depth outreach and consulting independently, and can
  explain how a specific piece of feedback changed a product or policy
  decision.
- 4 — Expert: Has shaped the organisation's own diversity and inclusion plan
  or a comparable strategic initiative, and can show, with a concrete
  example, how their outreach strategy improved outcomes for a range of
  stakeholders.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe an outreach or consultation strategy you
  designed to reach a group a project would otherwise have missed, and what
  changed as a result.
- Ask how you have contributed to, or led, a wider organisational diversity
  and inclusion initiative.
- Ask for an example of using knowledge of bias or discrimination to change a
  specific data ethics recommendation.

### Skill: Managing decisions and risks

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a head of data ethics to work with consequential or
complex risks; build consensus between services or independent stakeholders;
lead others to make good design decisions; and apply different risk
methodologies in proportion to the risk.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "Consequential or complex risks" and "lead others to
make good design decisions" describe judgement exercised on behalf of the
organisation and passed on to others, which is full mastery at this level.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot explain why one risk was treated more seriously than
  another on a project.
- 1 — Beginner: Applies a given risk methodology to a straightforward case,
  with supervision.
- 2 — Intermediate: Selects an appropriate risk methodology for a moderately
  complex case, with some guidance.
- 3 — Advanced: Works independently on consequential risks, and builds
  consensus between two services or stakeholders with different priorities.
- 4 — Expert: Leads a group of people through a complex, high-consequence
  risk decision to a good outcome, applying a proportionate methodology and
  explaining the reasoning so others can follow it next time.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe the most consequential risk decision you
  have led, including which methodology you chose and why.
- Give a short scenario involving two stakeholders with conflicting priorities
  and a data ethics risk, and ask how you would build consensus.
- Ask a referee for an example of you leading others to a good
  design decision under real risk pressure.

### Skill: Problem management

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a head of data ethics to ensure that the right actions
are taken to investigate, resolve and anticipate problems, and to co-ordinate
the team to investigate problems, implement solutions and take preventive
measures.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. The bullets describe co-ordinating a team through
investigation and prevention — a strong, working command of the skill — but
they are worded more operationally than the organisation-wide, "expertly"
language the summary uses for this level's other skills, so the baseline sits
a point below the rest of this block.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Reacts to problems individually as they arise, with no attempt to
  anticipate or prevent recurrence.
- 1 — Beginner: Investigates a reported problem when asked, with supervision.
- 2 — Intermediate: Investigates and resolves a problem independently, and
  documents what was done.
- 3 — Advanced: Co-ordinates a small team through investigation, resolution
  and preventive action on a data ethics problem, without needing to be asked.
- 4 — Expert: Anticipates a class of problem before it occurs, and has put in
  place preventive measures that visibly reduced how often it recurred.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe a data ethics problem you co-ordinated a
  team to investigate and resolve, and what preventive measure followed.
- Ask a referee whether you anticipates problems, or mainly reacts
  to you once raised.
- Give a short scenario — a recurring data quality issue with ethical
  implications — and ask how you would co-ordinate a response.
