# Trainee Analytics Engineer - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This is for you: a chance to rate your own current strengths against the
baseline the UK GDAD Profession Capability Framework sets for this role
level, using the framework's own words for what it covers.

An analytics engineer transforms data relevant to the organisation into structures that enable analysis and decision-making. A trainee analytics engineer attends training and develops skills on the job.

Work through the competency matrix below, one skill at a time, then fill in
the overall determination at the end. Treat this as a mirror, not a test —
there's no assessor watching, so being honest with yourself only costs you a
slightly less flattering picture today in exchange for a far more useful one.
Read `## Advisory` before you begin.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating you
give yourself here must never be treated as equivalent to a formal
assessment for a hiring, promotion, pay or performance decision. Use it for
your own reflection, and talk to your manager, a mentor, or your
organisation's people development team about what you find.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you're rating
yourself against what this level actually expects rather than the level
above or below it. Then think back over recent, real examples of your work —
not how you'd like to have handled something, but what you actually did.

**Rate on evidence, not impression.** For every skill, try to recall a
specific moment that supports the rating you give yourself. If you can't
think of one, that's useful information too — it might mean the rating
should be lower, or just that you haven't had the chance to show this skill
yet, which is worth noting separately.

**Be consistent with yourself.** Try to apply the same standard across every
skill, rather than being generous where you feel confident and harsh where
you feel unsure. Coming back to this after a few days, with fresh examples in
mind, often sharpens a rating more than sitting with it in one go.

**Be kind, and be honest.** This only works if you can look at the result
without flinching. A low rating here is not a verdict on you — it's a
starting point. Everyone doing this role, at every level, has skills they are
still building.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix below is rated on the same five-point
scale. This is a **proficiency** scale — it rates how well someone does the
thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it or how they feel
about it.

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

**Worked example — Data analysis and synthesis.** At this role level, the
framework expects a trainee to "interpret data to find key insights". Someone who, asked to look at a short spreadsheet extract, can only describe
what the columns contain is showing evidence for **0 — None**: no
interpretation, only description. Someone who spots an obvious pattern in
the data (for example, that one region's figures are consistently missing) and
says what it might mean is showing **1 — Beginner**: a first, supervised step
into interpretation. Someone who, unprompted, cross-checks two related
figures, notices they do not reconcile, and proposes a plausible reason is
showing evidence closer to **2 — Intermediate** — more than this role level's
own baseline requires, and worth recording as a strength.

## About this role level

The Data profession's Analytics engineer role transforms data relevant to the
organisation into structures that enable analysis and decision-making. Across
the role, an analytics engineer works with subject matter experts to
understand organisational processes and translate these into data structures
optimised for analysis; works with data users to design and build data models
they can use for effective analysis and decision-making, using modelling
techniques such as Kimball or Inmon; supports data quality improvement;
develops standards for data transformation; creates and maintains data
documentation; refines requirements in response to feedback from users and
changes in the organisation; and provides ongoing support to users.

The role has five levels, in order: Trainee analytics engineer, Analytics
engineer, Senior analytics engineer, Lead analytics engineer, and Head of
analytics engineering. This assessment is for the first of them.

A **trainee analytics engineer** attends training and develops skills on the
job. At this role level, they will spend time shadowing other analytics
engineers, build their knowledge of the organisation, learn skills for
managing data, learn to use different applications, tools, templates and best
practices, handle simple queries from users and document their data
requirements, and contribute to data documentation and user training. It is a
supervised, learning-first level: the accountabilities are about building
capability, not about independent delivery.

The framework names seven skills for this role level: Communicating between
the technical and non-technical, Data analysis and synthesis, Data
innovation, Data modelling, cleansing and enrichment, Metadata management,
Problem management, and Testing.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What the framework expects at this level**

A trainee analytics engineer is expected to explain why it is important to
communicate technical concepts in non-technical language, and to explain the
types of communication that can be used with internal and external
stakeholders, and their impact. This is about understanding communication as
a concept — not yet about running it unsupervised.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. The framework's own wording asks the trainee to
"explain" rather than "communicate effectively" or "manage" — that is
knowledge, not yet demonstrated skill under real conditions, so the baseline
sits at the first rung rather than at None, since some grasp of the idea is
expected from day one.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot say why a technical explanation might confuse a
  non-technical colleague, or treats all audiences the same way.
- 1 — Beginner: Can explain, when asked, why jargon should be avoided with a
  non-technical stakeholder, and can name a couple of communication channels
  (for example, email versus a short briefing) and roughly how they differ.
- 2 — Intermediate: Adapts a short explanation of a data topic for a specific
  non-technical audience with some coaching, and checks whether the listener
  has understood.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently tailors technical explanations to different
  stakeholders, anticipates likely confusion, and adjusts on the fly.
- 4 — Expert: Coaches others in adapting technical communication, and is
  sought out to explain data work to senior or external audiences.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd explain, in plain language, what a data model is, as if
  to a colleague with no data background.
- In a structured interview, ask you to describe a time you had to explain
  something technical to someone who did not understand it, and what you
  changed about how you explained it.
- Observe you, if possible, in a shadowing session, checking whether you ask
  clarifying questions when a user's request is unclear.

### Skill: Data analysis and synthesis

**What the framework expects at this level**

A trainee analytics engineer is expected to interpret data to find key
insights — a single, foundational accountability at this level, without the
profiling or source-system analysis expected of the next level up.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. Interpreting data for key insights is a real skill,
not just an awareness of the idea, but at trainee level it is expected to
happen with support and on simple data, so the baseline sits just above None.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Can describe what is in a data set but cannot say what it means.
- 1 — Beginner: With guidance, picks out an obvious pattern or outlier in a
  small, simple data set and states what it might indicate.
- 2 — Intermediate: Unprompted, finds a straightforward insight in a
  moderately complex data set and explains it clearly.
- 3 — Advanced: Reliably interprets data from multiple related sources and
  distinguishes a genuine insight from noise.
- 4 — Expert: Synthesises insight across complex, ambiguous data and teaches
  others how to do the same.

**Evidence to gather**

- Give yourself a short, simple data extract (a spreadsheet of a few
  dozen rows) and try to describe what stands out and why.
- Ask about a piece of coursework, a personal project, or a shadowing
  experience where you looked at real data and found something worth
  reporting.
- Ask a referee whether you have, under supervision, spotted an issue
  or pattern in data that someone more senior had not already flagged.

### Skill: Data innovation

**What the framework expects at this level**

A trainee analytics engineer is expected to show an awareness of opportunities
for innovation with new tools and uses of data — noticing that something new
exists, not yet judging when or whether the organisation should adopt it.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. "Show an awareness" is explicitly a lower bar than
judging or implementing innovation, so the baseline sits at Beginner: some
curiosity and some noticing, expected with encouragement rather than
independently.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Shows no interest in or awareness of tools or approaches beyond
  what they have been directly taught.
- 1 — Beginner: Can name a tool, technique or way of working they have come
  across that seemed useful, when prompted.
- 2 — Intermediate: Proactively mentions a new tool or technique to a
  colleague or manager without being asked.
- 3 — Advanced: Evaluates whether a new tool or technique is actually useful
  for the organisation's context, not just novel.
- 4 — Expert: Scans the field for emerging approaches and introduces genuinely
  useful ones into team practice.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd name a data tool, technique or trend you have read
  about or tried recently, and what interested you about it.
- Ask about any personal learning — a course, a tutorial, a side project —
  you have pursued off your own initiative.

### Skill: Data modelling, cleansing and enrichment

**What the framework expects at this level**

A trainee analytics engineer is expected to show an awareness of different
data models and tools and understand when they could be used, and to show an
awareness of industry-recognised data modelling patterns and standards. This
is understanding of concepts, ahead of the practical modelling work asked of
the next level.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. The framework twice uses "show an awareness", which is
knowledge of the landscape rather than hands-on modelling, so the baseline
sits at Beginner rather than higher.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe what a data model is or name any modelling
  approach.
- 1 — Beginner: Can describe, in general terms, what a data model does and
  name one recognised pattern (for example, a star schema) when prompted.
- 2 — Intermediate: Explains, with some accuracy, when a particular type of
  model or pattern would be appropriate for a given problem.
- 3 — Advanced: Builds a simple data model independently, following an
  established pattern, and explains the design choices.
- 4 — Expert: Designs and reviews complex data models and sets modelling
  standards for others to follow.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe, in your own words, what a data model is and
  give an example of one you have seen or heard about, such as a star schema
  or a snowflake schema.
- Ask about any coursework or self-study covering data modelling concepts such
  as Kimball or Inmon approaches.

### Skill: Metadata management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A trainee analytics engineer is expected to explain what metadata is, and to
maintain the information stored in metadata repositories under the direction
of others. The accountability is directed maintenance, not independent
repository work.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. "Under the direction of others" caps the expectation:
the trainee needs to understand the concept and be able to follow instructions
to update a repository, which is Beginner-level practice.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot explain what metadata is or why it matters.
- 1 — Beginner: Explains what metadata is in simple terms, and, when given
  clear instructions, updates an entry in a metadata repository correctly.
- 2 — Intermediate: Updates metadata entries with only light supervision and
  spots an obvious inconsistency in existing records.
- 3 — Advanced: Maintains a metadata repository independently and uses it to
  support moderately complex tasks.
- 4 — Expert: Designs metadata repositories and governs their accuracy for
  others.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd explain, in plain terms, what metadata is and give an
  example (for example, a data dictionary entry or a field description).
- Ask about a time you were asked to update a record, a spreadsheet, or a
  document to a set standard, and how you checked your own work.

### Skill: Problem management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A trainee analytics engineer is expected to investigate problems in systems,
processes and services, with an understanding of the level of a problem — for
example, strategic, tactical or operational — and to contribute to the
implementation of remedies and preventative measures. Investigation and
contribution, not ownership of resolution.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. Investigating and contributing under guidance is a
real activity expected from day one, but the framework's own wording for this
level stops short of resolving or deciding, so the baseline sits at Beginner.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Does not notice or investigate when something is not working as
  expected.
- 1 — Beginner: With guidance, looks into a simple, contained problem (for
  example, a report that will not run) and describes what they found.
- 2 — Intermediate: Investigates a moderately complex problem with some
  independence and suggests a plausible next step.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently investigates and resolves problems, and
  distinguishes a one-off issue from a recurring one.
- 4 — Expert: Anticipates problems before they surface and builds others'
  capability to investigate them.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe a time something went wrong with a task or a
  piece of work, and how you went about finding out why.
- Ask a referee whether you have, under supervision, correctly
  identified the cause of a simple problem.
- Present a short, made-up scenario (for example, "a scheduled data refresh
  did not run last night — what would you check first?") and see how you
  reason through it.

### Skill: Testing

**What the framework expects at this level**

A trainee analytics engineer is expected to correctly execute test scripts
under supervision, and to understand the role of testing and how it works.
Execution against an existing script, not designing tests.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. Executing a supplied script correctly, under
supervision, is the defining activity, so the baseline sits at Beginner —
above None because it is a real, checkable activity, and no higher because
independence and design are not yet expected.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot follow a test script accurately, or does not understand why
  testing matters.
- 1 — Beginner: Follows a supplied test script correctly under supervision and
  can explain, in general terms, why testing is done.
- 2 — Intermediate: Executes test scripts with minimal supervision and flags
  an unexpected result clearly.
- 3 — Advanced: Designs simple test cases as well as executing them, and
  reports results in a way others can act on.
- 4 — Expert: Sets testing standards and reviews others' test coverage.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd walk through how you would follow a written set of
  test steps for checking that a report shows the right numbers.
- Ask about any experience — from a course, a previous job, or a personal
  project — of following instructions to check a piece of work step by step.
