# Business Architect - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This is for you: a chance to rate your own current strengths against the
baseline the UK GDAD Profession Capability Framework sets for this role
level, using the framework's own words for what it covers.

A business architect develops an integrated view of an enterprise, such as a business area or organisation. They use this view to help their organisation put its strategy in place. A business architect uses their knowledge and experience to understand business scenarios, communicate the issues and recommend next steps. They support and guide other business architects.

Work through the competency matrix below, one skill at a time, then fill in
the overall determination at the end. Treat this as a mirror, not a test —
there's no assessor watching, so being honest with yourself only costs you a
slightly less flattering picture today in exchange for a far more useful one.
Read `## Advisory` before you begin.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating you
give yourself here must never be treated as equivalent to a formal
assessment for a hiring, promotion, pay or performance decision. Use it for
your own reflection, and talk to your manager, a mentor, or your
organisation's people development team about what you find.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you're rating
yourself against what this level actually expects rather than the level
above or below it. Then think back over recent, real examples of your work —
not how you'd like to have handled something, but what you actually did.

**Rate on evidence, not impression.** For every skill, try to recall a
specific moment that supports the rating you give yourself. If you can't
think of one, that's useful information too — it might mean the rating
should be lower, or just that you haven't had the chance to show this skill
yet, which is worth noting separately.

**Be consistent with yourself.** Try to apply the same standard across every
skill, rather than being generous where you feel confident and harsh where
you feel unsure. Coming back to this after a few days, with fresh examples in
mind, often sharpens a rating more than sitting with it in one go.

**Be kind, and be honest.** This only works if you can look at the result
without flinching. A low rating here is not a verdict on you — it's a
starting point. Everyone doing this role, at every level, has skills they are
still building.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

This is a **proficiency** scale, not a frequency scale: it rates how well someone does the thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it.

**Worked example — Business architecture.** Someone rated 2 would still
need support to advise the organisation on implementing its strategy. Someone rated 3 (the baseline expected at this level) applies and develops
business architecture frameworks in their organisation independently,
advises and guides the organisation on implementing its strategy, and
produces clear, multidimensional views to support decision making without
close supervision. Someone rated 4 would already be shaping how the
whole organisation values and uses business architecture, and coaching other
business architects in the practice — more than this level expects, and
worth noting as a strength for succession planning.

## About this role level

**Architecture role: Business architect.** A business architect develops an
integrated view of an enterprise, such as a business area or organisation,
and uses that view to help their organisation put its strategy in place.

In this role, a business architect will:

- understand and apply business architectural principles, techniques, and
  tools to business challenges
- ensure design work from projects and programmes aligns with strategy
- ensure business change is implemented effectively to meet strategy
- aggregate and disaggregate complex information from across the organisation
- communicate complex information about the organisation to stakeholders

**Role level: Business architect.** This is the third of four levels in the
business architect role, in order: trainee business architect, associate
business architect, business architect, and lead business architect. A
business architect uses their knowledge and experience to understand
business scenarios, communicate the issues, and recommend next steps. They
support and guide other business architects.

At this role level, a business architect will:

- support executive level work and management of the business architecture
  practice
- guide others in using appropriate business architectural tools and methods
- understand and apply business architectural principles, techniques, and
  tools to business scenarios
- create business architecture artefacts, including abstract representations
  of the organisation, knowing when to apply them
- develop standard and scalable models to collect, aggregate, or disaggregate
  complex information

The framework names eight skills for this role level: Business architecture,
Communicating information, Consultancy, Making and guiding decisions, Methods
and tools, Stakeholder relationship management, Strategic design and
business change, and Strategic thinking.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Business architecture

**What the framework expects at this level**

A business architect applies and develops business architecture frameworks,
principles and techniques in their organisation, advises and guides the
organisation to inform the implementation of its strategy, shares the value
of business architecture with the organisation, and produces clear
multidimensional views of the organisation to support decision making.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. The framework's wording moves from supporting the
adoption of business architecture, as at associate level, to applying,
developing and advising on it — work this level owns and delivers
independently.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot describe how business architecture frameworks apply to
  the organisation's own strategy.
- 1 — Beginner: applies a framework or technique with support, and needs
  guidance to advise the organisation on implementing strategy.
- 2 — Intermediate: applies frameworks with occasional guidance, and produces
  parts of a multidimensional organisational view with support.
- 3 — Advanced: applies and develops frameworks independently, advises and
  guides the organisation on implementing its strategy, and produces clear
  multidimensional views unsupported.
- 4 — Expert: shapes how the whole organisation values and uses business
  architecture, and develops the practice's maturity.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd review a summarised business strategy and describe how
  you would advise the organisation on implementing it, using business
  architecture techniques.
- A structured interview question: "Tell us about a time you developed or
  adapted a business architecture framework or technique to fit your
  organisation's own needs."
- If already in post, review a multidimensional organisational view — such as
  a capability model or value stream map — the person produced independently.

### Skill: Communicating information

**What the framework expects at this level**

A business architect mediates between people and mends relationships,
communicating with stakeholders at all levels, manages stakeholder
expectations and hosts or moderates discussions about high risk and
complexity — even within constrained timescales — and speaks on behalf of
and represents the community to large audiences inside and outside
government.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. The framework describes this skill in identical terms
at lead business architect level; the baseline sits at 3 rather than 4 here
because this level's own accountabilities are about supporting
executive-level work rather than the lead's fuller ownership of the
practice's direction — the difference is in scope and authority, not in the
communication itself, which the framework already expects to be strong here.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot mediate between people in conflict or manage stakeholder
  expectations under pressure.
- 1 — Beginner: manages stakeholder expectations in routine cases but needs
  support to mediate a serious disagreement.
- 2 — Intermediate: mediates straightforward disagreements and manages
  expectations for moderately complex stakeholder situations.
- 3 — Advanced: mediates and mends relationships involving high risk or
  complexity, including under time pressure, and represents the community
  competently to audiences beyond the immediate organisation.
- 4 — Expert: is someone the organisation turns to for its hardest
  stakeholder situations and its highest-profile external representation.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Tell us about a time you mediated between
  two people or teams whose relationship had broken down. What did you do,
  and what happened?"
- Ask a referee: "Have you seen this person represent the business
  architecture community to an audience outside the immediate team? How did
  it go?"
- If already in post, review a case where the person managed a
  high-complexity or high-risk stakeholder situation, including under a tight
  deadline.

### Skill: Consultancy

**What the framework expects at this level**

A business architect provides advice and recommendations to stakeholders
based on their significant specialist knowledge and experience, leads the
definition of guidance, and informs how the organisation approaches delivery
or uses digital and data skills more effectively.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. The framework describes this skill in identical
terms at lead business architect level too; the baseline here reflects that
this level already leads the definition of guidance, but does so in support
of executive-level work rather than with the lead's fuller organisational
mandate.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: gives advice with no clear grounding in specialist experience.
- 1 — Beginner: gives advice on straightforward matters but needs support for
  anything organisation-wide.
- 2 — Intermediate: gives sound advice on moderately complex matters and
  contributes to guidance documents with oversight.
- 3 — Advanced: gives advice and recommendations grounded in significant
  specialist knowledge, and leads the definition of guidance that changes how
  the organisation approaches delivery.
- 4 — Expert: is the acknowledged specialist whose advice shapes strategy
  across the organisation.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Describe a piece of guidance you led the
  definition of. What difference did it make?"
- Think about how you'd advise, in a short scenario, on how an organisation
  should approach a specific delivery problem, and explain your reasoning.
- If already in post, review a guidance document the person led.

### Skill: Making and guiding decisions

**What the framework expects at this level**

A business architect makes decisions characterised by medium levels of risk
and complexity, recommends decisions as risk and complexity increase further,
and builds consensus between services or independent stakeholders.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. This is a genuine increase from the associate level's
managed-risk decisions, to medium risk and complexity, and to building
consensus across whole services rather than between individual peers.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: avoids owning decisions of any real risk or complexity.
- 1 — Beginner: makes decisions with support once risk or complexity rises
  above the routine.
- 2 — Intermediate: makes decisions of moderate risk and complexity with
  occasional guidance.
- 3 — Advanced: makes decisions characterised by medium risk and complexity
  unsupported, recommending rather than deciding as it rises further, and
  builds consensus between services or independent stakeholders.
- 4 — Expert: is trusted with the highest-risk, most complex decisions and
  builds consensus across the whole organisation.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Tell us about a decision of real
  consequence you made, involving more than one team or service. How did you
  build agreement?"
- Ask a referee: "Can you describe a case where this person built consensus
  between stakeholders who did not initially agree?"
- If already in post, review a documented decision showing the reasoning and
  the stakeholders consulted.

### Skill: Methods and tools

**What the framework expects at this level**

A business architect builds capability in methods and tools, ensuring they
are adopted consistently and used effectively.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. The framework's wording moves from an associate's own
selection of methods and tools within a project, to building capability in
methods and tools across a wider group — a step from personal use to
organisational adoption.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has no involvement in how methods and tools are adopted beyond
  their own work.
- 1 — Beginner: uses methods and tools well themselves but has not helped
  others adopt them.
- 2 — Intermediate: helps individual colleagues adopt a method or tool with
  support.
- 3 — Advanced: builds capability in methods and tools across a team or
  practice, ensuring consistent, effective adoption.
- 4 — Expert: sets the organisation's direction on methods and tools and
  gives others the authority to advise on them.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Tell us about a time you helped a team
  or practice adopt a new method or tool. What did you do to make sure it
  stuck?"
- Think about how you'd describe how you would check that a method, once
  introduced, was being used effectively and consistently.
- If already in post, review evidence of a method or tool rollout the person
  led or supported.

### Skill: Stakeholder relationship management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A business architect works with the team to develop and maintain an
understanding of stakeholders, works with the team to develop and implement
stakeholder communications strategies, identifies and resolves issues,
influences stakeholders and manages relationships effectively, and builds
long-term strategic relationships, communicating clearly and regularly with
stakeholders.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. This level moves from an associate's own stakeholder
communication to working with the team to build and implement a stakeholder
communications strategy, and to building relationships that are long-term
and strategic rather than task-focused.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has no involvement in developing the team's shared understanding
  of stakeholders.
- 1 — Beginner: contributes to the team's understanding of stakeholders when
  asked, but does not yet help shape a communications strategy.
- 2 — Intermediate: helps develop and implement a stakeholder communications
  strategy with support, and resolves straightforward stakeholder issues.
- 3 — Advanced: works with the team to build and implement stakeholder
  communications strategies, resolves issues and influences stakeholders
  effectively, and builds long-term strategic relationships.
- 4 — Expert: directs the stakeholder relationship strategy for the whole
  practice and negotiates at the most senior level.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Tell us about a stakeholder relationship
  you built over a long period. How did you keep it strong?"
- Think about how you'd describe how you would help a team develop a
  stakeholder communications strategy for a significant change programme.
- If already in post, review a stakeholder communications strategy the person
  helped develop or implement.

### Skill: Strategic design and business change

**What the framework expects at this level**

A business architect demonstrates a good understanding of business issues,
events and activities, and their short to long term impact, supports
defining principles, patterns, standards, policies, roadmaps and vision
statements, effectively focuses on outcomes rather than solutions, and helps
develop, maintain or update strategy in response to feedback and findings.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. This is a new skill at this level and, unlike
most of the other skills here, the framework's own wording still says
"supports defining" and "helps develop" rather than owning the work
outright — a supported contribution rather than full ownership, which comes
at lead business architect level, where the same wording drops the
qualifiers.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot connect a business issue to its likely short or long term
  impact.
- 1 — Beginner: describes the short to long term impact of a business issue
  when prompted, but needs significant support to contribute to defining
  principles or standards.
- 2 — Intermediate: demonstrates a good understanding of business issues and
  their impact unprompted, and supports defining principles, patterns,
  standards, policies, roadmaps or vision statements with some oversight.
- 3 — Advanced: focuses consistently on outcomes rather than solutions, and
  helps develop or update strategy in response to feedback with little
  support.
- 4 — Expert: independently defines principles, patterns, standards,
  policies, roadmaps and vision statements for the organisation.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Tell us about a time you contributed to
  defining a principle, standard or roadmap for your organisation. What was
  your part in it?"
- Think about how you'd review a short business scenario and describe its
  likely short-term and long-term impact.
- If already in post, review a roadmap, vision statement or set of principles
  the person contributed to.

### Skill: Strategic thinking

**What the framework expects at this level**

A business architect defines strategies and policies, providing guidance to
others on working in the strategic context, and evaluates current strategies
to ensure business requirements are being met and exceeded where possible.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. The framework describes this skill in identical
terms at lead business architect level; the baseline here reflects that this
level defines and evaluates strategy in support of executive-level work,
while the lead does so with fuller organisational direction and authority.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot evaluate whether a current strategy is meeting business
  requirements.
- 1 — Beginner: contributes an opinion on a strategy's fit when asked, but
  needs support to define strategy or guide others.
- 2 — Intermediate: evaluates a strategy against business requirements with
  guidance, and drafts strategy or policy content for others to review.
- 3 — Advanced: defines strategies and policies, guides others in working
  strategically, and evaluates current strategies unsupported, identifying
  where they fall short.
- 4 — Expert: sets the organisation's strategic direction and develops
  others' capability to think and act strategically.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Tell us about a strategy or policy you
  helped define. How did you evaluate whether it was working?"
- Think about how you'd review a short strategy summary and identify where it
  may not be meeting business requirements.
- If already in post, review a strategy or policy document the person
  authored or co-authored.
