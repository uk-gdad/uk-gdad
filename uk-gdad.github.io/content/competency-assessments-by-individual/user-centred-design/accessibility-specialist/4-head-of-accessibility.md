# Head of Accessibility - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This is for you: a chance to rate your own current strengths against the
baseline the UK GDAD Profession Capability Framework sets for this role
level, using the framework's own words for what it covers.

An accessibility specialist provides support, advice and guidance to other roles in the Government Digital and Data profession about how to create accessible digital services. A head of accessibility is an expert practitioner who can create an accessibility vision or strategy for an organisation.

Work through the competency matrix below, one skill at a time, then fill in
the overall determination at the end. Treat this as a mirror, not a test —
there's no assessor watching, so being honest with yourself only costs you a
slightly less flattering picture today in exchange for a far more useful one.
Read `## Advisory` before you begin.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating you
give yourself here must never be treated as equivalent to a formal
assessment for a hiring, promotion, pay or performance decision. Use it for
your own reflection, and talk to your manager, a mentor, or your
organisation's people development team about what you find.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you're rating
yourself against what this level actually expects rather than the level
above or below it. Then think back over recent, real examples of your work —
not how you'd like to have handled something, but what you actually did.

**Rate on evidence, not impression.** For every skill, try to recall a
specific moment that supports the rating you give yourself. If you can't
think of one, that's useful information too — it might mean the rating
should be lower, or just that you haven't had the chance to show this skill
yet, which is worth noting separately.

**Be consistent with yourself.** Try to apply the same standard across every
skill, rather than being generous where you feel confident and harsh where
you feel unsure. Coming back to this after a few days, with fresh examples in
mind, often sharpens a rating more than sitting with it in one go.

**Be kind, and be honest.** This only works if you can look at the result
without flinching. A low rating here is not a verdict on you — it's a
starting point. Everyone doing this role, at every level, has skills they are
still building.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point
proficiency scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

**Worked example**, using Leadership and guidance, a skill this level names:

- Someone who can only make and justify decisions with medium levels of
  risk, and needs support for genuinely high-impact decisions, sits at
  **3 — Advanced**, below this assessment's baseline.
- Someone who makes and justifies decisions with high levels of risk,
  impact and complexity, builds consensus between whole organisations, and
  solves issues between departments at the highest level sits at
  **4 — Expert** — the baseline this assessment sets for this skill.

## About this role level

**The role: accessibility specialist.** An accessibility specialist provides
support, advice and guidance to other roles in the GDAD profession about how
to create accessible digital services, using knowledge of accessibility
guidelines, technical expertise, and the voice of disabled users, so that
product teams make services everyone can use.

**This level: head of accessibility.** The framework describes this level as
an expert practitioner who can create an accessibility vision or strategy
for an organisation. At this level, the framework expects someone to:

- define and assure best practice while influencing, leading and mentoring
  others
- influence and develop organisational strategy and priorities, working with
  counterpart colleagues across government
- ensure the right conditions and environment for accessibility specialists
  to work effectively
- develop departmental capability by supporting internal movement of staff
  between roles, shaping career paths and recruiting talent
- understand and own the overall approach to accessibility risk, including
  how it is communicated and how corporate legal compliance risk is managed
- engage with and contribute to the cross-government accessibility community

**Where this level sits.** Accessibility specialist has four levels:

1. Junior accessibility specialist
2. Accessibility specialist
3. Senior accessibility specialist
4. **Head of accessibility** — assessed here

**A note on Technical understanding (accessibility).** The framework's
published skill text for this level repeats, word for word, the text
published for accessibility specialist (level 2) — it does not build on the
more advanced wording published for senior accessibility specialist (level
3), such as excellent knowledge of edge cases or a range of assistive
technologies. This assessment sets its baseline from the framework's actual
published text for this skill at this level rather than assuming a higher
bar the text does not state, and it says so plainly here rather than
smoothing the anomaly away. In practice, expect a genuine head of
accessibility to substantially exceed this particular floor — this level's
other accountabilities, and the role's own seniority, make deep technical
grounding a practical necessity even where the framework's wording for this
one skill has not kept pace with the level.

**The six skills this assessment covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Communicating information
2. Governance and assurance (accessibility)
3. Leadership and guidance
4. Technical understanding (accessibility)
5. Testing
6. User focus

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Communicating information

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects someone to mediate between people and mend
relationships, communicating with stakeholders at all levels; manage
stakeholder expectations and host or moderate discussions about high risk
and complexity, even within constrained timescales; and speak on behalf of
and represent the community to large audiences inside and outside of
government.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Mediating and mending relationships, and representing
the whole community to large external audiences, is communication as an
organisational function rather than a personal skill, which is what this
assessment's Expert point describes.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has never represented the accessibility community to an audience
  outside their own team.
- 1 — Beginner: Manages stakeholder expectations for familiar, lower-risk
  situations only.
- 2 — Intermediate: Hosts moderately difficult discussions with support, and
  has represented the team, not yet the wider community, externally.
- 3 — Advanced: Hosts high-risk, time-constrained discussions unsupported,
  but has not yet mediated a genuinely broken relationship between senior
  parties — one step below this level's baseline.
- 4 — Expert: Mediates and mends a relationship between people who had
  stopped working effectively together, and represents the community to a
  large audience inside or outside government.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe a relationship you mended between people or
  teams that had genuinely broken down.
- Think about how you'd describe a time you represented the accessibility community
  to a large audience, and what the audience took away.
- Ask a referee, ideally a senior stakeholder, how you are regarded
  in a high-risk, time-constrained discussion.

### Skill: Governance and assurance (accessibility)

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects someone to analyse governance and assurance
systems and add appropriate measures to ensure accessibility is built into
all project, programme and change activity.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. This has moved from senior accessibility
specialist's challenging of teams within an existing system to designing and
adding measures to the governance system itself, organisation-wide — the
clearest possible marker of this assessment's Expert point.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe how accessibility is built into the
  organisation's governance system, only how it is checked within one team.
- 1 — Beginner: Understands the organisation's governance system but has not
  added measures to it.
- 2 — Intermediate: Proposes an improvement to a governance process with
  support from others.
- 3 — Advanced: Adds a measure to a project or programme governance process
  that improves accessibility assurance — one step below this level's
  baseline.
- 4 — Expert: Analyses the whole governance and assurance system and adds
  measures that build accessibility into project, programme and change
  activity organisation-wide.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe a governance or assurance measure you
  introduced, and the difference it made across more than one team.
- Look back at a governance framework, standard or assurance process you have
  designed or substantially changed.
- Ask a referee how accessibility governance changed under your
  influence.

### Skill: Leadership and guidance

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects someone to change organisational structures to
fixable and sustainable designs, lead on the strategy for the whole
organisation, make and justify decisions characterised by high levels of
risk, impact and complexity, build consensus between organisations or
highly independent and diverse stakeholders, solve and unblock issues
between teams or departments at the highest level, understand the psychology
of a team with strong mediation skills, and coach the organisation on team
dynamics and conflict resolution.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Every clause here operates at organisation or
cross-organisation scale — structural change, whole-organisation strategy,
high-impact decisions, disputes at departmental level — which is this
assessment's Expert point by definition.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has never led or justified a decision with genuinely high risk,
  impact and complexity.
- 1 — Beginner: Makes decisions with high impact only with close support
  from more senior colleagues.
- 2 — Intermediate: Makes and justifies some high-impact decisions, with
  review from a peer or senior colleague.
- 3 — Advanced: Makes and justifies high-risk, high-impact decisions
  unsupported, but has not yet changed an organisational structure or settled
  a dispute between departments — one step below this level's baseline.
- 4 — Expert: Changes an organisational structure to something more
  sustainable, leads whole-organisation strategy, and unblocks a genuine
  dispute between departments.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe an organisational structure you changed,
  and how you knew the new design was more sustainable.
- Think about how you'd describe a dispute between teams or departments you resolved
  at the highest level, and what made it resolvable.
- If you can, ask a manager, mentor or colleague to describe your strategic contribution to the
  organisation, not just your own team.

### Skill: Technical understanding (accessibility)

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects demonstrated knowledge of accessibility standards and
legislation and the ability to communicate their requirements, advocacy for
appropriate technical solutions to a range of accessibility issues,
performing detailed audits of websites, services and documents with clear
documented findings, advocacy for the people affected by accessibility
barriers across the department, provision of introductory awareness and
training, and demonstrated confidence using one or more assistive
technologies with knowledge of others.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. As explained in `## About this role level`, the
framework's published wording for this skill at head of accessibility
matches accessibility specialist's wording rather than a level above senior
accessibility specialist's more advanced description — this assessment sets
the baseline to what the framework actually states for this skill at this
level, and expects you to use judgement, and the evidence log, if a real person's practical technical grounding needs to be weighed more
heavily than this floor suggests.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot perform a detailed audit or explain a standard's
  requirement without help.
- 1 — Beginner: Explains standards with help, and performs only basic checks
  unsupported.
- 2 — Intermediate: Performs basic checks confidently but is not yet
  independent on a full, detailed audit.
- 3 — Advanced: Performs a detailed audit unsupported, documents findings
  clearly for others to work from, and is confident using at least one
  assistive technology — this level's published baseline.
- 4 — Expert: Demonstrates excellent knowledge including edge cases, and
  trains others in ARIA techniques and a range of assistive technologies —
  the more advanced description published for senior accessibility
  specialist, which many real head of accessibility people will also
  meet even though the framework does not require it in this skill's text
  at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- Work sample: give a real or representative service and ask for a detailed
  audit with clearly documented findings.
- Reflect on a recent time you using an assistive technology you claim confidence
  with.
- Think about how you'd deliver a short piece of introductory accessibility awareness
  to a mixed, non-specialist audience.

### Skill: Testing

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects someone to manage the planning of system and
acceptance tests, co-ordinating both functional and non-functional
specifications; provide authoritative advice and guidance on test planning;
and identify process improvements and contribute to the definition of best
practice.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "Authoritative advice and guidance" and defining best
practice, applied to whole system and acceptance test planning, describe
someone others in the organisation defer to, which is this assessment's
Expert point.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe how functional and non-functional test
  specifications are co-ordinated for a system, only how one script is run.
- 1 — Beginner: Contributes to test planning under someone else's authority.
- 2 — Intermediate: Plans testing for a defined piece of work with support.
- 3 — Advanced: Manages the planning of system and acceptance tests for a
  piece of work unsupported, co-ordinating functional and non-functional
  specifications — one step below this level's baseline.
- 4 — Expert: Provides authoritative advice and guidance on test planning
  across the organisation, and has contributed to the organisation's
  definition of best practice.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe a piece of test planning you managed that
  co-ordinated functional and non-functional specifications.
- Think about how you'd describe a process improvement you identified and how it
  became part of the organisation's best practice.
- Look back at a test plan or piece of authoritative guidance you have written.

### Skill: User focus

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects someone to give direction on which tools or
methods to use, demonstrate experience in meeting the needs of users across
a variety of channels, bring insight and expertise in how user needs have
changed over time to ensure they are met by the business, and apply
strategic thinking to provide the best service for the end user.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Giving direction on tools and methods, and applying
strategic thinking at business level rather than to one piece of research,
describes someone setting the organisation's approach rather than following
it, which is this assessment's Expert point.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe how user needs have changed over time for the
  organisation's services, only for one piece of work.
- 1 — Beginner: Meets user needs on one channel with support.
- 2 — Intermediate: Meets user needs across more than one channel, with some
  support choosing the right tools or methods.
- 3 — Advanced: Gives direction on tools and methods for a team, and brings
  insight into how needs have changed for that team's users — one step
  below this level's baseline.
- 4 — Expert: Applies strategic thinking to how the whole organisation meets
  end user needs, and brings insight into how needs have changed at that
  scale.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe how your view of an organisation's user
  needs changed over several years, and what you did differently as a
  result.
- Think about how you'd describe a strategic decision about the best service for end
  users that you influenced or made.
- Ask a referee about a time you directed which tools or methods a
  team should use, and why.
