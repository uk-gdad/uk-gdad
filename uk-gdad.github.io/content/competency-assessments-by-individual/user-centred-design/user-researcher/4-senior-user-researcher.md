# Senior User Researcher - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This is for you: a chance to rate your own current strengths against the
baseline the UK GDAD Profession Capability Framework sets for this role
level, using the framework's own words for what it covers.

User researchers plan, design and carry out research activities with users that help teams get a deep understanding of the people that use government services. This research informs policy, proposition, service, content and interaction design so that services work well for users and achieve policy intent. A senior user researcher is an experienced practitioner who can plan and lead user research activities in larger teams and on more complex services.

Work through the competency matrix below, one skill at a time, then fill in
the overall determination at the end. Treat this as a mirror, not a test —
there's no assessor watching, so being honest with yourself only costs you a
slightly less flattering picture today in exchange for a far more useful one.
Read `## Advisory` before you begin.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating you
give yourself here must never be treated as equivalent to a formal
assessment for a hiring, promotion, pay or performance decision. Use it for
your own reflection, and talk to your manager, a mentor, or your
organisation's people development team about what you find.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you're rating
yourself against what this level actually expects rather than the level
above or below it. Then think back over recent, real examples of your work —
not how you'd like to have handled something, but what you actually did.

**Rate on evidence, not impression.** For every skill, try to recall a
specific moment that supports the rating you give yourself. If you can't
think of one, that's useful information too — it might mean the rating
should be lower, or just that you haven't had the chance to show this skill
yet, which is worth noting separately.

**Be consistent with yourself.** Try to apply the same standard across every
skill, rather than being generous where you feel confident and harsh where
you feel unsure. Coming back to this after a few days, with fresh examples in
mind, often sharpens a rating more than sitting with it in one go.

**Be kind, and be honest.** This only works if you can look at the result
without flinching. A low rating here is not a verdict on you — it's a
starting point. Everyone doing this role, at every level, has skills they are
still building.

## Rating scale

This is a proficiency scale, not a frequency scale — it measures how well
someone can do something, not how often they do it.

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

Worked example, using **User research methods**: someone at 1 (Beginner) can
run a single, well-defined method such as a moderated usability test, but only
with close supervision from someone more experienced. Someone at 3 (Advanced)
uses a wide range of methods independently and can plan research for a service
with challenging user needs and a complex user journey. Someone at 4 (Expert)
is someone other researchers ask before choosing an unfamiliar method,
because their advice is what assures best practice across the team. The
baseline for User research methods at senior user researcher is 4 — this
matches the baseline set for it in `## Competency matrix` below.

## About this role level

**The role.** A user researcher plans, designs and carries out research
activities with users, to help teams get a deep understanding of the people
who use government services. This research informs policy, proposition,
service, content and interaction design, so that services work well for users
and achieve their policy intent.

**The level.** A senior user researcher is an experienced practitioner who
can plan and lead user research activities in larger teams and on more
complex services. The role has six levels in total, in order: associate user
researcher, junior user researcher, user researcher, **senior user
researcher** (this level), lead user researcher, and head of user research.
At this level, you are expected to build user-centred practices in teams that
are new to that way of working, align user research activities with the
team's wider plans so the research informs the service proposition, and
supervise and develop other user researchers so that research practice
across the team is assured and improved.

**The skills this level names**, in the order the summary lists them: Agile
research practices, Analysis and synthesis, Inclusive research, Research
management, leadership and assurance, Stakeholder relationship management,
User research methods, and User-centred practice and advocacy. Three of
these — Agile research practices, Inclusive research, and User-centred
practice and advocacy — are worded word-for-word identically to how the
framework describes them at lead user researcher, the level above. That does
not mean they are unimportant here; it means the framework treats them as
already mature by this level, and the step up at lead user researcher shows
up elsewhere, in that level's own distinct accountabilities rather than in
new wording for these three skills.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Agile research practices

**What the framework expects at this level.** A senior user researcher
advocates for agile ways of working and adapts how they design and conduct
research to match the complexity of the product environment. They understand
the strategic decisions the team needs to make, so that the research they
design actually answers those decisions, and they influence decisions about
priorities and agile processes within the team.

**Baseline for this role level:** Baseline: 3 — Advanced. A senior user
researcher is expected to work independently within a team's agile process
and to shape its priorities, which sits at the Advanced end of the scale
rather than requiring full organisational mastery.

**Behavioural indicators**

| Value | Indicator |
| ---: | --- |
| 0 | Has not worked in or observed an agile team, and cannot describe how research fits into sprint cycles or agile ceremonies. |
| 1 | Attends stand-ups and retrospectives when directed, but needs support to see how research findings should feed into the backlog or sprint plan. |
| 2 | Plans research tasks around a team's existing sprint cadence with some guidance, and can explain agile terms and practices to others on request. |
| 3 | Independently adapts the research approach to the pace and complexity of the product environment, and works with the team to understand the strategic decisions it needs to make so research answers the right question at the right time. |
| 4 | Actively shapes how a team runs its agile ceremonies and sequences its backlog, using research findings to influence prioritisation, and is asked by other teams for advice on integrating research into agile delivery. |

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask: "Tell me about a time you changed how your team planned its sprints
  because of a research finding. What did you change, and what happened?"
- A sprint or research plan you produced, showing how research
  activities were sequenced against the team's roadmap.
- Ask a referee whether you have influenced a team's agile process,
  not just fitted your own work around it.

### Skill: Analysis and synthesis

**What the framework expects at this level.** A senior user researcher
understands a range of methods for analysing research data and synthesising
findings, and helps teams apply them. They can engage sceptical colleagues in
the analysis and synthesis process, and they advise on the choice and
application of techniques and critique colleagues' findings, so that the
team's work stays at a high standard.

**Baseline for this role level:** Baseline: 3 — Advanced. This level's own
accountability to supervise and develop other user researchers is what
raises this skill above straightforward independent practice, without yet
requiring the organisation-wide assurance role that a lead user researcher
holds.

**Behavioural indicators**

| Value | Indicator |
| ---: | --- |
| 0 | Cannot describe or apply any structured method for analysing qualitative or quantitative research data. |
| 1 | Applies a single analysis method, such as basic thematic coding, when closely supported by a more experienced colleague. |
| 2 | Applies a small number of established analysis and synthesis methods correctly with some guidance, and produces findings a colleague can follow. |
| 3 | Helps the team choose and apply an appropriate range of analysis and synthesis methods, engages sceptical colleagues in the process, and can explain the reasoning behind a set of findings under challenge. |
| 4 | Advises other researchers on the choice and application of analysis and synthesis techniques, and critiques colleagues' findings to assure the work meets best practice before it is shared. |

**Evidence to gather**

- Work sample: a set of findings you synthesised from raw research
  data, and the reasoning you can give for the themes you drew out.
- Ask: "A colleague disagrees with how you've grouped your findings. Talk me
  through how you'd respond."
- Ask a referee for an example of you critiquing another
  researcher's analysis constructively.

### Skill: Inclusive research

**What the framework expects at this level.** A senior user researcher helps
teams understand the diversity of users of government services, effectively
includes all kinds of users in appropriate research activities, and advocates
inclusive practices so that teams design and deliver services that work for
everyone.

**Baseline for this role level:** Baseline: 3 — Advanced. This wording is
identical at lead user researcher, the level above — the framework expects
the same standard of inclusive practice at both levels, and the real step up
at lead shows in that level's wider, cross-team accountabilities rather than
in this skill's own text.

**Behavioural indicators**

| Value | Indicator |
| ---: | --- |
| 0 | Has not considered how research participants might differ from the researcher's own assumptions about a "typical" user. |
| 1 | Recruits participants using a standard recruitment brief, with support to widen it to reflect a fuller range of users. |
| 2 | Adapts recruitment and research materials for some access needs, with guidance from a more experienced colleague or an accessibility specialist. |
| 3 | Helps a team understand the diversity of users of a government service, and independently designs and runs research that includes a broad range of users, including those with access needs. |
| 4 | Sets the standard for inclusive research across a team, coaching other researchers on inclusive methods and resolving difficult inclusion trade-offs without needing to escalate them. |

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask: "Tell me about a piece of research you adapted to include participants
  with an access need. What did you change, and why?"
- A recruitment screener or research plan showing how you widened
  participation beyond an easy-to-reach group.
- Ask a referee whether you have coached someone else on inclusive
  research practice.

### Skill: Research management, leadership and assurance

**What the framework expects at this level.** A senior user researcher
evaluates the quality of research against accepted professional standards,
can explain to others what good user research practice involves, and gives
other user researchers constructive feedback so their work meets those
standards.

**Baseline for this role level:** Baseline: 4 — Expert. This skill maps
directly onto this level's own accountability to supervise and develop other
user researchers to assure and improve research practice, which asks for more
than independent competence: it asks the person to hold and pass on the
standard.

**Behavioural indicators**

| Value | Indicator |
| ---: | --- |
| 0 | Cannot describe what distinguishes good user research practice from poor practice. |
| 1 | Follows a research plan or process set by someone else, without yet being able to evaluate whether the approach is sound. |
| 2 | Can describe accepted professional standards for user research and apply them to their own work with some guidance. |
| 3 | Evaluates the quality of research against accepted professional standards, and explains what good user research practice involves to others on the team. |
| 4 | Gives other user researchers regular, constructive feedback that measurably improves the quality of their work, and is trusted to confirm that research meets good practice standards before it informs a decision. |

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask: "Describe a time you gave feedback that changed how a colleague ran
  your research. What did you say, and what changed?"
- A review or feedback note you wrote on a colleague's research
  plan or findings.
- Ask a referee whether other researchers seek you out for
  feedback before you publish findings.

### Skill: Stakeholder relationship management

**What the framework expects at this level.** A senior user researcher works
with the team to build and maintain an understanding of stakeholders,
develops and implements a stakeholder communications approach, identifies and
resolves issues, influences stakeholders, and builds long-term strategic
relationships, communicating with them clearly and regularly.

**Baseline for this role level:** Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Building long-term
strategic relationships and resolving issues independently is squarely
Advanced practice for a team-level senior practitioner, short of the
director-level negotiation expected further up the ladder.

**Behavioural indicators**

| Value | Indicator |
| ---: | --- |
| 0 | Has no experience identifying or working with stakeholders on a service. |
| 1 | Attends stakeholder meetings arranged by others, with support to prepare what to say. |
| 2 | Maintains a basic map of who the stakeholders are and what they need, with some guidance from the team. |
| 3 | Works with the team to develop and maintain an understanding of stakeholders, implements a stakeholder communications approach, and resolves everyday issues by influencing stakeholders directly. |
| 4 | Builds long-term strategic relationships with senior stakeholders, communicating clearly and regularly, and is someone the team turns to when a stakeholder relationship needs repairing. |

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask: "Tell me about a stakeholder relationship that started badly. What did
  you do, and how did it end up?"
- A stakeholder map or communications plan you produced or
  maintained.
- Ask a referee for an example of you resolving a disagreement
  with a stakeholder without escalating it.

### Skill: User research methods

**What the framework expects at this level.** A senior user researcher uses a
wide range of user research methods and helps teams adopt them, plans
research for services with challenging user needs and complex user journeys,
and advises colleagues on the choice and application of research methods to
assure best practice.

**Baseline for this role level:** Baseline: 4 — Expert. This is one of the
role's core, defining skills, and the framework's own wording — advising
colleagues to assure best practice — describes someone other researchers rely
on, which is Expert-level mastery rather than independent Advanced practice.

**Behavioural indicators**

| Value | Indicator |
| ---: | --- |
| 0 | Can name a research method but has not planned or run one unsupervised. |
| 1 | Runs a single, well-defined research method, such as a moderated usability test, with close supervision. |
| 2 | Plans and runs a small number of established research methods independently, on services with reasonably well-understood user needs. |
| 3 | Uses a wide range of user research methods and helps teams adopt them, and plans research for services with challenging user needs and complex user journeys. |
| 4 | Advises other researchers on the choice and application of research methods, and is someone colleagues check with before choosing an unfamiliar method, to assure best practice. |

**Evidence to gather**

- Work sample: a research plan for a service with a complex or unfamiliar
  user journey, and your reasoning for the methods chosen.
- Ask: "Talk me through the widest range of methods you've used on one
  project, and why each one was the right choice."
- Ask a referee for an example of a colleague choosing a different method
  because of your advice.

### Skill: User-centred practice and advocacy

**What the framework expects at this level.** A senior user researcher
evaluates and chooses approaches for building their team's understanding of
the user, advocates for user research with sceptical colleagues and
stakeholders, helps inexperienced teams adopt user-centred practices,
identifies the most important challenges and opportunities for their team's
service development, and aligns research plans to address team priorities.

**Baseline for this role level:** Baseline: 3 — Advanced. This wording is
identical at lead user researcher, the level above — the framework expects
the same standard of advocacy at both levels, with the step up at lead
showing in that level's wider accountability to align activity across
several teams rather than in new wording here.

**Behavioural indicators**

| Value | Indicator |
| ---: | --- |
| 0 | Has not advocated for user research to a colleague or stakeholder who was sceptical of its value. |
| 1 | Explains what user research is when asked, but needs support to make the case for it to a sceptical audience. |
| 2 | Helps a team follow an existing user-centred practice with some guidance, and can explain why a particular research activity matters. |
| 3 | Evaluates and chooses approaches for building a team's understanding of the user, advocates for user research with sceptical colleagues and stakeholders, and aligns research plans to the team's priorities. |
| 4 | Turns an inexperienced team into a user-centred one, identifying the most important challenges and opportunities for the service and shaping the team's whole approach to development around them. |

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask: "Tell me about a team that didn't value user research when you
  joined. What did you do, and did it change?"
- A document you wrote to make the case for research to a
  sceptical stakeholder.
- Ask a referee whether you have changed how an inexperienced team
  works, not just delivered research for it.
