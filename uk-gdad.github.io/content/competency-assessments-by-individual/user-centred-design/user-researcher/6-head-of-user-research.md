# Head of User Research - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This is for you: a chance to rate your own current strengths against the
baseline the UK GDAD Profession Capability Framework sets for this role
level, using the framework's own words for what it covers.

User researchers plan, design and carry out research activities with users that help teams get a deep understanding of the people that use government services. This research informs policy, proposition, service, content and interaction design so that services work well for users and achieve policy intent. A head of user research leads user researchers in an organisation and attracts and builds talent.

Work through the competency matrix below, one skill at a time, then fill in
the overall determination at the end. Treat this as a mirror, not a test —
there's no assessor watching, so being honest with yourself only costs you a
slightly less flattering picture today in exchange for a far more useful one.
Read `## Advisory` before you begin.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating you
give yourself here must never be treated as equivalent to a formal
assessment for a hiring, promotion, pay or performance decision. Use it for
your own reflection, and talk to your manager, a mentor, or your
organisation's people development team about what you find.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you're rating
yourself against what this level actually expects rather than the level
above or below it. Then think back over recent, real examples of your work —
not how you'd like to have handled something, but what you actually did.

**Rate on evidence, not impression.** For every skill, try to recall a
specific moment that supports the rating you give yourself. If you can't
think of one, that's useful information too — it might mean the rating
should be lower, or just that you haven't had the chance to show this skill
yet, which is worth noting separately.

**Be consistent with yourself.** Try to apply the same standard across every
skill, rather than being generous where you feel confident and harsh where
you feel unsure. Coming back to this after a few days, with fresh examples in
mind, often sharpens a rating more than sitting with it in one go.

**Be kind, and be honest.** This only works if you can look at the result
without flinching. A low rating here is not a verdict on you — it's a
starting point. Everyone doing this role, at every level, has skills they are
still building.

## Rating scale

This is a proficiency scale, not a frequency scale — it measures how well
someone can do something, not how often they do it.

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

Worked example, using **Stakeholder relationship management**: someone at 2
(Intermediate) can set stakeholder objectives for one part of the
organisation, with guidance from another senior leader. Someone at 3
(Advanced) directs the stakeholder relationship strategy for the teams and
programmes they lead, independently. Someone at 4 (Expert) influences and
negotiates with the most senior stakeholders in the organisation, and across
other government organisations, to resolve issues that no single team could
unblock alone. The baseline for Stakeholder relationship management at head
of user research is 4 — this matches the baseline set for it in
`## Competency matrix` below.

## About this role level

**The role.** A user researcher plans, designs and carries out research
activities with users, to help teams get a deep understanding of the people
who use government services. This research informs policy, proposition,
service, content and interaction design, so that services work well for
users and achieve their policy intent.

**The level.** A head of user research leads user researchers in an
organisation and attracts and builds talent. The role has six levels in
total, in order: associate user researcher, junior user researcher, user
researcher, senior user researcher, lead user researcher, and **head of user
research** (this level, the most senior). At this level, you are expected to
be an expert practitioner who defines and assures best practice, to
influence organisational strategy and priorities, and to collaborate with
colleagues across government — accountable for the discipline as a whole
within your organisation, not for any single programme.

**The skills this level names**, in the order the summary lists them: Agile
research practices, Analysis and synthesis, Inclusive research, Research
management, leadership and assurance, Stakeholder relationship management,
User research methods, and User-centred practice and advocacy. Three of
these — Analysis and synthesis, Research management, leadership and
assurance, and Stakeholder relationship management — are worded
word-for-word identically to how the framework describes them at lead user
researcher, the level below: the framework treats organisation-wide mastery
of these three as already reached by lead user researcher, and does not ask
for anything further in the wording at head of user research. User research
methods is also identical to lead user researcher. Agile research practices
and Inclusive research keep the same overall shape but are reworded to speak
of organisations rather than teams and of coaching other researchers,
including other leads. User-centred practice and advocacy changes
substantially, from team- and programme-level advocacy to ensuring that
senior, cross-organisational discussions consider user needs at all.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Agile research practices

**What the framework expects at this level.** A head of user research
advocates for agile working at organisational level and with senior
stakeholders, helps organisations adopt agile research practices, sets
standards and expectations for how research insight should be delivered to
support delivery by agile teams, and coaches and guides other user
researchers to adopt agile ways of working.

**Baseline for this role level:** Baseline: 3 — Advanced. The wording here is
reworded rather than identical to lead user researcher, extending advocacy
to senior stakeholders and coaching to other researchers, including leads.
It is a clear, senior duty, but the head's most defining accountabilities —
setting organisational standards and directing stakeholder strategy — sit in
other skills below, so this one is Advanced rather than requiring the
organisation's single reference point.

**Behavioural indicators**

| Value | Indicator |
| ---: | --- |
| 0 | Cannot describe how agile research practice is expected to work across the organisation. |
| 1 | Advocates for agile working in conversations with their own former team, but needs support to make the case at senior or organisational level. |
| 2 | Helps one part of the organisation adopt agile research practices, with guidance from other senior leaders. |
| 3 | Advocates for agile working with senior stakeholders, and sets standards and expectations for how research insight should be delivered to support agile teams across the organisation. |
| 4 | Coaches and guides other user researchers, including other leads, to adopt agile ways of working, and their standards are the ones the organisation's agile teams are held to. |

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask: "Tell me about a time you made the case for agile research practice
  to a senior stakeholder who wasn't convinced. What did you say, and what
  changed?"
- A standard or set of expectations you set for how research
  insight is delivered into agile teams across the organisation.
- Ask a referee whether you have coached another lead or senior
  researcher, not just a junior one, on agile ways of working.

### Skill: Analysis and synthesis

**What the framework expects at this level.** A head of user research helps
the organisation adopt a wide range of analysis and synthesis techniques,
and works to continually assure, improve and innovate those practices, so
the organisation generates clear and valuable findings.

**Baseline for this role level:** Baseline: 4 — Expert. This wording is
identical to lead user researcher, the level below, so the baseline stays
the same: the framework already asks for organisation-wide mastery from lead
level upward, and does not raise the bar further in the text at head of user
research.

**Behavioural indicators**

| Value | Indicator |
| ---: | --- |
| 0 | Has not shaped how more than one part of the organisation analyses or synthesises research data. |
| 1 | Supports one team's adoption of an analysis technique when asked, with close guidance from peers. |
| 2 | Helps two or three teams adopt a shared analysis or synthesis approach, with some guidance. |
| 3 | Helps the organisation adopt a wide range of analysis and synthesis techniques, and improves how more than one part of the organisation applies them. |
| 4 | Continually assures, improves and innovates the organisation's analysis and synthesis practice at large, so that leaders outside research trust the findings that come out of it. |

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask: "Tell me about a time you changed how the organisation as a whole
  analyses or synthesises research, not just one team's practice."
- A standard, framework, or tool you introduced that other
  research teams across the organisation now use.
- Ask a referee whether leaders outside the research profession trust and
  cite findings shaped by your standards.

### Skill: Inclusive research

**What the framework expects at this level.** A head of user research helps
organisations understand the diversity of users of government services, and
guides organisations to adopt inclusive practices and apply what they learn
to design and deliver accessible services that work for all users.

**Baseline for this role level:** Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Reworded from lead
user researcher to speak of organisations rather than teams, this remains a
clear senior duty; it is Advanced here because the head's central,
distinguishing accountabilities for defining and assuring best practice sit
more directly in the Research management and Stakeholder skills below.

**Behavioural indicators**

| Value | Indicator |
| ---: | --- |
| 0 | Has not helped the organisation, as opposed to a single team, think about the diversity of the users it serves. |
| 1 | Repeats guidance on inclusive research written by someone else, with support to apply it more widely. |
| 2 | Helps a second part of the organisation adopt an inclusive research practice already used elsewhere, with some guidance. |
| 3 | Helps the organisation understand the diversity of users of government services, and guides more than one part of it to adopt inclusive practices. |
| 4 | Their guidance visibly changes how the organisation designs and delivers accessible services, and other leaders defer to their judgement on inclusion trade-offs. |

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask: "Tell me about a change to how the organisation, not just a single
  team, approaches inclusive research or accessible service design. What was
  your part in it?"
- Organisation-level guidance or a policy you authored or
  sponsored on inclusive research practice.
- Ask a referee whether other senior leaders have deferred to your judgement on an inclusion trade-off.

### Skill: Research management, leadership and assurance

**What the framework expects at this level.** A head of user research
establishes and aligns user research programmes and teams to meet
organisational goals, synthesises findings from research programmes into
strategic insight that informs the organisation's roadmap, sets and assures
good practice user research standards for the organisation — including
ethics and safeguarding — and assures user research quality across the
organisation.

**Baseline for this role level:** Baseline: 4 — Expert. This wording is
identical to lead user researcher, the level below, so the baseline stays
the same. This skill is the clearest expression of the head's own defining
accountability to define and assure best practice, and the framework already
places it at full mastery from lead level upward.

**Behavioural indicators**

| Value | Indicator |
| ---: | --- |
| 0 | Has not set a user research standard that applies beyond a single programme. |
| 1 | Applies a standard someone else set, with support, to a new programme. |
| 2 | Helps align a second research programme to organisational goals, with some guidance. |
| 3 | Establishes and aligns user research programmes and teams across the organisation to meet organisational goals, and synthesises findings into strategic insight for the organisation's roadmap. |
| 4 | Sets and assures the organisation's good practice user research standards, including ethics and safeguarding, and is accountable for research quality across the whole organisation. |

**Evidence to gather**

- Work sample: an organisation-wide research standard, ethics framework, or
  safeguarding policy you owns.
- Ask: "Tell me about a time you were accountable for the quality of
  research you had no direct hand in producing. What did you do?"
- Ask a referee whether your standards are the ones the
  organisation's research function is actually measured against.

### Skill: Stakeholder relationship management

**What the framework expects at this level.** A head of user research
directs the stakeholder relationship strategy for their teams, ensures
stakeholders' objectives are set and supports teams to meet them, and
influences and negotiates with senior stakeholders to resolve issues and
enable progress.

**Baseline for this role level:** Baseline: 4 — Expert. This wording is
identical to lead user researcher, the level below, so the baseline stays
the same. At head of user research this is exercised with the most senior
stakeholders in the organisation and with other government organisations,
which is where the practical step up is seen.

**Behavioural indicators**

| Value | Indicator |
| ---: | --- |
| 0 | Has not directed a stakeholder strategy at organisational level. |
| 1 | Follows an existing stakeholder strategy, with support, when representing research to a new senior stakeholder. |
| 2 | Sets stakeholder objectives for one part of the organisation, with guidance from another senior leader. |
| 3 | Directs the stakeholder relationship strategy for the teams and programmes they lead, ensuring stakeholders' objectives are set and supported. |
| 4 | Influences and negotiates with the most senior stakeholders in the organisation, and across other government organisations, to resolve issues and enable progress that no single team could unblock alone. |

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask: "Tell me about a negotiation with a very senior stakeholder, or with
  another government organisation, that you led. What was at stake, and how
  did it resolve?"
- A document or record showing you directing stakeholder strategy
  at organisational level.
- Ask a referee whether you are the person the organisation sends
  into the most difficult senior-stakeholder conversations about research.

### Skill: User-centred practice and advocacy

**What the framework expects at this level.** A head of user research finds
ways to help an organisation adopt user-centred practices and appreciate the
value of understanding users, ensures discussions at senior levels or
between organisations consider user needs and evolving user behaviour, and
helps an organisation understand the complete journey of the user and use
that knowledge to improve its services.

**Baseline for this role level:** Baseline: 4 — Expert. This text is
substantially rewritten from lead user researcher, moving from team- and
programme-level advocacy to ensuring that the most senior, cross-organisational
conversations consider user needs at all — a distinct and demanding duty
that the framework's own wording places at full mastery.

**Behavioural indicators**

| Value | Indicator |
| ---: | --- |
| 0 | Has not raised user needs in a discussion between senior leaders or between organisations. |
| 1 | Raises user needs in a discussion when prompted by someone more senior. |
| 2 | Makes the case for considering user needs in one senior-level discussion, with some guidance on how to frame it. |
| 3 | Finds ways to help the organisation adopt user-centred practices and appreciate the value of understanding users, and ensures some senior discussions consider user needs. |
| 4 | Ensures discussions at senior levels, or between organisations, routinely consider user needs and evolving user behaviour, and uses their understanding of the complete user journey to improve services at organisational scale. |

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask: "Tell me about a senior-level or cross-organisational decision that
  changed because you brought user needs into the room. What was the
  decision, and what changed?"
- A strategy document, board paper, or similar record showing user needs
  shaping a senior or cross-organisational decision.
- Ask a referee whether senior discussions you are not personally
  in still tend to consider user needs, because of standards or habits you
  established.
