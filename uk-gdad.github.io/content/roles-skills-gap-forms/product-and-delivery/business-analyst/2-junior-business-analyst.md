# Junior business analyst - skills gap form

## Introduction

Hello, and welcome to this form.

It is for you if you work as a junior business analyst in the UK Government
Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that
you can say what you already do, what you have not had a chance to try, and what
would help you most over the next few months.

Nothing here is scored, marked or graded, and nobody is comparing your answers
with a colleague's. Junior is a learning level by design: you receive direction
from more senior analysts but you are responsible for the output of specific
tasks, and you support stakeholder relationship management.

The form walks through the nine skills the framework names for your level. Two
of them are new compared with trainee — business modelling, and user experience
analysis.

Most people take about 40 minutes. You can stop and come back.

## Advisory

This skills gap form is created by AI and is work in progress for educational
purposes. If you intend to use it in any way, please consult with your
organization's appropriate teammates, such as with human resources staff, or

## How to fill in this form

You can fill this form in on screen. Type into the boxes, tick the boxes that
apply, and pick a rating where one is asked for. Your answers are saved in
this browser, on this device, so you can close the page and come back to them.
Nothing is sent anywhere.

When you have finished, use Export TSV or Export JSON at the top of the
page, or print it, or copy your answers into a document or an email, or answer
in whatever tool your organisation already uses. Every question has a number,
like **Q7**, so your answers make sense on their own. Clear answers empties
the form and deletes the saved copy. If other people use this computer, press
it when you have finished.

Before you start:

- **"I don't know" is a real answer**, and it tells people what to teach you.
- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a mark against you.
- **One real example beats a general claim.**
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers or what
  they are used for, ask before you fill it in.

## About this role level

**The role: business analyst.** Business analysts help teams analyse and
understand a business problem or opportunity; research how a business area works
across people, organisation, processes, information, data and technology;
identify improvements, explore options, analyse the effects of change and define
success measures; identify and elaborate user and business needs; support
prioritisation and minimum viable product decisions; make sure new products and
services meet business and user needs; and understand the business and policy
constraints that apply.

**Your level: junior business analyst.** At this level you receive direction from
more senior business analysts but are responsible for the output of specific
tasks. The framework says you will have limited skills, will work with others or
under supervision, and will support stakeholder relationship management.

**Where this level sits.** The business analyst role has six levels:

1. Trainee business analyst
2. **Junior business analyst** — you are here
3. Business analyst
4. Senior business analyst
5. Lead business analyst
6. Head of business analysis

Your step up is business analyst, and it is a real one: *every* skill below
changes. The pattern is consistent — supervision drops away, you plan the
approach rather than following it, and you start challenging requirements rather
than capturing them.

**One thing about the framework's own text.** It lists *context, problem and
option analysis* twice for your level, with identical wording both times. That is
a duplication in the published source, not two expectations, so this form treats
it as one skill.

**The skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Adapting to delivery methodologies
2. Business modelling
3. Business process improvement
4. Context, problem and option analysis
5. Defining and managing business needs, user needs and requirements
6. Stakeholder relationship management
7. Digital and data systems analysis
8. Testing (business analysis)
9. User experience analysis

## Job skills

### Skill: Adapting to delivery methodologies

**What this means.** Knowing how teams organise work — scrum, kanban, a staged
plan — and how analysis fits each.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: explain the
differences between delivery methodologies; explain how your approach would
change based on the methodology; explain why iteration is important; and explain
how roles in a multidisciplinary team work together.

**What that looks like here.** You can say what your team's ceremonies are for
and when they are wasted. You know why analysis in an iterative team is done in
slices rather than up front.

**The next step.** At business analyst you *adapt* how you work and what you
produce to fit the methodology, rather than explaining it.

### Skill: Business modelling

**What this means.** Representing how a business works — processes, information,
roles, systems — so people can see it and reason about changing it. This skill is
new at your level.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: explain basic
business modelling techniques; support the representation of business situations
within a defined task; and visualise business processes.

**What that looks like here.** You can produce a process model people recognise
as their own work. You know at least a couple of notations and what each is good
for. Your models get used in conversations rather than filed.

**The next step.** At business analyst you model various elements of the business
with limited direction, and understand the impact of potential changes and how
processes, systems, data, roles and responsibilities interact.

### Skill: Business process improvement

**What this means.** Understanding how work actually gets done, finding what is
wrong with it, and helping change it.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: contribute to the
analysis and evaluation of business processes to identify problems and
opportunities; contribute to the design and implementation of improvements; and
contribute to business process testing and usability evaluation.

**What that looks like here.** You have found the difference between the
documented process and the real one. You have contributed a change that people
actually adopted. You have checked afterwards whether it helped.

**The next step.** At business analyst you analyse and evaluate processes
yourself, design and prioritise improvements with some support, and validate them
through testing.

### Skill: Context, problem and option analysis

**What this means.** Working out what the real problem is, what is happening
around it, and what could be done about it.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: describe the analysis
techniques you could use; explain the importance of understanding that internal
and external factors influence the organisation; and contribute to identifying
and assessing multiple options and formulating recommendations, under
supervision.

**What that looks like here.** You have helped put together options rather than
one answer. You know what is going on around your work — policy, funding, other
programmes. You can say what a technique is for rather than only its name.

**The next step.** At business analyst you plan and use the most appropriate
techniques yourself, define problem statements, evaluate multiple options, and
formulate recommendations considering feasibility, benefits and impact.

### Skill: Defining and managing business needs, user needs and requirements

**What this means.** Finding out what is actually needed, writing it down so it
can be built and tested, and keeping track of it as things change.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: support identifying,
analysing, capturing and validating business and user requirements; work under
supervision to manage requirements and support their prioritisation within a
defined scope; and provide distinct task-based outputs as directed, using a
predetermined requirements management life cycle.

**What that looks like here.** Your requirements can be built and tested from.
You know which are agreed and which are still someone's preference. You keep the
record current rather than letting it drift.

**The next step.** At business analyst you identify, analyse, *challenge* and
validate requirements, and co-ordinate and review prioritisation with limited
supervision.

### Skill: Stakeholder relationship management

**What this means.** Knowing who has an interest in the work, what each of them
cares about, and helping keep those relationships working.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: describe who your
stakeholders are and the importance of managing relationships with them; and
explain what your stakeholders find important and why.

**What that looks like here.** You could list your stakeholders and say what each
wants. You know who must be consulted and who merely likes to be. You notice when
two of them want incompatible things.

**The next step.** At business analyst you identify important stakeholders and
communicate with them clearly and regularly, tailor communication to their needs,
build consensus, and improve relationships using evidence.

### Skill: Digital and data systems analysis

**What this means.** Understanding what systems can and cannot do, and turning
business needs into system requirements.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: support the analysis of

requirements; and support the development of specifications and models for
bespoke systems or software packages.

**What that looks like here.** You know what the systems in your area do and
where their limits are. You have helped write a specification. You can ask a
developer a sensible feasibility question.

**The next step.** At business analyst you identify and analyse system
capabilities yourself, and develop models and system requirements with minimal
supervision.

### Skill: Testing (business analysis)

**What this means.** Understanding how what was asked for gets checked, and
making sure requirements can be traced through to working functionality.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: understand the stages
and purpose of testing and have an awareness of the tools and techniques used;
and work under supervision to support the representation of business scenarios
and to trace requirements to developed functionality.

**What that looks like here.** You can turn a requirement into a scenario someone
could test. You know what each testing stage is for. You understand why
traceability matters when someone asks whether something was delivered.

**The next step.** At business analyst you identify business scenarios and
develop acceptance criteria, and review prototypes, test plans and test outcome
reporting.

### Skill: User experience analysis

**What this means.** Using what user research finds to shape what gets built, and
connecting user needs to system, product, or service requirements. This skill is
new at your level.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: understand the
importance of making data-informed decisions based on user research findings; and
support the analysis and prioritisation of user experience needs, understanding
how those needs tie to system, product or service requirements.

**What that looks like here.** You have watched or read user research and used it
rather than noted it. You can connect a research finding to a requirement. You
know when a decision is being made on opinion where evidence exists.

**The next step.** At business analyst you apply basic techniques to analyse,
validate and prioritise user experience needs, and present findings accessibly to
support data-informed decisions.
