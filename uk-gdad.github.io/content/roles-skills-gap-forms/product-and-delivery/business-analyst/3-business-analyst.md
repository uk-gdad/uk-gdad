# Business analyst - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a business analyst in the UK Government
Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that
you can set out what you do now, what you have not had the chance to do, and what
support would be most useful over the coming months.

At this level you lead small to medium projects and support larger, more complex
ones. The framework expects you to manage stakeholder relationships, work
independently, have a good understanding of your work area, and develop the
business analysis community by sharing good practice and mentoring others.

This is the level where the supervision drops away. Every skill below is worded
differently from junior — the pattern is that you now plan the approach rather
than follow it, and challenge requirements rather than capture them.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Each of the nine skills gets an
explanation, a note on what changes at senior, a rating and some questions.
Expect about 40 minutes.

## How to fill in this form

You can fill this form in on screen: the boxes below take typing, the tick
boxes tick, and each skill has a rating to choose. Your answers are saved in
this browser, on this device, so you can close the page and come back to them.
Nothing is sent anywhere.

When you have finished, use Export TSV or Export as JSON at the top of the
page to take your answers with you, or print the page, or copy them into
whatever your organisation uses for development conversations. Every question
is numbered, so answers travel back on their own. Clear answers empties the
form and deletes the saved copy, which is worth doing on a computer other
people use.

Worth knowing:

- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a finding.
- **"I haven't had the chance" is a real answer**, and more useful than a
  generous reading of a thin example.
- **Name a real project** rather than describing what you generally do.
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers, what they
  are used for, or how long they are kept, ask before you fill it in.

## About this role level

**The role: business analyst.** Business analysts help teams analyse and
understand a business problem or opportunity; research how a business area works
across people, organisation, processes, information, data and technology;
identify improvements, explore options, analyse the effects of change and define
success measures; identify and elaborate user and business needs; support
prioritisation and minimum viable product decisions; make sure new products and
services meet business and user needs; and understand business and policy
constraints.

**Your level: business analyst.** At this level you lead small to medium size
projects and support larger and more complex ones. The framework expects that
you:

- manage stakeholder relationships
- work independently
- have a good understanding of your work area
- develop the business analysis community by sharing best practice and mentoring
  others

**Where this level sits.** The business analyst role has six levels:

1. Trainee business analyst
2. Junior business analyst
3. **Business analyst** — you are here
4. Senior business analyst
5. Lead business analyst
6. Head of business analysis

Your step up is senior business analyst, where every skill moves again — this
time from doing the work well to *leading the approach* to it, with functional or
people management responsibilities added.

**One thing about the framework's own text.** It lists *context, problem and
option analysis* twice at your level, with identical wording. That is a
duplication in the published source, not two expectations, so this form treats it
as one skill.

**The skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Adapting to delivery methodologies
2. Business modelling
3. Business process improvement
4. Context, problem and option analysis
5. Defining and managing business needs, user needs and requirements
6. Stakeholder relationship management
7. Digital and data systems analysis
8. Testing (business analysis)
9. User experience analysis

## Job skills

Nine skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the level expects,
what it looks like in practice, and what changes at senior.

### Skill: Adapting to delivery methodologies

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: explain how products
and services are delivered through different methodologies; follow an appropriate
approach to complete your work within your team's methodology; adapt how you work
and what you produce based on that methodology to ensure you deliver value; and
adapt how you work with other roles in a multidisciplinary team accordingly.

**What that looks like here.** You produce analysis in slices when the team works
in slices, and a fuller picture when the delivery is staged. You do not insist on
artefacts the team will not use.

**The next step.** At senior you *determine* the approach for your role, define
and agree the scope of your work within the team, negotiate delivery priorities,
and communicate the value of iterative delivery and early benefits.

### Skill: Business modelling

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: model various elements
of the business with limited direction; and understand the impact of potential
changes and how business processes, systems, data, roles and responsibilities
interact.

**What that looks like here.** You model more than processes — data, roles,
responsibilities, and how they interact. You can trace a proposed change through
the model and say what else it touches.

**The next step.** At senior you *lead* modelling of processes, systems and data
at varying levels of complexity across a project or programme, and manage the
impact of proposed changes.

### Skill: Business process improvement

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: analyse and evaluate
business processes to identify problems and opportunities; design, prioritise and
facilitate the implementation of improvements, with some support; and validate
improvements through testing.

**What that looks like here.** You find the problem and design the change. You
prioritise between improvements rather than doing them in the order they arrived.
You check afterwards whether the change worked.

**The next step.** At senior you *lead* process analysis and evaluation, lead the
design, testing, implementation and assessment of improvements, and manage the
design and execution of process tests and usability evaluations.

### Skill: Context, problem and option analysis

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: plan and use the most
appropriate analysis techniques to understand the context, the problem and root
causes; communicate how your work supports and aligns to strategic contexts and
goals; define problem statements and develop and evaluate multiple options; and
formulate recommendations considering feasibility, benefits and impact.

**What that looks like here.** You choose the technique to fit the question. Your
problem statements are recognised by the people who have the problem. Your
options are genuinely different, and your recommendation says what it would cost
as well as what it would give.

**The next step.** At senior you *lead* the approach to understanding context and
root causes, set goals aligned to strategic context, and lead the development and
evaluation of options while advocating for recommendations to gain support.

### Skill: Defining and managing business needs, user needs and requirements

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: identify, analyse,
*challenge* and validate business and user requirements; work under limited
supervision to co-ordinate and review the prioritisation of requirements; and use
appropriate requirements management life cycle methods to complete tasks and
outputs.

**What that looks like here.** You push back on a requirement that is really a
preferred solution. You keep prioritisation honest when everything is called
essential. Your requirements can be traced from need to build to test.

**The next step.** At senior you *advise on the approach* to requirements
management, define the most appropriate life cycle methods and ensure
traceability through design, build, test, tender and evaluation, and negotiate
solutions to meet programme objectives.

### Skill: Stakeholder relationship management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: identify important
stakeholders and communicate with them clearly and regularly; tailor
communication to their needs and work with them to build relationships while
meeting user needs; build and reach consensus with stakeholders; and work to
improve stakeholder relationships using evidence to explain decisions.

**What that looks like here.** People hear from you before they have to ask. You
have brought two stakeholders to an agreement neither started with. You use
evidence rather than authority — which is all you have.

**The next step.** At senior you work *with the team* to develop and maintain
stakeholder understanding and communications strategies, resolve issues and
influence, and build long-term strategic relationships.

### Skill: Digital and data systems analysis

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: identify and analyse IT
system capabilities; and develop models and system requirements for bespoke
systems or software packages, with minimal supervision.

**What that looks like here.** You can say what the systems can and cannot do
without asking. You write system requirements developers can work from. You know
which limits are real and which are habit.

**The next step.** At senior you *lead* the identification and analysis of system
capabilities, use systems analysis to find gaps, and work with other digital
professionals on interfaces and the effect of software changes.

### Skill: Testing (business analysis)

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: understand the stages
and purpose of testing and support the use of relevant tools and techniques;
identify business scenarios and develop acceptance criteria so requirements can be
traced to developed functionality; and review prototypes, test plans and test
outcome reporting.

**What that looks like here.** Your acceptance criteria are testable rather than
aspirational. You review test plans and notice the scenario nobody covered. You
read outcome reports critically.

**The next step.** At senior you *recommend the value of testing* and apply
relevant tools to ensure test validity, and advise on business scenarios and
acceptance criteria.

### Skill: User experience analysis

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: apply basic techniques
to analyse, validate and prioritise user experience needs; and present findings
in an accessible, easy to understand way to support data-informed decisions based
on user research.

**What that looks like here.** You use research rather than quoting it. You can
prioritise user needs against business needs honestly. Your findings are
presented so a decision can actually be taken from them.

**The next step.** At senior you *advise on the approach* to analysing,
prioritising and validating user experience needs, and recommend a range of
techniques.
