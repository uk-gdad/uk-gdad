# Senior business analyst - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a senior business analyst in the UK
Government Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent
it so that you can set out what you do now, where the gaps are, and what would
close them.

The framework describes a senior business analyst as having a good understanding
of strategic arenas and leading large or complex projects: with functional or
people management responsibilities or both, mentoring others, developing best
practice, owning stakeholder relationships, and managing community of practice
activities within a business area.

Every skill below moves from *doing* to *leading the approach*. That is the
substance of this level: other people's analysis now depends on how you decide it
should be done.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Each of the nine skills gets an
explanation, a note on what changes at lead, a rating and some questions. Expect
about 40 minutes.

## Advisory

This skills gap form is created by AI and is work in progress for educational

Worth knowing:

- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a finding.
- **"The team does this" is not an answer to "what do you do".** Name your part.
- **"I haven't had the chance" is a real answer**, and more useful than a
  generous reading of a thin example.
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers, what they
  are used for, or how long they are kept, ask before you fill it in.

## Rating scale

Where a question asks for a rating, use this scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | You have not worked with this |
| 1 | Awareness | You know what it is and why it matters |
| 2 | Working | You do it with support |
| 3 | Practitioner | You do it unsupported, and handle the usual exceptions |
| 4 | Expert | You set the approach and develop others in it |

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not the remit on your job
  description.
- **A 4 is a claim about other people** — that someone else's practice changed
  because of you and would stay changed. At senior, mentoring is in the job
  description, so one or two are reasonable.

## About this role level

**The role: business analyst.** Business analysts help teams analyse and
understand a business problem or opportunity; research how a business area works;
identify improvements, explore options and define success measures; elaborate
business and user needs; support prioritisation decisions; and understand the
business and policy constraints that apply.

**Your level: senior business analyst.** At this level you have a good
understanding of strategic arenas and lead large or complex projects. The
framework expects that you:

- have functional or people management responsibilities, or both
- mentor others
- develop best practice
- own stakeholder relationships
- manage community of practice activities within a business area

**Where this level sits.** The business analyst role has six levels:

1. Trainee business analyst
2. Junior business analyst
3. Business analyst
4. **Senior business analyst** — you are here
5. Lead business analyst
6. Head of business analysis

Your step up is lead business analyst. Two new skills appear there — *consultancy*
and *enterprise and business architecture* — and most of the others move from
leading an approach to setting direction, advising the organisation, and coaching
others. The lead level also expects you to be a leader in the business analysis
community across government and externally, which is a step outside your own
organisation.

**One thing about the framework's own text.** It lists *context, problem and
option analysis* twice at your level, with identical wording. That is a
duplication in the published source, not two expectations.

**The skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Adapting to delivery methodologies
2. Business modelling
3. Business process improvement
4. Context, problem and option analysis
5. Defining and managing business needs, user needs and requirements
6. Stakeholder relationship management
7. Digital and data systems analysis
8. Testing (business analysis)
9. User experience analysis

## Job skills

Nine skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the level expects,
what it looks like in practice, and what changes at lead.

### Skill: Adapting to delivery methodologies

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: determine the approach
for your role within the team's delivery methodology; define and agree the scope
of delivery for your role within a multidisciplinary team; negotiate and agree
delivery priorities with the team and stakeholders; and clearly communicate the
value of iterative delivery and realising benefits early.

**What that looks like here.** You decide what analysis your role will do and
agree it rather than being handed it. You negotiate priorities rather than
receiving them. You can make the case for early benefits to people who want
everything at the end.

**The next step.** Identical wording at lead business analyst. It changes at head
of business analysis, where you set and iterate standards for working within
different methodologies and experiment with new ways of working.

### Skill: Business modelling

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: lead the modelling of
business processes, systems and data at varying levels of complexity across a
project or programme; and manage the impact of proposed changes.

**What that looks like here.** You decide what gets modelled and to what depth.
You manage the consequences of change rather than only illustrating them — which
means knowing who has to be told and what has to be re-agreed.

**The next step.** At lead you determine the scope and methodology for
representing complex programmes across the organisation, and document conceptual
models supporting strategic planning and decision making.

### Skill: Business process improvement

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: lead process analysis
and evaluation to define business performance problems and opportunities; lead
analysis and evaluation to design, test, implement and assess improvements; and
manage the design, execution and assessment of business process tests and
usability evaluations.

**What that looks like here.** You lead the whole cycle rather than a stage of
it. You define what the performance problem actually is, in terms the business
recognises. You assess afterwards rather than declaring success.

**The next step.** At lead you identify *large-scale* opportunities, set the
direction for analysis, design and evaluation including methods, tools and
standards, and coach others in doing it.

### Skill: Context, problem and option analysis

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: lead the approach to
understanding the context, the problem and root causes; set goals for a piece of
work that align to the organisation's strategic context; and lead the approach to
developing and evaluating multiple options, sharing and advocating for
recommendations to gain support.

**What that looks like here.** You decide how the problem will be investigated,
not just what the answer is. You set goals that connect to strategy in terms a
director recognises. You advocate — which means the recommendation has to survive
a room, not just a document.

**The next step.** At lead you *advise on approaches* to options analysis,
feasibility and impact assessment, ensure all streams of work align to strategic
goals, advise how the organisation can use business analysis to add value, and
influence strategic plans.

### Skill: Defining and managing business needs, user needs and requirements

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: advise on the
approach to requirements management within a project or programme; define the most
appropriate life cycle methods and ensure requirements can be traced through
design, build, test, tender and evaluation; and co-ordinate and review
prioritisation, engaging in negotiating solutions to meet programme objectives.

**What that looks like here.** You set how requirements will be managed. You make
traceability survive procurement, which is where most of it dies. You negotiate
solutions rather than passing requirements over a wall.

**The next step.** At lead you *direct* the approach, enable others to select and
apply life cycle methods, influence prioritisation of complex requirement sets,
and ensure proposed solutions align with organisational strategy and vision.

### Skill: Stakeholder relationship management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: work with the team to
develop and maintain an understanding of stakeholders; work with the team to
develop and implement stakeholder communications strategies; identify and resolve
issues, influence stakeholders and manage relationships effectively; and build
long-term strategic relationships, communicating clearly and regularly.

**What that looks like here.** Stakeholder knowledge is shared across your team
rather than held in your head. Communication happens on a rhythm. You have
relationships that predate this programme and will outlast it.

**The next step.** At lead you *direct* the stakeholder relationship strategy for
your teams, ensure stakeholders' objectives are set and support teams to meet
them, and negotiate with senior stakeholders to unblock progress.

### Skill: Digital and data systems analysis

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: lead the
identification and analysis of IT system capabilities, developing models and
system requirements; use systems analysis to support a deeper understanding of
how processes and systems work and to identify gaps; and work with other digital
professionals to design and develop system and user interfaces, identify gaps in
functionality, and assess the effect of proposed software changes on users or the
organisation.

**What that looks like here.** You find the gap between what the process needs
and what the systems do. You work with designers and developers as a peer. You
can say what a proposed software change would do to the people using it.

**The next step.** At lead you *set the direction* for identifying and analysing
system capabilities, enable others to assess the effect of software changes, and
bring business and technical perspectives together into recommendations.

### Skill: Testing (business analysis)

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: recommend the value
of testing and apply relevant tools and techniques to ensure test validity;
advise on business scenarios and develop acceptance criteria so requirements can
be traced to developed functionality; and review prototypes, test plans and test
outcome reporting.

**What that looks like here.** You argue for testing where it is being skipped.
You can tell when a test proves less than it claims. Your acceptance criteria
survive contact with a tester.

**The next step.** Identical wording at lead and at head of business analysis —
this skill does not change again in this role.

### Skill: User experience analysis

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: **advise on the
approach to analysis, prioritisation and validation of user experience needs**;
**recommend a range of techniques to analyse the user experience** and ensure
that it meets business and user needs; and **articulate and communicate how user
experience needs affect the design of a system** in a project or programme of
work.

**What that looks like here.** You advise on how user needs will be understood
rather than doing it all yourself. You can recommend a technique to fit a
question. You hold the line when user needs are being traded away quietly.

**The next step.** At lead this adds articulating and communicating how user
experience needs affect the design of a system across a project or programme.
