# Lead business analyst - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a lead business analyst in the UK Government
Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that
you can set out what you do now, where the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes a lead business analyst as having a good understanding of
the enterprise arena and working in highly complex problem spaces, typically
spanning multiple products or services. You are expected to be a leader in the
business analysis community — across government and externally — to hold
functional or people management accountabilities, to actively share knowledge and
be seen as a go-to person in the field, to mentor others, and to own the main
stakeholder relationships.

Two new skills appear at your level: *consultancy*, and *enterprise and business
architecture*. Both take you beyond analysing a problem into shaping how the
organisation is arranged and how it approaches work.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Each of the eleven skills gets an
explanation, a note on what changes at head of business analysis, a rating and
some questions. Expect about 45 minutes.

## About this role level

**The role: business analyst.** Business analysts help teams analyse and
understand a business problem or opportunity; research how a business area works;
identify improvements, explore options and define success measures; elaborate
business and user needs; support prioritisation decisions; and understand the
business and policy constraints that apply.

**Your level: lead business analyst.** At this level you have a good
understanding of the enterprise arena and work in highly complex problem spaces,
typically spanning multiple products or services. The framework expects that you:

- are a leader in the business analysis community, across government and
  externally
- have functional management or people management accountabilities, or both
- actively share knowledge and are seen as a go-to person in the field
- mentor others
- own the main stakeholder relationships

**Where this level sits.** The business analyst role has six levels:

1. Trainee business analyst
2. Junior business analyst
3. Business analyst
4. Senior business analyst
5. **Lead business analyst** — you are here
6. Head of business analysis

Your step up is head of business analysis, and it is narrow on the published
skills. Only two change: *adapting to delivery methodologies*, which becomes
setting and iterating standards and experimenting with new ways of working; and
*consultancy*, which becomes leading the definition of guidance for how the whole
organisation approaches delivery. Everything else keeps your wording. The
difference is the job itself — leading the business analysis function across an
enterprise area and building the organisation's capability.

**One anomaly worth knowing.** At head of business analysis, the framework's
wording for *digital and data systems analysis* is narrower than yours: it
reverts to the senior business analyst text. So on that one skill the published
expectation goes down rather than up. It is the framework's text, not an error in
this form.

**The skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Adapting to delivery methodologies
2. Business modelling
3. Business process improvement
4. Consultancy
5. Context, problem and option analysis
6. Enterprise and business architecture (business analyst)
7. Defining and managing business needs, user needs and requirements
8. Stakeholder relationship management
9. Digital and data systems analysis
10. Testing (business analysis)

## Job skills

Eleven skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the level expects,

### Skill: Adapting to delivery methodologies

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: determine the approach
for your role within the team's delivery methodology; define and agree the scope
of delivery for your role in a multidisciplinary team; negotiate and agree
delivery priorities; and clearly communicate the value of iterative delivery and
realising benefits early.

**What that looks like here.** You decide and agree what analysis is for on each
piece of work. You negotiate priorities across several teams. You make the case
for early benefits where the instinct is a single big launch.

**The next step.** At head of business analysis you *set and iterate standards*
for working within different methodologies, promote collaboration, experiment
with new ways of working, and research evolving methodologies.

### Skill: Consultancy

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: provide advice,
guidance and recommendations based on your specialist knowledge and experience;
propose methodologies to follow and approaches to implementation; and frame
problems so they can easily be understood, troubleshooting where required, to
support the business in operating more effectively. This skill is new at your
level.

**What that looks like here.** People come to you for advice on things that are
not your project. You propose how a piece of work should be approached, not just
what the answer is. You are called in when something is stuck.

**The next step.** At head of business analysis this becomes advice based on
*significant* specialist knowledge, and *leading the definition of guidance* that
informs how the organisation approaches delivery or uses digital and data skills.

### Skill: Digital and data systems analysis

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: set the direction for
the identification and analysis of IT system capabilities; identify gaps in
functionality and enable others to explore and assess the effect of software
changes; demonstrate a strong understanding of business perspectives and
technical concepts and bring them together into recommendations and a common
understanding; and communicate this effectively between users, development teams
and external suppliers.

**What that looks like here.** You are the translator between business and
technical worlds, including with suppliers — which is where most misunderstanding
becomes expensive. You enable others to assess software change rather than doing
it yourself.

*narrower* than yours: it reverts to the senior business analyst text. That is

### Skill: Testing (business analysis)

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: recommend the value
of testing and apply relevant tools and techniques to ensure test validity;
advise on business scenarios and develop acceptance criteria so requirements can
be traced to developed functionality; and review prototypes, test plans and test
outcome reporting.

**What that looks like here.** You argue for testing where it is being cut. You
can tell when a test proves less than it claims. You review the plans that matter
rather than all of them.

**The next step.** Identical wording at head of business analysis — and identical
