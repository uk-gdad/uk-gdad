# Digital portfolio manager - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a digital portfolio manager in the UK
Government Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent
it so that you can set out what you do now, what you have not had the chance to
do, and what support would be most useful over the coming months.

At this level you help co-ordinate and manage a portfolio of digital projects.
The framework expects you to help create environments that enable teams to
deliver, support the prioritisation of a group of projects and programmes, help
teams follow governance and delivery processes, help stakeholders understand the
performance of work and the related risks, and support more senior portfolio
managers and leadership in making improvements.

**One thing to know at the outset.** Compared with the level below, the framework
changes exactly one skill — governance and assurance. Everything else is worded
identically. The step is almost entirely in those accountabilities rather than in
the published skills, which is worth knowing if you are trying to evidence a
promotion.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Each of the six skills gets an
explanation, a note on what changes at senior, a rating and some questions.
Expect about 35 minutes.

## How to fill in this form

You can fill this form in on screen: the boxes below take typing, the tick
boxes tick, and each skill has a rating to choose. Your answers are saved in
this browser, on this device, so you can close the page and come back to them.
Nothing is sent anywhere.

When you have finished, use Export TSV or Export JSON at the top of the
page to take your answers with you, or print the page, or copy them into
whatever your organisation uses for development conversations. Every question
is numbered, so answers travel back on their own. Clear answers empties the
form and deletes the saved copy, which is worth doing on a computer other
people use.

Worth knowing:

- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a finding.
- **"I haven't had the chance" is a real answer**, and more useful than a
  generous reading of a thin example.
- **Name a real project or decision** rather than describing what you generally
  do.
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers, what they
  are used for, or how long they are kept, ask before you fill it in.

## About this role level

**The role: digital portfolio manager.** A digital portfolio manager co-ordinates
and manages a group — a portfolio — of digital projects, using an iterative and
flexible approach to help define governance processes for that portfolio.

**Your level: digital portfolio manager.** At this level you help co-ordinate and
manage the portfolio. The framework expects that you:

- help to create environments that enable teams to deliver
- support the prioritisation of a group of projects and programmes
- help teams to follow governance and delivery processes
- help stakeholders understand the performance of work and any related risks
- support more senior portfolio managers and leadership in making improvements

**Where this level sits.** This role has four levels:

1. Digital portfolio analyst
2. **Digital portfolio manager** — you are here
3. Senior digital portfolio manager
4. Head of portfolio

Your step up is senior digital portfolio manager, and unlike the step below it,
this one is substantial. Four skills change: communicating analysis becomes
turning complex data into actionable stories and communicating negative as well as
positive information; governance becomes developing processes and recommending
improvements; planning grows from awareness into a full planning discipline; and
stakeholder management and strategic thinking both move up.

**One thing worth knowing about this role.** *Agile working* is worded identically
at all four levels, including head of portfolio. The framework does not raise
that expectation as you become more senior.

**The skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Agile working
2. Communicating analysis and insight
3. Governance and assurance
4. Planning
5. Stakeholder relationship management
6. Strategic thinking

## Job skills

Six skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the level expects,
what it looks like in practice, and what changes at senior.

### Skill: Agile working

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: show an awareness of
Agile methodology and how to apply the principles in practice; take an open-minded
approach; explain why iteration is important; and iterate quickly.

**What that looks like here.** You do not force every team into the same shape.
You iterate the portfolio's own products — reporting, governance, the way risks
are surfaced — rather than running last year's version.

**The next step.** Identical wording at senior and at head of portfolio. This
skill does not change anywhere in the role.

### Skill: Communicating analysis and insight

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: choose the appropriate
medium to communicate findings; shape and share communications relevant to the
audience; present analysis and visualisations clearly to specialist and
non-specialist audiences, with support; and give tactical recommendations based on
your analysis.

**What that looks like here.** Your reporting causes decisions rather than
recording them. You know which audience needs which format. You recommend rather
than only describe.

**The next step.** At senior it becomes turning complex data into compelling,
clear and actionable stories, sharing those skills across government, and
communicating *negative* as well as positive information — which is where most
portfolio reporting quietly fails.

### Skill: Governance and assurance

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: explain how governance
and assurance works; follow appropriate governance and assurance processes; and
contribute to deciding what governance and assurance is required for a product or
service. This is the only skill that changes between analyst and your level.

**What that looks like here.** You can explain the governance landscape to a
project team who find it opaque. You have had a view on how much governance
something needs — which means arguing for less, sometimes.

**The next step.** At senior you *develop* governance and assurance processes,
identify and recommend improvements, and support others in applying them.

### Skill: Planning

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: understand the
importance of planning and forecasting; and show an awareness of the different
ways to develop a plan. This is the same wording as the analyst level.

**What that looks like here.** You maintain a portfolio view that reflects
reality rather than intention. You know more than one way to build a plan and can
say which suits which project.

**The next step.** At senior this grows a great deal: prioritising high-value
work, using data to inform planning, managing complex internal and external
dependencies, providing delivery confidence, removing blockers, and ensuring teams
plan for their capacity. If senior is where you are heading, this is the skill
with furthest to travel.

### Skill: Stakeholder relationship management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: identify important
stakeholders and communicate with them clearly and regularly; tailor
communication to their needs while meeting user needs; build and reach consensus;
and work to improve relationships using evidence to explain decisions.

**What that looks like here.** Stakeholders hear from you before they have to
ask. You use evidence rather than position, because position is not something a
portfolio role has. You have got two projects to agree a sequence neither wanted.

**The next step.** At senior you work with the team to develop and maintain
stakeholder understanding and communications strategies, resolve issues and
influence, and build long-term strategic relationships.

### Skill: Strategic thinking

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: explain the strategic
context of your work and why it is important; and support strategic planning in
an administrative capacity. This is the same wording as the analyst level.

**What that looks like here.** You can say what the portfolio is for, and which
projects genuinely serve it. You have supported a planning round with more than
data collection.

**The next step.** At senior you work within the strategic context and communicate
how activities meet strategic goals, and you contribute to developing strategy and
policies.

## Legal compliance

Portfolio work handles information about projects, people and money, and portfolio
reports become records that may be disclosable. Portfolio governance is often
where accessibility, equality and public money obligations get checked — or
missed. These questions ask about awareness, training and support, not about
whether you can state the law correctly.
