# Digital portfolio analyst - skills gap form

## Introduction

Hello, and welcome to this form.

It is for you if you work as a digital portfolio analyst in the UK Government
Digital and Data profession. Your organisation may call the role associate
digital portfolio manager. Human resources or your manager has sent this so that
you can say what you already do, what you have not met yet, and what would help
you most over the next few months.

Nothing here is scored, marked or graded, and nobody is comparing your answers
with anyone else's. This is a learning level: you support the tracking, analysis
and delivery of outcomes across a portfolio of digital projects, you are learning
basic portfolio management skills, and you report to a more senior portfolio
manager.

The form walks through the six skills the framework names for your level. Each is
explained before you are asked anything about it.

Most people take about half an hour. You can stop and come back.

## Advisory

This skills gap form is created by AI and is work in progress for educational
purposes. If you intend to use it in any way, please consult with your
organization's appropriate teammates, such as with human resources staff, or
people and organizational development staff, or legal compliance staff.

## How to fill in this form

You can fill this form in on screen. Type into the boxes, tick the boxes that
apply, and pick a rating where one is asked for. Your answers are saved in
this browser, on this device, so you can close the page and come back to them.
Nothing is sent anywhere.

When you have finished, use Export TSV or Export as JSON at the top of the
page, or print it, or copy your answers into a document or an email, or answer
in whatever tool your organisation already uses. Every question has a number,
like **Q7**, so your answers make sense on their own. Clear answers empties
the form and deletes the saved copy. If other people use this computer, press
it when you have finished.

Before you start:

- **"I don't know" is a real answer**, and it tells people what to teach you.
- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a mark against you.
- **Small examples are fine.** One report, one risk, one board paper.
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers or what
  they are used for, ask before you fill it in.

## About this role level

**The role: digital portfolio manager.** A digital portfolio manager co-ordinates
and manages a group — a portfolio — of digital projects, using an iterative and
flexible approach to help define governance processes for that portfolio.

**Your level: digital portfolio analyst.** You support the tracking, analysis and
delivery of outcomes across the portfolio. The framework says you will be
learning basic portfolio management skills and will report to a more senior
portfolio manager. Your organisation may call this level associate digital
portfolio manager.

**Where this level sits.** This role has four levels:

1. **Digital portfolio analyst** — you are here
2. Digital portfolio manager
3. Senior digital portfolio manager
4. Head of portfolio

Your next step is digital portfolio manager, and on the published skills it is a
remarkably narrow one: only *governance and assurance* changes. The real
difference is in the accountabilities — helping create environments where teams
can deliver, supporting prioritisation across projects and programmes, and
helping stakeholders understand performance and risk.

**One thing worth knowing about this role.** *Agile working* is worded
identically at all four levels, including head of portfolio. The framework does
not raise that expectation as you become more senior.

**The skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Agile working
2. Communicating analysis and insight
3. Governance and assurance
4. Planning
5. Stakeholder relationship management
6. Strategic thinking

## Job skills

Six skills, in the framework's order. Each one explains what the skill means,
what is expected of you now, what it looks like in real work, and what changes at

### Skill: Agile working

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: show an awareness of
Agile methodology and how to apply the principles in practice; take an open-minded
approach; explain why iteration is important; and iterate quickly.

**What that looks like here.** You know how the teams in your portfolio actually
work, and that they do not all work the same way. You iterate your own products —
the report, the dashboard — rather than producing the same thing every month
because that is the template.

**The next step.** Identical wording at every level of this role, including head
of portfolio. The framework does not raise it.

### Skill: Communicating analysis and insight

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: choose the appropriate
medium to communicate findings; shape and share communications relevant to the
audience; present analysis and visualisations clearly to specialist and
non-specialist audiences, with support; and give tactical recommendations based on
your analysis to inform decision making.

**What that looks like here.** Your reports get read rather than filed. You
choose what to leave out. You offer a recommendation rather than only numbers.

**The next step.** Identical wording at digital portfolio manager. At senior it
becomes turning complex data into compelling, actionable stories, sharing those
skills across government, and communicating negative as well as positive
information.

### Skill: Governance and assurance

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: explain why governance
and assurance are important; and follow governance and assurance processes, under
supervision.

**What that looks like here.** You know what each board and gate is for. You
prepare what governance needs rather than being chased for it. You can explain to
a frustrated project why the process exists.

**The next step.** At digital portfolio manager you explain how governance works,
follow appropriate processes without the supervision, and *contribute to deciding
what governance is required* for a product or service. This is the only skill
that changes at that step.

### Skill: Planning

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: understand the
importance of planning and forecasting; and show an awareness of the different
ways to develop a plan.

**What that looks like here.** You know why a portfolio plan matters even when
individual projects will move. You know more than one way to build one. You can
say what the portfolio expects to deliver and roughly when.

**The next step.** Identical wording at digital portfolio manager. At senior it
becomes a much bigger skill: prioritising high-value work, using data to inform
planning, managing complex dependencies, providing delivery confidence, removing
blockers and ensuring teams plan for capacity.

### Skill: Stakeholder relationship management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: identify important
stakeholders and communicate with them clearly and regularly; tailor
communication to their needs and work with them to build relationships while
meeting user needs; build and reach consensus with stakeholders; and work to
improve stakeholder relationships using evidence to explain decisions.

**What that looks like here.** You know who needs to hear what, and how often.
You use evidence rather than authority — which is all a portfolio role has. You
have brought two people to an agreement neither started with.

**The next step.** Identical wording at digital portfolio manager. At senior you
work with the team to develop stakeholder understanding and communications
strategies, resolve issues and influence, and build long-term strategic
relationships.

### Skill: Strategic thinking

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: explain the strategic

## Legal compliance

Portfolio work handles information about projects, people and money, and the
reports you produce can become records that are disclosable. Government projects
also carry accessibility, equality and public money obligations that portfolio
governance is often where they get checked.

These questions ask about **training and support**, not about whether you get the
law right.
