# Head of product - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you are a head of product in the UK Government Digital and
Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set
out what you do now, where the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this role as providing support to all product managers in
the organisation and to the product community across government. You lead on
product strategy, working closely with lead product managers and their business
units, and you are responsible for setting and raising standards for product
management in your organisation. It expects you to coach others in product
management; work with organisation management and other communities to represent
product community interests; oversee every member of the community, including
their role, assignments, development, pay and performance; manage recruitment,
development, moves and exits covering civil servants and contractors; and line
manage senior and lead product managers.

That list is unusual in the framework. **Pay and exits are in it.** This is a
profession-leadership job with real employment consequences attached, and most of
the difficulty is there rather than in product craft.

Head of product is the top of this role. Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked.
Nine skills, each with an explanation, a rating and some questions. Expect about
35 minutes.

| 0 | None | You have not worked with this |
| 1 | Awareness | You know what it is and why it matters |
| 2 | Working | You do it with support |
| 3 | Practitioner | You do it unsupported, and handle the usual exceptions |
| 4 | Expert | You set the approach and develop others in it |

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not the remit on your job
  description.
- **A 4 is a claim about other people** — that someone else's practice changed
  because of you and would stay changed if you left. Several are reasonable here,
  and the honest test is whether your standards would survive your departure.

## About this role level

**The role: product manager.** A product manager makes sure that their products
provide value and achieve the right outcomes, by balancing user and business
needs. In this role you will:

- work with multidisciplinary teams, bringing together their views, user needs
  and business needs, to frame the problem
- work with multidisciplinary teams to provide the right outcomes for users and
  the organisation
- make decisions on priorities for your teams
- ensure teams comply with appropriate standards

**Your level: head of product.** You provide support to all product managers in
the organisation and to the product community across government. You lead on
product strategy, working closely with lead product managers and their business
units, and you are responsible for setting and raising standards for product
management in your organisation. The framework expects that you:

- coach others in product management
- work with organisation management and other communities to represent product
  community interests
- oversee every member of the community, including their role, assignments,
  development, pay and performance
- manage recruitment, development, moves and exits from the community, covering
  civil servants and contractors
- line manage senior and lead product managers

**There is no level above this one.** This role has five levels and this is the
highest. Where people go next is a question about other roles — digital
leadership, service ownership, or leadership outside the profession — and the
framework does not define it as a next step, so this form does not invent one.

**Where this level sits:**

1. Associate product manager
2. Product manager
3. Senior product manager
4. Lead product manager
5. **Head of product** — you are here

**One thing worth knowing about this role.** Six of your nine skills are worded
**identically** at lead product manager. Only three are yours alone: creating
value for money (owning and iterating a business case, and building the case for
an organisational approach to value), managing product outcomes (using data to
influence senior stakeholders' priorities, and challenging teams to demonstrate
outcomes), and product leadership (co-ordinating the community, leading on
coaching and mentoring for the organisation, and seeking reuse across
government).

The rest of what makes this job different is in the accountabilities rather than
in the skills — the community, the standards, and the people decisions.

**The nine skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Agile and Lean practices
2. Applying user-centred insights
3. Creating value for money
4. Life cycle management
5. Managing product outcomes
6. Product leadership
7. Product management
8. Stakeholder relationship management
9. Strategic ownership

### Skill: Agile and Lean practices

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: coach and lead teams
in Agile and Lean good practices; create and tailor the right approach for a team,
challenging, evaluating and iterating the approach through the life cycle;
experiment with new and innovative ways of working to improve delivery across the
organisation; and act as a recognised expert and advocate for Agile and Lean
approaches.

**Same wording as lead product manager.**

**What that looks like here.** Whether new ways of working spread across the
organisation depends largely on whether you sponsor them. An experiment you
protect survives; one you do not, does not.

### Skill: Creating value for money

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: develop and own a
business case and iterate it throughout the life cycle; coach others on
identifying benefits while balancing user and business needs; communicate the
delivery of incremental value; build the case for an organisational approach to
value through product strategy; and coach and empower teams to take a value for
money approach.

**What that looks like here.** *Iterate the business case throughout the life
cycle* is the demanding part, because most business cases are written once to
release money and never revisited when what was promised turns out to be wrong.
Communicating incremental value means people accept steady delivery instead of
waiting for a launch.

**Different from lead**, which contributes to business cases and communicates
value propositions rather than owning, iterating and building an organisational
approach.

### Skill: Managing product outcomes

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: use data to make
prioritisation decisions for multiple or complex projects, products or services;
use data to influence priorities of senior stakeholders; guide and challenge
teams to make better use of data; guide and challenge teams to demonstrate
achievement of outcomes; and identify opportunities to share and connect product
data across government.

**What that looks like here.** *Challenge teams to demonstrate achievement* is
the uncomfortable one — asking a team that shipped something to show that it
worked, and accepting the answer when it did not. Influencing senior priorities
with data means bringing evidence to a room that runs on conviction.

**Different from lead**, which leads teams to create measurement methods and
advocates for data-driven decisions, rather than challenging and influencing at
senior level and connecting data across government.

### Skill: Product leadership

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: seek opportunities for
reuse and learning across government; co-ordinate the product management
community; lead on coaching and mentoring for product management in your
organisation; lead some activities in cross-government communities, establishing
and maintaining wider relationships across the profession; and represent product
management in your organisation in conversations with senior stakeholders.

**What that looks like here.** You are the product profession, as far as your
organisation's leadership is concerned. Co-ordinating the community is real work
that gets squeezed by everything urgent, and it is the part people notice when it
stops.

**Different from lead**, which seeks reuse across the organisation and leads some
community activities, rather than co-ordinating the community, leading on
coaching for the whole organisation, and working across government.
