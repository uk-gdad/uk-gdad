# Head of (Agile) delivery management - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you are a head of Agile delivery management in the UK
Government Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent
it so that you can set out what you do now, where the gaps are, and what would
close them.

The framework describes this role as an experienced practitioner who exemplifies
what good looks like across the delivery roles. You are expected to represent and
champion the role within your department, across government and in industry; lead
the community of practice and build capability and excellence; be responsible for
recruiting the right people to the right teams; support the professional
development of your community; work with other heads of role to promote effective
cross-functional delivery; be a skilled team leader who can communicate the value
of the role to digital and non-digital stakeholders; and be credible and
influential across departments.

That is a lot of outward-facing work — champion, community, recruitment,
cross-departmental credibility — and almost none of it appears in a delivery
plan.

Head of delivery management is the top of this role. Nothing here is scored,
marked or ranked. Each of the nine skills gets an explanation, a rating and some
questions. Expect about 40 minutes.

## Rating scale

Where a question asks for a rating, use this scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | You have not worked with this |
| 1 | Awareness | You know what it is and why it matters |
| 2 | Working | You do it with support |
| 3 | Practitioner | You do it unsupported, and handle the usual exceptions |
| 4 | Expert | You set the approach and develop others in it |

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not the remit on your job
  description.
- **A 4 is a claim about other people** — that someone else's practice changed
  because of you and would stay changed if you left. Several are reasonable
  here; nine would be worth interrogating.

## About this role level

**The role: delivery manager.** A delivery manager is accountable for the
delivery of products and services.

**Your level: head of (Agile) delivery management.** An experienced practitioner
who exemplifies what good looks like across the delivery roles. The framework
expects that you:

- represent and champion the role within your department, across government and
  in industry
- lead the community of practice for this role and build capability and
  excellence
- are responsible for recruiting the right people to the right teams
- support the professional development and continuous improvement of your
  community
- work with other heads of role to promote effective cross-functional delivery
- are a skilled team leader who can confidently communicate the value of the role
  to digital and non-digital stakeholders
- are credible and influential across departments

**There is no level above this one.** The delivery manager role has four levels
and this is the highest. Where people go next is a question about other roles —
portfolio, service ownership, or wider leadership — and the framework does not
define it as a next step, so this form does not invent one.

**Where this level sits:**

1. Associate delivery manager
2. Delivery manager
3. Senior delivery manager
4. **Head of (Agile) delivery management** — you are here

Three skills are yours rather than shared with senior: commercial management,
which becomes acting as the escalation point for large or high-risk commercial
issues and coaching others; life cycle management, which becomes leading and
coaching teams through phases, ensuring effective support for products and users,
and contributing to the assessment of other teams; and team dynamics and
collaboration, which becomes something very much larger — see that block below.

**The skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Agile and Lean practices
2. Commercial management
3. Communicating between the technical and non-technical
4. Financial management
5. Life cycle management
6. Maintaining delivery momentum
7. Making a process work
8. Planning
9. Team dynamics and collaboration

## Job skills

Nine skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the framework
expects, what it looks like in practice, and where it differs from senior.

### Skill: Agile and Lean practices

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: coach and lead teams
in Agile and Lean good practices; create and tailor the right approach for a team,
challenging, evaluating and iterating it through the life cycle; experiment with
new and innovative ways of working to improve delivery across the organisation;
and act as a recognised expert and advocate.

**What that looks like here.** You are the recognised expert, which means being
asked and also being wrong in public occasionally. You run real experiments in
ways of working. You defend Agile where it is being used as a synonym for
"fast".

**Same wording as senior delivery manager**, and there is no level above.

### Skill: Commercial management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: act as the escalation
point and resolve large or high-risk commercial management issues; and coach
others in appropriate commercial management.

**What that looks like here.** The commercial problems that could not be solved
lower down arrive with you. You coach delivery managers to handle suppliers
rather than handling suppliers for them.

**Different from senior**, which takes responsibility for complex supplier
relationships and negotiates directly, rather than escalating and coaching.

### Skill: Life cycle management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: lead and coach teams
through different phases of the life cycle; ensure effective support for the
product or service and its users; predict, prevent, mitigate and manage potential
risks at different phases; and contribute to the assessment of other teams,
providing guidance and support.

**What that looks like here.** You coach teams through phases rather than
managing them through. You care about what happens to a service after launch —
support, iteration, the users who have nobody to ask. You assess other teams and
help rather than judge.

**Different from senior**, which consults the right people, uses evidence to
decide direction, and guides stakeholders, rather than leading and coaching and
assessing other teams.

### Skill: Maintaining delivery momentum

### Skill: Planning

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: lead a continual
planning process in a very complex environment; plan beyond product delivery;
identify dependencies in plans across services and co-ordinate delivery; and
coach other teams as the central point of expertise.

**What that looks like here.** Planning is continual rather than annual. You plan
for the whole life of a service. You are the central point of expertise, which
means other people's plans become your problem.

### Skill: Team dynamics and collaboration

**What the framework expects at this level.** Seven things: change organisational
structures to fixable and sustainable designs; lead on strategy for an entire
organisation, joining up business needs with innovative analysis; make and justify
decisions characterised by high levels of risk, impact and complexity;
effectively build consensus between organisations, private or public, or highly
independent and diverse stakeholders; solve and unblock issues within teams or
departments at the highest level; understand the psychology of a team and have
strong mediation skills; and coach an organisation on team dynamics and conflict
resolution.

That is a strikingly broad list for a skill called *team dynamics and
collaboration* — it reaches organisational structure and enterprise strategy. Few
people at this level have the authority for all of it. Rate what you actually do
and be specific about the rest.

**Different from senior**, which identifies and rectifies problems in team
dynamics, uses health checks, chooses feedback types, and accelerates team
development.
