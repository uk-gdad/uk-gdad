# Senior delivery manager - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a senior delivery manager in the UK
Government Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent
it so that you can set out what you do now, where the gaps are, and what would
close them.

The framework describes a senior delivery manager as accountable for the
effective delivery of complex, high-risk products and services — similar to a
delivery manager, but with more experience across a range of products and
services throughout the entire life cycle, greater responsibility, and the role
of **main point of escalation**. You are expected to have strong communication
skills, engage senior stakeholders, and coach and mentor delivery managers.

Being the main point of escalation is the part that reshapes a week. Everything
that nobody else could resolve arrives at you, usually late and usually with an
audience.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Each of the nine skills gets an
explanation, a note on what changes at head of delivery management, a rating and
some questions. Expect about 40 minutes.

## Rating scale

Where a question asks for a rating, use this scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | You have not worked with this |
| 1 | Awareness | You know what it is and why it matters |
| 2 | Working | You do it with support |
| 3 | Practitioner | You do it unsupported, and handle the usual exceptions |
| 4 | Expert | You set the approach and develop others in it |

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not the remit on your job
  description.
- **A 4 is a claim about other people** — that someone else's practice changed
  because of you and would stay changed. At senior, coaching delivery managers is
  in the job description, so one or two are reasonable.

## About this role level

**The role: delivery manager.** A delivery manager is accountable for the
delivery of products and services.

**Your level: senior delivery manager.** At this level you are accountable for
the effective delivery of complex, high-risk products and services. The framework
expects that you:

- have strong communication skills and engage senior stakeholders
- coach and mentor delivery managers

and describes the role as similar to a delivery manager but with more experience
across a range of products and services through the entire life cycle, greater
responsibility and accountability, and the role of main point of escalation.

**Where this level sits.** The delivery manager role has four levels:

1. Associate delivery manager
2. Delivery manager
3. **Senior delivery manager** — you are here
4. Head of (Agile) delivery management

Your step up is head of (Agile) delivery management. Only three skills change:
commercial management becomes acting as the escalation point for large or
high-risk commercial issues and coaching others; life cycle management becomes
leading and coaching teams through phases, ensuring effective support for
products and users, and contributing to the assessment of other teams; and team
dynamics and collaboration changes out of all recognition — to changing
organisational structures, leading strategy for an entire organisation, making
high-risk decisions and building consensus between organisations. The rest of the
difference is the job: representing and championing the role across government
and industry, leading the community of practice, and being responsible for
recruitment.

**The skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Agile and Lean practices
2. Commercial management
3. Communicating between the technical and non-technical
4. Financial management
5. Life cycle management
6. Maintaining delivery momentum
7. Making a process work
8. Planning
9. Team dynamics and collaboration

## Job skills

### Skill: Agile and Lean practices

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: coach and lead teams
in Agile and Lean good practices; create and tailor the right approach for a
team, challenging, evaluating and iterating it through the life cycle; experiment
with new and innovative ways of working to improve delivery across the
organisation; and act as a recognised expert and advocate.

**What that looks like here.** You tailor the approach to the team rather than
applying a standard one. You have run a real experiment in ways of working. You
are the person others ask when a team's way of working is not working.

**The next step.** Identical wording at head of (Agile) delivery management.

### Skill: Commercial management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: take responsibility for
complex relationships with contracted suppliers; identify appropriate contractual
frameworks and identify appropriate suppliers; negotiate with contracted
suppliers; and get good value out of contracts and suppliers.

cost. You have negotiated a change rather than accepted one. You can tell when a
supplier relationship is comfortable rather than productive.

**The next step.** At head of delivery management you act as the escalation point
for large or high-risk commercial issues, and coach others in commercial
management.

### Skill: Life cycle management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: work and consult with
the right people at the right time to move through the life cycle and deliver
value; use evidence to decide when a team should continue, change direction or
stop; identify tools and techniques required at different phases; and guide
colleagues and stakeholders through the phases.

**What that looks like here.** You have used evidence to stop or redirect
something, which is the hardest of these. You know which tools suit which phase.
You guide stakeholders who think every phase should look like delivery.

**The next step.** At head of delivery management this becomes leading and
coaching teams through phases, ensuring effective support for the product and its
users, predicting and mitigating risks at different phases, and contributing to
the assessment of other teams.

### Skill: Maintaining delivery momentum

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: optimise the delivery
flow of teams; actively address the most complicated risks, issues and
dependencies, including where ownership exists outside the team or no clear
ownership exists; and identify innovative ways to unblock issues.

**What that looks like here.** You deal with the dependencies nobody owns — the
hardest category, because there is no one to chase. You optimise flow across
teams rather than within one. You have unblocked something by an unusual route.

### Skill: Planning

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: lead a continual

**What that looks like here.** Planning is continual rather than an annual event.
You plan for what happens after launch — support, iteration, decommissioning. You
are the person other teams come to when a plan will not hold together.

### Skill: Team dynamics and collaboration

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: identify problems or
issues in the team dynamic and rectify them; identify issues through team health
checks and help stimulate the right responses; engage in varying types of
feedback, choosing the right type at the appropriate time and ensuring the
discussion and decision stick; and accelerate the team development cycle.

**What that looks like here.** You spot a dynamic problem before it becomes a
performance problem. You choose the kind of feedback the situation needs. You can
get a new team through its awkward early phase faster than it would manage alone.

**The next step.** The largest change in this role. At head of delivery management
it becomes changing organisational structures, leading strategy for an entire
organisation, making and justifying high-risk decisions, building consensus
between organisations, unblocking at the highest level, and coaching an
organisation on team dynamics and conflict resolution.
