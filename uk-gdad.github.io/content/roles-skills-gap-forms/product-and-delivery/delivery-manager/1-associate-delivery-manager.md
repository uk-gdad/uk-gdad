# Associate delivery manager - skills gap form

## Introduction

Hello, and welcome to this form.

It is for you if you work as an associate delivery manager in the UK Government
Digital and Data profession. Someone in your organisation — human resources, or
your manager — has sent it so that you can say what you already do, what you have
not met yet, and what would help you most over the next few months.

Nothing here is scored, marked or graded, and nobody is comparing your answers
with anyone else's. Associate is a learning level: you work on a small or mature
Agile team, learn on the job, and may work alongside a delivery manager on a
larger team — shadowing, supporting, or delivering one element under guidance.

The form walks through the nine skills the framework names for your level. Each
is explained before you are asked anything about it.

Most people take about 40 minutes. You can stop and come back.

## Advisory

This skills gap form is created by AI and is work in progress for educational
purposes. If you intend to use it in any way, please consult with your
organization's appropriate teammates, such as with human resources staff, or
people and organizational development staff, or legal compliance staff.

## How to fill in this form

You can fill this form in on screen. Type into the boxes, tick the boxes that
apply, and pick a rating where one is asked for. Your answers are saved in
this browser, on this device, so you can close the page and come back to them.
Nothing is sent anywhere.

When you have finished, use Export TSV or Export as JSON at the top of the
page, or print it, or copy your answers into a document or an email, or answer
in whatever tool your organisation already uses. Every question has a number,
like **Q7**, so your answers make sense on their own. Clear answers empties
the form and deletes the saved copy. If other people use this computer, press
it when you have finished.

Before you start:

- **"I don't know" is a real answer**, and it tells people what to teach you.
- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a mark against you.
- **Small examples are fine.** One stand-up, one blocker, one dependency.
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers or what
  they are used for, ask before you fill it in.

## About this role level

**The role: delivery manager.** A delivery manager is accountable for the
delivery of products and services.

**Your level: associate delivery manager.** The framework expects that you:

- work on a small or mature Agile team
- learn on the job
- may work alongside a delivery manager on a larger team — shadowing and
  providing support, or delivering one element under guidance and mentorship

Notice *small or mature*. A mature team is one that already knows how to work
together, which is exactly the right place to learn what a delivery manager
actually does — because you can see the practice without also having to build the
team.

**Where this level sits.** The delivery manager role has four levels:

1. **Associate delivery manager** — you are here
2. Delivery manager
3. Senior delivery manager
4. Head of (Agile) delivery management

Your next step is delivery manager, where you become accountable for the
performance of the team: building and maintaining it, removing obstacles,
focusing it on what matters, and coaching people in Agile and Lean techniques.

**The skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Agile and Lean practices
2. Commercial management
3. Communicating between the technical and non-technical
4. Financial management
5. Life cycle management
6. Maintaining delivery momentum
7. Making a process work
8. Planning
9. Team dynamics and collaboration

### Skill: Agile and Lean practices

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: demonstrate experience
in applying Agile principles in practice; provide a clear, open and transparent
framework in which teams can deliver; show an awareness of Agile tools and start
to use them intelligently; and visualise and make visible the work of the team.

**What that looks like here.** The team's work is visible to anyone who looks.
You use the tools to help the team rather than to produce reports. You can say
why a practice exists rather than just running it.

**The next step.** At delivery manager you compare and select delivery methods,
adapt when something does not work, use a blended approach, help teams measure
and visualise outcomes, and encourage reflective practice.

### Skill: Commercial management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: act as the point of
contact for contracted suppliers; understand appropriate internal contacts and
processes within a government department; and understand how and when third
parties should be brought into digital, data and technology projects.

**What that looks like here.** You know who your suppliers are and who in your
organisation holds the contract. You know when bringing in a third party is the
answer and when it is a way of avoiding a decision.

**The next step.** Identical wording at delivery manager. It changes at senior,
where you take responsibility for complex supplier relationships, identify
contractual frameworks and suppliers, negotiate, and get good value from
contracts.

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: communicate
effectively with technical and non-technical stakeholders; support and host
discussions within a multidisciplinary team, including ones with difficult
dynamics; be an advocate for the team externally; and manage differing
stakeholder perspectives.

**What that looks like here.** You can explain a technical delay in terms of what
it means for users and for the date. You have run a meeting where two people
disagreed. You defend your team's position when they are not in the room.

**The next step.** At delivery manager you listen to and interpret needs and
manage expectations, manage active and reactive communication, and host difficult
discussions with diverse senior stakeholders.

### Skill: Financial management

that a delay has a cost even when nobody names it.

the impact of user needs, report on financial delivery, monitor cost and budget,

### Skill: Life cycle management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: explain what each phase
of the product or service life cycle is for; and explain how the needs of the
product or service vary across phases, and how a team responds.

**What that looks like here.** You can say which phase your service is in and
what that means for how the team should work. You know that a discovery team and
a live service team need different things from a delivery manager.

**The next step.** At delivery manager you manage delivery through different
phases with some support, identify whether a product should move to the next
phase, and make sure it is developed in line with the appropriate service
standards.

### Skill: Maintaining delivery momentum

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: actively address
internal risks and issues and know when to escalate them; set the team cadence
and tempo, ensuring it is sustainable; track, manage, escalate and communicate
dependencies; actively remove or minimise risks, issues or dependencies where
possible; and understand how they affect the work of a team.

**What that looks like here.** You know your dependencies and chase them before
they bite. You can tell when the team's pace is not sustainable. You escalate at
the right time rather than too late or reflexively.

**The next step.** At delivery manager you facilitate the delivery flow of a team
and manage its pace, and address internal *and external* risks and dependencies
including those owned outside the team.

### Skill: Making a process work

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: guide teams to focus
on the output rather than the process; help the team find a process that works
for them; and support teams in establishing a process.

**What that looks like here.** You notice when a ceremony has become a ritual.
You help the team change something rather than telling them what to do. You care
more about whether the work flows than whether the board is tidy.

**The next step.** At delivery manager you identify and challenge organisational
processes that are unnecessarily complicated, coach the organisation to inspect
and adapt, and guide teams through implementing a new process.

### Skill: Planning

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: understand the

finish and roughly when, with honest uncertainty.

inform planning, manage complex dependencies, provide delivery confidence, remove

### Skill: Team dynamics and collaboration

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: explain the importance
of team dynamics, collaboration and empowering delivery teams; and understand the
importance of feedback.

**What that looks like here.** You notice when the team is not talking to each
other. You can say why empowering a team produces better work than directing it.
You give and take feedback without it becoming personal.

**The next step.** At delivery manager you bring people together into a motivated
team, create the right environment and empower them, recognise and deal with
issues, and help create the best team makeup for the situation.

## Legal compliance

Delivery managers sit next to two things with legal weight: contracts with
suppliers, and public money. Government services also have to meet accessibility
and equality duties, and delivery is where those either get planned in or
squeezed out.

These questions ask about **training and support**, not about whether you get the
law right.
