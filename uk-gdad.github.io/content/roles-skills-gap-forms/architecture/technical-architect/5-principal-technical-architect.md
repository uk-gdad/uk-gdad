# Principal technical architect - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you are a principal technical architect in the UK
Government Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent
it so that you can set out what you do now, where the gaps are, and what would
close them.

Principal is the top of this role. The framework has you leading at the highest
level and being responsible for making sure the strategy is agreed and followed:
networking and communicating with senior stakeholders across organisations,
proactively seeking opportunities for digital transformation, supporting multiple
teams while finding and using best practice and emerging technologies, inspiring
other architects, and being responsible for governance, solving complex and
high-risk issues, or delivering architecture design.

That last accountability is worded as a choice — governance, hard problems, *or*
design. In practice most people at this level are asked for all three, and which
of them actually gets your week is worth knowing.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Each of the six skills gets an
explanation, a rating and some questions. Expect about 40 minutes.

When you have finished, use Export TSV or Export JSON at the top of the

Worth knowing:

- **"I am responsible for it" is not an answer to "what do you do".** Name your

- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers, what they
  are used for, or how long they are kept, ask before you fill it in.

## Rating scale

Where a question asks for a rating, use this scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | You have not worked with this |
| 1 | Awareness | You know what it is and why it matters |
| 2 | Working | You do it with support |
| 3 | Practitioner | You do it unsupported, and handle the usual exceptions |
| 4 | Expert | You set the approach and develop others in it |

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not what you were expert in when
  you last built something.
- **A 4 is a claim about other people** — that someone else's practice changed
  because of you and would stay changed if you left. Several are reasonable
  here; six would be worth interrogating.

## About this role level

**The role: technical architect.** A technical architect provides technical
leadership and architectural design.

**Your level: principal technical architect.** At this level you lead at the
highest level and are responsible for making sure the strategy is agreed and
followed. The framework expects that you:

- network and communicate with senior stakeholders across organisations
- proactively seek opportunities for digital transformation
- support multiple teams, finding and using best practice and emerging
  technologies
- inspire other architects and help them understand how to deliver the goals of
  the organisation
- are responsible for governance, solving complex and high risk issues, or
  delivering architecture design

**There is no level above this one.** The technical architect role has five
levels and this is the highest. Where people go next is a question about other
roles — solution or enterprise architecture, chief technology leadership — and
the framework does not define it as a next step for this role, so this form does
not invent one.

**Where this level sits:**

1. Associate technical architect
2. Technical architect
3. Senior technical architect
4. Lead technical architect
5. **Principal technical architect** — you are here

Only two skills are yours rather than shared with lead: making architectural
decisions, which rises to high risk and complexity, risks across the organisation
or wider government, leading and evolving governance, and representing
architectural governance within wider governance such as legal or commercial; and
strategy design, which becomes defining and connecting strategies across the
organisation or wider government and enabling their implementation.

**One thing worth noticing.** Unlike principal solution architects and principal
enterprise architects, your *architect for the whole context* skill does not rise
to assessing societal and industry trends or informing policy outside the
organisation. The framework keeps you at organisational scope. If your job in
practice involves shaping standards beyond your organisation, that is beyond the
published expectation and worth recording.

**The skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Architect for the whole context
2. Architecture communication
3. Community collaboration
4. Making architectural decisions
5. Strategy design
6. Technical design throughout the life cycle

## Job skills

Six skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the framework
expects, what it looks like in practice, and where it differs from lead.

### Skill: Architect for the whole context

**What this means.** Working for the organisation rather than your own teams, and
tracking the slow-moving things that will matter later.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: work to support wider
organisational objectives beyond your immediate goals; track emerging internal and
external issues over time that could affect the work of teams across the
organisation; and take action to solve or mitigate problems by influencing
colleagues across the organisation.

**What that looks like here.** You give up technically better answers when the
organisation needs something else, and can explain why without resentment. You
track the things nobody has been asked to watch. You change things by influence.

**Same wording as lead technical architect**, and — unlike the principal levels of
the solution and enterprise architect roles — it does not extend to societal
trends or policy outside the organisation.

### Skill: Architecture communication

**What this means.** Communicating and mediating across organisations, winning
support for high-risk technical decisions, and coaching others.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: communicate with
technical and non-technical stakeholders at all levels and across organisations,
using architecture communication techniques; mediate between people in difficult
architectural discussions; gain support from business and technical stakeholders
for architectural topics with high levels of risk, impact and complexity; and
coach and support others in architecture communication.

**What that looks like here.** You can hold a board on a technical subject
without either alarming or patronising it. You mediate where both sides have
expertise and authority. Your architects present major decisions without you.

**Same wording as lead technical architect**, and there is no level above.

### Skill: Community collaboration

**What this means.** Working across teams and organisations, giving feedback that
lasts, and dealing with dynamics that stop technical work.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: work collaboratively
in a group, actively networking with others; adapt feedback to ensure it is
effective and lasting; use your subject to identify problems or issues in the team
dynamic and rectify them; and identify issues through team health checks and help
stimulate the right responses.

**What that looks like here.** Your network is what lets you find out how another
department solved something. Your feedback changes behaviour. You can tell when a
technical argument is a proxy for something else.

**Same wording as lead technical architect**, and there is no level above.

### Skill: Making architectural decisions

**What this means.** Owning the highest-risk technical decisions, and owning the
governance that decides how such decisions get made.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: make and guide
architectural design decisions characterised by high levels of risk and
complexity; identify and address architectural risks across the organisation or
wider government; lead and evolve architectural governance and assurance; and
represent architectural governance as part of wider governance, for example legal
or commercial.

**What that looks like here.** You make the calls where being wrong is public and
expensive. Governance is yours to change and you have changed it. You sit in
investment, legal or commercial governance as the architectural voice rather than
being consulted afterwards.

**Different from lead**, which decides at medium risk and contributes to
governance rather than leading and evolving it.

### Skill: Strategy design

**What this means.** Defining and connecting technical strategy across the
organisation and wider government, and clearing the way for it to happen.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: define and connect
strategies or visions across the organisation or wider government; and enable the
implementation of strategies or visions across the organisation or wider
government, for example by advocating for resources and removing blockers.

**What that looks like here.** You connect technical strategies written
separately that would otherwise conflict. You spend credibility moving resources
and removing blockers. The strategy is acted on rather than published.

**Different from lead**, which defines strategies across teams and directs
implementation rather than connecting across the organisation and enabling by
advocacy.

### Skill: Technical design throughout the life cycle

**What this means.** Designing at high risk, leading others who design, and
improving the standards designs are held to.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: create technical
designs characterised by high risk, impact and complexity; lead and guide others
in creating technical designs that achieve organisational objectives; and use
feedback to optimise and refine standards for technical designs throughout the
life cycle.

**What that looks like here.** You still design the things where being wrong
would be public. You guide other architects without taking their work over. What
delivery teaches changes the standard rather than one design — and you can name a
standard that exists because something went wrong.

**Same wording as lead technical architect**, and there is no level above.

what records they create. At this level you also represent architecture inside

conduct.
