# Technical architect - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a technical architect in the UK Government
Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that
you can set out what you do now, what you have not had the chance to do, and what
support would be most useful over the coming months.

At this level you are responsible for the design and build of technical
architecture. The framework expects you to undertake structured analysis of
technical issues and translate it into designs, be consulted about design and
provide design patterns, identify deeper issues that need fixing, and look for
opportunities to collaborate and reuse components while communicating with
technical and non-technical stakeholders.

*Identify deeper issues that need fixing* is the one that separates this role
from drawing what you were asked to draw. The problem in front of you is often a
symptom of something older.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Each of the six skills gets an
explanation, a note on what changes at senior, a rating and some questions.
Expect about 35 minutes.

## How to fill in this form

You can fill this form in on screen: the boxes below take typing, the tick
boxes tick, and each skill has a rating to choose. Your answers are saved in
this browser, on this device, so you can close the page and come back to them.
Nothing is sent anywhere.

When you have finished, use Export TSV or Export as JSON at the top of the
page to take your answers with you, or print the page, or copy them into
whatever your organisation uses for development conversations. Every question
is numbered, so answers travel back on their own. Clear answers empties the
form and deletes the saved copy, which is worth doing on a computer other
people use.

Worth knowing:

- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a finding.
- **"I haven't had the chance" is a real answer**, and more useful than a
  generous reading of a thin example.
- **Name a real design** rather than describing what you generally do.
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers, what they
  are used for, or how long they are kept, ask before you fill it in.

## About this role level

**The role: technical architect.** A technical architect provides technical
leadership and architectural design.

**Your level: technical architect.** At this level you are responsible for the
design and build of technical architecture. The framework expects that you:

- undertake structured analysis of technical issues, translating it into
  technical designs that describe a solution
- are consulted about design and provide design patterns
- identify deeper issues that need fixing
- look for opportunities to collaborate and reuse components, communicating with
  technical and non-technical stakeholders

**Where this level sits.** The technical architect role has five levels:

1. Associate technical architect
2. **Technical architect** — you are here
3. Senior technical architect
4. Lead technical architect
5. Principal technical architect

Your step up is senior technical architect. Three skills change: architecture
communication moves to leading the communication of complex or risky topics with
senior stakeholders; community collaboration moves to active networking and
lasting feedback; and technical design rises to medium risk with the addition of
maintaining architectural coherence in response to change. Notably, making
architectural decisions and strategy design keep their wording — including "with
support" for governance — right through senior.

**Something worth knowing about this role.** Technical architects are given six
skills; solution and enterprise architects are given the same six plus
*commercial perspective* and *problem definition and shaping*. The framework does
not expect a commercial view or formal problem-framing from you at any level. If
your job involves either, you are working beyond the published expectation and it
is worth recording.

**The skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Architect for the whole context
2. Architecture communication
3. Community collaboration
4. Making architectural decisions
5. Strategy design
6. Technical design throughout the life cycle

## Job skills

Six skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the level expects,
what it looks like in practice, and what changes at senior.

### Skill: Architect for the whole context

**What this means.** Making sure your work fits with everything else in motion —
other architects, other teams, and how technology and policy are moving.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: align your work with
the work being done by other architects and technical professionals; track
emerging issues, strategies, roadmaps, patterns and technologies over time to
assess opportunities and risks to your work; and identify how other teams
contribute to delivering outcomes through change.

**What that looks like here.** You know what the other architects are designing
and where your work touches theirs. You track things over time rather than
checking once. You can name the teams whose delivery your outcome depends on.

**The next step.** Identical wording at senior technical architect. It changes at
lead: supporting wider organisational objectives, tracking issues affecting teams
across the organisation, and influencing colleagues across it.

### Skill: Architecture communication

**What this means.** Turning technical intent into something people can see,
understand and agree with.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: listen to the needs
of technical and business stakeholders; create and use different architecture
representations to communicate effectively, achieving agreement with technical
and non-technical stakeholders; and provide support in discussions about
architectural topics within a multidisciplinary team.

**What that looks like here.** You draw the picture that ends the argument. You
know when a context diagram helps and when a sequence diagram is needed. You
listen well enough that people feel understood before you propose anything.

**The next step.** At senior you *lead* the communication of complicated, complex
or risky topics, communicate with senior stakeholders across the organisation,
adapt your techniques to the audience, advocate for a team, and manage
expectations.

### Skill: Community collaboration

**What this means.** Working well with the people around you, and making the
conditions in which good technical work happens.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: contribute to the work
of others; motivate and empower teams; create the right environment for teams to
work in and identify the best team makeup for the situation; and recognise and
deal with issues.

**What that looks like here.** You make other people's work better rather than
only producing your own. You know what mix of people a piece of technical work
needs. You say something when a team is not working well rather than designing
around it.

**The next step.** At senior the wording shifts to active networking, adapting
feedback so it lasts, using your subject to identify problems in team dynamics,
and stimulating the right responses to issues found in health checks.

### Skill: Making architectural decisions

**What this means.** Making choices that are hard to reverse, seeing the risks
they carry, and working within governance.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: work with others to
make architectural design decisions characterised by managed levels of risk and
complexity; identify and address architectural risks relevant to your team or
domain — business, data or security, for example; and engage with architectural
governance and assurance to manage decisions and risks, with support.

**What that looks like here.** You decide with others and record the reasoning
where the next person will find it. You can name the architectural risks in your
area. You take things to governance before they are irreversible.

**The next step.** The framework repeats this wording at senior technical
architect, including "with support" — unusual for a senior level, and worth
noticing if you are already doing more. It changes at lead: making and guiding
decisions at medium risk, addressing risks across multiple teams, and
contributing to how governance itself develops.

### Skill: Strategy design

**What this means.** Supporting the direction the organisation sets, and using
the principles and patterns that carry it.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: support the
development of a strategy or vision that aligns with organisational objectives;
challenge requirements and assumptions and identify opportunities to develop
strategy; support implementation, for example through a roadmap or plan; and use
architectural principles, patterns and constraints when appropriate.

**What that looks like here.** You have contributed to a strategy rather than
only working under one. You ask the question that exposes an untested assumption.
You use the principles that exist rather than inventing your own each time.

**The next step.** Identical wording at senior technical architect. It changes at
lead: defining strategies or visions across teams, directing implementation, and
defining architectural principles and patterns.

### Skill: Technical design throughout the life cycle

**What this means.** Designing the technical solution and staying with it —
guiding the teams building it and adapting the design when reality disagrees.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: create technical
designs characterised by managed levels of risk, impact and complexity; provide
guidance and support to teams using technical designs throughout the life cycle;
adapt a technical design if needed during delivery; and work with well-understood
technology, identifying appropriate patterns.

**What that looks like here.** Your designs get built. You are still involved in
month three. When an assumption proves wrong you change the design and record
why, so nobody re-litigates it later.

**The next step.** At senior the risk band rises to medium, and two things are
added: maintaining appropriate quality and architectural coherence in response to
change, and using feedback to optimise and refine designs.
