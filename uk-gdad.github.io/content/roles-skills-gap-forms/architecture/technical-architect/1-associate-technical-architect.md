# Associate technical architect - skills gap form

## Introduction

Hello, and welcome to this form.

It is for you if you work as an associate technical architect in the UK
Government Digital and Data profession. Someone in your organisation — human
resources, or your manager — has sent it so that you can say what you already do,
what you have not met yet, and what would help you most over the next few months.

Nothing here is scored, marked or graded, and nobody is comparing your answers
with anyone else's. Associate is a learning level: you support technical
architects in putting forward designs as solutions to technology challenges,
usually under supervision. Gaps are expected.

The form walks through the six skills the framework names for your level. Each is
explained before you are asked anything about it.

Most people take about half an hour. You can stop and come back.

## Advisory

This skills gap form is created by AI and is work in progress for educational
purposes. If you intend to use it in any way, please consult with your
organization's appropriate teammates, such as with human resources staff, or

You can fill this form in on screen. Type into the boxes, tick the boxes that

So pick whichever suits you:

- Copy the questions into a document or an email, and answer there.
- Answer in whatever tool your organisation already uses.

Every question has a number, like **Q7**, so your answers make sense on their own
without the questions attached.

Before you start:

- **"I don't know" is a real answer**, and a useful one — it tells people what to
  teach you.
- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a mark against you.
- **Small examples are fine.** One design, one diagram, one decision.
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers or what
  they are used for, ask before you fill it in.

## About this role level

**The role: technical architect.** A technical architect provides technical
leadership and architectural design.

**Your level: associate technical architect.** At this level you support
technical architects in putting forward designs as solutions to technology
challenges, usually under supervision. The framework expects that you:

- work closely with developers when designing appropriate solutions
- have an understanding of the overall strategy and how your work supports it

Working *closely with developers* is the phrase that defines this role's
character. A technical architecture that the developers cannot build, run or
change is not a design; it is a drawing.

**Where this level sits.** The technical architect role has five levels:

1. **Associate technical architect** — you are here
2. Technical architect
3. Senior technical architect
4. Lead technical architect
5. Principal technical architect

**Something worth knowing about this role.** Technical architects are given six
skills. Solution architects and enterprise architects are given eight — the same
six plus *commercial perspective* and *problem definition and shaping*. So the
framework does not expect you to develop a commercial view or formal
problem-framing at any level of this role. If your job involves either — and many
technical architect jobs do — you are working beyond the published expectation,
and it is worth recording that rather than assuming it is normal.

**The skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Architect for the whole context
2. Architecture communication
3. Community collaboration
4. Making architectural decisions
5. Strategy design
6. Technical design throughout the life cycle

## Job skills

Six skills, in the framework's order. Each explains what the skill means, what is
expected of you now, what it looks like in real work, and what changes at the
next level.

### Skill: Architect for the whole context

**What this means.** Knowing what else is going on — strategies, roadmaps,
policies, technology trends — and understanding how your work fits the change
your team is enabling.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: identify relevant
information that can inform your architectural work, such as strategies,
roadmaps, policies and technical trends; and understand how your work supports
the team in enabling change.

**What that looks like here.** You know where the strategies and standards live
and you read them before designing. You can say what change your team's work is
meant to enable, not only what it builds.

**The next step.** At technical architect this becomes aligning your work with
other architects and technical professionals, tracking emerging issues and
technologies over time, and identifying how other teams contribute to outcomes.

### Skill: Architecture communication

**What this means.** Turning technical intent into something people can see and
agree with — diagrams, documents, conversation.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: show an awareness of
different ways of creating architecture representations for a limited audience,
including technical and non-technical stakeholders; and gather and explain
information to be used in architecture representations.

**What that looks like here.** You know that a diagram for developers and a
diagram for a steering group are different artefacts. You can gather the
information a representation needs, and explain what it shows.

**The next step.** At technical architect you listen to the needs of technical
and business stakeholders, create and use different representations to reach
agreement, and support discussions about architecture within a multidisciplinary
team.

### Skill: Community collaboration

**What this means.** Working well with the people around you, and understanding
that how a team works together affects what it can build.

**What the framework expects at this level.** That you understand the work of
others and the importance of team dynamics, collaboration and feedback.

**What that looks like here.** You know what everyone in your team does. You give
and take design feedback without it becoming personal. You notice when a team is
working well and when it is not.

**The next step.** At technical architect this grows to contributing to the work
of others, motivating and empowering teams, creating the right environment and
identifying the best team makeup, and recognising and dealing with issues.

### Skill: Making architectural decisions

**What this means.** Making choices that will be hard to reverse, and recording
why — so the next person understands rather than guesses.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: describe the
reasoning behind architectural design decisions; gather information to inform
decisions; and understand architectural governance and assurance relevant to your
work.

**What that looks like here.** You write decision records someone can read in two
years and understand. You know what information a decision needs before it can be
made. You know which forums your designs go through and what each is for.

**The next step.** At technical architect you work with others to *make*
decisions of managed risk, identify and address architectural risks in your team
or domain, and engage with governance and assurance with support.

### Skill: Strategy design

**What this means.** Understanding what strategies, standards, patterns,
policies, roadmaps and visions are *for*, and how they connect to what the
organisation is trying to achieve.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: explain how
organisational objectives link to designing strategy; and describe the purpose
and application of strategy, standards, patterns, policies, roadmaps, vision and
mission statements.

**What that looks like here.** You can say what each of those is for without
using the words interchangeably. You can trace your current work back to an
organisational objective.

**The next step.** At technical architect you *support the development* of a
strategy or vision, challenge requirements and assumptions, support
implementation through a roadmap or plan, and use architectural principles,
patterns and constraints.

### Skill: Technical design throughout the life cycle

**What this means.** Designing the technical solution and then staying with it —
supporting the teams building it and adapting the design when reality disagrees.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: create technical
designs characterised by managed levels of risk, impact and complexity; provide
guidance and support to teams using technical designs throughout the life cycle;
adapt a technical design if needed during delivery; and work with well-understood
technology, identifying appropriate patterns.

**What that looks like here.** Your designs get built. You are still available to
the team in month three. When an assumption turns out to be wrong, you change the
design rather than defending it.

**The next step.** The framework repeats this wording at technical architect. It
changes at senior, where the risk band rises to medium and you maintain
architectural coherence in response to change.
