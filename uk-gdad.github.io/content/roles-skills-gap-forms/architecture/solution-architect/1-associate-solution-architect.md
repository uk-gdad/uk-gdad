# Associate solution architect - skills gap form

## Introduction

Hello, and welcome to this form.

It is for you if you work as an associate solution architect in the UK
Government Digital and Data profession. Someone in your organisation — human
resources, or your manager — has sent it to you so that you can say what you
already do, what you have not met yet, and what would help you most over the
next few months.

Nothing here is scored, marked or graded, and nobody is comparing your answers
with anyone else's. Associate is a learning level: you support other solution
architects to design solutions, usually under supervision. Gaps are expected.

The form walks through the eight skills the framework names for your level. Each
is explained before you are asked anything about it, so you will not need another
page open.

Most people take about 40 minutes. You can stop and come back.

## About this role level

**The role: solution architect.** A solution architect designs solutions for
problems that affect the organisation. In this role you make sure the problem and
the desired outcomes are properly defined, make sure a solution's scope meets the
organisation's requirements, stay current on technology trends, understand
organisational objectives and external drivers such as legislation or financial
constraints, work with others on business and technical strategies, work within
constraints, design and document solutions so they can actually be implemented,
comply with standards and governance, communicate with stakeholders, and manage
risks and decisions transparently.

**Your level: associate solution architect.** At this level you support other
solution architects to design solutions, usually under supervision. The framework
expects that you:

- support the design of solutions by working with stakeholders
- help your team achieve its objectives

That is a short list, and deliberately so. Your job is to learn the craft by
doing parts of it alongside someone who already can.

**Where this level sits.** The solution architect role has five levels:

1. **Associate solution architect** — you are here
2. Solution architect
3. Senior solution architect
4. Lead solution architect
5. Principal solution architect

Your next step is solution architect, where you become responsible for a single
solution and work independently where risk is low.

**How this role relates to enterprise architect.** Seven of your eight skills are
worded by the framework as they are for enterprise architects. The published
difference between the two roles is the eighth: yours is *technical design
throughout the life cycle* — staying with a design while it is built and adapting
it — and theirs is *enterprise architecture*, designing how the whole
organisation achieves its objectives. If you enjoy staying with a thing until it
works, you are in the right role.

**The skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Architect for the whole context
2. Architecture communication
3. Commercial perspective
4. Community collaboration
5. Making architectural decisions
6. Problem definition and shaping
7. Strategy design
8. Technical design throughout the life cycle

## Job skills

Eight skills, in the framework's order. Each one explains what the skill means,
what is expected of you now, what it looks like in real work, and what changes at
the next level.

### Skill: Architect for the whole context

**What this means.** Knowing what else is going on — strategies, roadmaps,
policies, technology trends — and understanding how your work fits into the
change your team is enabling.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: identify relevant
information that can inform your architectural work, such as strategies,
roadmaps, policies and technical trends; and understand how your work supports
the team in enabling change.

**What that looks like here.** You know where your organisation's strategies and
roadmaps live, and you read them before designing. You can say what change your
team's work is meant to enable, not just what it is building.

**The next step.** At solution architect this becomes aligning your work with
other architects and technical professionals, tracking emerging issues and
technologies over time to assess opportunities and risks, and identifying how
other teams contribute to delivering outcomes.

### Skill: Architecture communication

**What this means.** Making a design understandable — through diagrams, documents
and conversation — to people who need to agree with it or build it.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: listen to the needs
of technical and business stakeholders; create and use different architecture
representations to communicate effectively, reaching agreement with technical and
non-technical stakeholders; and provide support in discussions about
architectural topics within a multidisciplinary team.

**What that looks like here.** You draw the picture that ends an argument. You
know when a context diagram helps and when a sequence diagram is needed. You
listen well enough that people feel understood before you propose anything.

**The next step.** At solution architect you *lead* the communication of
complicated, complex or risky topics, communicate with senior stakeholders,
adapt your techniques to the audience, advocate for a team, and manage
expectations.

### Skill: Commercial perspective

**What this means.** Knowing that designs turn into purchases, contracts and
legal obligations — and that those outlast the design.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: show an awareness of
government commercial processes; and show an awareness of legal and compliance
rules.

**What that looks like here.** You know your organisation cannot simply buy
whatever looks best, and roughly why. You know that some of your design
constraints are legal rather than technical, and you ask rather than guessing.

**The next step.** At solution architect this becomes understanding commercial
processes and the appropriate internal contacts within a government department,
and understanding different sourcing strategies and when to apply them.

### Skill: Community collaboration

**What this means.** Working well with the people around you, and understanding
that how a team works together affects what it can build.

**What the framework expects at this level.** That you understand the work of
others and the importance of team dynamics, collaboration and feedback.

**What that looks like here.** You know what everyone in your team does and why.
You give and take feedback on designs without it becoming personal. You notice
when a team is working well and when it is not.

**The next step.** At solution architect this grows to contributing to the work
of others, motivating and empowering teams, creating the right environment and
identifying the best team makeup, and recognising and dealing with issues.

### Skill: Making architectural decisions

**What this means.** Making choices that will be hard to reverse, and recording
why — so the next person understands rather than guessing.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: describe the
reasoning behind architectural design decisions; gather information to inform
decisions; and understand architectural governance and assurance relevant to your
work.

**What that looks like here.** You write decision records that someone can read
in two years and understand. You know what information a decision needs before it
can be made. You know which forums your designs pass through and what each is
for.

**The next step.** At solution architect you work with others to *make* decisions
of managed risk, identify and address architectural risks in your team or domain,
and engage with governance and assurance with support.

### Skill: Problem definition and shaping

**What this means.** Turning a mess into a stated problem, and helping produce
options rather than a single answer.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: help to frame a problem
characterised by managed levels of complexity, complication or risk so that a
solution can be created; and help to create options for solving problems at an
appropriate level of detail.

**What that looks like here.** You can take a request for a particular solution
and find the problem underneath it. You help produce two or three options with
honest trade-offs rather than one preferred answer with two straw men.

**The next step.** At solution architect you frame problems of medium complexity
yourself, produce representations that give different teams a shared
understanding, and describe options so delivery methods can be chosen.

### Skill: Strategy design

**What this means.** Understanding what strategies, standards, patterns,
policies, roadmaps and visions are *for*, and how they connect to what the
organisation is trying to achieve.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: explain how
organisational objectives link to designing strategy; and describe the purpose
and application of strategy, standards, patterns, policies, roadmaps, vision and
mission statements.

**What that looks like here.** You can say what each of those documents is for
without using the words interchangeably. You can trace your current work back to
an organisational objective.

**The next step.** At solution architect you *support the development* of a
strategy or vision, challenge requirements and assumptions, support
implementation through roadmaps or plans, and use architectural principles,
patterns and constraints.

### Skill: Technical design throughout the life cycle

**What this means.** Designing the technical solution and then staying with it —
supporting the teams building it, and adapting the design when reality disagrees
with it. This is the skill that makes this a solution architect role rather than
an enterprise architect one.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: create technical
designs characterised by managed levels of risk, impact and complexity; provide
guidance and support to teams using technical designs throughout the life cycle;
adapt a technical design if needed during delivery; and work with well-understood
technology, identifying appropriate patterns.

**What that looks like here.** Your designs get built rather than admired. You
are still available to the team in the third month, not only at the design
review. When something turns out to be wrong, you change the design instead of
defending it.

**The next step.** The framework repeats this wording at solution architect; it
changes at senior, where the risk band rises to medium and you maintain
architectural coherence in response to change and use feedback to refine designs.

## Legal compliance

Solution designs carry legal consequences: what data the solution holds, whether
it is accessible, how it is bought, and what records it creates. Your level's
commercial skill explicitly includes awareness of legal and compliance rules, so
these questions are part of the job rather than an add-on.

They ask about **training and support**, not about whether you get the law right.
