# Chief data officer - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you are a chief data officer in the UK Government
Digital and Data profession, or are doing that job under another title. Human
resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out what you do now,
where the gaps are, and what would close them.

**One thing to know before you start.** The framework publishes this role and
describes what it involves, but it marks the role level as **not in use** and
publishes no expectations against any of its named skills. That is the
framework's own position, not an omission in this form. It means there is no
published standard to compare yourself against here — so instead of telling you
what good looks like, this form asks you to describe your own practice against
the skill names the framework publishes, and to say where you think the gaps
are.

That makes this form more of a structured self-account than the others in this
set. It is still worth doing: nobody else will ask you these questions, and a
role with no published standard is one where expectations tend to be assumed
rather than agreed.

Expect about 40 minutes.

## Advisory

This skills gap form is created by AI and is work in progress for educational
purposes. If you intend to use it in any way, please consult with your
organization's appropriate teammates, such as with human resources staff, or
people and organizational development staff, or legal compliance staff.

## How to fill in this form

You can fill this form in on screen: the boxes below take typing, the tick
boxes tick, and each skill has a rating to choose. Your answers are saved in
this browser, on this device, so you can close the page and come back to them.
Nothing is sent anywhere.

When you have finished, use Export TSV or Export JSON at the top of the
page to take your answers with you, or print the page, or copy them into
whatever your organisation uses for development conversations. Every question
is numbered, so answers travel back on their own. Clear answers empties the
form and deletes the saved copy, which is worth doing on a computer other
people use.

Worth knowing:

- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a finding.
- **Do not put anything sensitive on this form** — no unpublished organisational
  risk, no personal data, nothing above the classification the form will be
  handled at.
- **"I am accountable for it" is not an answer to "what do you do".** Name your
  part.
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers, what they
  are used for, or how long they are kept, ask before you fill it in.

## Rating scale

Where a question asks for a rating, use this scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | You have not worked with this |
| 1 | Awareness | You know what it is and why it matters |
| 2 | Working | You do it with support |
| 3 | Practitioner | You do it unsupported, and handle the usual exceptions |
| 4 | Expert | You set the approach and develop others in it |

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not the remit on your job
  description.
- **Because the framework publishes no expectation for these skills**, your
  rating is a statement about your own practice rather than a measure against a
  standard. Rate honestly and use the evidence line — the evidence is the useful
  part here, not the number.

## About this role level

**The role: chief data officer.** A chief data officer makes sure the
organisation can use data to make better decisions, policies and services, and
creates an environment where data protection and privacy are balanced with
access, sharing and innovation. The framework says that in this role you:

- define a vision and strategy for data that enables the organisation to achieve
  its objectives and aligns with wider government data strategy
- lead the organisation in implementing the data strategy
- work with senior leaders to align priorities and secure their support
- determine the data and data technology the organisation needs, and the strategy
  for sourcing it
- ensure data can be easily used, reused and shared by those who need it
- improve the quality of data used across the organisation
- ensure the organisation uses data ethically and complies with data standards
  and regulations
- increase data literacy and ensure data is properly managed, by coaching the
  whole organisation
- work with cyber security to ensure data is secure while staying accessible and
  controllable
- ensure the organisation has people with the level of data skills it needs

**The role level is published as NOT IN USE.** The framework lists this role in
the chief digital and data profession, keeps the description above, and marks
the level itself — and every skill expectation under it — as not in use. There
is no level above or below within this role.

**The skills the framework names for this role**, with no expectations published
against any of them:

1. Capability building for digital, data and technology
2. Data architecture
3. Data governance leadership
4. Data risk management
5. Innovation in digital, data and technology
6. Strategic data planning

## Legal compliance

A chief data officer sits closer to statutory duty than almost any other role in
this framework: the framework's own description makes you responsible for
ethical use of data and compliance with data standards and regulations. These
questions ask about awareness, training and support, not about whether you can
state the law correctly, and not as a check on your conduct.
