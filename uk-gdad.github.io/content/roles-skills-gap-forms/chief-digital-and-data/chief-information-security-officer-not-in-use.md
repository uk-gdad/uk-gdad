# Chief information security officer - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you are a chief information security officer in the UK
Government Digital and Data profession, or are doing that job under another
title. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out what
you do now, where the gaps are, and what would close them.

**Two things to know before you start.**

First, the framework publishes this role and describes what it involves, but it
marks the role level as **not in use** and publishes no expectations against any
of its named skills. That is the framework's own position, not an omission in
this form. There is no published standard to measure yourself against — so this
form asks you to describe your own practice against the skill names the framework
publishes, and to say where the gaps are.

Second, the framework notes that this role sits in both the Government Digital
and Data profession and the Government Security profession. If your organisation
holds you to the security profession's standards, those are the ones to answer
against, and it is worth saying so.

Expect about 40 minutes.

## How to fill in this form

You can fill this form in on screen: the boxes below take typing, the tick
boxes tick, and each skill has a rating to choose. Your answers are saved in
this browser, on this device, so you can close the page and come back to them.
Nothing is sent anywhere.

When you have finished, use Export TSV or Export as JSON at the top of the
page to take your answers with you, or print the page, or copy them into
whatever your organisation uses for development conversations. Every question
is numbered, so answers travel back on their own. Clear answers empties the
form and deletes the saved copy, which is worth doing on a computer other
people use.

Worth knowing:

- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a finding.
- **Do not put anything sensitive on this form.** No live vulnerabilities, no
  unpublished incident detail, no system detail that would help an attacker,
  nothing above the classification this form will be handled at. Describe your
  work at a level you would be comfortable saying in a corridor. This matters
  more for your role than for any other in the framework.
- **"I am accountable for it" is not an answer to "what do you do".** Name your
  part.
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers, what they
  are used for, or how long they are kept, ask before you fill it in.

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not the remit on your job
  description.
- **Because the framework publishes no expectation for these skills**, your
  rating is a statement about your own practice rather than a measure against a
  standard. The evidence line is the useful part, not the number.

## About this role level

**The role: chief information security officer.** A chief information security
officer creates an environment and culture that ensures the security of the
organisation's information and technology, enabling it to achieve its objectives
and deliver services safely and securely. The framework says that in this role
you:

- create a strategy for information and cyber security that supports both the
  organisation's strategy and wider government security strategy
- lead the organisation in implementing that strategy
- evaluate the current status and maturity of information and cyber security
- determine how to reach the level of maturity the organisation needs
- understand risks across the organisation and advise the board and other leaders
  on mitigating them, in their areas and in future plans
- enable the organisation to be innovative in a safe and secure way
- ensure the organisation is prepared for cyber attacks and can detect, respond to
  and recover from an attack
- ensure the information and cyber security aspects of crisis management are
  effective
- encourage a culture of cyber security awareness and good security practices
- implement practices to increase security maturity

**The role level is published as NOT IN USE.** The framework lists this role,
keeps the description above, and marks the level itself — and every skill
expectation under it — as not in use. There is no level above or below within
this role.

**The framework also notes** that this role is part of both the Government
Digital and Data profession and the Government Security profession.

**The skills the framework names for this role**, with no expectations published
against any of them:

1. Capability building for digital, data and technology
2. Changing security culture
3. Cyber incident management
4. Cyber risk management
5. Cyber security governance
6. Innovation in digital, data and technology
7. Security architecture
8. Strategic cyber security planning
