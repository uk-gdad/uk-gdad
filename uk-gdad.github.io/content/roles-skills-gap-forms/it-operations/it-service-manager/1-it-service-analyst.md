# IT service analyst - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as an IT service analyst in the UK Government Digital and Data profession.
Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out what you do now, where the gaps are, and
what would close them.

The framework describes this level as responsible for **frontline technical support to end users** on issues
relating to hardware, software and peripherals — responding to, documenting and resolving service desk
incident tickets in a timely manner according to a service level agreement. At this level you:

- have **excellent problem solving skills** to diagnose, evaluate and resolve complex problem situations or,
  when appropriate, escalate or route them to IT resolver groups
- **perform root cause analysis**
- develop checklists for typical problems
- recommend procedures and controls for problem prevention
- **work independently**, share information and assist others with requests for service

One thing is worth saying before you start. This is the first level of its role, but it is **not a trainee
level**. It asks for excellent problem solving, root cause analysis, independent working and a **Level 3
service management framework qualification**. Several first levels elsewhere in IT operations are explicitly
traineeships; yours is not. If you came in expecting to be trained up, the published expectations are higher
than that, and it is fair to say so.

Nine skills. Expect about 30 minutes.

## Rating scale

Where a question asks for a rating, use this scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | You have not worked with this |
| 1 | Awareness | You know what it is and why it matters |
| 2 | Working | You do it with support |
| 3 | Practitioner | You do it unsupported, and handle the usual exceptions |
| 4 | Expert | You set the approach and develop others in it |

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you actually do at work**, not what you could probably manage.
- **The gap between 2 and 3 is the exceptions.** Handling the tickets that follow the script is a 2. Handling
  the one that does not fit anything, without escalating out of habit, is a 3.

A filled-in answer looks like this:

> **IT service reporting**

>

> numbers mean, and I would struggle to interpret them for someone.

Names the routine and the gap without dressing either up.

## About this role level

**The role: IT service manager.** IT service managers are responsible for managing the service delivery of
information and communications technology (ICT) services, and working with teams from IT service operations.

**Your level: IT service analyst.** You provide frontline technical support to end users, with the
accountabilities listed in the introduction.

**Where this level sits.** This role has four levels:

1. **IT service analyst** — you are here
2. IT service manager
3. Senior IT service manager
4. Head of IT service management

Your next step is IT service manager: responsible for defining service level agreements (SLAs) and
operational level agreements (OLAs) across all relevant business functions, and managing a multi-sourced
service support team.

**Two things worth knowing about this role.**

First, **five of your nine skills are worded identically at the level above**: IT service reporting,
ownership and initiative, stakeholder relationship management, strategic thinking and user focus. The step to
IT service manager is not mostly a skills step — it is a step into defining agreements and running a team.
That is unusual, and it means the useful preparation is exposure rather than training.

Second, **you already carry strategic thinking and stakeholder relationship management at the same wording as
the level above**, which is a lot for a frontline support role. If nobody involves you in strategy or
stakeholder work, that is a gap in your job rather than in you, and this form is a reasonable place to say
so.

**The nine skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Asset and configuration management
2. Business analysis (IT operations)
3. IT service reporting
4. Ownership and initiative
5. Service focus
6. Service management framework knowledge
7. Stakeholder relationship management (IT operations)
8. Strategic thinking
9. User focus

### Skill: Asset and configuration management

**What the framework expects at this level.** One thing: **track, log and correct information to protect
assets and components.**

**What that looks like here.** Keeping the record straight as things move — a laptop reassigned, a licence
freed, a device retired. Correcting is the part that matters: noticing a record is wrong and fixing it rather
than working around it.

**The next step.** At IT service manager it becomes maintaining secure configuration and accurate
information, controlling IT assets in one or more areas, and verifying their location and state.

### Skill: IT service reporting

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: **produce relevant reports in an appropriate
format and agreed timeframe**; work with stakeholders to discuss any changes in the reporting processes; and
**interpret a data set and communicate this to others**.

**Identical wording at IT service manager.** At senior IT service manager it collapses to one line — "use
data analytics skills to make decisions that enhance business performance" — so producing reports is assumed
from there upwards, and deciding from them is the expectation.

**The part people miss.** Producing is the easy half. Interpreting — saying what the numbers mean and what
should change — is where it becomes useful.

### Skill: Service management framework knowledge

**What the framework expects at this level.** One thing: **show you have a Level 3 service management
framework qualification.**

**Unusual for a first level.** Most entry levels in IT operations expect familiarity rather than a
certificate. Yours names one.

**Why that matters.** It is a document, not a practice — so if you do not hold it, the ask is funding and
study time, which is a concrete request rather than a vague development area. The level above requires this
same Level 3 qualification **plus** knowledge of the life cycle or capability elements of ITIL, so getting it
now is the first half of that step as well.

### Skill: Stakeholder relationship management (IT operations)

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: **identify important stakeholders and communicate
with them clearly and regularly**; tailor communication to stakeholders' needs and work with them to build
relationships, while also meeting user needs; **build and reach consensus**; and work to improve stakeholder
relationships, **using evidence to explain decisions made**.

**Identical wording at IT service manager.** It is set high for a frontline role — the same four
expectations are asked of the person running the service. At senior it changes to negotiating, influencing
and building long-term strategic relationships.

**What that looks like here.** Every difficult ticket is a relationship as well as a fault. The senior
manager whose laptop failed before a board meeting needs different communication from a colleague reporting a
slow printer, and neither needs jargon.
