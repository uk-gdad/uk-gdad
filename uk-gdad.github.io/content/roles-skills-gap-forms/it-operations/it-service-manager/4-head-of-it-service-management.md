# Head of IT service management - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a head of IT service management in the UK Government Digital and Data
profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out what you do now, where the
gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this level as responsible for **setting the vision and strategy for service
management**, ensuring **processes are owned and maturing**. At this level you:

- lead a team, ensuring adequate resources and capacity
- **represent the IT service management function at a senior level**
- **act as an escalation point for business stakeholders**

This is the top of the role. Nothing above it in the framework tells you what good looks like, so the
questions here ask you to argue for your own judgement rather than measure yourself against a next step.

Eight skills — one fewer than every level below you. Expect about 30 minutes.

## About this role level

**The role: IT service manager.** IT service managers are responsible for managing the service delivery of
information and communications technology (ICT) services, and working with teams from IT service operations.

**Your level: head of IT service management.** You set the vision and strategy for service management,
ensuring processes are owned and maturing, with the accountabilities listed in the introduction.

**Where this level sits.** This role has four levels:

1. IT service analyst
2. IT service manager
3. Senior IT service manager
4. **Head of IT service management** — you are here

There is no level above this in the role. Movement from here is usually into a wider IT operations or chief
technology office post.

**Two things worth knowing about this level.**

First, **you have eight skills where every level below has nine.** The one that disappears is **service
focus** — seeing the bigger picture across groups of services and investigating how to get the best of the
underlying ones. It runs from the analyst's "awareness of different products and services" up through the
manager and senior manager, and then stops before you. Whether that is deliberate or an artefact of how the
framework was written, the published text does not say. It is recorded here rather than explained away, and
in practice most organisations would expect a head of service management to hold exactly that view.

Second, **three of your eight skills are worded identically at senior IT service manager**: business
analysis, IT service reporting, and ownership and initiative. What makes your level different is concentrated
in asset control at organisational scale, the strategic direction of stakeholder relationships, leading
strategy design, and the escalation role.

**The eight skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Asset and configuration management
2. Business analysis (IT operations)
3. IT service reporting
4. Ownership and initiative
5. Service management framework knowledge
6. Stakeholder relationship management (IT operations)
7. Strategic thinking
8. User focus

## Job skills

Eight skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the level expects, what it looks like in
practice, and — because there is no level above — what stretching it further would mean.

### Skill: Asset and configuration management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: **manage configuration management for the
organisation**, ensuring value for the business and adherence to company policies; **ensure that changes to
assets are recorded and controlled appropriately**; and **advocate organisational commitment to asset
control**.

**A step up from senior IT service manager**, where it was managing configuration items and service
compliance and risk. Yours is organisational scale, plus advocacy.

**Advocacy is the unusual word.** No other level in this role asks you to persuade the organisation to care
about something. Asset control is a discipline that decays without visible senior commitment, and you are the
one asked to secure it.

**Stretching further.** Making the asset position a live input to financial and security planning rather than
a record assembled when someone asks.

### Skill: Service management framework knowledge

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: **show you have an expert certificate in the service
management framework qualification**; and **demonstrate an industry understanding of how to implement the
framework into numerous environments**.

**The top of a four-step ladder.** Level 3 at analyst; Level 3 plus ITIL life cycle or capability elements at
manager; expert certificate at senior; expert certificate plus industry understanding at yours.

**The second item is the interesting one.** *Numerous environments* — plural, and not only this one. It asks
for knowledge of how the framework lands in organisations shaped differently from yours, which comes from
outside contact rather than from a course.

### Skill: Stakeholder relationship management (IT operations)

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: **manage long-term strategic relationships**,
identifying where new connections need to be made and existing ones nurtured; **direct the strategic approach
for stakeholder relationships**, establishing stakeholder objectives and ensuring these are clearly
represented; **act as a point of escalation if stakeholder relationships break down** or become challenging
for more junior members of the team; **influence important senior stakeholders and arbitrate when blockers
are escalated**; and **facilitate discussions across high risk and complex areas or projects under
constrained timelines**.

**The most detailed skill at this level**, and a clear step up from senior IT service manager, where it was
negotiating, influencing and building long-term relationships.

**The escalation item is worth noticing.** You are expected to take over relationships that have broken down
for more junior colleagues. That is a protective function as much as a diplomatic one, and doing it well
means people bring you problems early rather than after they have become unrecoverable.

### Skill: User focus

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: **give direction on which tools or methods to
use**; demonstrate experience in meeting the needs of users across a variety of channels; **bring insight and
expertise in how user needs have changed over time** to ensure they are met by the business; and **apply
strategic thinking to provide the best service for the end user**.

**A step up from senior IT service manager**, where it was collaborating with researchers and championing
research.

**What that looks like here.** Insight over time is the distinctive one. How colleagues and the public expect
to get help has changed substantially, and a service model designed for one era serves another badly.
