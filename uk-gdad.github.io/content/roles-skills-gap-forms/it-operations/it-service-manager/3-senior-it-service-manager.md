# Senior IT service manager - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a senior IT service manager in the UK Government Digital and Data
profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out what you do now, where the
gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this level in a single sentence: you are responsible for **service management across
platforms, multiple channels and services** to ensure **service availability, performance and process
maturity**.

That is all the published text gives. Unlike the two levels below, your level has **no list of
accountabilities** — the framework sets out nine skills and leaves the job description at one line. In
practice that means the skills below carry more weight here than they do elsewhere, because there is nothing
else to read.

Process maturity is the phrase worth pausing on. It is the only place in this role where the published text
asks you to be accountable for how good the processes are, rather than for the service they produce.

Nine skills. Expect about 30 minutes.

Worth knowing:

- **"My team does this" is not an answer to "what do you do".** Name your part.
- **"I have not had the chance" is a real answer.**
- **Do not include anything sensitive** — no contract detail, no supplier disputes, no colleagues' names.
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers, what they are used for, or how long
  they are kept, ask before you fill it in.

Where a question asks for a rating, use this scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not the remit on your job description.
- **A 4 is a claim about other people** — that someone else's practice changed because of you and would stay
  changed if you left.

## About this role level

**The role: IT service manager.** IT service managers are responsible for managing the service delivery of
information and communications technology (ICT) services, and working with teams from IT service operations.

**Your level: senior IT service manager.** You are responsible for service management across platforms,
multiple channels and services, to ensure service availability, performance and process maturity.

**Where this level sits.** This role has four levels:

1. IT service analyst
2. IT service manager
3. **Senior IT service manager** — you are here
4. Head of IT service management

Your next step is head of IT service management: setting the vision and strategy for service management,
ensuring processes are owned and maturing, leading a team, representing the function at a senior level, and
acting as an escalation point for business stakeholders.

**Two things worth knowing about this role.**

First, **your level raises six of the nine skills at once.** Asset and configuration management, business
analysis, IT service reporting, ownership and initiative, service management framework knowledge, stakeholder
relationship management and strategic thinking all step up from the level below. That is an unusually broad
step, and it includes the jump to an **expert certificate** in the service management framework.

Second, **four of your nine skills are worded identically at head of IT service management**: business
analysis, IT service reporting, ownership and initiative, and — in effect — much of what follows. The step
above yours is about vision, representation and escalation rather than about a broad skills increase. If you
are aiming there, the preparation is visibility and standing rather than another certificate.

**The nine skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Asset and configuration management
2. Business analysis (IT operations)
3. IT service reporting
4. Ownership and initiative
5. Service focus
6. Service management framework knowledge
7. Stakeholder relationship management (IT operations)
8. Strategic thinking
9. User focus

### Skill: Asset and configuration management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: **manage configuration items and related
information**; and **manage service compliance and risk**.

**A reframing from IT service manager**, where it was maintaining accurate information and verifying the
location and state of assets. Compliance and risk, now.

**The next step.** At head of IT service management it becomes managing configuration management for the
organisation — ensuring value for the business and adherence to company policies, ensuring changes to assets
are recorded and controlled, and advocating organisational commitment to asset control.

### Skill: Service management framework knowledge

**What the framework expects at this level.** One thing: **show you have an expert certificate in the service
management framework qualification.**

**The largest jump in this role's qualification ladder.** The analyst needs a Level 3; the manager needs a
Level 3 plus knowledge of ITIL life cycle or capability elements; you need an expert certificate.

**Worth being plain about.** This is a document, not a practice. If you do not hold it, the ask is funding and
study time — a concrete, costed request rather than a development aspiration.

**The next step.** At head of IT service management it is the expert certificate **plus** demonstrating an
industry understanding of how to implement the framework into numerous environments.

### Skill: User focus

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: **collaborate with user researchers and represent
users internally**; **explain the difference between user needs and the desires of the user**; **champion
user research to focus on all users**; prioritise and define approaches to understand the user story, guiding
others in doing so; and offer recommendations on the best tools and methods to use.

**A step up from the two levels below**, where it was engaging with users and using data about them.

**What that looks like here.** Across multiple channels, "all users" is a real constraint. People who cannot
use the digital channel are usually served by the channel with the least investment in it.

**The next step.** At head of IT service management you give direction on tools and methods, demonstrate
experience across channels, bring insight into how user needs have changed over time, and apply strategic
thinking to providing the best service.
