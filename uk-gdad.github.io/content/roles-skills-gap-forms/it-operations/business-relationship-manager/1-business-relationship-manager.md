# Business relationship manager - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a business relationship manager — sometimes called a business
partner — in the UK Government Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent
it so that you can set out what you do now, where the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this role as establishing and maintaining meaningful relationships with
colleagues to deliver positive impact across the organisation. At this level you are responsible for
ensuring consistency in engagement across the business. It expects you to prepare documentation and
obtain support to progress new ideas, opportunities and investments; use relevant systems to maintain a
view of activity and business-related knowledge; and support continuous improvement activities that
mature the capability of the team.

This is a role with no delivery of its own. Your output is other people's decisions being better
informed, which is real and almost impossible to count — worth naming early in any conversation about
how your work is judged.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Seven skills, each with an explanation, a rating and some
questions. Expect about 30 minutes.

## About this role level

**The role: business relationship manager.** You establish and maintain meaningful relationships with
colleagues to deliver positive impact across the organisation. In this role you will:

- bring together different functions to ensure value across the organisation
- work strategically across teams to ensure digital and data investment adds value
- explore the perspectives, challenges and ambitions of teams to shape a pipeline of opportunities
- act as a point of focus, for example for strategic development, collaborating with colleagues who
  influence strategic direction
- set the conditions for a culture of creating meaningful connections

**Your level: business relationship manager.** You are responsible for ensuring consistency in
engagement across the business. The framework expects that you:

- prepare documentation and obtain support to progress new ideas, opportunities and investments
- use relevant systems to maintain a view of activity and business-related knowledge
- support continuous improvement activities that mature the capability of the team

**Where this level sits.** This role has three levels:

1. **Business relationship manager** — you are here
2. Senior business relationship manager
3. Lead business relationship manager

Your next step is senior business relationship manager: developing relationships across the
organisation to understand business ambitions, exploring shared needs and reuse, leading activities
that improve relationship maturity, and ensuring strategic demands such as spending reviews are
supported.

**One thing worth knowing about this role.** *Service management framework knowledge* at your level is
worded as holding a **Level 3 service management framework qualification**. That is one of very few
places in the framework where a specific qualification is named — and it is unusual to find it in a
relationship-focused role rather than a technical one. If you do not hold it, this form is a good
place to ask.

**The seven skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Business analysis (IT operations)
2. Ownership and initiative
3. Service focus
4. Service management framework knowledge
5. Stakeholder relationship management (IT operations)
6. Strategic thinking
7. User focus

## Job skills

Seven skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the level expects, what it looks like in
practice, and what changes at senior business relationship manager.

### Skill: Business analysis (IT operations)

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: investigate problems and opportunities in
existing processes, and contribute to recommending solutions; and work with stakeholders to identify
objectives and potential benefits.

**What that looks like here.** Turning "our system is rubbish" into something specific enough to act
on — what exactly fails, how often, what it costs. That translation is most of the value you add
early in a conversation.

**The next step.** At senior you lead investigative work, lead the collection of information and
creation of recommendations, and absorb large amounts of conflicting information to produce solutions.

### Skill: Ownership and initiative

**What the framework expects at this level.** One thing: own an issue until a new owner has been found
or the problem has been mitigated or resolved.

**What that looks like here.** In a relationship role, this is what makes you trusted. Things that
cross team boundaries have a habit of belonging to nobody, and being the person who holds them until
they land somewhere is the whole point of the job.

**The next step.** At senior you take accountability for issues that occur, are proactive in searching
for potential problems, and achieve excellent user outcomes.

### Skill: Service management framework knowledge

**What the framework expects at this level.** One thing: show you have a **Level 3 service management
framework qualification**.

**What that looks like here.** A named, checkable qualification. In this role it matters because you
are often explaining to business colleagues why an IT process works the way it does — and knowing the
framework properly makes that explanation credible rather than defensive.

**The next step.** At senior it adds knowledge of the life cycle or capability elements of ITIL.

### Skill: Stakeholder relationship management (IT operations)

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: negotiate with and influence stakeholders,
and manage relationships effectively; influence decisions, deal with challenging situations and
**remove blockers**; and build long-term strategic relationships, communicating clearly and regularly,
supporting mutual needs and commitments while focusing on user needs.

**What that looks like here.** This is the core of the job, and the wording is already demanding at
your level — removing blockers and building long-term strategic relationships. In practice most of
your influence comes from being reliably useful rather than from any authority.

**The next step.** Identical wording at senior business relationship manager. It changes at lead,
where you direct the strategic approach, act as an escalation point and arbitrate escalated blockers.

### Skill: Strategic thinking

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: work within a strategic context and
communicate how activities meet strategic goals; and contribute to the development of strategy and
policies.

**What that looks like here.** Being able to connect what a business area wants to what the
organisation has said it is trying to achieve — and, when they do not connect, saying so early rather
than letting a request travel a long way before being refused.

**The next step.** At senior you define strategies and policies, guide others on working in the
strategic context, and evaluate current strategies against business requirements.

### Skill: User focus

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: identify and engage with users or
stakeholders to collate user needs evidence; understand and define research that fits user needs; and
use quantitative and qualitative data about users to turn user focus into outcomes.

**What that looks like here.** Business areas usually arrive with a solution rather than a need.
Getting back to who is affected and what they are trying to do — before a system is specified — is
where this role can save an organisation a great deal.

**The next step.** At senior you collaborate with user researchers and represent users internally,
explain the difference between needs and desires, champion research covering all users, and guide
others.
