# Senior business relationship manager - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a senior business relationship manager in the UK Government
Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out what
you do now, where the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this level as developing relationships across the organisation to understand
business ambitions and, where appropriate, ensuring digital products and services support them. It
expects you to explore shared needs, encouraging reuse, central management, sharing and
commoditisation aligned to digital, data, technology and security strategies; lead activities that
improve relationship maturity, increase value, improve user experience and increase efficiency; ensure
strategic demands such as **spending reviews** are supported; partner with others to provide strategic
guidance; promote sustained investment in user-centred approaches; and ensure complex needs and
challenges are understood, prioritised and communicated.

Encouraging reuse is the quietly difficult one. Every business area wants its own system, and the
argument for sharing is always made against someone's preference.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Seven skills. Expect about 30 minutes.

## About this role level

**The role: business relationship manager.** You establish and maintain meaningful relationships with
colleagues to deliver positive impact across the organisation. In this role you will:

- bring together different functions to ensure value across the organisation
- work strategically across teams to ensure digital and data investment adds value
- explore the perspectives, challenges and ambitions of teams to shape a pipeline of opportunities
- act as a point of focus, for example for strategic development
- set the conditions for a culture of creating meaningful connections

**Your level: senior business relationship manager.** You develop relationships across the organisation
to understand business ambitions and ensure digital products and services support them. The framework
expects that you:

- explore shared needs, encouraging reuse, central management, sharing and commoditisation, aligned to
  digital, data, technology and security strategies
- lead activities that improve relationship maturity, increase value, improve user experience and
  increase efficiency
- ensure strategic demands, such as spending reviews, are supported
- partner with others to provide strategic guidance on meeting goals
- promote sustained investment of user-centred approaches to ensure products and services meet user
  needs
- ensure complex needs and challenges are understood, prioritised and communicated

**Where this level sits.** This role has three levels:

1. Business relationship manager
2. **Senior business relationship manager** — you are here
3. Lead business relationship manager

Your next step is lead business relationship manager: leading engagement with internal and external
stakeholders, ensuring strategic demands are met and communicated, setting conditions for a culture of
positive impact, and possibly leading a team.

**One thing worth knowing about this role.** *Stakeholder relationship management* — the core skill of
this role — is worded **identically at the level below yours**. And *Service focus* is too. What rose
at your level is business analysis, ownership and initiative, service management framework knowledge,
strategic thinking and user focus. Your case rests on those and on the accountabilities.

**The seven skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Business analysis (IT operations)
2. Ownership and initiative
3. Service focus
4. Service management framework knowledge
5. Stakeholder relationship management (IT operations)
6. Strategic thinking
7. User focus

## Job skills

Seven skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the level expects, what it looks like in
practice, and what changes at lead business relationship manager.

### Skill: Business analysis (IT operations)

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: lead investigative work into problems and
opportunities in existing processes; lead the collection of information and creation of recommendations
for improvements; and **absorb large amounts of conflicting information and use it to produce
solutions**.

**What that looks like here.** Conflicting information is the normal condition: three areas describing
the same process differently, each convinced theirs is how it works. Producing something coherent from
that without flattening the real differences is the skill.

**The next step.** At lead you direct business analysis, ensure recommendations fit strategic
objectives, communicate them persuasively to important stakeholders, and identify the simplest of a
variety of approaches.

### Skill: Stakeholder relationship management (IT operations)

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: negotiate with and influence stakeholders,
and manage relationships effectively; influence decisions, deal with challenging situations and remove
blockers; and build long-term strategic relationships, communicating clearly and regularly, supporting
mutual needs and commitments while focusing on user needs.

**Same wording as business relationship manager** — the core skill of this role does not change
between the first two levels.

**What that looks like here.** At senior the difference is usually seniority of the people you deal
with and the size of what is at stake, rather than a different technique.

**The next step.** At lead you manage long-term strategic relationships and identify new connections,
direct the strategic approach, act as an escalation point when relationships break down for junior
colleagues, influence senior stakeholders and arbitrate escalated blockers, and facilitate discussions
across high-risk areas under constrained timelines.

### Skill: User focus

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: collaborate with user researchers and
represent users internally; explain the difference between user needs and the desires of the user;
champion user research to focus on all users; prioritise and define approaches to understand the user
story, guiding others in doing so; and offer recommendations on the best tools and methods.

**What that looks like here.** Your accountabilities include promoting **sustained investment** in
user-centred approaches — which is the difficult part, because research budgets are the first thing cut
and the last thing missed.

**The next step.** At lead you give direction on tools and methods, demonstrate experience meeting user
needs across channels, bring insight into how needs have changed over time, and apply strategic
thinking to providing the best service.
