# Lead application operations engineer - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a lead application operations engineer in the UK Government
Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out what
you do now, where the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this level as **defining the application strategy and the strategy for
security administration**. It expects you to liaise with leads within IT operations on how
applications are performing and changes that need to be put in place, and possibly to manage and
maintain certain aspects of an application depending on its complexity, size and business impact.

Owning the security administration strategy is a sharp responsibility. Who can get into which system,
how that is granted, reviewed and removed — those are your decisions, and they are the ones an audit
or an incident will examine most closely.

Eleven skills. Expect about 35 minutes.

Where a question asks for a rating, use this scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not the remit on your job description.
- **A 4 is a claim about other people** — that someone else's practice changed because of you and
  would stay changed if you left. Setting strategy and standards is written into your level, so
  several are reasonable.

## About this role level

**The role: application operations engineer.** You support, manage and maintain a single application
or a suite of applications.

**Your level: lead application operations engineer.** You define the application strategy and the
strategy for security administration. The framework expects that you:

- liaise with leads within IT operations on how applications are performing and changes that need to
  be put in place
- potentially manage and maintain certain aspects of an application, depending on complexity, size
  and likely business impact

**Where this level sits.** This role has five levels:

1. Associate application operations engineer
2. Application operations engineer
3. Senior application operations engineer
4. **Lead application operations engineer** — you are here
5. Principal application operations engineer

Your next step is principal application operations engineer: owning the application strategy, leading
on resourcing, learning and development for the team, having expert technical understanding, and being
accountable for vendor and supplier management.

**Two things worth knowing about this role.**

First, *Service management framework knowledge* at your level adds knowledge of the **life cycle or
capability elements of ITIL** on top of the Level 3 qualification. At principal it becomes an expert
certificate. These are among the few places in the whole framework where a specific qualification is
named, so they are concrete and fundable.

Second, four of your eleven skills are worded **identically at principal** — change management,
incident management, ownership and initiative, and user focus.

**The eleven skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Asset and configuration management
2. Availability and capacity management
3. Change management
4. Incident management
5. Ownership and initiative
6. Problem management
7. Service focus
8. Service management framework knowledge
9. Technical specialism
10. Testing
11. User focus

### Skill: Asset and configuration management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: maintain secure configuration and accurate
information; control IT assets in one or more areas; and verify the location and state of IT assets.

**Same wording as the two levels below.** This skill does not change until principal, where it becomes
managing configuration items and related information, and managing service compliance and risk.

**What that looks like here.** At lead the useful question is whether configuration accuracy is a
process or an individual's diligence. Processes survive people leaving.

### Skill: Testing

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: **manage the planning of system and
acceptance tests, co-ordinating both functional and non-functional specifications**; provide
authoritative advice and guidance on test planning; and identify process improvements and contribute
to the definition of best practice.

**What that looks like here.** Non-functional specifications — performance, resilience, security — are
where operations most often gets handed something untested. Making sure those are specified and
verified before acceptance is your lever.

**The next step.** At principal this becomes managing testing activities within development or
integration, managing risks, and managing customer relations — which is a differently shaped
expectation rather than obviously a higher one.

### Skill: User focus

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: collaborate with user researchers and
represent users internally; explain the difference between user needs and the desires of the user;
champion user research to focus on all users; prioritise and define approaches to understand the user
story, guiding others in doing so; and offer recommendations on the best tools and methods to use.

**Same wording at principal.**

**What that looks like here.** Unusually research-heavy wording for an operations lead. In practice
the valuable version is representing users in decisions about resilience investment — arguing that
the people who suffer an outage are not an abstraction.
