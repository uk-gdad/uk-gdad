# Application operations engineer - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as an application operations engineer in the UK Government Digital
and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out what you do
now, where the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this level as maintaining services and being responsible for conducting user
and security administration. It expects you to monitor applications and be accountable for batch
processes, and — in some organisations — to carry out the duties of an end user computing engineer,
such as fixing technical problems, maintaining hardware and liaising with the problem or incident
manager.

**You jumped from four skills to eleven** when you reached this level. That is the largest single
increase anywhere in the framework, and it is worth knowing if this level feels like more than the
title suggests. It is.

Eleven skills is a lot to hold. Nobody is strong across all of them, and a spread of ratings is the
honest picture. Expect about 35 minutes.

You can fill this form in on screen: the boxes below take typing, the tick

When you have finished, use Export TSV or Export as JSON at the top of the

is numbered, so answers travel back on their own. Clear answers empties the
form and deletes the saved copy, which is worth doing on a computer other
people use.

Worth knowing:

- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a finding.
- **Answer about the job you do**, not the one on the job description.
- **"I haven't had the chance" is a real answer.** With eleven skills, several blanks are expected.
- **Do not include anything sensitive about your systems** — no hostnames, credentials, or details of
  an unresolved weakness. Describe the work at a level you would be comfortable saying in a corridor.
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers, what they are used for, or how
  long they are kept, ask before you fill it in.

## About this role level

**The role: application operations engineer.** You support, manage and maintain a single application
or a suite of applications.

**Your level: application operations engineer.** You maintain services and are responsible for
conducting user and security administration. The framework expects that you:

- monitor applications and be accountable for batch processes
- in some organisations, carry out the duties of an end user computing engineer, such as fixing
  technical problems, maintaining hardware and liaising with the problem or incident manager

**Where this level sits.** This role has five levels:

1. Associate application operations engineer
2. **Application operations engineer** — you are here
3. Senior application operations engineer
4. Lead application operations engineer
5. Principal application operations engineer

**Two things worth knowing about this role.**

First, *Service management framework knowledge* at your level is worded as **holding a Level 3 service
management framework qualification**. The framework rarely names a qualification. If you do not have
one, that is a concrete thing to ask about — including who pays and whether study time is given.

Second, six of your eleven skills are worded **identically at senior application operations engineer**.
The step up is concentrated in a few skills rather than spread across all of them, which is useful to
know when planning what to work on.

**The eleven skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Asset and configuration management
2. Availability and capacity management
3. Change management
4. Incident management
5. Ownership and initiative
6. Problem management
7. Service focus
8. Service management framework knowledge
9. Technical specialism
10. Testing

## Job skills

Eleven skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the level expects, what it looks like
in practice, and what changes at senior application operations engineer.

### Skill: Asset and configuration management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: maintain secure configuration and accurate
information; control IT assets in one or more areas; and verify the location and state of IT assets.

**What that looks like here.** Knowing what is actually deployed, in what version, with what
settings. Configuration records that drift from reality are worse than none, because people make
decisions from them.

**The next step.** At senior you would maintain information on assets and verify location and state
across a wider scope.

### Skill: Change management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: implement change requests; and apply change
control procedures **under supervision**.

**What that looks like here.** Most incidents follow a change. Doing changes properly — a plan, a way
back, a check afterwards, and a record of what was actually done — is the single most effective way to
reduce your own out-of-hours calls.

**The next step.** At senior you analyse and assess the impact of change, document change requests,
and action changes from change requests — the supervision clause drops away and impact assessment
arrives.

### Skill: Ownership and initiative

**What the framework expects at this level.** One thing: **own an issue until a new owner has been

**What that looks like here.** This is the real change from trainee. Nothing you pick up gets dropped
— it either gets fixed or it gets handed to a named person who has accepted it. Issues that fall
between people are how operations teams lose trust.

### Skill: Technical specialism

**What the framework expects at this level.** One thing: assist in technical support activities and
carry out agreed or routine maintenance and administration tasks.

**What that looks like here.** Depth in the specific application stack you support — how it is
configured, what it depends on, how it behaves when a dependency is slow. That knowledge is usually
learned rather than taught, and rarely written down.

**The next step.** At senior this skill changes shape entirely: using management system software and
tools, using logical schemata to investigate problems and create reports, carrying out routine
configuration, installation and reconfiguration of **database** and related products, and optimising
performance and forecasting resource needs.

### Skill: Testing

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: correctly execute test scripts under
supervision; and understand the role of testing and how it works.

**What that looks like here.** In operations, testing usually means verifying a change did what it
should and nothing else moved. A change that "went fine" because nobody checked is not a successful
change.

**The next step.** At senior you review requirements and specifications and define test conditions,
identify issues and risks associated with the work, and analyse and report test activities and
results.

### Skill: User focus

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: identify and engage with users or
stakeholders to collate user needs evidence; understand and define research that fits user needs; and
use quantitative and qualitative data about users to turn user focus into outcomes.

**What that looks like here.** This is a large jump from trainee and unusually research-flavoured for
an operations role. The practical version is knowing who is affected when your application degrades,
and what "degraded" means to them rather than to a dashboard.
