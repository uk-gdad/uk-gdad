# Lead infrastructure engineer - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a lead infrastructure engineer in the UK Government Digital and Data
profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out what you do now, where the
gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this level as **managing third party provision of infrastructure services** and the
provision of expertise to develop architectural solutions for infrastructure services, **throughout the
service product life cycle**. At this level you:

- oversee programmes and projects
- work with technical architects to translate architectural designs into operations, and support them in
  operationalising the designs
- lead and direct infrastructure specialist teams in building, managing, supporting, and maintaining solutions
  according to departmental policy, **if taking a managerial path**

Two things separate you from senior: suppliers and programmes. Neither is a technical skill, and both are
where the job is most likely to be difficult.

Eleven skills. Expect about 35 minutes.

## Rating scale

Where a question asks for a rating, use this scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | You have not worked with this |
| 1 | Awareness | You know what it is and why it matters |
| 2 | Working | You do it with support |
| 3 | Practitioner | You do it unsupported, and handle the usual exceptions |
| 4 | Expert | You set the approach and develop others in it |

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not the remit on your job description.
- **A 4 is a claim about other people** — that someone else's practice changed because of you and would stay
  changed if you left.

A filled-in answer looks like this:

> **Systems integration**
>
> - [x] 2 — Working: I do it with support
>
> *Evidence:* I wrote our integration standards and they are reasonable. Ensuring practitioners adhere to
> them is where it falls down — I have no way of knowing whether they are followed outside my own team.

Separates the written thing from the enforced thing, which is usually the honest picture.

## About this role level

**The role: infrastructure engineer.** An infrastructure engineer designs, builds, manages and supports the
infrastructure services that underpin all internal user services and services to the public.

**Your level: lead infrastructure engineer.** You manage third party provision of infrastructure services and
the provision of expertise to develop architectural solutions for infrastructure services, throughout the
service product life cycle — with the additional accountabilities listed in the introduction.

**Where this level sits.** This role has five levels:

1. Associate infrastructure engineer
2. Infrastructure engineer
3. Senior infrastructure engineer
4. **Lead infrastructure engineer** — you are here
5. Principal infrastructure engineer

Your next step is principal infrastructure engineer: an accomplished technical leader who develops strategic
and tactical engineering roadmaps, and potentially owns operational relationships with suppliers.

**Two things worth knowing about this role.**

First, **five of your eleven skills are worded identically at principal**: coding and scripting, information
security, modern development standards, ownership and initiative, and systems integration. The difference
between you and the top of the role is concentrated in four skills — IT infrastructure, problem management,
service focus and systems design — plus the roadmap and supplier accountabilities in the level description.

Second, **on IT infrastructure the published wording places you above the principal.** Yours is leading teams
and departments in design, implementation, administration, and support. The principal's is designing and
implementing solutions — the senior's wording. It is recorded here because it is in the published text, not
because it should guide a career decision.

**The eleven skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Coding and scripting
2. IT infrastructure
3. Information security
4. Modern development standards
5. Ownership and initiative
6. Problem management
7. Service focus
8. Systems design
9. Systems integration
10. Testing
11. Troubleshooting and problem resolution

### Skill: Systems design

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: design systems characterised by medium levels of
risk, impact, and business or technical complexity; select appropriate design standards, methods and tools,
and ensure they are applied effectively; and review the systems designs of others.

**Identical to senior infrastructure engineer.** Design is not what separates you from the level below.

**The next step.** At principal you design systems of **high** risk and complexity, control design practice
within an enterprise or industry architecture, influence industry-based models, develop implementation and
procurement strategies, and ensure adherence to technical strategies and policies. That is the largest single
jump in the role.

### Skill: Testing

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: **manage the planning of system and acceptance

**A step up from senior**, where it was defining test conditions and reporting results.

**The next step.** At principal it becomes managing testing activities within development or integration,
managing risks, and managing customer relations.
