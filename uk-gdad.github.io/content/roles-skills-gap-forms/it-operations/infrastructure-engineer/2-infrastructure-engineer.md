# Infrastructure engineer - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as an infrastructure engineer in the UK Government Digital and Data
profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out what you do now, where the
gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this level as working in a team of infrastructure specialists and engineers,
**supporting and maintaining infrastructure solutions and services as directed and according to departmental
policy**. At this level you ensure services are delivered and used as required, and you work with and support
third parties to provide infrastructure services.

This is the first level where you are properly doing the job rather than learning it. You have **eleven
skills** — three more than the associate level below, gaining coding and scripting, service focus and testing
all at once. If you have come up through that level, expect this to feel like a step.

Expect about 35 minutes.

  they are kept, ask before you fill it in.

## Rating scale

Where a question asks for a rating, use this scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | You have not worked with this |
| 1 | Awareness | You know what it is and why it matters |
| 2 | Working | You do it with support |
| 3 | Practitioner | You do it unsupported, and handle the usual exceptions |
| 4 | Expert | You set the approach and develop others in it |

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you actually do at work**, not what you could probably manage.
- **The gap between 2 and 3 is the exceptions.** Plenty of people can do the standard build and stall on the
  one that behaves oddly. That is a 2, and it is a perfectly good place to be at this level.

## About this role level

**The role: infrastructure engineer.** An infrastructure engineer designs, builds, manages and supports the
infrastructure services that underpin all internal user services and services to the public.

**Your level: infrastructure engineer.** Working in a team of infrastructure specialists and engineers, you
support and maintain infrastructure solutions and services as directed and according to departmental policy.
At this level you:

- ensure services are delivered and used as required
- work with and support third parties to provide infrastructure services

**Where this level sits.** This role has five levels:

1. Associate infrastructure engineer
2. **Infrastructure engineer** — you are here
3. Senior infrastructure engineer
4. Lead infrastructure engineer
5. Principal infrastructure engineer

Your next step is senior infrastructure engineer: building, administering, supporting, and maintaining
solutions, working with technical architects to translate architectural designs into operations, and
providing input into workforce, budget, and technology roadmaps.

**Two things worth knowing about this role.**

First, **coding and scripting is worded identically at your level and at senior** — "describe different
scripting tools and software that are available and currently in use". Describing, both times. The framework
asks less of this skill in this role than most engineers actually need, so treat the published wording as a
floor rather than a target.

Second, **systems design is where the biggest change happens between your level and senior**, and it is worth
looking at that wording early.

**The eleven skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Coding and scripting
2. IT infrastructure
3. Information security
4. Modern development standards
5. Ownership and initiative
6. Problem management
7. Service focus
8. Systems design
9. Systems integration
10. Testing
11. Troubleshooting and problem resolution

### Skill: Coding and scripting

**What the framework expects at this level.** One thing: **describe different scripting tools and software
that are available and currently in use.**

**Worded identically at senior infrastructure engineer.** The framework does not raise this skill between the
two levels, and it does not ask you to write anything at either — only to describe. In practice most
infrastructure work now involves writing scripts, so this is one to hold yourself to a higher standard on
than the published text does.

**What that looks like here.** Knowing what your team uses — shell, PowerShell, Python, a configuration
management tool — and what each is good for.

### Skill: Systems design

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: **translate logical designs into physical
designs**; produce detailed designs; effectively document all work using required standards, methods and
tools, including prototyping tools where appropriate; **design systems characterised by managed levels of
risk, manageable business and technical complexity, and meaningful impact**; and work with well understood
technology and identify appropriate patterns.

**The biggest jump from associate**, where it was assisting in the design of components.

**What that looks like here.** A logical design says what is needed. A physical design says exactly what will
be built — which machines, which sizes, which network segments, which failure modes are accepted. Turning
one into the other is the work.

**The next step.** At senior you design for *medium* levels of risk and complexity, select the design
standards and methods, and review other people's designs.
