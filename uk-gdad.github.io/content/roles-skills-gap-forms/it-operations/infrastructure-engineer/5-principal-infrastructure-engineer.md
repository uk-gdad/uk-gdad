# Principal infrastructure engineer - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a principal infrastructure engineer in the UK Government Digital and Data
profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out what you do now, where the
gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this level in one line: **an accomplished technical leader**. What that means is set
out in the accountabilities:

- **develop strategic and tactical engineering roadmaps** for technologies and services in your area,
  ensuring they are future-proofed and that the organisation derives maximum value from investment in
  technologies
- **potentially own the operational relationships with suppliers**, ensuring services and products are
  delivered and aligned to industry best practice and regulatory and contractual requirements
- work with technical architects to translate architectural designs into operations
- lead and direct infrastructure specialist teams, **if taking a managerial path**

This is the top of the role. Nothing above it in the framework tells you what good looks like, so the
questions here ask you to argue for your own judgement rather than measure yourself against a next step.

Eleven skills. Expect about 35 minutes.

At this level the scale is worth reading carefully. A principal who rates everything 4 has stopped
distinguishing between what they are accountable for and what they are actually good at, and the gap between
those two things is where the useful conversation is.

## About this role level

**The role: infrastructure engineer.** An infrastructure engineer designs, builds, manages and supports the
infrastructure services that underpin all internal user services and services to the public.

**Your level: principal infrastructure engineer.** An accomplished technical leader, with the
accountabilities listed in the introduction.

**Where this level sits.** This role has five levels:

1. Associate infrastructure engineer
2. Infrastructure engineer
3. Senior infrastructure engineer
4. Lead infrastructure engineer
5. **Principal infrastructure engineer** — you are here

There is no level above this in the role. Movement from here is usually into technical architecture, a head
of infrastructure post, or a chief technology office.

**Two things worth knowing about this level.**

First, **systems design is where your level genuinely separates from lead**. Lead designs at medium risk;
you design at **high** risk, control design practice within an enterprise or industry architecture, influence
industry-based models, develop implementation and **procurement** strategies, and ensure adherence to
technical strategies and policies. Five expectations where lead has three.

Second, **on IT infrastructure the published wording places lead above you.** Lead leads teams and
departments in design, implementation, administration and support; yours is designing and implementing
solutions — the senior's wording. That is what the framework says. It is recorded here rather than smoothed
over, and in practice most organisations expect a principal to do both.

**The eleven skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Coding and scripting
2. IT infrastructure
3. Information security
4. Modern development standards
5. Ownership and initiative
6. Problem management
7. Service focus
8. Systems design
9. Systems integration
10. Testing

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: interact with, read and write code; and carry out
baseline repair activities.

cannot read the code has to take someone's word for everything, which is a slow way to lose authority.

**Stretching further.** Keeping current with what your engineers actually use now, rather than what you were
expert in when you last built things.

### Skill: Systems design

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: **design systems characterised by high levels of
risk, impact, and business or technical complexity**; **control system design practice within an enterprise
or industry architecture**; **influence industry-based models for the development of new technology
applications**; **develop effective implementation and procurement strategies, consistent with business
needs**; and **ensure adherence to relevant technical strategies, policies, standards and practices**.

**The largest single jump in the role.** Lead has three expectations at medium risk; you have five at high,
including procurement and industry influence.

**What that looks like here.** Procurement strategy is the one people are least prepared for. What you buy,
how you buy it and what the contract permits shapes the technical options available for years.

**Stretching further.** Influencing industry models means contributing beyond your organisation — cross-
government forums, standards work, published practice.
