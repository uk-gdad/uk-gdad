# Lead service transition manager - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a lead service transition manager in the UK Government Digital and Data
profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out what you do now, where the
gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this level as responsible for **the planning and co-ordination of resources to ensure
that services are effectively transitioned into service operation**. It then adds a sentence that appears
nowhere else in this role:

> At this level, you will be **solely responsible** for the co-ordination activities across projects,
> suppliers and service teams.

*Solely* is doing a lot of work in that sentence. Sole responsibility across projects, suppliers, and service
teams — three groups, none of which report to you — is a demanding position, and one where the authority
rarely matches the accountability.

This is the top of the role. Nothing above it in the framework tells you what good looks like, so the
questions here ask you to argue for your own judgement rather than measure yourself against a next step.

Eleven skills. Expect about 30 minutes.

## About this role level

**The role: service transition manager.** IT service transition managers provide overall planning for service
transitions and co-ordinate the required resources. The role controls the product life cycle of all changes,
enabling beneficial changes to be made with minimum disruption to IT services.

**Your level: lead service transition manager.** You are responsible for the planning and co-ordination of
resources to ensure services are effectively transitioned into service operation, and solely responsible for
co-ordination across projects, suppliers and service teams.

**Where this level sits.** This role has four levels:

1. Service acceptance analyst
2. Service readiness analyst
3. Service transition manager
4. **Lead service transition manager** — you are here

There is no level above this in the role. Movement from here is usually into IT service management, a
programme or delivery post, or a wider IT operations leadership role.

**Two things worth knowing about this level.**

First, **your qualification requirement does not rise.** Level 3 plus knowledge of the life cycle or
capability elements of ITIL is the same requirement as at service transition manager. This role tops out
lower on certification than IT service management or infrastructure operations, both of which require an
expert certificate at their upper levels.

Second, **community collaboration changes shape rather than rising.** The two levels below you are asked to
motivate and empower teams and identify the best team makeup. Yours is asked to network actively, adapt
feedback so it is effective and lasting, spot problems in team dynamics and use **Agile health checks** to
stimulate the right responses. That is a facilitator's skill set rather than a manager's — which fits a job
where you have responsibility across teams you do not run.

### Skill: Community collaboration

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: **work collaboratively in a group, actively
networking with others**; **adapt feedback to ensure it is effective and lasting**; **use your topic to
identify problems or issues in the team dynamic and rectify them**; and **identify issues through Agile
"health checks" with the team, and help to stimulate the right responses**.

**A change of shape from the two levels below**, which ask you to motivate and empower teams and identify the
best team makeup. Yours is facilitation: networking, feedback, diagnosis and health checks.

**What that looks like here.** This is the skill your level description actually depends on. Sole
responsibility across teams you do not manage is delivered through relationships and honest feedback, not
through instruction.

**An Agile health check** is a short structured conversation where a team rates how it is doing on things
like clarity, trust and pace, and talks about the low scores. It surfaces problems people would not raise
individually.

**Stretching further.** Making feedback *lasting* is the hard half. Most feedback changes behaviour for a
fortnight.

### Skill: Service focus

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: **maintain focus on the entire life cycle of the
service and ensure it is at the forefront of readiness activities**; **understand and communicate service
benefits to end users**; and **ensure that service focus is championed within your team and actively
recommend its adoption**.

**A change of shape from service transition manager**, where it was seeing the bigger picture across groups of
services. Yours is the whole life cycle, plus communication and advocacy.

**The second item is unusual.** Communicating service *benefits to end users* is not obviously a transition
job — but it is in your level, and it points at something real: a service transitioned without anyone
explaining what it is for gets adopted badly.

### Skill: User focus

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: **collaborate with user researchers and represent
users internally**; **explain the difference between user needs and the desires of the user**; **champion
user research to focus on all users**; prioritise and define approaches to understand the user story, guiding
others in doing so; and offer recommendations on the best tools and methods to use.

**The one place this skill rises in the role** — the three levels below all read the same, about engaging
with users and using data about them.

**What that looks like here.** Championing research for **all** users at the point of transition means asking
who has not been tested with. Services usually go live having been validated with the people easiest to
reach.
