# Service transition manager - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a service transition manager in the UK Government Digital and Data
profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out what you do now, where the
gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this level as **accepting products, determining whether they are fit for purpose and
assessing readiness against agreed service acceptance criteria**. At this level you:

- **make recommendations on go-live, early-life support and service acceptance**
- **ensure that the acceptance criteria are understood by the wider IT operations**

You are the person who says whether something is ready. That judgement lands in front of people who have
spent months building it and have a date they have already announced.

Eleven skills. Expect about 30 minutes.

## About this role level

**The role: service transition manager.** IT service transition managers provide overall planning for service
transitions and co-ordinate the required resources. The role controls the product life cycle of all changes,
enabling beneficial changes to be made with minimum disruption to IT services.

**Your level: service transition manager.** You accept products, determine whether they are fit for purpose
and assess readiness against agreed service acceptance criteria, with the accountabilities listed in the
introduction.

**Where this level sits.** This role has four levels:

1. Service acceptance analyst
2. Service readiness analyst
3. **Service transition manager** — you are here
4. Lead service transition manager

Your next step is lead service transition manager: responsible for the planning and co-ordination of
resources to ensure services are effectively transitioned into service operation, and **solely responsible
for co-ordination activities across projects, suppliers and service teams**.

**Two things worth knowing about this role.**

First, **your qualification requirement is the same as at lead** — a Level 3 service management framework
qualification plus knowledge of the life cycle or capability elements of ITIL. This role does not escalate to
an expert certificate the way IT service management does, so once you hold both, that skill is finished for
the rest of the role.

Second, **community collaboration is worded identically to the level below yours** and asks you to motivate
and empower teams, create the right environment for them, and identify the best team makeup. At lead it
changes rather than rises — into networking, feedback that lasts, and Agile health checks. This skill does
not follow a normal ladder in this role, so read the wording rather than assuming seniority.

**The eleven skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Asset and configuration management
2. Availability and capacity management
3. Change management
4. Community collaboration
5. Continuity management
6. Incident management
7. Ownership and initiative

9. Service focus
10. Service management framework knowledge
11. User focus

### Skill: Asset and configuration management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: **maintain secure configuration and accurate
information**; **control IT assets in one or more areas**; and **verify the location and state of IT assets**.

**A step up from the two levels below**, where it was tracking, logging and correcting information.

**Identical wording at lead service transition manager.**

**What that looks like here.** Every transition changes what exists. If the configuration record does not
move with it, the operational teams inherit a service they cannot describe.

### Skill: Community collaboration

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: **contribute to the work of others**; **motivate
and empower teams**; **create the right environment for teams to work in, and identify the best team makeup
depending on the situation**; and **recognise and deal with issues**.

**Identical to service readiness analyst.** It does not rise at your level, which is worth noticing given the
step-change in the rest of your accountabilities.

**What that looks like here.** You need co-operation from teams that do not report to you, at the point where
you may be about to tell them their product is not ready. Motivating and empowering in that context means
making the criteria feel like shared quality rather than an obstacle.

### Skill: Service focus

**What the framework expects at this level.** One thing: **see the bigger picture by taking groups of services
and investigating how to get the best of underlying services.**

**A step up from the two levels below**, where it was taking inputs and establishing coherent frameworks.

**The next step.** At lead it changes again — maintaining focus on the **entire life cycle** of the service
and keeping it at the forefront of readiness activities, understanding and communicating service benefits to
end users, and championing service focus within the team.

**What that looks like here.** Transition work sees products one at a time. Taking groups of services means
noticing what several transitions have in common — the same integration, the same platform limit, the same

### Skill: User focus

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: identify and engage with users or stakeholders to
collate user needs evidence; understand and define research that fits user needs; and use quantitative and
qualitative data about users to turn user focus into outcomes.

**Identical to the two levels below** — this skill does not rise until lead.

**The next step.** At lead it becomes collaborating with user researchers, representing users internally,
explaining the difference between user needs and desires, championing user research for all users, and
guiding others.

**What that looks like here.** "Fit for purpose" is a user question as much as an operational one. Transition
is one of the last moments anyone can still ask whether the thing is usable, not just runnable.
