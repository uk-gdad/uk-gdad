# Associate end user computing engineer - skills gap form

## Introduction

Hello, and welcome. This form is for you if you work as an associate end user computing engineer in the
UK Government Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can
say what you do now, what you would like to get better at, and what would help.

The framework describes this level plainly: as a trainee, you work in an established team and support end
user computing engineers in their daily work, under their direction.

End user computing is the part of IT that people actually meet. When someone's laptop will not start
before an important meeting, or their access has stopped working on their first day, the person who
sorts it out is the face of the whole digital function.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Five skills — the smallest set in this role — each with a
plain explanation, a rating, and a couple of questions. Take about 20 minutes.

  how long they will be kept, it is completely reasonable to ask before you fill it in.

## About this role level

**The role: end user computing engineer.** You are responsible for managing the product life cycle of all
service-raised incidents and all service requests, using knowledge management. You also inform customers
of progress and advise on workarounds where necessary, and support and maintain end user services.

**Your level: associate end user computing engineer.** As a trainee, you work in an established team and
support end user computing engineers in their daily work, under their direction.

**Where this level sits.** This role has five levels:

1. **Associate end user computing engineer** — you are here
2. End user computing engineer
3. Senior end user computing engineer
4. Lead end user computing engineer
5. Principal end user computing engineer

Your next step is end user computing engineer: working on-site maintaining hardware and fixing technical
problems quickly, providing first-time resolution by troubleshooting and diagnosing or escalating faults,
and coaching associates.

**One thing worth knowing about this role.** Your level has **five** skills; the level above has
**eleven**. Six appear at once: asset and configuration management, change management, service focus,
service management framework knowledge, technical specialism and testing. That is a large step, and one of
those — service management framework knowledge — is worded as holding a **Level 3 qualification**, which is
worth asking about now rather than later.

**The five skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. IT service reporting
2. Incident management
3. Ownership and initiative
4. Problem management
5. User focus

### Skill: IT service reporting

**What the framework expects at your level.** Two things: collate data from data repositories and provide
basic reporting; and explain what a key performance indicator is and why it is important.

**What that means day to day.** Pulling numbers out of the ticket system — how many raised, how many
closed, how long they took. Knowing what the numbers are for stops reporting becoming a chore with no
audience.

**What changes at end user computing engineer.** Nothing — the wording is **identical** at that level. So
this is something you can get genuinely good at now.

### Skill: Incident management

**What the framework expects at your level.** One thing: identify and register incidents, gathering the
required information and allocating them to the appropriate channel.

**What that means day to day.** A well-written ticket saves the next person a phone call: what is broken,
since when, what has been tried, what device, what has changed. A ticket that just says "laptop not
working" costs everyone time.

**What changes at end user computing engineer.** Nothing — the wording is **identical**. It first changes
at senior.

### Skill: Ownership and initiative

**What the framework expects at your level.** Two things: show an awareness of problem resolution
processes; and **pass problems on to your team**.

**What that means day to day.** At this level you are not expected to see everything through. Handing over
well — with what you have already tried — is the skill, and it is what stops the next person repeating
your first twenty minutes.

**What changes at end user computing engineer.** You would own an issue until a new owner has been found
or the problem has been mitigated or resolved. That is a real change.

### Skill: Problem management

**What the framework expects at your level.** Two things: investigate problems in systems, processes and
services, with an understanding of the level of a problem — strategic, tactical or operational; and
contribute to the implementation of remedies and preventative measures.

**What that means day to day.** You see the same faults repeatedly, so you are well placed to notice the
pattern. Fifteen people with the same problem after a software update is a problem, not fifteen incidents.

**What changes at end user computing engineer.** Nothing — the wording is **identical**.

### Skill: User focus

**What the framework expects at your level.** Two things: show an awareness or understanding of user
experience analysis and its principles; and explain the purpose of user stories and the focus on user
needs.

**What that means day to day.** The people you help are trying to do a job, and your fix either lets them
get on with it or does not. Explaining what happened in plain language, without jargon and without making
them feel foolish, is a real part of the work.

**What changes at end user computing engineer.** A big jump: identifying and engaging with users or
stakeholders to collate evidence of user needs, defining research that fits, and using data to turn user
focus into outcomes.
