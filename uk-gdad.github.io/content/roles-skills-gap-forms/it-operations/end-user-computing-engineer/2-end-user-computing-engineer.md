# End user computing engineer - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as an end user computing engineer in the UK Government Digital and Data
profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out what you do now, where the
gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this level as working on-site, maintaining hardware and fixing technical problems
as quickly as possible. It expects you to provide **first-time resolution** by troubleshooting and
diagnosing or escalating faults to service desk managers, incident managers and problem managers, and to
coach associates and share knowledge with colleagues.

First-time resolution is the measure this job is usually judged on, and it is a double-edged one. Fixing
something quickly by repeating a workaround keeps the number high and leaves the underlying fault in place
for the next person.

**You jumped from five skills to eleven** at this level. Expect about 35 minutes.

This is a static web page. There is nothing to type into and nothing to send from here — your answers never
touch this site.

Print it, copy the questions into a document, or paste them into whatever your organisation uses for
development conversations. Every question is numbered, so answers travel back on their own.

Worth knowing:

- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a finding.
- **Answer about the job you do**, not the one on the job description.
- **"I haven't had the chance" is a real answer.** With eleven skills, several blanks are expected.
- **Do not include anything about a named colleague's device or account.**
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers, what they are used for, or how long
  they are kept, ask before you fill it in.

## About this role level

**The role: end user computing engineer.** You manage the product life cycle of all service-raised
incidents and all service requests, using knowledge management. You also inform customers of progress and
advise on workarounds where necessary, and support and maintain end user services.

**Your level: end user computing engineer.** You work on-site, maintaining hardware and fixing technical
problems as quickly as possible. The framework expects that you:

- provide first-time resolution by troubleshooting and diagnosing or escalating faults to service desk
  managers, incident managers and problem managers to investigate and resolve
- coach associates and share knowledge with team colleagues

**Where this level sits.** This role has five levels:

1. Associate end user computing engineer
2. **End user computing engineer** — you are here
3. Senior end user computing engineer
4. Lead end user computing engineer
5. Principal end user computing engineer

Your next step is senior end user computing engineer: managing the team while still solving technical
problems, co-ordinating and prioritising tasks, and working closely with infrastructure, capacity and
availability managers.

**Two things worth knowing about this role.**

First, you jumped from five skills to eleven at this level — the second-largest single increase in the
framework. If this level felt like a bigger step than the title suggested, the published wording agrees.

Second, *Service management framework knowledge* here is worded as holding a **Level 3 service management
framework qualification**. It is one of very few named qualifications in the framework, so it is concrete
and fundable — worth raising in this form if you do not hold it.

**The eleven skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Asset and configuration management

3. IT service reporting
4. Incident management
5. Ownership and initiative

### Skill: Asset and configuration management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: maintain secure configuration and accurate
information; control IT assets in one or more areas; and verify the location and state of IT assets.

**Same wording at senior and lead.**

**What that looks like here.** Knowing where every device is, who has it, and what is on it. In end user
computing this record decays fastest — people move teams, leave, or quietly keep a second laptop.

### Skill: Change management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: implement change requests; and apply change

### Skill: Ownership and initiative

**What the framework expects at this level.** One thing: **own an issue until a new owner has been found or
the problem has been mitigated or resolved.**

**What that looks like here.** The real change from associate. Someone whose problem you own does not have
to chase — which, from a user's point of view, is most of what good IT support feels like.

**The next step.** At lead you take accountability for issues that occur, are proactive in searching for
potential problems, and achieve excellent user outcomes.
