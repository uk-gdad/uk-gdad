# Head of service desk - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a head of service desk in the UK Government Digital and Data profession.
Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out what you do now, where the gaps are, and
what would close them.

The framework describes this level as responsible for **managing a team who provides first- and second-line
technical support**. At this level you:

- ensure support of existing and emerging information and communications technology (ICT) services, and
  **provide technical advice to project teams**
- **ensure that the support team undertakes development** so that they can advise on future projects and
  current issues
- **manage and monitor service desk service level agreements and performance**

This is the top of the role. Nothing above it in the framework tells you what good looks like, so the
questions here ask you to argue for your own judgement rather than measure yourself against a next step.

Eight skills. Expect about 30 minutes.

## About this role level

**The role: service desk manager.** A service desk manager is responsible for managing the first- and
second-line technical support for all departmental IT applications and services across sites, including end
user computing — multi-function devices and specialised IT equipment included. The role is also responsible
for ensuring support for existing and emerging information and communications technology services, including
providing technical advice to project teams.

**Your level: head of service desk.** You manage a team providing first- and second-line technical support,
with the accountabilities listed in the introduction.

**Where this level sits.** This role has four levels:

1. Service desk analyst
2. Senior service desk analyst
3. Service desk manager
4. **Head of service desk** — you are here

There is no level above this in the role. Movement from here is usually into IT service management, IT
operations more broadly, or a wider technology leadership post.

**Two things worth knowing about this level.**

First, **four of your eight skills are worded identically at service desk manager**: asset and configuration
management, customer service management, ownership and initiative, and service management framework
knowledge. Only three genuinely rise for you — IT service reporting, problem management and service focus,
plus user focus. If your organisation treats the two levels as interchangeable, the published text does not
entirely disagree.

Second, **IT service reporting is the standout.** At your level it becomes using **prediction
methodologies** to produce and maintain a holistic view of important trends across multiple processes. That
is forecasting, and it is the only place in this whole role where it appears. It is also the expectation
most likely to be unmet, because most service desks have reporting tools rather than forecasting ones.

**The eight skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Asset and configuration management
2. Customer service management
3. IT service reporting
4. Ownership and initiative
5. Problem management
6. Service focus
7. Service management framework knowledge
8. User focus

### Skill: IT service reporting

**What the framework expects at this level.** One thing: **use prediction methodologies to produce and
maintain a holistic view of important trends across multiple processes.**

**The clearest step up in this role**, and unique to your level. Service desk manager uses data analytics to
make decisions; you forecast.

**What prediction actually means here.** Knowing that demand will rise before it does — a rollout landing, a
seasonal peak, an end-of-support date, a change to a business process that will generate calls for a month.
It is the difference between staffing for last quarter and staffing for next.

**Being fair to yourself.** Most service desks have reporting tools, not forecasting ones. A low rating here
may describe your tooling rather than your capability — say which it is, because the ask is different.

### Skill: Problem management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: **ensure that the right actions are taken to
investigate, resolve and anticipate problems**; and **co-ordinate the team** to investigate problems,
implement solutions and take preventive measures.

**A shift from service desk manager**, where you initiated and monitored actions yourself. Ensuring and
co-ordinating, now.

**The hard part at this level.** Most of the causes behind your calls belong to other teams. Ensuring the
right actions are taken means influence across the organisation, not authority within your own.

### Skill: User focus

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: **give direction on which tools or methods to
use**; demonstrate experience in meeting the needs of users across a variety of channels; **bring insight and
expertise in how user needs have changed over time** to ensure they are met by the business; and **apply
strategic thinking to provide the best service for the end user**.

**A step up from service desk manager**, where it was collaborating with researchers and championing
research.

**What that looks like here.** How people expect to get help has changed substantially — self-service, chat,
messaging, phone. A desk built for one channel serves the others as an afterthought, and the people who need
the afterthought channel are usually the ones with the least room to work around it.
