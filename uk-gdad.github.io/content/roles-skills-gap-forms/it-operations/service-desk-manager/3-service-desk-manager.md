# Service desk manager - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a service desk manager in the UK Government Digital and Data profession.
Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out what you do now, where the gaps are, and
what would close them.

The framework describes this level in two short statements: you **identify emerging issues** and **are a
knowledge expert**; and you are **responsible for the resourcing and development of the team, including the
preparation and co-ordination of service transition activities**.

Service transition is the phrase worth noticing. It appears nowhere else in this role. When something new
comes into service, you are the one preparing the desk to support it — which usually means finding out about
it late and building the knowledge anyway.

Eight skills. Expect about 30 minutes.

## Advisory

This skills gap form is created by AI and is work in progress for educational purposes. If you intend to use
it in any way, please consult with your organization's appropriate teammates, such as with human resources

## How to fill in this form

This is a static web page. There is nothing to type into and nothing to send from here — your answers never
touch this site.

Print it, copy the questions into a document, or paste them into whatever your organisation uses for
development conversations. Every question is numbered, so answers travel back on their own.

Worth knowing:

- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a finding.
- **"My team does this" is not an answer to "what do you do".** Name your part.
- **"I have not had the chance" is a real answer.**
- **Do not include anything confidential** — no ticket details, no names of callers or colleagues.
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers, what they are used for, or how long
  they are kept, ask before you fill it in.

- **A 4 is a claim about other people** — that someone else's practice changed because of you and would stay

## About this role level

**The role: service desk manager.** A service desk manager is responsible for managing the first- and
second-line technical support for all departmental IT applications and services across sites, including end
user computing — multi-function devices and specialised IT equipment included. The role is also responsible
for ensuring support for existing and emerging information and communications technology services, including
providing technical advice to project teams.

**Your level: service desk manager.** You identify emerging issues, are a knowledge expert, and are
responsible for the resourcing and development of the team, including preparing and co-ordinating service
transition activities.

**Where this level sits.** This role has four levels:

1. Service desk analyst
2. Senior service desk analyst
3. **Service desk manager** — you are here
4. Head of service desk

Your next step is head of service desk: managing a team providing first- and second-line technical support,
ensuring the team develops so it can advise on future projects, and managing and monitoring service level
agreements and performance.

**Two things worth knowing about this role.**

First, **four of your eight skills are worded identically at head of service desk**: asset and configuration
management, customer service management, ownership and initiative, and service management framework
knowledge. The step above yours is not mostly a skills step — it is a step into managing the function and
owning the agreements. Worth knowing if you are aiming at it, because the preparation is exposure rather than
another certificate.

Second, **your qualification requirement does not rise at all above you.** Level 3 plus knowledge of the life
cycle or capability elements of ITIL is the requirement at your level and at head of service desk. This role
tops out lower on certification than IT service management does, where the top two levels need an expert
certificate.

**The eight skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Asset and configuration management
2. Customer service management
3. IT service reporting
4. Ownership and initiative
5. Problem management
6. Service focus
7. Service management framework knowledge
8. User focus

### Skill: Problem management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: **initiate and monitor actions to investigate
patterns and trends** to resolve problems; effectively consult specialists where required; **determine the
appropriate resolution and assist with its implementation**; and **determine preventative measures**.

**A step up from senior service desk analyst**, where it was investigating and contributing.

**What that looks like here.** The desk holds the evidence — every symptom the organisation produces arrives
in your queue. Determining resolutions usually means persuading a team that owns the system, not fixing it
yourself.

**The next step.** At head of service desk you ensure the right actions are taken and co-ordinate the team to
do it.

### Skill: User focus

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: **collaborate with user researchers and represent
users internally**; **explain the difference between user needs and the desires of the user**; **champion
user research to focus on all users**; prioritise and define approaches to understand the user story, guiding
others in doing so; and offer recommendations on the best tools and methods to use.

**A step up from senior service desk analyst**, where it was engaging with users and using data about them.

**Representing users internally is the interesting one for a service desk.** You have more contact with more
colleagues than anyone else in technology. Whether that gets heard depends on whether anyone has built a
route for it.

**The next step.** At head of service desk you give direction on tools and methods, demonstrate experience
across channels, bring insight into how needs have changed over time, and apply strategic thinking.
