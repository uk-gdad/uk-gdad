# Configuration analyst - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a configuration analyst in the UK Government Digital and Data
profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out what you do now, where
the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this level as providing administrative support to the change and release
manager, helping to maintain the integrity of the **configuration management database** and
independently solving smaller problems.

The configuration database is one of those things that is either trusted or ignored, with very little in
between. When it is accurate, incidents get resolved faster and changes get assessed properly. When it
has drifted, everybody works around it and nobody says so.

Eleven skills. Expect about 35 minutes.

## About this role level

**The role: change and release manager.** Change and release managers lead the change advisory board,
ensuring adequate risk assessment and scheduling of technical changes and releases. They own the
configuration management database and the access, security and configuration of change activities and
release procedures.

**Your level: configuration analyst.** You provide administrative support to the change and release
manager, helping to maintain the integrity of the configuration management database and independently
solving smaller problems.

**Where this level sits.** This role has three levels:

1. **Configuration analyst** — you are here
2. Change and release analyst
3. Change and release manager

**One thing worth knowing about this role — and it is striking.** **Ten of your eleven skills are worded
identically at change and release analyst**, the level above. The only one that changes is *Change
management*, which moves from implementing change requests under supervision to analysing and assessing
the impact of change, documenting change requests and actioning them.

That is the narrowest published gap between two levels anywhere in this framework. If you want to move
up, change impact assessment is essentially the whole list — which is unusually clear guidance, and
worth using.

**The eleven skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Asset and configuration management
2. Availability and capacity management
3. Change management
4. Community collaboration
5. Continuity management
6. Incident management
7. Ownership and initiative
8. Problem management
9. Service focus

### Skill: Asset and configuration management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: maintain secure configuration and accurate
information; control IT assets in one or more areas; and verify the location and state of IT assets.

**Same wording at change and release analyst.**

**What that looks like here.** This is the core of your job. Verification is the part that keeps the
database honest — records that are never checked against reality drift within months, and once people
notice they stop trusting any of it.

### Skill: Change management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: implement change requests; and apply change

senior wants to skip a step because it is urgent.

### Skill: User focus

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: **show an awareness or understanding of user
experience analysis and its principles**; and **explain the purpose of user stories and the focus on user
needs**.

**A user story** is a short sentence describing what someone needs and why: "As a caseworker, I need the
release notes in advance so that I can warn my team." It keeps the reason attached to the requirement.

**Same wording at change and release analyst.**

**What that looks like here.** Changes affect people. Knowing which services matter most to users helps
you flag a change request that deserves more care than its paperwork suggests.
