# Lead performance analyst - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a lead performance analyst in the UK Government
Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can
set out what you do now, where the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this level as managing junior performance analysts at any level
and co-ordinating teams or groups of related analysts. It expects you to assist, take
forward and influence the strategy determined by the head of performance analysis,
**including data implementation and data architecture**; remove blockers and act as an
escalation point for important issues; be an ambassador for analytics across communities,
departments and government; be responsible for the accuracy and quality of data and
analysis and how they are used; proactively share knowledge and promote a data-driven
culture as a leader in the community; and coach and mentor senior, mid-level and
associate performance analysts.

*Data implementation and data architecture* is the phrase that expands this job beyond
analysis. You are expected to shape how data gets collected and structured, not only what
is done with it once it exists.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Seven skills, each with an explanation, a
rating and some questions. Expect about 30 minutes.

## How to fill in this form

This is a static web page. There is nothing to type into and nothing to send from here —
your answers never touch this site.

Print it, copy the questions into a document, or paste them into whatever your
organisation uses for development conversations. Every question is numbered, so answers
travel back on their own.

Worth knowing:

- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a finding.
- **"My analysts do this" is not an answer to "what do you do".** Name your part.
- **"I haven't had the chance" is a real answer** at this level too.
- **Do not paste real data.** Describe the work, not the contents.
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers, what they are
  used for, or how long they are kept, ask before you fill it in.

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not the remit on your job description.
- **A 4 is a claim about other people** — that someone else's practice changed because of
  you and would stay changed if you left. Coaching and mentoring are written into your

## About this role level

**The role: performance analyst.** A performance analyst conducts analysis. In this role
you develop performance measurement frameworks — key performance indicators, goals, user
needs and benefits — and analyse the performance of a service or product against them,
adapting your approach and framework as things change.

**Your level: lead performance analyst.** You manage junior performance analysts at any
level, co-ordinating teams or groups of related analysts. The framework expects that you:

- assist, take forward and influence the strategy as determined by the head of
  performance analysis, including data implementation and data architecture
- remove blockers and act as an escalation point for important issues
- be an ambassador for analytics across communities, departments and government
- be responsible for the accuracy and quality of data and analysis, and of how data and
  analysis are used
- proactively share knowledge and promote a data-driven culture, as a leader in the
  community
- coach and mentor senior performance analysts, performance analysts and associate
  performance analysts

**Where this level sits.** This role has five levels:

1. Associate performance analyst
2. Performance analyst
3. Senior performance analyst
4. **Lead performance analyst** — you are here
5. Head of performance analysis

**One thing worth knowing about this role.** **Six of your seven skills are worded
identically at head of performance analysis.** Only *Analysis and insight* differs — and
it differs in your favour: yours is teaching and supervising a wide range of techniques
and helping organisations learn from strategic insights, while the head's returns to the
performance analyst wording.

The published difference between lead and head is therefore almost entirely in the
accountabilities — leading the community, setting rather than influencing strategy,
defining the quality assurance framework, and business planning and recruitment. If you
are thinking about that step, those are the things to seek out, because the skills will
not distinguish you.

**The seven skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Analysis and insight
2. Communicating analysis and insight
3. Performance measurement
4. Quality assurance of data and analysis
5. Technical understanding (performance analyst)
6. Understanding analysis across the product life cycle
7. User-centred analysis

## Job skills

Seven skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the level expects, what it

### Skill: Analysis and insight

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: understand, **teach and
supervise** a wide range of analysis techniques; help organisations create and learn from
strategic insights; and apply innovative approaches to resolve problems.

**This is the only skill where your published wording is higher than the head of
performance analysis's**, which returns to the performance analyst wording. Yours is the
most demanding analysis expectation in the role.

**What that looks like here.** *Help organisations learn* is the hard half. Insight that
reaches a director and stops there has not been learned from; learning shows up as a
changed decision, a changed process, or a changed assumption that stays changed.

### Skill: Performance measurement

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: oversee the development and
consistency of performance measurement frameworks across multiple projects; and ensure
those frameworks are aligned to business needs and strategy.

**Same wording at head of performance analysis.**

**What that looks like here.** *Consistency across projects* is what makes a portfolio
view possible. If every team defines completion differently, the aggregate is fiction —
and getting agreement on shared definitions is negotiation, not analysis.

### Skill: Quality assurance of data and analysis

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: set up tools and systems for

attention. The test is whether a weak piece of analysis would be caught without you seeing

### Skill: Understanding analysis across the product life cycle

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: develop sustainable support
models; identify and deal with potential risks across or between all stages of the product
life cycle; coach others; contribute to the assessment of other teams, providing guidance
and support as they move through the life cycle phases; and lead and champion best
practice, identifying improvements to current approaches.

**Same wording at head of performance analysis.**

**What that looks like here.** *Sustainable support models* means someone maintains the
measurement after the project team disperses. Dashboards that nobody owns decay quietly
and are then cited long after they stopped being right.

### Skill: User-centred analysis

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: give direction on which tools or
methods to use; bring insight and expertise to how user needs have changed over time and
ensure they are met; apply strategic thinking to how to provide the best service for the
end user; and **consider the political strategy and implications of any analyses and
insights**.

**Same wording at head of performance analysis.**

**What that looks like here.** That last item is unusual and honest. Analysis in government
lands in a political context, and thinking about implications is not the same as changing
findings to suit them. Knowing where that line is — and staying on the right side of it —
is the skill.
