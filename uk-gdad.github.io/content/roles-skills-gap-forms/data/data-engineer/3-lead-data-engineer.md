# Lead data engineer - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a lead data engineer in the UK Government
Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that
you can set out what you do now, where the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this level as responsible for the design and implementation
of numerous complex data flows connecting operational systems, analytics and
business intelligence systems. It expects you to recognise and share opportunities
to reuse existing data flows *between teams*; be responsible for the build of
data-streaming systems; co-ordinate teams and set best practice and standards; apply
knowledge of systems integration to your work; and **champion data engineering
across government**.

That last accountability is unusual — the framework rarely asks a lead to work
outside their own organisation. It is also the first thing that disappears when
delivery pressure arrives.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Ten skills, each with an explanation, a
rating and some questions. Expect about 35 minutes.

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not the remit on your job
  description.
- **A 4 is a claim about other people** — that someone else's practice changed
  because of you and would stay changed if you left. Setting standards is written

## About this role level

**The role: data engineer.** A data engineer develops and constructs data products
and services, and integrates them into systems and business processes.

**Your level: lead data engineer.** You are responsible for the design and
implementation of numerous complex data flows connecting operational systems,
analytics and business intelligence systems. The framework expects that you:

- recognise and share opportunities to reuse existing data flows between teams
- be responsible for the build of data-streaming systems
- co-ordinate teams and set best practice and standards
- apply knowledge of systems integration to your work
- champion data engineering across government

**Where this level sits.** This role has four levels:

1. Data engineer
2. Senior data engineer
3. **Lead data engineer** — you are here
4. Head of data engineering

Your next step is head of data engineering: leading multi-functional delivery teams
to deliver data services for your department, other departments and private sector
partners; inspiring best practice; building capability through technical leadership
and career development; and working with other senior members to identify, plan and
deliver services.

**Two things worth knowing about this role.**

First, three of your ten skills are worded **identically** at head of data
engineering — data analysis and synthesis, data development process, and data
integration design. And *Testing* is identical at every level of this role. Four of
the ten do not distinguish your level from the top.

Second, and more oddly: **your published wording for *Data modelling* is more
demanding than the head of data engineering's.** The head's text is the same as the
entry level's. That is how the framework publishes it. It means data modelling is
not a place where a case for the next level can be made on wording, and the useful
comparison is with senior instead.

**The ten skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Communicating between the technical and non-technical
2. Data analysis and synthesis
3. Data development process
4. Data innovation
5. Data integration design
6. Data modelling
7. Metadata management
8. Problem management
9. Programming and build (data and analytics engineering)
10. Testing

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: listen to and interpret
the needs of technical and non-technical stakeholders, and manage their
expectations; manage active and reactive communication; and support or host
difficult discussions within the team or with diverse senior stakeholders.

**What that looks like here.** Reactive communication for a lead data engineer often
means a major failure with an audience — a report that did not land, a service
running on stale data. Being the calm, clear source during that is worth more than
any architecture diagram.

**The next step.** At head you mediate between people at all levels, moderate
difficult discussions about high-risk topics under time pressure, and represent the
community to large audiences inside and outside the organisation.

migrating under pressure with no goodwill left.

### Skill: Data modelling

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: understand the concepts
and principles of data modelling and produce relevant data models; work across
government and industry, recognising opportunities for the reuse and alignment of
data models in different organisations; and design the method to categorise data
models within an organisation.

**What that looks like here.** Alignment across organisations is the most ambitious
modelling expectation anywhere in this role. It means cross-departmental agreement
on what an entity is, which is slow, political and occasionally transformative.

**A note on the wording.** The published text for this skill at **head of data
engineering** is the same as at the entry level, and less demanding than yours. That
is how the framework publishes it, so this skill will not show progression above

### Skill: Metadata management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: design an appropriate

### Skill: Problem management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: ensure that the right
actions are taken to investigate, resolve and anticipate problems; and co-ordinate
the team to investigate problems, implement solutions and take preventive measures.

**What that looks like here.** You are accountable for problems being handled rather
than for handling them, which means resisting the pull to fix things yourself when
you can see the answer immediately.

right time, understand how a problem fits the larger picture, help others describe
