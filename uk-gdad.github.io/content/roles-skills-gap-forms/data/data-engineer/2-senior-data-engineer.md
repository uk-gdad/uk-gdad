# Senior data engineer - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a senior data engineer in the UK Government
Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that
you can set out what you do now, where the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this level as designing and leading the implementation of
data flows that connect operational systems, analytics and business intelligence
systems. It expects you to recognise opportunities to reuse existing data flows;
lead the build of data streaming systems; optimise code so processes perform well;
and lead work on database management.

The shift from the level below is that **the design is now yours**. At data engineer
you delivered someone else's; here you decide what gets built and how, and you carry
the consequences when a shortcut taken today costs three weeks next year.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Ten skills, each with an explanation, a
rating and some questions. Expect about 35 minutes.

- **"The team does this" is not an answer to "what do you do".** Name your part.
- **"I haven't had the chance" is a real answer**, and more useful than a

- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers, what they

## Rating scale

Where a question asks for a rating, use this scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | You have not worked with this |
| 1 | Awareness | You know what it is and why it matters |
| 2 | Working | You do it with support |
| 3 | Practitioner | You do it unsupported, and handle the usual exceptions |
| 4 | Expert | You set the approach and develop others in it |

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not the remit on your job
  description.
- **A 4 is a claim about other people** — that someone else's practice changed
  because of you and would stay changed if you left. One or two are reasonable at
  senior.

## About this role level

**The role: data engineer.** A data engineer develops and constructs data products
and services, and integrates them into systems and business processes.

**Your level: senior data engineer.** You design and lead the implementation of
data flows connecting operational systems, analytics and business intelligence
systems. The framework expects that you:

- recognise opportunities to reuse existing data flows
- lead the build of data streaming systems
- optimise the code to ensure processes perform optimally
- lead work on database management

**Where this level sits.** This role has four levels:

1. Data engineer
2. **Senior data engineer** — you are here
3. Lead data engineer
4. Head of data engineering

Your next step is lead data engineer: responsible for numerous complex flows,
sharing reuse opportunities *between* teams, co-ordinating teams, setting best
practice and standards, applying systems integration knowledge, and championing data
engineering across government.

**Two things worth knowing about this role.**

First, *Testing* is worded **identically at all four levels**. Your published
testing expectation is the same as a head of data engineering's. If you are setting
a testing approach rather than defining conditions within one, you are beyond the
published wording.

Second, your level has the most demanding published wording for *Data modelling* in
the whole role — alongside lead. The wording at head of data engineering is actually
the same as the entry level's. That is how the framework publishes it, and it means
data modelling is not a place where progression above lead is visible.

**The ten skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Communicating between the technical and non-technical
2. Data analysis and synthesis
3. Data development process
4. Data innovation
5. Data integration design
6. Data modelling
7. Metadata management
8. Problem management
9. Programming and build (data and analytics engineering)
10. Testing

## Job skills

Ten skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the level expects, what

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: communicate effectively
with technical and non-technical stakeholders; support and host discussions within a
multidisciplinary team, with potentially difficult dynamics; be an advocate for the
team externally; and manage differing stakeholder perspectives.

**What that looks like here.** Explaining why a design that is slower to build will
be cheaper to own, to people who are only measuring the build. That argument is lost
more often than won, and losing it well matters.

**The next step.** At lead you listen to and interpret stakeholder needs and manage
expectations, handle active and reactive communication, and host difficult
discussions with diverse senior stakeholders.

### Skill: Data analysis and synthesis

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: undertake data profiling
and source system analysis; and present clear insights to colleagues to support the
end use of the data.

**Same wording as data engineer.** This skill does not change between those two
levels.

**What that looks like here.** At senior the difference is breadth — enough sources
profiled that you recognise the failure patterns before you look, and enough
authority to say a source is not fit to build on.

**The next step.** At lead you help teams apply a range of profiling techniques, do
source system analysis from a complex single source, and bring multiple sources into
a conformed model.

### Skill: Data development process

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: design, build and test
data products that are complex or large scale; and build teams to complete data
integration services.

**What that looks like here.** *Build teams* is the surprising item at senior — the
framework expects you to assemble the people, not just the pipeline. In practice
that often means borrowing capacity and making the case for it.

**The next step.** At lead you establish enterprise-scale integration procedures
across the development life cycle and ensure teams adhere to them, and manage
resources so data services work at enterprise level.

### Skill: Data integration design

**What the framework expects at this level.** One thing: select and implement the
appropriate technologies to deliver resilient, scalable and future-proofed data
solutions.

**What that looks like here.** The technology choice is yours, which means the
lock-in is too. Future-proofed usually means being able to leave — could you move
this to a different platform in a year without rewriting everything?

**The next step.** At lead you establish standards, keep them up to date and ensure
adherence, and keep abreast of best practice in industry and across government.

### Skill: Data modelling

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: produce relevant data
models across multiple subject areas; explain which models to use for which purpose;
understand industry-recognised data modelling patterns and standards, and when to
apply them; and compare and align different data models.

**What that looks like here.** *Align different models* is the difficult one —
usually two teams with two definitions of the same entity and neither wanting to
move. The technical part is easy; the agreement is not.

**The next step.** At lead you work across government and industry to find reuse
and alignment opportunities between organisations, and design the method for
categorising models within an organisation.

### Skill: Metadata management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: design an appropriate
metadata repository; suggest changes to improve current repositories; understand a
range of tools for storing and working with metadata; and advise less experienced
members of the team about metadata management.

**What that looks like here.** Designing means deciding what to capture — capture
too much and it is filled in badly, too little and it answers nothing. Lineage
generated automatically from the pipelines beats lineage typed in by hand every
time.

### Skill: Problem management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: initiate and monitor
actions to investigate patterns and trends to resolve problems; effectively consult
specialists where required; determine the appropriate resolution and assist with its
implementation; and determine preventative measures.

**What that looks like here.** You decide when the fix is good enough, which means
you are also the person who decides to live with something. Recording that decision
matters — undocumented tolerated problems become someone else's mystery.

**The next step.** At lead you ensure the right actions are taken to investigate,
resolve and anticipate problems, and co-ordinate the team to do it.

### Skill: Programming and build (data and analytics engineering)

tools to design, code, test, correct and document moderate-to-complex programs and
scripts from agreed specifications and subsequent iterations; and collaborate with
others to review specifications where appropriate.

**What that looks like here.** Optimising code is in your accountabilities, and
optimisation without measurement is guessing. Knowing where the time actually goes
in a slow pipeline is the skill.

**The next step.** Identical wording at lead data engineer. It changes at head,
