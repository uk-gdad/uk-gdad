# Head of data ethics - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you are the head of data ethics in a UK government
organisation. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out
what you do now, where the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes a data ethicist as someone who assesses the societal effect
of technology and data and produces recommendations for other data professionals —
thinking about fairness, accountability, the law, moral dilemmas and risks in the
creation of technology and data products and policies. At your level you lead the
development and implementation of organisational data ethics policies.

You are almost certainly the only person in your organisation doing this job. That
shapes everything: there is nobody to compare notes with internally, nobody who can
cover for you, and no established view of what good looks like other than the one
you have built.

Head of data ethics is the top of this role. Nothing here is scored, marked or
ranked. Ten skills, each with an explanation, a rating and some questions. Expect
about 35 minutes.

## Advisory

This skills gap form is created by AI and is work in progress for educational
purposes. If you intend to use it in any way, please consult with your
organization's appropriate teammates, such as with human resources staff, or people
and organizational development staff, or legal compliance staff.

## How to fill in this form

This is a static web page. There is nothing to type into and nothing to send from
here — your answers never touch this site.

Print it, copy the questions into a document, or paste them into whatever your
organisation uses for development conversations. Every question is numbered, so
answers travel back on their own.

Worth knowing:

- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a finding.
- **"I haven't had the chance" is a real answer** at this level too.
- **Be careful how you describe live cases.** Some of your work concerns decisions
  that are contested, unresolved, or about identifiable people. Describe the work
  at a level you would be comfortable saying in a corridor, and do not name anyone.
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers, what they are
  used for, or how long they are kept, ask before you fill it in.

## Rating scale

Where a question asks for a rating, use this scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | You have not worked with this |
| 1 | Awareness | You know what it is and why it matters |
| 2 | Working | You do it with support |
| 3 | Practitioner | You do it unsupported, and handle the usual exceptions |
| 4 | Expert | You set the approach and develop others in it |

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not the remit on your job
  description.
- **A 4 is a claim about other people** — that someone else's practice changed
  because of you and would stay changed if you left. In a role whose entire output
  is other people's decisions, that is the only honest test.

A filled-in answer looks like this:

> **Product ownership (data ethics)**
>
> - [x] 2 — Working: I do it with support
>
> *Evidence:* I wrote our assessment tool and two directorates use it. Promoting
> it properly and gathering feedback systematically needs capacity I do not have,
> so adoption elsewhere has stalled.

Named practice, and a clear line about where it stops.

## About this role level

**The role: data ethicist.** A data ethicist assesses the societal effect of
technology and data, and produces recommendations for other data professionals.
This involves thinking about fairness, accountability, the law, moral dilemmas and
risks in the creation of technology and data products and policies. In this role you
will:

- provide research and expertise on data ethics
- enable others to implement data ethics best practice in their work — through
  training, advising data science teams, or demonstrating how to apply ethical
  principles through examples and case studies
- communicate effectively to explain and raise awareness of data ethics issues,
  and to listen to, convene, advise and mediate between parts of the organisation
- help people to ask questions, express concerns and discuss ethical dilemmas

**Your level: head of data ethics.** You lead the development and implementation of
organisational data ethics policies.

**There is no level above this one.** This role has two levels and this is the
highest. Where people go next is a question about other roles — data leadership,
policy, regulation or research — and the framework does not define it as a next
step, so this form does not invent one.

**Where this level sits:**

1. Data ethics lead
2. **Head of data ethics** — you are here

**Two things worth knowing about this role.**

First, **the framework publishes no accountabilities for either level** — a role
description, a one-sentence level description, and the skills. What you actually do
is defined locally, which makes the first section of this form the only written
record of it that many organisations have.

Second, four of your ten skills are worded **identically** at data ethics lead —
communication (data ethics), empathy and inclusivity, managing decisions and risks,
and problem management. The published difference between the two levels rests on the
other six.

**The ten skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Analysis and synthesis (data ethics)
2. Applied social sciences
3. Communicating between the technical and non-technical
4. Communication (data ethics)
5. Data ethics and privacy
6. Empathy and inclusivity
7. Managing decisions and risks
8. Problem management
9. Product ownership (data ethics)
10. Stakeholder relationship management

## Job skills

Ten skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the framework expects
and what it looks like in practice. There is no level above, so these say what the
skill demands rather than what comes next.

### Skill: Analysis and synthesis (data ethics)

**What the framework expects.** Six things: expertly draw together, analyse and
evaluate qualitative and quantitative data; quickly read and interpret complex
documents from a range of sources and distil what is relevant; turn research data
into clear findings that inform data ethics decisions for the entire organisation;
build capacity in analysis and synthesis and involve others to increase consensus and
challenge assumptions; help teams define project outcomes and ethical
considerations, and integrate ethical diagnostics and assessment; and help the
organisation continually assure and improve its practices to generate clear and
valuable data ethics findings.

**What that looks like here.** *For the entire organisation* is the change from the
level below. Your findings set precedent — the answer you give about one model
becomes the rule people apply to the next five, whether or not you intended that.

### Skill: Applied social sciences

**What the framework expects.** Five things: explain complex concepts across the
social sciences — anthropology, economics, sociology, philosophy, psychology or race
theory; apply various social science theories to the strategic oversight of data
projects, products and policies; apply them to evaluate and challenge assumptions
made in data science projects; identify and use best practice in data ethics; and
work with academics and external researchers to publish research on applied digital
ethics.

**What that looks like here.** *Publish research* is written into your level, and it
is the item most heads of data ethics never get to. It needs time nobody allocates
and permission nobody refuses but nobody grants either.

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What the framework expects.** Three things: mediate between people and strengthen

high-risk and complex topics, even within constrained timescales; and speak on behalf

organisation.

usually a launch decision, and you are the one asked whether it is acceptable — with
hours rather than weeks, and the answer will be quoted.

### Skill: Communication (data ethics)

**What the framework expects.** Four things: expertly translate technical concepts
to non-technical audiences so they are understood by all; demonstrate a good
understanding of how technology and data products and services are built; understand
technical jargon and have enough knowledge to hold meaningful conversations with data
science experts — on issues such as minimising bias in data, or gathering,
collecting, cleansing, triangulating and reusing data; and effectively support data
scientists and engineers in implementing data ethics.

**Same wording as data ethics lead.** This skill does not change between the two
levels.

**What that looks like here.** Technical credibility is what stops your advice being
politely absorbed and ignored. It also decays — the techniques you understood well
three years ago are not the ones being used now.

### Skill: Data ethics and privacy

**What the framework expects.** Four things: oversee compliance with data ethics
standards and legislation across the organisation; develop, manage and continuously
improve an ethical framework within your area; develop a data ethics culture,
influencing others to act on it; and ensure data ethics is applied appropriately when
formulating, implementing and evaluating organisational policies and programmes.

**What that looks like here.** *Culture* is the word that decides whether this job
works. A framework you enforce reaches the projects you see; a culture reaches the
ones you never hear about, which is most of them.

### Skill: Empathy and inclusivity

**What the framework expects.** Four things: incorporate a wide variety of views from
underrepresented groups into product and policy work, using in-depth consulting and
outreach strategies; be involved in the wider organisational diversity and inclusion
plan; draw on your multidisciplinary background and personal experience to understand
the consequences of data systems on a diverse range of stakeholders; and demonstrate a
thorough understanding of social issues, types of bias and discrimination different
groups can face, and use this to inform your data ethics work.

**Same wording as data ethics lead.**

**What that looks like here.** At head level the question is whether outreach is
funded. Consulting affected communities properly costs money, and it is the first
thing cut in a tight year.

### Skill: Product ownership (data ethics)

**What the framework expects.** Five things: define and create organisation-wide data
ethics tools and translate theoretical principles into practice; demonstrate
expertise in product management principles and approaches; set new ways of working
and adapt to change; capture and translate user needs into deliverables; and
demonstrate expertise in feedback gathering, evaluation mechanisms and product
promotion.

**What that looks like here.** Your tools have users, and unused tools have failed
however sound they are. *Product promotion* is in the framework's wording, which is
an unusually honest acknowledgement that good guidance does not spread on merit
alone.

### Skill: Stakeholder relationship management

**What the framework expects.** Three things: direct the stakeholder relationship
strategy for your teams; ensure stakeholders' objectives are set and support teams to
meet them; and influence and negotiate with senior stakeholders to resolve issues and
enable progress.

**What that looks like here.** There is no process compelling anyone to consult you,
so relationships are the mechanism. The most important is with whoever can stop
something — usually not you.
