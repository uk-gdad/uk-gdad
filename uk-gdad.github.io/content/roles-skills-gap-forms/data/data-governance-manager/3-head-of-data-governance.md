# Head of data governance - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you are a head of data governance in a UK government
organisation. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out
what you do now, where the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this role as working with the chief data officer across the
organisation and government, providing strategic oversight and leadership in all
aspects of data governance and use, and ensuring high standards are maintained in
line with regulations and ethical requirements. It expects you to provide strategic
oversight with the chief data officer; set and monitor adherence to policies,
frameworks and standards; be **accountable** for understanding and managing
organisational data risks and issues, co-ordinating with data owners to accept or
resolve them; act as a senior advisor across government on best practice in data
security development, in accordance with the data enterprise architecture; maintain
long-term senior relationships with decision-makers and influencers; advocate
improved data risk management; and encourage better data literacy and understanding
of governance.

**Accountable**, not responsible. At the level below the wording was *responsible for
understanding and managing* the risks; here it is *accountable*. That single word
change is the job.

Head of data governance is the top of this role. Thirteen skills. Expect about 40
minutes.

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not the remit on your job
  description. Being accountable for something is not the same as having the
  capability or the authority to deliver it, and the gap between those is what this
  form is for.
- **A 4 is a claim about other people** — that someone else's practice changed
  because of you and would stay changed if you left.

## About this role level

**The role: data governance manager.** You plan, monitor and enforce the management
of data assets. In this role you will:

- catalogue, monitor and identify data assets essential to the organisation's
  operations or services
- assign ownership of data, ensuring there is a clear responsible, accountable,
  consulted and informed matrix in the organisation
- incorporate governance of data into routine processes
- support or manage risk processes around data
- advocate for better use and management of data within the organisation
- encourage better data literacy within the organisation

**Your level: head of data governance.** You work with the chief data officer across
the organisation and government, providing strategic oversight and leadership in all
aspects of data governance and use, ensuring high standards are maintained in line
with regulations and ethical requirements. The framework expects that you:

- work with the chief data officer to provide strategic oversight of data governance
  throughout the organisation
- set and monitor adherence to data governance policies, frameworks and standards
- be accountable for understanding and managing organisational data risks and
  issues, co-ordinating with data owners to accept or resolve them
- act as a senior advisor across government on best practice in data security
  development, in accordance with the data enterprise architecture
- maintain effective long-term relationships at a senior level with important
  decision-makers and influencers
- advocate improved data risk management and practices within the organisation
- encourage better data literacy and understanding of data governance

**There is no level above this one.** This role has three levels and this is the
highest. Where people go next is a question about other roles — chief data officer,
information leadership, or leadership outside the profession — and the framework does
not define it as a next step, so this form does not invent one.

**Where this level sits:**

1. Data governance manager
2. Lead data governance manager
3. **Head of data governance** — you are here

**One thing worth knowing about this role.** *Enabling and informing risk-based
decisions* at your level says you should be **trusted by senior risk owners as an
expert in security**. That is the only place in this role where security expertise is
named, and it arrives without any security skill appearing at the levels below. If
you came up through governance rather than security, that is a real gap the framework
creates rather than one you brought.

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What the framework expects.** Three things: mediate between people and strengthen
relationships, adopting the appropriate communication method with stakeholders at all
levels; manage stakeholder expectations and moderate difficult discussions about
high-risk and complex topics, even within constrained timescales; and speak on behalf
of, and represent the community to, large audiences inside and outside the
organisation.

**What that looks like here.** The constrained-timescale discussion in governance is
usually an incident. Being accurate under pressure, and not over-claiming what is
known in the first hours, is what preserves credibility for the weeks afterwards.

### Skill: Data life cycle

**What the framework expects.** Two things: take responsibility for data governance
structures and principles, and ensure they are defined and applied with best practice
throughout a data life cycle; and direct continuous improvements to optimise
governance over the life cycle.

**What that looks like here.** Deletion and archiving remain the weakest phases in
most organisations, and they are the ones that produce both legal exposure and
storage cost. Being the person who makes deletion happen is unglamorous and valuable.
