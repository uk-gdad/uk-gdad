# Lead data governance manager - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a lead data governance manager in the UK
Government Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it
so that you can set out what you do now, where the gaps are, and what would close
them.

The framework describes this level as undertaking complex work related to the
principles and practices of data governance, and co-ordinating with data owners to
lead wider data management competencies. It expects you to lead the formation,
implementation and management of data governance frameworks, policies and standards;
implement and establish wider data management competencies — data quality assurance,
metadata management, master data management, data modelling, and the development of
data standards and data strategy; and be **responsible for understanding and managing
the organisational data risks and issues, and co-ordinating with data owners to
accept or resolve them**.

That last one is the weight of the job. Somebody has to know what the organisation's
data risks actually are, and get a named person to either fix each one or say out
loud that they accept it. Most of the difficulty is in the second half.

Thirteen skills at this level. Nobody is strong across all of them. Expect about 40
minutes.

## About this role level

**The role: data governance manager.** You plan, monitor and enforce the management
of data assets. In this role you will:

- catalogue, monitor and identify data assets essential to the organisation's
  operations or services
- assign ownership of data, ensuring there is a clear responsible, accountable,
  consulted and informed matrix in the organisation
- incorporate governance of data into routine processes
- support or manage risk processes around data
- advocate for better use and management of data within the organisation
- encourage better data literacy within the organisation

**Your level: lead data governance manager.** You undertake complex work related to
the principles and practices of data governance, and co-ordinate with data owners to
lead wider data management competencies. The framework expects that you:

- lead the formation, implementation and management of data governance frameworks,
  policies and standards
- implement and establish wider data management competencies within the
  organisation
- be responsible for understanding and managing the organisational data risks and
  issues, and co-ordinating with data owners to accept or resolve them

**Where this level sits.** This role has three levels:

1. Data governance manager
2. **Lead data governance manager** — you are here
3. Head of data governance

Your next step is head of data governance: working with the chief data officer to
provide strategic oversight across the organisation and government, setting policy
and monitoring adherence, being accountable for organisational data risk, acting as a
senior advisor across government, and maintaining long-term senior relationships.

**One thing worth knowing about this role.** Your level is the only one where the
framework names a concrete external requirement: *meet the requirement for relevant
data governance accreditation*. No other level in this role says anything like it. If
you do not hold one and nobody has raised it, that is a reasonable thing to put on
the table — including who would pay.

**The thirteen skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Communicating analysis and insight

## Job skills

Thirteen skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the level expects,

### Skill: Communicating analysis and insight

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: turn complex data into
compelling, clear and actionable stories; share data communication skills with the
team and across government; present analysis and visualisations to communicate
complex messages; **communicate negative and positive information to stakeholders**;
and give tactical and strategic recommendations.

**What that looks like here.** Communicating negative information is written in
explicitly, and in a governance role it is most of the output. Delivering it early
and plainly — before it is discovered rather than after — is what makes you credible.

**The next step.** At head you communicate relevant, compelling stories using the
most appropriate medium, and make complex recommendations at tactical and strategic
level.

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: listen to and interpret
the needs of technical and non-technical stakeholders, and manage their
expectations; manage active and reactive communication; and support or host
difficult discussions within the team or with diverse senior stakeholders.

**What that looks like here.** Reactive communication in governance means a breach,
an audit finding, or a story about to break. Being the calm and accurate source in
that moment matters more than any framework document.

**The next step.** At head you mediate at all levels, moderate difficult discussions
about high-risk topics under time pressure, and represent the community to large
audiences inside and outside the organisation.

### Skill: Data ethics and privacy

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: work with stakeholders to
identify and address ethical and privacy concerns; demonstrate and communicate how
data ethical issues fit into the wider organisational context; research developments
in data ethics and privacy to improve compliance and processes; and assess and
constructively challenge proposed data ethics policies.

**What that looks like here.** This is a steep rise from the level below, which is
worded as applying principles under supervision. You now challenge policy rather than
follow it, which requires you to have a view worth defending.

**The next step.** At head you oversee compliance with ethics standards and
legislation across the organisation, develop and improve an ethical framework,
develop a data ethics culture, and ensure ethics is applied in formulating and
evaluating policies and programmes.

### Skill: Data governance

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: implement and monitor
data governance using standard methodology throughout the data life cycle, within a
large organisation; **meet the requirement for relevant data governance
accreditation**; and take responsibility for specific parts of a data governance
framework within the organisation.

**What that looks like here.** The accreditation requirement is unique to your level
in this role. Beyond that, *monitor* is the operative word — governance that is
written and not monitored is a description of intentions.

**The next step.** At head you define the governance strategy for the organisation
and connect it to wider strategy, design and monitor governance across the life
cycle, set the strategy for end-to-end frameworks and assurance, and work with
technical leaders and senior stakeholders to ensure compliance.

### Skill: Data innovation

**What the framework expects at this level.** One thing: identify areas of

doing. The useful judgement is when to write policy for something new and when
waiting is wiser than a policy that will be wrong.

**The next step.** At head you investigate emerging trends, perform horizon scanning
for the organisation, and introduce innovative ways of working.

### Skill: Enabling and informing risk-based decisions

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: work with higher impact or
more complex risks, advising on the impact and whether it is within risk tolerance;
and apply different risk methodologies in proportion to the risk.

**What that looks like here.** You are responsible for the organisation's data risks
being understood and either accepted or resolved. The difficult part is getting a
named person to say "I accept this" in writing — which is precisely why it matters.

**The next step.** At head you act as a point of escalation, are trusted by senior
risk owners as an expert in security, and apply risk methodologies at the most
complex levels of risk.

### Skill: Stakeholder relationship management

influence stakeholders and manage relationships effectively; and build long-term
strategic relationships, communicating clearly and regularly.

**What that looks like here.** Data owners across an organisation did not choose the
role and mostly have other priorities. Long-term relationships are what turn an
annual chase into an ongoing conversation.

**The next step.** At head you direct the stakeholder relationship strategy for your
