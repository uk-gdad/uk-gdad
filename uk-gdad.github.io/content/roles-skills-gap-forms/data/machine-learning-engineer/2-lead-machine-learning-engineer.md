# Lead machine learning engineer - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a lead machine learning engineer in the UK
Government Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it
so that you can set out what you do now, where the gaps are, and what would close
them.

The framework describes this level as leading the technical development and
deployment of machine learning models. It expects you to lead the most complex
technical work; co-ordinate moving a model from research and development into
production; define ways of working across the machine learning life cycle; identify
training needs for machine learning engineers and related roles; help your team work
with other teams and disciplines; assure the effectiveness of models in use across the
organisation; and **define and communicate software standards and guidelines related
to ethics, risk and security**.

That last accountability is unusual and worth dwelling on. In most engineering roles
ethics arrives as a review someone else runs. Here, writing the standard is your job.

Lead machine learning engineer is the top of this role. Six skills. Expect about 30
minutes.

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not the remit on your job
  description.
- **A 4 is a claim about other people** — that someone else's practice changed

## About this role level

**The role: machine learning engineer.** A machine learning engineer develops,
assures and maintains machine learning models so they can be used in products and
services. In this role you will:

- be responsible for the software development and technical infrastructure needed to
  design, train, deploy and scale machine learning models
- provide and maintain effective, secure and sustainable models for use in products
  and services
- support all stages of the machine learning life cycle
- help product teams evaluate and choose appropriate machine learning solutions

**Your level: lead machine learning engineer.** You lead the technical development
and deployment of machine learning models. The framework expects that you:

- lead the most complex technical work needed to develop models for use in products
  and services
- co-ordinate moving a model from the research and development stage to production
- define ways of working across the machine learning life cycle
- identify training needs for machine learning engineers and related roles
- help your team work with other teams and disciplines
- assure the effectiveness of machine learning models in use across the organisation
- define and communicate software standards and guidelines related to ethics, risk
  and security

**There is no level above this one.** This role has two levels only — senior and
lead — and this is the higher. The framework publishes no junior level either, which
means the whole role occupies two rungs. Where people go next is a question about
other roles, and the framework does not define it, so this form does not invent one.

**Where this level sits:**

1. Senior machine learning engineer
2. **Lead machine learning engineer** — you are here

**One thing worth knowing about this role.** Two of your six skills — applied maths,
statistics and scientific practices, and data ethics and privacy — are worded
**identically** at senior. A third of the published skills do not distinguish the two
levels, which means your accountabilities carry most of the difference. That is worth
knowing before a conversation about grading or scope.

**The six skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Applied maths, statistics and scientific practices
2. Communicating between the technical and non-technical
3. Data ethics and privacy
4. Data science innovation
5. Programming and build (software engineering)
6. Systems integration

### Skill: Applied maths, statistics and scientific practices

**What the framework expects.** Four things: apply designated quantitative
techniques such as time series analysis, optimisation and simulation to create and
embed appropriate models for analysis and prediction; provide guidance on matching
data sources with relevant applied mathematics and statistical techniques to meet
analysis goals; apply appropriate statistical techniques to available data to
discover new relations and offer insight into research problems, helping improve
organisational processes and support decision making; and access and use the
statistical tools available within the organisation.

**Same wording as senior machine learning engineer.**

**What that looks like here.** At lead the useful contribution is often saying that a
proposed approach is more complicated than the problem needs — and having the
standing for that to settle the question rather than start an argument.

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What the framework expects.** Three things: mediate between people and strengthen
relationships, adopting the appropriate communication method with stakeholders at all
levels; manage stakeholder expectations and moderate difficult discussions about
high-risk and complex topics, even within constrained timescales; and speak on behalf
of, and represent the community to, large audiences inside and outside the
organisation.

**What that looks like here.** Machine learning attracts both enthusiasm and alarm,
often from the same room. Managing a discussion where one person wants to deploy
something tomorrow and another wants to ban it takes more patience than technique.

### Skill: Data science innovation

**What the framework expects.** Five things: be a leader in the data science space;
demonstrate in-depth knowledge of data science tools and techniques, used to solve
problems creatively and create opportunities for your team; act as a coach, inspiring
curiosity and creativity in others; demonstrate in-depth knowledge of your chosen
profession and keep up to date with changes in the industry; and challenge the status
quo and always look for ways to improve data science.

**What that looks like here.** Keeping current in this field is genuinely hard at
lead level, because the job pulls you into co-ordination while the techniques move
underneath you. Protecting the time is a decision, not something that happens.

### Skill: Programming and build (software engineering)

**What the framework expects.** Three things: advise on the right way to apply
standards and methods to ensure compliance; maintain technical responsibility for all
the stages and iterations of a software development project; and provide technical
advice to stakeholders and set the team-based standards for programming tools and
techniques.

**What that looks like here.** *Technical responsibility for all stages* means you
are answerable for engineering quality across the life cycle, not just for the
modelling. Machine learning teams that lack this end up with excellent models and no
way to deploy them safely.

### Skill: Systems integration

**What the framework expects.** Two things: establish standards and procedures across
a service product life cycle, including the development product life cycle, and
ensure that practitioners adhere to these; and manage resources to ensure that the
systems integration function works effectively.

**What that looks like here.** *Manage resources* assumes you have some. If
integration work is done by borrowing time from teams with other priorities, that is
worth saying, because it is where model deployments quietly stall.
