# Trainee data scientist - skills gap form

## Introduction

Hello, and welcome. This form is for you if you are a trainee data scientist in the
UK Government Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent
it so that you can say what you do now, what you would like to get better at, and
what would help.

The framework describes a trainee data scientist as inquisitive, curious about data,
and keen to learn and develop. That is the whole level description — three qualities,
none of them a technique. It expects you to have a basic understanding of software
development and analytical methods; interpret statistical outputs effectively; be
aware of how data science techniques such as machine learning can be used; work in a
multidisciplinary team; prepare and manipulate data; present effectively; be aware of
ethical considerations; and be aware of the technologies used.

**Aware** and **basic** appear repeatedly, and that is deliberate. Nobody expects you
to have depth yet. What they expect is curiosity and the willingness to ask.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. There are no right answers. Eight skills,
each with a plain explanation, a rating and a couple of questions. Take about 25
minutes, and stop and come back if you want to.

## Advisory

This skills gap form is created by AI and is work in progress for educational
purposes. If you intend to use it in any way, please consult with your
organization's appropriate teammates, such as with human resources staff, or people
and organizational development staff, or legal compliance staff.

## How to fill in this form

This is a web page you can read, not one you can type into. Nothing you write here
would be saved or sent — the page has no way to do that.

So: print it, or copy the questions into a document, or paste them into whatever your
organisation uses for development conversations. Every question is numbered, so your
answers make sense on their own.

A few things worth knowing before you start:

- **You can skip anything.** Leaving a question blank does not count against you.
- **Answer about what you actually do**, not what you are supposed to be learning.
- **"I have not done this yet" is the right answer at a trainee level** for a lot
  of these, and far more useful than stretching a small example.
- **Do not paste real data** into your answers — no records, no identifiers,
  nothing that would identify a person. Describe the work instead.
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who will see your answers, what they
  will be used for, or how long they will be kept, it is completely reasonable to
  ask before you fill it in.

## Rating scale

Some questions ask you to rate yourself. Use this scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | You have not worked with this |
| 1 | Awareness | You know what it is and why it matters |
| 2 | Working | You do it with support |
| 3 | Practitioner | You do it unsupported, and handle the usual exceptions |
| 4 | Expert | You set the approach and develop others in it |

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not what you covered on a course or
  in a degree.
- **0s and 1s are completely normal here.** Your level's own wording uses *show an
  understanding*, *show an awareness*, *explain*, and *under supervision*. A 1 is
  often exactly the published expectation.

Here is what a filled-in answer looks like:

> **Data engineering**
>
> - [x] 1 — Awareness: I know what it is and why it matters
>
> *Evidence:* I can explain why data has to be cleaned before it is useful, because
> I spent three weeks on it. I could not tell you the difference between our cloud
> setup and an on-premise one.

That is a good answer. It says what is known, why, and exactly where it stops.

## About this role level

**The role: data scientist.** Data science is a broad and fast-moving field spanning
maths, statistics, software engineering and communications. Data scientists often work
as part of a multidisciplinary team, using data and analytics to inform and achieve
organisational goals. In this role you will:

- be inquisitive
- explore and visualise data
- make recommendations to address complex problems and inform strategic and
  operational decision making
- use data ethically and appropriately
- be innovative and adaptable
- explore existing and new data using a range of statistical tools and techniques,
  such as machine learning and predictive analytics
- find patterns in data and transform them into organisational insight

**Your level: trainee data scientist.** You are inquisitive, curious about data, and
keen to learn and develop. The framework expects that you:

- have a basic understanding of software development and analytical methods
- interpret statistical outputs effectively
- be aware of how data science techniques can be used, and have a basic knowledge of
  key techniques such as machine learning
- work as part of a multidisciplinary team to develop data science solutions and
  outputs
- prepare and manipulate data
- present effectively
- be aware of ethical considerations
- be aware of the technologies used

**Where this level sits.** This role has six levels — the longest ladder in the data
profession:

1. **Trainee data scientist** — you are here
2. Associate data scientist
3. Data scientist
4. Senior data scientist
5. Lead data scientist
6. Principal data scientist

Your next step is associate data scientist: developing basic models yourself,
implementing a range of techniques including machine learning, and writing and testing
scripts rather than simple ones under supervision.

**One thing worth knowing about this role.** *Developing data science capability* —
one of your eight skills — asks you to create a continuing professional development
plan with support from your manager. That is unusually specific, and it means having
a plan is itself part of the job. If you do not have one, that is a concrete thing to
ask for rather than a shortfall.

**The eight skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Applied maths, statistics and scientific practices
2. Data engineering
3. Data ethics and privacy
4. Data science innovation
5. Delivering business impact
6. Developing data science capability
7. Programming and build (data science)
8. Understanding product delivery

### Skill: Applied maths, statistics and scientific practices

**What the framework expects at your level.** Four things: show an understanding of
the benefits of applied mathematics and statistics and use that knowledge to carry out
data science tasks; carry out general analysis techniques for data inspection,
exploration and visualisation; interpret statistical output effectively and
accurately; and show an awareness of different performance and accuracy metrics for
statistical assessment and validation.

**What that means day to day.** Looking at data before doing anything clever with it,
and reading a statistical output without over-reading it. *Significant* is the trap —
it means something specific in statistics and something different in ordinary speech,
and confusing them is how confident wrong conclusions get published.

**What changes at associate.** You would apply analytical methods including
exploratory analysis and statistical testing to reach reliable conclusions, use
different metrics for model validation, and compare methods and explain their
differences.

### Skill: Data engineering

**What the framework expects at your level.** Three things: explain why you need
engineering support to design and provide products for the organisation; explain why
you need to cleanse and prepare data for use in data science products; and explain
different architectures, including cloud and on-premise, and data manipulation and
transformation tools.

**What that means day to day.** Knowing where the data comes from and who keeps it
flowing. Most people are surprised by how much of data science is preparing data
rather than modelling it — often most of the time on a project.

**What changes at associate.** You would identify the engineering support needed, put
reusable cleansing processes and checks in place, and use different architectures and
transformation tools yourself.

### Skill: Data ethics and privacy

**What the framework expects at your level.** Two things: explain the importance of
using data ethics and privacy in your work; and identify appropriate channels to
discuss ethical issues, with support.

**What that means day to day.** Knowing that being able to do something with data
does not make it appropriate, and knowing who to ask. Nobody expects you to resolve an
ethical question at this level — but you should know where it goes.

**What changes at associate.** You would apply the fundamental principles under
supervision and raise ethics and privacy risks through the proper channels yourself.

### Skill: Data science innovation

**What the framework expects at your level.** Three things: adopt an inquisitive and
curious approach to data; be receptive to learning about data science and its
techniques; and be unafraid to ask questions and discover what there is to learn.

**What that looks like day to day.** This is the only skill in the framework worded
almost entirely as an attitude. *Unafraid to ask questions* is the one that matters —
in a fast-moving field, the person who asks is the one who learns, and everyone
senior has gaps they are quietly working around too.

**What changes at associate.** You would seek out and research new techniques, ask
questions to learn about data science norms, and see possibilities for improvement.

### Skill: Delivering business impact

**What the framework expects at your level.** Four things: show an understanding of
the organisation and the benefits of data science; develop data science products as
part of a team; create basic visuals and presentations to communicate data science
effectively; and show knowledge of data and analysis and discuss how to align them to
meet user needs.

**What that means day to day.** Knowing why anyone wanted the work. A chart that
answers the actual question beats a technically better one that answers a question
nobody asked.

**What changes at associate.** You would collaborate to identify user needs and
develop and deliver products, and tailor your presentation to your audience.

### Skill: Developing data science capability

**What the framework expects at your level.** Three things: show an awareness of
various sources of training and self-directed learning for data science; **create a
continuing professional development plan with support from your manager**; and
demonstrate a basic understanding of key techniques such as machine learning and how
they can be used.

**What that means day to day.** Having a plan for what you are learning and why,
agreed with your manager, rather than picking things up as they arrive. It is the
most concrete expectation at your level and the easiest to skip.

**What changes at associate.** You would manage your own plan and link learning to
objectives and organisational goals, talk confidently about the benefits of data
science, and use key techniques to build solutions.

### Skill: Programming and build (data science)

**What the framework expects at your level.** One thing: show a basic understanding
of software development principles and write simple scripts under supervision.

**What that means day to day.** Usually Python or R, and SQL. The habits matter more
than the volume at this stage — putting work in version control, giving things
sensible names, and writing code someone else could follow next month.

**What changes at associate.** You would write and test scripts and create basic
models in one or more languages, and collaborate on shared codebases using a variety
of methodologies.

### Skill: Understanding product delivery

**What the framework expects at your level.** One thing: manage your contribution to
tasks to fit in with the work of your wider team.

**What that means day to day.** Knowing when your piece is needed, saying early if it
will not be ready, and understanding that other people's work depends on yours. This
is the shortest expectation in the whole role, and it is about reliability rather than
knowledge.

**What changes at associate.** You would show awareness of the differences between
delivery methods such as Agile and waterfall, and propose the most appropriate one.
