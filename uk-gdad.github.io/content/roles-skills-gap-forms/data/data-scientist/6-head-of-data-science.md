# Head of data science - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you are a head of data science in a UK government organisation.
Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out what you do now,
where the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this role as having complete oversight of data science within
your organisation. It expects you to set direction; build capability; oversee
resourcing, budgeting, professionalism and outputs and products; support and enable
future developments; understand and use a wide range of techniques, tools and
technologies; **lead on ethics**; communicate and present data science and data ethics
effectively to ministers and senior leaders; champion the role of data science in
supporting organisational priorities and collaborative working across professions; and
represent the department on data science matters.

*Communicate to ministers* appears in almost no other role in this framework. It sets
the standard for everything else here: whatever your team produces has to survive being
explained, briefly, to someone who will be held publicly accountable for acting on it.

Head of data science is the top of this role. Eight skills. Expect about 30 minutes.

## How to fill in this form

This is a static web page. There is nothing to type into and nothing to send from here —
your answers never touch this site.

Print it, copy the questions into a document, or paste them into whatever your
organisation uses for development conversations. Every question is numbered, so answers
travel back on their own.

Worth knowing:

- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a finding.
- **"The organisation does this" is not an answer to "what do you do".** Name your
  part.
- **"I haven't had the chance" is a real answer** at this level too.
- **Do not paste real data.** Describe the work, not the contents.
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers, what they are
  used for, or how long they are kept, ask before you fill it in.

## Rating scale

Where a question asks for a rating, use this scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | You have not worked with this |
| 1 | Awareness | You know what it is and why it matters |
| 2 | Working | You do it with support |
| 3 | Practitioner | You do it unsupported, and handle the usual exceptions |
| 4 | Expert | You set the approach and develop others in it |

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not the remit on your job description.
- **A 4 is a claim about other people** — that someone else's practice changed because
  of you and would stay changed if you left. The honest test here is whether data
  science practice in your organisation would survive your departure.

A filled-in answer looks like this:

> **Programming and build (data science)**
>
> - [x] 1 — Awareness: I know what it is and why it matters
>
> *Evidence:* I have not written production code in three years. I can read what my
> teams write and ask sensible questions, and I rely entirely on my leads for
> standards.

Named practice, and a clear line about where it stops. That is a perfectly reasonable
answer at this level, and the note below on the published wording explains why.

## About this role level

**The role: data scientist.** Data science is a broad and fast-moving field spanning
maths, statistics, software engineering and communications. Data scientists often work
as part of a multidisciplinary team, using data and analytics to inform and achieve
organisational goals. In this role you will:

- be inquisitive
- explore and visualise data
- make recommendations to address complex problems and inform strategic and
  operational decision making
- use data ethically and appropriately
- be innovative and adaptable
- explore existing and new data using a range of statistical tools and techniques,
  such as machine learning and predictive analytics
- find patterns in data and transform them into organisational insight

**Your level: head of data science.** You have complete oversight of data science within
your organisation. The framework expects that you:

- set direction
- build capability
- oversee resourcing, budgeting, professionalism, and outputs and products
- support and enable future developments
- understand and use a wide range of data science techniques, tools and technologies
- lead on ethics
- communicate and present data science and data ethics effectively to ministers and
  senior leaders
- champion the role of data science in supporting organisational priorities, and in
  collaborative working across professions
- represent the department on data science matters

**There is no level above this one.** This role has six levels and this is the highest.
Where people go next is a question about other roles — chief data officer, wider digital
leadership, or leadership outside the profession — and the framework does not define it
as a next step, so this form does not invent one.

**Where this level sits:**

1. Trainee data scientist
2. Associate data scientist
3. Data scientist
4. Principal data scientist
5. Lead data scientist
6. **Head of data science** — you are here

**One thing worth knowing about this role — and it is a substantial one.** Four of your
eight skills are published with **less demanding wording than at lead or principal
data scientist**:

- *Programming and build (data science)* is worded exactly as it is for an **associate
  data scientist** — writing and testing scripts and creating basic models. Lead and
  principal are worded as writing complex programs and setting coding standards.
- *Data science innovation* returns to the **data scientist** wording — practical
  knowledge — while lead and principal are described as leaders in the data science
  space.
- *Data engineering* returns to a single line, while lead and principal have three
  detailed items.
- *Understanding product delivery* returns to the **data scientist** wording, while
  lead leads a team through the delivery life cycle and has oversight of data science
  features across products.

That is how the framework publishes it, recorded here rather than adjusted. It has two
practical consequences. If you rate yourself low on programming, you are still meeting
the published expectation. And if you are in fact setting technical direction across
your organisation — which your accountabilities imply — you are working well beyond
these four skill descriptions, which is worth stating rather than assuming it is
understood.

**The eight skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Applied maths, statistics and scientific practices
2. Data engineering
3. Data ethics and privacy
4. Data science innovation
5. Delivering business impact
6. Developing data science capability
7. Programming and build (data science)
8. Understanding product delivery

## Job skills

Eight skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the framework expects and
what it looks like in practice. There is no level above, so these say what the skill

### Skill: Applied maths, statistics and scientific practices

**What the framework expects.** Five things: identify opportunities to develop
statistical insight, reports and models to support organisational objectives,
collaborating across the organisation; critique statistical analyses; use a variety of
analytics techniques such as data mining and prescriptive and predictive analytics for
complex analysis across the whole data life cycle; use model outputs to produce evidence
and help design services and policies; and understand a broad range of statistical
tools, particularly those deployed in the organisation, and help others use them.

**Same wording at principal and lead.** The top three levels share this expectation.

**What that looks like here.** You are the last line of defence on whether an analysis
is sound enough to put in front of a minister. That does not require you to redo the
work; it requires you to know which questions expose a weak result.

### Skill: Understanding product delivery

**What the framework expects.** Three things: understand the differences between
delivery methods, such as Agile and waterfall, and choose the most appropriate method for
each product; define the minimum viable product and support decisions about priorities;
and work with specialists in multidisciplinary teams to smoothly deliver data science
products into the organisation.

**A note on the wording.** This is the **data scientist** wording — less than at lead,
where you would lead a team through the delivery life cycle and have oversight of data
science features across products and services.

**What that looks like here.** Complete oversight of data science is in your level
description, which sits oddly against a delivery skill worded three levels down. In
practice the question is whether you know about every data science feature in your
