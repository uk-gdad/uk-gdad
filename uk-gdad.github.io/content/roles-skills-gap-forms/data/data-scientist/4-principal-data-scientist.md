# Principal data scientist - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a principal data scientist in the UK Government
Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you
can set out what you do now, where the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes a principal data scientist as a leader of data science, quite
often with responsibility for managing and developing teams. It expects you to have
broad knowledge of data science techniques, use cases and potential impact as well as
the tools and technologies; have extensive experience in scoping, designing and
delivering data science outputs and products; work collaboratively with a range of
experts in support of organisational objectives; communicate effectively and
**challenge delivery plans and priorities**; appreciate and understand data ethics,
data preparation and manipulation; and appreciate and understand delivery methods and
how to deliver supported solutions at scale.

*Challenge delivery plans and priorities* is the phrase that marks this level. You are
expected to say when a plan is wrong, not only to execute it well.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Eight skills, each with an explanation, a
rating and some questions. Expect about 30 minutes.

## About this role level

**The role: data scientist.** Data science is a broad and fast-moving field spanning
maths, statistics, software engineering and communications. Data scientists often work
as part of a multidisciplinary team, using data and analytics to inform and achieve
organisational goals. In this role you will:

- be inquisitive
- explore and visualise data
- make recommendations to address complex problems and inform strategic and
  operational decision making
- use data ethically and appropriately
- be innovative and adaptable
- explore existing and new data using a range of statistical tools and techniques,
  such as machine learning and predictive analytics
- find patterns in data and transform them into organisational insight

**Your level: principal data scientist.** You are a leader of data science, quite often
with responsibility for managing and developing teams. The framework expects that you:

- have a broad knowledge of data science techniques, use cases and potential impact,
  as well as the tools and technologies
- have extensive experience in scoping, designing and delivering data science outputs
  and products
- work collaboratively with a range of experts in support of organisational objectives
- communicate effectively and challenge delivery plans and priorities
- appreciate and understand data ethics, data preparation and manipulation
- appreciate and understand delivery methods, and how to deliver supported solutions
  at scale

**Where this level sits.** This role has six levels, published in this order:

1. Trainee data scientist
2. Associate data scientist
3. Data scientist
4. **Principal data scientist** — you are here
5. Lead data scientist
6. Head of data science

**One thing worth knowing about this role.** In most roles in this framework, *lead*
comes below *principal*. In this one the published order is the other way round: lead
data scientist sits **above** principal. That is how the framework publishes it, and it
matters when you compare job adverts or grades across professions, because the same
two words mean the opposite here.

The practical difference is real, not just naming. At lead, *data ethics, and privacy*
rises to overseeing compliance across the organisation and developing an ethical
framework — yours is worded as working with stakeholders on concerns. *Delivering
business impact* rises to championing data science and taking responsibility for
scalable products. *Developing data science capability* rises to setting the direction
of professional development. And *understanding product delivery* rises to leading a
team through the delivery life cycle. Those four are the published gap between you and
lead.

**Two of your skills do not distinguish you from a data scientist** three levels down:
data ethics and privacy, and developing data science capability are worded identically
at level 3 and at yours.

**The eight skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Applied maths, statistics and scientific practices
2. Data engineering
3. Data ethics and privacy
4. Data science innovation
5. Delivering business impact
6. Developing data science capability

## Job skills

Eight skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the level expects, what it
looks like in practice, and what changes at lead data scientist.

### Skill: Applied maths, statistics and scientific practices

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: identify opportunities to
develop statistical insight, reports and models to support organisational objectives,
collaborating across the organisation effectively; **critique statistical analyses**;
use a variety of data analytics techniques such as data mining and prescriptive and
predictive analytics for complex analysis through the whole data life cycle; use model
outputs to produce evidence and help design services and policies; and understand a
broad range of statistical tools, particularly those deployed in the organisation, and
help others use them.

**What that looks like here.** *Critique statistical analyses* is the item that makes
this a principal-level skill. You are the person who can say why a result will not hold,
in enough detail that the author agrees rather than resents it.

**The next step.** Identical wording at lead data scientist **and** head of data
science. This is the top published expectation for the skill in this role.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: work with stakeholders to

standards and legislation across the organisation, developing and improving an ethical
framework, developing an ethics culture, and ensuring ethics is applied when

### Skill: Delivering business impact

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: lead and support your
organisational area by using data science to create change; identify opportunities to
develop products supporting organisational objectives, collaborating across the
organisation; show an understanding of the role of user research and design and manage
processes to gather and establish user needs; communicate relevant and compelling
stories and present analysis and visualisations clearly to get across complex messages;
and work with colleagues to implement scalable products and understand maintenance
requirements.

**What that looks like here.** *Understand maintenance requirements* is the honest
item. A data science product that nobody has agreed to maintain will decay, and
noticing that before launch is a principal's job.

**The next step.** At lead you champion the role of data science, champion user
research, create opportunities, and take responsibility for delivering scalable products
and establishing maintenance support — responsibility rather than understanding.

### Skill: Developing data science capability

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: manage your continuing
professional development and link learning to objectives and organisational goals;
support data science capability building across the team and wider organisation;
confidently talk about the benefits of data science approaches to existing and potential
customers; and demonstrate a good understanding of a range of techniques such as
machine learning and natural language processing, using them to build solutions.

**Same wording as data scientist**, three levels below.

**What that looks like here.** In practice principals usually shape how their team
develops. If you do, that is beyond the published wording and worth recording.

detailed guidance, **set the direction of professional development within your team**,
keep up with developments and match them to opportunities, and have detailed knowledge

### Skill: Programming and build (data science)

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: write complex programs and
scripts; **seek to make code open source where appropriate**; supervise junior analysts
and set coding standards for your team; understand software architecture and how to
write efficient, optimised code; and perform user testing on products prior to launch.

**What that looks like here.** Open sourcing is named explicitly, and government has a
long-standing default of coding in the open where there is no good reason not to. If
nothing in your area has been published, that is worth asking about.

**The next step.** Identical wording at lead data scientist. At head of data science
the published wording drops back to writing and testing scripts and creating basic
models — the same text used for an associate.

### Skill: Understanding product delivery

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: understand the differences
between delivery methods, such as Agile and waterfall, and choose the most appropriate
method for each product; define the minimum viable product and support decisions about
priorities; and work with specialists in multidisciplinary teams to smoothly deliver
data science products into the organisation.

**Same wording as data scientist.**

**What that looks like here.** Your accountabilities go further than this skill —
delivering supported solutions **at scale** is in them and not in the skill. If you are
doing that, it is beyond the published wording.

**The next step.** At lead you set out how your team should use and adapt delivery
methods, lead a team through the delivery life cycle, collaborate with the product
manager to influence direction, ensure products are monitored and continually improved,
and have oversight of data science features in products and services.
