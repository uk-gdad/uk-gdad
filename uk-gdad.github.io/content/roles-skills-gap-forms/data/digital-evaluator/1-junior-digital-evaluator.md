# Junior digital evaluator - skills gap form

## Introduction

Hello, and welcome. This form is for you if you work as a junior digital evaluator in
the UK Government Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent
it so that you can say what you do now, what you would like to get better at, and what
would help.

The framework is direct about this level: a junior digital evaluator is an entry-level
role, with an understanding of the role and the potential to grow into it. It expects
you to support the work of more experienced digital evaluators, develop your skills
through training, and receive guidance on how to produce good work.

**Receiving guidance is written into the level.** That is not a soft expectation — it
means being given proper guidance is something you are entitled to, and if you are not
getting it, that is a finding about your organisation rather than about you.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. There are no right answers. Seven skills, each
with a plain explanation, a rating and a couple of questions. Take about 25 minutes.

## How to fill in this form

This is a web page you can read, not one you can type into. Nothing you write here would
be saved or sent — the page has no way to do that.

So: print it, or copy the questions into a document, or paste them into whatever your
organisation uses for development conversations. Every question is numbered, so your
answers make sense on their own.

A few things worth knowing before you start:

- **You can skip anything.** Leaving a question blank does not count against you.
- **Answer about what you actually do**, not what you are supposed to be learning.
- **"I have not done this yet" is the right answer at a junior level** for a lot of
  these, and much more useful than stretching a small example.
- **Do not paste real data or anything about identifiable people** into your answers.
  Describe the work instead.
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who will see your answers, what they
  will be used for, or how long they will be kept, it is completely reasonable to ask
  before you fill it in.

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not what you studied.
- **0s and 1s are completely normal here.** Five of your seven skills are worded as

## About this role level

**The role: digital evaluator.** A digital evaluator assesses the design,
implementation and outcomes of a digital product or service, and whether it delivers
value for the public and society. Some digital evaluators are also members of a
government analytical profession where evaluation is a core skill. In this role you
will:

- work closely with delivery and policy teams
- develop theories of change for products and services, explaining the links between
  activities, outputs, intended outcomes and unintended effects
- follow established monitoring and evaluation methods and government guidance
- produce evaluation plans
- design quantitative and qualitative research
- collect and analyse data
- communicate findings, including by publishing reports

**Your level: junior digital evaluator.** This is an entry-level role. You have an
understanding of the role and can show potential. The framework expects that you:

- support the work of more experienced digital evaluators
- develop your skills through training
- receive guidance from other digital evaluators on how to produce good work

**Where this level sits.** This role has five levels:

1. **Junior digital evaluator** — you are here
2. Digital evaluator
3. Senior digital evaluator
4. Lead digital evaluator
5. Head of digital evaluation

Your next step is digital evaluator: working with a more senior colleague to plan and
deliver evaluations, managing the day-to-day development of monitoring and analytical
tools, supporting research design, and communicating findings.

**One thing worth knowing about this role.** *Evaluation delivery* — the skill that is
most obviously the job — is worded **identically at your level and at digital
evaluator**, the level above. Both say "with support" three times. The published
expectation for actually delivering evaluation does not change when you are promoted;
what changes is everything around it, particularly planning, communication and quality
assurance. That is worth knowing when you think about what to develop.

**The seven skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Communicating analysis and insight
2. Data ethics and privacy
3. Evaluation delivery
4. Evaluation planning and strategy
5. Monitoring and evaluation across the product life cycle
6. Product and service monitoring
7. Quality assurance of data and analysis

### Skill: Communicating analysis and insight

**What the framework expects at your level.** Two things: explain why it is important
to clearly communicate findings from analysis; and explain some methods for effectively
communicating findings.

**What that means day to day.** Evaluation findings only matter if someone acts on
them, and evaluations are frequently ignored because they arrive late, long, or hedged
into uselessness. Noticing which reports get read is a useful habit to start now.

**What changes at digital evaluator.** You would choose the appropriate medium, shape
communications for the audience, present to specialist and non-specialist audiences
with support, and give tactical recommendations.

### Skill: Data ethics and privacy

**What the framework expects at your level.** Two things: explain the importance of
using data ethics and privacy in your work; and identify appropriate channels to
discuss ethical issues, with support.

**What that means day to day.** Evaluation often involves talking to people about
services they depend on, sometimes at difficult moments in their lives. Knowing that
consent, anonymity and careful handling are not paperwork but the basis of the work is
the point at this level.

**What changes at digital evaluator.** You would apply the fundamental principles under
supervision and raise ethics and privacy risks through the proper channels yourself.

### Skill: Evaluation delivery

**What the framework expects at your level.** Four things: evaluate interventions with
support, using techniques including process evaluation, impact evaluation, theory of
change, experimentation and economic evaluation; collect quantitative and qualitative
data with support; analyse and interpret data you have collected and data from other
sources with support; and use some analytical approaches, including descriptive and
inferential analysis.

**What that means day to day.** A quick guide to the techniques named: *process
evaluation* asks whether the thing was delivered as intended; *impact evaluation* asks
whether it made a difference; *economic evaluation* asks whether the difference was
worth the cost; *experimentation* is comparing what happened with what would have
happened otherwise. The government's Magenta Book is the standard guidance on all of
this and is worth reading a chapter at a time.

**What changes at digital evaluator.** Nothing — the wording is **identical** at that
level, "with support" included. This skill first changes at senior, where the support
qualifiers drop away.

### Skill: Evaluation planning and strategy

**What the framework expects at your level.** Three things: describe how you might work
with a stakeholder to understand what they need to evaluate and why; describe a process
for translating stakeholder needs into potential research questions and evaluation
approaches; and describe some methods for monitoring and evaluating a product or
service.

**What that means day to day.** All three are *describe* — the framework wants you to
understand the shape of planning before you do it. The hardest part in practice is that
stakeholders often want an evaluation to prove something rather than find something
out, and turning that into an answerable question is the craft.

**What changes at digital evaluator.** You would work with stakeholders directly,
translate their needs into research questions, present options, and influence decisions
on evaluation plans with support.

### Skill: Monitoring and evaluation across the product life cycle

**What the framework expects at your level.** One thing: describe how evaluation needs
and activities vary at different phases of the product life cycle.

**What that means day to day.** Digital services move through discovery, alpha, beta and
live. Evaluation looks different in each — in discovery you are helping define what
success would even mean; in live you are measuring whether it is being achieved. Knowing
which phase your team is in tells you what kind of evidence is useful.

**What changes at digital evaluator.** You would make sure the team is measuring
appropriately for the service standard phase, manage delivery of evaluation at different
phases, and plan for data requirements in future phases.

### Skill: Product and service monitoring

**What the framework expects at your level.** Four things: describe what a theory of
change is; identify some appropriate stakeholders to involve in developing one; describe
common issues when measuring performance indicators; and explain the difference between
analysis of digital performance and broader monitoring of a product, for example against
strategic objectives.

**What that means day to day.** A theory of change is a written account of how the
activities you do are supposed to lead to the outcomes you want, including the
assumptions in between. Writing one down often reveals that nobody agreed on the
outcome. That last item is important too: how many people completed a form is digital
performance; whether their problem got solved is the outcome.

**What changes at digital evaluator.** You would develop a theory of change with
stakeholders with support, present measurement options, turn indicators into measurement
frameworks, and support development of a performance dashboard.

### Skill: Quality assurance of data and analysis

**What the framework expects at your level.** Four things: describe some basic data
issues and how to check that data and analysis look right; explain the concept of data
being fit for purpose and the context of the data; describe some methods for preparing
data for analysis; and describe quality assurance processes.

**What that means day to day.** *Fit for purpose* is the key idea. Data collected for
running a service is often not suitable for evaluating it — a field completed only when
someone remembered, or a category that changed meaning in 2022. Spotting that is more
valuable than any technique.

**What changes at digital evaluator.** You would identify how to collect and prepare
data, decide whether data and analysis are accurate and fit for purpose, cleanse data
with limited guidance, undertake quality assurance, and peer-review colleagues' outputs.
