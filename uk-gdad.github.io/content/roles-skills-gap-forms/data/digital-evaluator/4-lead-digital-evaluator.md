# Lead digital evaluator - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a lead digital evaluator in the UK Government
Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can
set out what you do now, where the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this level as planning and leading evaluation activities in
larger teams and for more complex services, usually managing multiple evaluations and
possibly leading a team of digital evaluators. It expects you to influence stakeholders
to secure resources for evaluation; provide guidance on evaluation strategy for a group
of digital products and services; and ensure high evaluation standards for the wider
evaluation team are set and met.

Three accountabilities, and the first is about money. Evaluation is chronically
underfunded because its benefit arrives later than its cost, and securing resources for
it is a large part of what makes this job difficult.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Seven skills, each with an explanation, a
rating and some questions. Expect about 30 minutes.

This is a static web page. There is nothing to type into and nothing to send from here —
your answers never touch this site.

Print it, copy the questions into a document, or paste them into whatever your
organisation uses for development conversations. Every question is numbered, so answers

Worth knowing:

- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a finding.
- **"My team does this" is not an answer to "what do you do".** Name your part.
- **"I haven't had the chance" is a real answer** at this level too.
- **Do not paste real data or anything about identifiable people.**
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers, what they are
  used for, or how long they are kept, ask before you fill it in.

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not the remit on your job description.
- **A 4 is a claim about other people** — that someone else's practice changed because
  of you and would stay changed if you left. Coaching and setting standards are written
  into your level, so several are reasonable.

## About this role level

**The role: digital evaluator.** A digital evaluator assesses the design, implementation
and outcomes of a digital product or service, and whether it delivers value for the
public and society. Some are also members of a government analytical profession where
evaluation is a core skill. In this role you will:

- work closely with delivery and policy teams
- develop theories of change for products and services, explaining the links between
  activities, outputs, intended outcomes and unintended effects
- follow established monitoring and evaluation methods and government guidance
- produce evaluation plans
- design quantitative and qualitative research
- collect and analyse data
- communicate findings, including by publishing reports

**Your level: lead digital evaluator.** You plan and lead evaluation activities in
larger teams and for more complex services, usually managing multiple evaluations and
possibly leading a team of digital evaluators. The framework expects that you:

- influence stakeholders to secure resources for evaluation
- provide guidance on evaluation strategy for a group of digital products and services
- ensure high evaluation standards for the wider evaluation team are set and met

**Where this level sits.** This role has five levels:

1. Junior digital evaluator
2. Digital evaluator
3. Senior digital evaluator
4. **Lead digital evaluator** — you are here
5. Head of digital evaluation

Your next step is head of digital evaluation: leading the community, acting as the point
of contact with the rest of the organisation, setting the evaluation strategy, defining a
quality assurance framework, advocating across government, and managing capability
through business planning, development and recruitment.

**One thing worth knowing about this role.** **Five of your seven skills are worded
identically at head of digital evaluation** — communicating analysis and insight, data
ethics and privacy, evaluation planning and strategy, product and service monitoring,
and quality assurance of data and analysis. Only two change: evaluation delivery, which
becomes providing guidance and coaching rather than doing; and monitoring and evaluation
across the life cycle, which becomes leading and championing best practice.

The published difference between lead and head is therefore almost entirely in the
accountabilities — community leadership, strategy, business planning and recruitment —
rather than in the skills. Worth knowing before a promotion conversation.

**The seven skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Communicating analysis and insight
2. Data ethics and privacy
3. Evaluation delivery
4. Evaluation planning and strategy
5. Monitoring and evaluation across the product life cycle
6. Product and service monitoring
7. Quality assurance of data and analysis

## Job skills

Seven skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the level expects, what it
looks like in practice, and how it compares with head of digital evaluation.

### Skill: Communicating analysis and insight

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: communicate relevant,
compelling stories using the most appropriate medium; and make complex recommendations at
a tactical and strategic level.

**Same wording at head of digital evaluation.**

**What that looks like here.** Your audience is now the people who decide whether a
service continues. A recommendation that is strategically framed but evidentially thin
is worse than useless, because it will be quoted.

### Skill: Data ethics and privacy

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: oversee compliance with data
ethics standards and legislation across the organisation; develop, manage and
continuously improve an ethical framework within your area; develop a data ethics
culture, influencing others to act on it; and ensure data ethics is applied appropriately
when formulating, implementing and evaluating organisational policies and programmes.

**Same wording at head of digital evaluation.** This is a large jump from senior, where
the wording was working with stakeholders on concerns.

**What that looks like here.** Overseeing compliance *across the organisation* is a wide
remit for a lead, and in practice it usually overlaps with a data governance or ethics
function. Where that boundary sits is worth being explicit about.

### Skill: Evaluation delivery

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: evaluate digital services
using some appropriate techniques including process evaluation, impact evaluation, theory
of change, experimentation and economic evaluation; collect quantitative and qualitative
data; analyse and interpret data you have collected and data from other sources; and use
a wide range of analytical approaches, including descriptive and inferential analysis.

**Same wording as senior digital evaluator.**

**What that looks like here.** You are still expected to do the work, not only oversee
it — which keeps your judgement current but competes with everything else on your plate.

**The next step.** At head this changes to providing guidance on techniques, assuring the
work of evaluator teams, defining best practice and coaching people across the
organisation. That is the point where you stop delivering.

### Skill: Evaluation planning and strategy

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: influence stakeholders to
secure resources for an evaluation project; identify evaluation and evidence needs;
ensure plans are appropriate and proportionate; consider wider policy and practical
constraints that affect evaluation strategy; and agree the evaluation approach with
diverse stakeholders.

**Same wording as senior digital evaluator, and as head of digital evaluation is
different only in scope** — at head you influence the organisation's senior stakeholders
and coach teams on creating strategies.

**What that looks like here.** Securing resources is your first accountability and the
hardest part of it. The strongest argument is usually a past evaluation that saved money
or prevented something — which means keeping a record of those is itself strategic work.

### Skill: Monitoring and evaluation across the product life cycle

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: apply monitoring and
evaluation techniques in multiple parts of the product life cycle; **recommend when to
move forward and when to stop**; work with other Agile delivery disciplines throughout
the life cycle; and plan and proactively engage with stakeholders at the appropriate
stage of the project.

**What that looks like here.** *Recommend when to stop* is the most consequential thing
in this role, and the least often exercised. Services rarely stop, and an evaluator who
never recommends stopping is either lucky or not being listened to.

**The next step.** At head you lead and champion best practice and identify improvements
to monitoring and evaluation approaches, identify and raise evaluation or product risks
at any stage, and coach teams through phases.

### Skill: Product and service monitoring

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: get support from senior
stakeholders for the need to develop theories of change; provide guidance on how to
develop them; and define best practice and methodologies for measuring indicators,
including economic outcomes, and communicating limitations.

**Same wording at head of digital evaluation.**

**What that looks like here.** Getting senior support for theories of change means
persuading people that an afternoon spent agreeing what success means will save months.
It is an easy argument to make and a hard one to win.

### Skill: Quality assurance of data and analysis

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: set up tools and systems for
effectively assuring data and analysis across multiple teams; use appropriate approaches
for verifying and validating data and analysis; influence senior stakeholders over data
and analysis approaches; and coach and mentor others in assuring data and analysis.

**Same wording at head of digital evaluation.**

**What that looks like here.** Assurance across multiple teams only works if it is a
system rather than your attention. Assurance that depends on you reading everything does
not scale and does not survive your absence.
