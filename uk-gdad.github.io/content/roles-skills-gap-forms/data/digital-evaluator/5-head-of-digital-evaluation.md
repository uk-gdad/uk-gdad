# Head of digital evaluation - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you are a head of digital evaluation in a UK government
organisation. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out what
you do now, where the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this role as leading the digital evaluation community and acting
as the point of contact between the community and the rest of the organisation. It
expects you to remove blockers and act as an escalation point for important issues; set
the digital evaluation strategy with input from lead and senior digital evaluators;
define a framework for quality assurance within evaluation; advocate for digital
evaluation, working with colleagues across government, other Government Digital and Data
communities and analytical professions; and manage the organisation's evaluation
capability through business planning, development and recruitment.

**Advocacy is in the list.** Evaluation has to keep making the case for its own
existence, because its value shows up as decisions not taken and money not wasted —
neither of which appears in anyone's delivery metrics.

Head of digital evaluation is the top of this role. Seven skills. Expect about 30
minutes.

## About this role level

**The role: digital evaluator.** A digital evaluator assesses the design, implementation
and outcomes of a digital product or service, and whether it delivers value for the
public and society. Some are also members of a government analytical profession where
evaluation is a core skill. In this role you will:

- work closely with delivery and policy teams
- develop theories of change for products and services, explaining the links between
  activities, outputs, intended outcomes and unintended effects
- follow established monitoring and evaluation methods and government guidance
- produce evaluation plans
- design quantitative and qualitative research
- collect and analyse data
- communicate findings, including by publishing reports

**Your level: head of digital evaluation.** You lead the digital evaluation community and
act as the point of contact between the community and the rest of the organisation. The
framework expects that you:

- remove blockers and act as an escalation point for important issues
- set the digital evaluation strategy with input from lead and senior digital evaluators
- define a framework for quality assurance within evaluation
- advocate for digital evaluation, working with colleagues across government, other
  Government Digital and Data communities and analytical professions
- manage the organisation's digital evaluation capability through business planning,
  development and recruitment

**There is no level above this one.** This role has five levels and this is the highest.
Where people go next is a question about other roles — analytical leadership, data
leadership, or leadership outside the profession — and the framework does not define it
as a next step, so this form does not invent one.

**Where this level sits:**

1. Junior digital evaluator
2. Digital evaluator
3. Senior digital evaluator
4. Lead digital evaluator
5. **Head of digital evaluation** — you are here

**One thing worth knowing about this role.** **Five of your seven skills are worded
identically at lead digital evaluator.** Only two are yours alone: evaluation delivery,
which becomes guidance, assurance, defining best practice and coaching rather than doing;
and monitoring and evaluation across the life cycle, which becomes leading and
championing best practice and coaching teams through phases.

What actually distinguishes this job is in the accountabilities — community leadership,
strategy, the quality assurance framework, cross-government advocacy, and business
planning and recruitment. None of those appear in the skills at all, which is worth
knowing when the skills are used to assess you.

**The seven skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Communicating analysis and insight
2. Data ethics and privacy
3. Evaluation delivery
4. Evaluation planning and strategy
5. Monitoring and evaluation across the product life cycle
6. Product and service monitoring
7. Quality assurance of data and analysis

### Skill: Communicating analysis and insight

**What the framework expects.** Two things: communicate relevant, compelling stories
using the most appropriate medium; and make complex recommendations at a tactical and
strategic level.

**Same wording as lead digital evaluator.**

**What that looks like here.** You are the person who tells the organisation what its
services are actually achieving. That message competes with delivery narratives that are
more optimistic and better resourced, and the medium matters as much as the finding.

### Skill: Data ethics and privacy

**What the framework expects.** Four things: oversee compliance with data ethics
standards and legislation across the organisation; develop, manage and continuously
improve an ethical framework within your area; develop a data ethics culture, influencing
others to act on it; and ensure data ethics is applied appropriately when formulating,
implementing and evaluating organisational policies and programmes.

**Same wording as lead digital evaluator.**

**What that looks like here.** Evaluation reaches people who have had a difficult
experience of a service. The ethical questions are about power as much as data — whether
people can decline, whether declining costs them anything, and whether what they say
reaches anyone.

### Skill: Evaluation delivery

**What the framework expects.** Three published bullets, carrying four expectations:
provide guidance on evaluation techniques for digital services to evaluators; assure the
evaluation work of digital evaluator teams; define best practice and methodologies for
evaluation and ensure they are shared across teams; and coach people across the
organisation to use appropriate evaluation techniques.

**A note on the published wording.** The second and third of those arrive as a single
bullet with no separator between them — "assure the evaluation work of digital evaluator
teams define best practice and methodologies for evaluation and ensure they are shared
across teams". They are two different things, so they are separated here. If you compare
this against the framework site you will count three bullets, not four.

**This is one of only two skills that distinguish your level from lead.** You have moved
from doing evaluation to guiding, assuring and coaching it.

**What that looks like here.** Coaching people *across the organisation* — not just
evaluators — is the ambitious part. Delivery managers and product managers making
evaluative judgements without an evaluator present is normal, and improving how they do
it reaches further than your team ever could.

### Skill: Evaluation planning and strategy

**What the framework expects.** Four things: influence the organisation's senior

evaluation strategy for a group of digital products and services; ensure evaluation plans

recurring argument of this job. The most persuasive material is a record of decisions
evaluation has improved and money it has saved — keeping that record is strategic work,

### Skill: Monitoring and evaluation across the product life cycle

**What the framework expects.** Three things: lead and champion best practice, and
identify improvements to current approaches for monitoring and evaluation through the
product life cycle; identify and raise evaluation or product risks during any stage of
the life cycle; and coach, guide and support teams moving through the product life cycle
phases.

**This is the second of the two skills that distinguish your level from lead.**

**What that looks like here.** *Raise product risks at any stage* gives you standing to
intervene early, before an evaluation would normally begin. Using that standing without
becoming the person who slows everything down is the judgement.

### Skill: Quality assurance of data and analysis

**What the framework expects.** Four things: set up tools and systems for effectively
assuring data and analysis across multiple teams; use appropriate approaches for
verifying and validating data and analysis; influence senior stakeholders over data and
analysis approaches; and coach and mentor others in assuring data and analysis.

**Same wording as lead digital evaluator** — though defining a quality assurance
framework is separately in your accountabilities, which the skill does not mention.

**What that looks like here.** A framework that depends on you reading everything is not
a framework. The test is whether a poor piece of analysis would be caught by the system
rather than by you noticing.
