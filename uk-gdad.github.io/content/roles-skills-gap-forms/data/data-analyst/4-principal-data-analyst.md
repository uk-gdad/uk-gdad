# Principal data analyst - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you are a principal data analyst in the UK Government
Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that
you can set out what you do now, where the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this level as leading teams or significant areas of work
in data analysis, and determining and implementing the standards, processes and
structures that ensure data is prepared, managed and used effectively across the
organisation. It expects you to influence organisational decision making on data
analysis and the use of data; oversee continuous improvement of the organisation's
data practices and management; and represent the data analyst community inside and
outside the organisation.

*Influence organisational decision making* is the accountability that defines the
job. The analysis has to be good, but at this level being right is not the same as
being effective — decisions get made in rooms where evidence competes with
conviction.

Principal is the top of this role. Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Nine
skills, each with an explanation, a rating and some questions. Expect about 35
minutes.

## Rating scale

Where a question asks for a rating, use this scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | You have not worked with this |
| 1 | Awareness | You know what it is and why it matters |
| 2 | Working | You do it with support |
| 3 | Practitioner | You do it unsupported, and handle the usual exceptions |
| 4 | Expert | You set the approach and develop others in it |

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not the remit on your job
  description.
- **A 4 is a claim about other people** — that someone else's practice changed
  because of you and would stay changed if you left. Several are reasonable at
  principal, and the honest test is whether the standards you set would survive
  your departure.

A filled-in answer looks like this:

> **Data management**
>
> - [x] 3 — Practitioner: I do it unsupported, and handle the usual exceptions
>
> *Evidence:* I lead our data governance forum and defined the responsibilities
> model that the directorate uses. Training requirements are drafted and have not
> been funded, so implementation is partial.

Named practice, and a clear line about where it stops.

## About this role level

**The role: data analyst.** A data analyst collects, manages, explores and shares
data to support organisational objectives and deliver business impact. In this
role you will:

- identify, collect and move data between a range of systems
- apply data analysis and data visualisation tools and techniques
- prepare, manage and model data, following appropriate standards and practices
- communicate data, insights and conclusions in appropriate formats

**Your level: principal data analyst.** You lead teams or significant areas of
work in data analysis, and determine and implement appropriate standards,
processes and structures to ensure data is prepared, managed and used effectively
within the organisation. The framework expects that you:

- influence organisational decision making on data analysis and the use of data
- oversee continuous improvement of the organisation's data practices and
  management
- represent the data analyst community inside and outside of the organisation

**There is no level above this one.** This role has four levels and this is the
highest. Where people go next is a question about other roles — data leadership,
a chief data officer role, or leadership outside the profession — and the
framework does not define it as a next step, so this form does not invent one.

**Where this level sits:**

1. Associate data analyst
2. Data analyst
3. Senior data analyst
4. **Principal data analyst** — you are here

**One thing worth knowing about this role.** *Data ethics and privacy* is worded
**identically** at your level and at senior. It is the only one of the nine that
does not rise. If your job in fact requires you to own the organisation's position
on data ethics — and at principal it usually does — you are working beyond the
published expectation, and that is worth recording rather than assuming.

**The nine skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Applying statistical and analytical tools and techniques
2. Communicating between the technical and non-technical
3. Data ethics and privacy
4. Data management
5. Data preparation and linkage
6. Data visualisation
7. Delivering business impact through data
8. Developing code for analysis
9. Managing a data project

### Skill: Applying statistical and analytical tools and techniques

**What the framework expects.** Four things: identify opportunities to develop
statistical insights and reports that support organisational objectives; develop
and guide others in a range of statistical and analytical techniques and tools;
oversee the quality assurance of statistical techniques and analytical outputs,
continually improving and innovating practices; and use statistical and analytical
outputs to influence stakeholders across the organisation and beyond.

**What that looks like here.** You spot the analysis nobody asked for that would
change something, and you are answerable for the quality of everything that leaves
your area. Influencing beyond the organisation means other departments and,
sometimes, the public.

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What the framework expects.** Three things: mediate between people and
strengthen relationships, adopting the appropriate communication method with
stakeholders at all levels; manage stakeholder expectations and moderate difficult
discussions about high-risk and complex topics, even within constrained
timescales; and speak on behalf of, and represent the community to, large audiences
inside and outside the organisation.

**What that looks like here.** Moderating a high-risk discussion under time
pressure is where the job is hardest — a number is wrong, or contested, and a
decision is being made this afternoon. Representing the community to large
audiences means public speaking is part of the role.

### Skill: Data ethics and privacy

**What the framework expects.** Four things: work with stakeholders to identify

### Skill: Data management

**What the framework expects.** Four things: champion and lead data management and
governance processes within your team and across your organisation; define and
lead on the implementation of data management responsibilities; define data
management tools and develop training requirements to implement governance
processes effectively; and enable continuous improvement of data management
practices and compliance with governance policies.

**What that looks like here.** *Define and lead the implementation of
responsibilities* means saying who owns what — the single most useful and most
avoided thing in data governance, because it makes people accountable for data
they inherited.

### Skill: Data preparation and linkage

**What the framework expects.** Four things: develop analytical data models using
a deep understanding of data sources; ensure data models are implemented using
tools and systems that align with modelling standards; set data quality standards
in the organisation or beyond, and ensure stakeholders understand their
importance; and mentor others in data quality and modelling.

**What that looks like here.** *Or beyond* is unusual wording — the framework
envisages you setting standards that reach outside your organisation. Deep
understanding of sources means knowing why a field is unreliable before 2019, not
just that it is.

### Skill: Data visualisation

**What the framework expects.** Five things: translate business requirements into
goals for data visualisations; set and advocate for new standards and practices in
inclusive, accessible data visualisation; identify data visualisation capability
gaps and implement appropriate training; promote the value and adoption of data
visualisations across the organisation; and strategically support stakeholders in
understanding and using a story.

**What that looks like here.** You own whether your organisation's charts are
accessible, and the accessibility regulations apply to anything published on a
public sector website. Identifying capability gaps and *implementing* training is
the item that needs budget as well as intention.

### Skill: Delivering business impact through data

with people who do not report to you — product, policy, operations — and it means

### Skill: Developing code for analysis

**What the framework expects.** Three things: oversee and promote the
implementation of coding standards and practices on existing and new analytical
outcomes, products and services; contribute to coding standards across the
organisation; and ensure reproducibility of code and code documentation across the
organisation.

**What that looks like here.** *Across the organisation* is a large claim. In
practice, reproducibility is usually good in new work and poor in the legacy
analysis that still feeds published figures, and closing that gap is slow,
unglamorous and valuable.

### Skill: Managing a data project

**What the framework expects.** Four things: advise on problem statement
development, ensuring standards are met; oversee and advise on the planning and
implementation of the project life cycle on varied data projects; share good
practice in adapting teams' ways of working to deliver project outcomes; and lead
and develop stakeholder relationships for your team's data projects.

**What that looks like here.** You advise rather than run, which is a harder
discipline than it sounds when a project is going wrong and you can see exactly
what to do.
