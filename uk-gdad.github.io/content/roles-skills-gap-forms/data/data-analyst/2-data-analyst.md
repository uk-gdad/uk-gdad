# Data analyst - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a data analyst in the UK Government Digital
and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can
set out what you do now, where the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this level as contributing to the planning, analysis and
implementation of data, working to appropriate standards and ensuring data is used
to achieve the team's goals. It expects you to prepare and manage data; apply
appropriate techniques, tools and processes to analyse it; and communicate data to
stakeholders with different needs.

That last one is where the job gets interesting. The analysis is often the
straightforward part; explaining it to three audiences who want three different
things from the same numbers is what takes the practice.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Nine skills, each with an explanation, a
rating and some questions. Expect about 30 minutes.

Worth knowing:

- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a finding.
- **Answer about the job you do**, not the one on the job description.
- **"I haven't had the chance" is a real answer**, and more useful than a
  generous reading of a thin example.
- **Be careful with data.** Describe the work, not the contents. No records, no
  identifiers, nothing that would identify a person.
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers, what they
  are used for, or how long they are kept, ask before you fill it in.

Where a question asks for a rating, use this scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |

| 0 | None | You have not worked with this |
| 1 | Awareness | You know what it is and why it matters |
| 2 | Working | You do it with support |
| 3 | Practitioner | You do it unsupported, and handle the usual exceptions |
| 4 | Expert | You set the approach and develop others in it |

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not the remit on your job
  description.
- **2s and 3s are the honest shape of this level.** Three of your nine skills say
  *under supervision*, *with some support* or *with some support when required*
  in the framework's own wording. A 2 on those is the level, not a shortfall.

## About this role level

**The role: data analyst.** A data analyst collects, manages, explores and shares
data to support organisational objectives and deliver business impact. In this
role you will:

- identify, collect and move data between a range of systems
- apply data analysis and data visualisation tools and techniques
- prepare, manage and model data, following appropriate standards and practices
- communicate data, insights and conclusions in appropriate formats

**Your level: data analyst.** You contribute to the planning, analysis and
implementation of data, work to appropriate standards, and ensure data is used to
achieve the team's goals. The framework expects that you:

- prepare and manage data
- apply appropriate techniques, tools and processes to effectively analyse data
- communicate data to stakeholders with different needs

**Where this level sits.** This role has four levels:

1. Associate data analyst
2. **Data analyst** — you are here
3. Senior data analyst
4. Principal data analyst

Your next step is senior data analyst: leading data analysis in a team or across
an area, guiding others in how data is prepared and used, leading improvements to
ways of working, and leading communication with stakeholders.

**One thing worth knowing about this role.** The framework's wording for
*Communicating between the technical and non-technical* at your level is
noticeably ambitious — it asks you to host discussions in a team with
"potentially difficult dynamics", advocate for the team externally, and manage
differing stakeholder perspectives. That is a lot for the second level of a role,
and more than many data analysts are given the opportunity to do. It is quoted as
published. If it reads as beyond your current remit, rate it honestly and say so.

**The nine skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Applying statistical and analytical tools and techniques
2. Communicating between the technical and non-technical
3. Data ethics and privacy
4. Data management
5. Data preparation and linkage
6. Data visualisation
7. Delivering business impact through data
8. Developing code for analysis
9. Managing a data project

## Job skills

Nine skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the level expects,
what it looks like in practice, and what changes at senior data analyst.

### Skill: Applying statistical and analytical tools and techniques

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: contribute to the
development of statistical and analytical insights and reports; apply appropriate
statistical and analytical techniques under supervision to answer research
questions and organisational needs; follow and apply quality assurance standards;
and respond to stakeholder questions about analytical and statistical techniques.

**What that looks like here.** You choose techniques with someone checking, and
you follow a quality assurance process rather than inventing one. Answering a
stakeholder's question about *why this method* is the part that exposes whether
you understood the choice or copied it.

**The next step.** At senior you lead the development of insights, apply and
support others in a range of techniques, quality assure across a team, and engage
stakeholders to build trust in the methods.

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: communicate
effectively with technical and non-technical stakeholders; support and host
discussions within a multidisciplinary team, with potentially difficult dynamics;
be an advocate for the team externally; and manage differing stakeholder
perspectives.

**What that looks like here.** Managing differing perspectives usually means two
stakeholders who want the same analysis to say different things. The honest move
is to present what the data supports and be clear about what it does not — which
takes more nerve than skill.

**The next step.** At senior you interpret and manage stakeholder expectations,
handle active and reactive communication, and host difficult discussions with
diverse senior stakeholders.

### Skill: Data ethics and privacy

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: apply fundamental
principles of data ethics and privacy in your work under supervision; and share
data ethics and privacy risks through appropriate channels.

**What that looks like here.** Noticing when a linkage would make people
identifiable, when a published breakdown has cells small enough to expose
individuals, or when an analysis would be used for something the data was not
collected for — and raising it rather than assuming someone senior already
thought about it.

**The next step.** At senior you work with stakeholders to address ethical and
privacy concerns, place them in the organisational context, research
developments, and constructively challenge proposed policies.

### Skill: Data management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: follow organisational
data governance, including policies on data access, sharing, dissemination and
protection; participate in or deliver data management across services or products;
and use appropriate data management tools, procedures and methods with some
support.

**What that looks like here.** Knowing what you are permitted to do with each
dataset you can reach, and not assuming that access implies permission. Data
management across services means the boring, valuable work: documentation,
lineage, knowing where a field came from.

**The next step.** At senior you advocate for effective governance across the
organisation, monitor and improve practice in your team, and help define the tools
and methods.

### Skill: Data preparation and linkage

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: support the planning
of a data model based on appropriate data sources; help ensure new and existing
data models and pipelines can be reused or reproduced; identify and resolve data
quality issues; and prepare and cleanse data, ensuring it is fit for purpose.

**What that looks like here.** Reusable and reproducible is the item that
separates a data analyst from someone with a very good spreadsheet. If your
pipeline runs only on your machine, in an order only you remember, it is neither.

**The next step.** At senior you plan the framework of an analytical data model,
support its implementation, and make sure your team understands why data quality
and modelling matter.

### Skill: Data visualisation

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: use the most
appropriate data visualisation to tell a focussed story relevant to the team's
goals; develop inclusive, accessible data visualisations that recognise different
user needs; and work with others in a multidisciplinary team to ensure a
visualisation provides the information they need.

**What that looks like here.** Accessibility is now yours to get right, not
someone else's check — colour contrast, never relying on colour alone, readable
labels, and a text alternative for anything conveying meaning. If your charts go
on a public website, the accessibility regulations apply to them.

**The next step.** At senior you use a range of visualisations to simplify a
complex story, advise others on accessible practice, and suggest improvements to
standards.

### Skill: Delivering business impact through data

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: explain how your work
aligns to the organisation's priorities and delivers business impact; work with
others to help identify business requirements; identify the data needed to
address business requirements; and develop and deliver data products that align
to organisational priorities and enable decision making, with some support when
required.

**What that looks like here.** Being able to say what data would answer a question
before anyone asks you to go and get it — and sometimes saying that no available
data answers it, which is a real finding rather than a failure.

**The next step.** At senior you lead and support others in delivering impact,
define and communicate the scope of that impact, spot opportunities for data
products, and build scalable ones.

### Skill: Developing code for analysis

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: design the analytical
approach and the code needed to address simple research questions; explain the
importance of testing code; review and improve analytical approaches, including
code, under supervision; and produce analytical approach documentation that
describes the code you wrote.

**What that looks like here.** Analysis code is code — it deserves version
control, review and tests, and government analysis has been wrong in public
because none of those were there. Testing analysis code usually means checking
known inputs give known answers.

**The next step.** At senior you design and implement the approach to standards
with tests, lead collaborative review of shared code, and guide others in
documentation.

### Skill: Managing a data project

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: contribute to defining
problem statements and gathering project requirements; explain the importance of
the different processes and stages of the data project life cycle and contribute
to their planning; and adapt your ways of working to deliver different project
outcomes and requirements.

**What that looks like here.** Being in the room when the question is framed, not
just when it is handed over. A badly framed question wastes weeks, and the person
best placed to spot it is usually the analyst.

**The next step.** At senior you identify and define the problem statement, apply
and share project management methods, plan and implement the life cycle stages,
and communicate progress to stakeholders.
