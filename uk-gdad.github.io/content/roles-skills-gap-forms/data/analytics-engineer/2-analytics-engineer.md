# Analytics engineer - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as an analytics engineer in the UK Government
Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that
you can set out what you do now, where the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this level as developing and testing data models assigned
by more senior analytics engineers, to help people in a defined area access and
use data. It expects you to draft documentation of data that meets standards; work
with other analytics engineers to resolve issues and risks; support trainee
analytics engineers; provide training and support for users of data sets; and work
with more experienced colleagues to develop your skills.

Two of those pull in opposite directions and both are correct: you are supporting
someone below you while still being developed yourself. That is a normal shape for
this level, and it is worth being honest about how much of each you actually get.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Eight skills, each with an explanation, a
rating and some questions. Expect about 30 minutes.

## About this role level

**The role: analytics engineer.** An analytics engineer transforms data relevant
to the organisation into structures that enable analysis and decision making. In
this role you will:

- work with subject matter experts to understand organisational processes and
  translate these into data structures optimised for analysis
- work with data users to design and build data models they can use, using
  modelling techniques such as Kimball or Inmon
- support data quality improvement
- develop standards for data transformation
- create and maintain data documentation
- refine requirements in response to feedback and changes in the organisation
- provide ongoing support to users

**Your level: analytics engineer.** You develop and test data models assigned by
more senior analytics engineers, to help people in a defined area access and use
data. The framework expects that you:

- draft documentation of data that meets standards
- work with other analytics engineers to resolve issues and risks
- support trainee analytics engineers
- provide training and support for users of data sets
- work with more experienced analytics engineers to develop your skills

**Where this level sits.** This role has five levels:

1. Trainee analytics engineer
2. **Analytics engineer** — you are here
3. Senior analytics engineer
4. Lead analytics engineer
5. Head of analytics engineering

Your next step is senior analytics engineer: overseeing the development and testing
of models, directing other analytics engineers' work, coaching them, building
stakeholder relationships in a defined area, and exploring new ways of working
with data.

**Two things worth knowing about this role.**

First, your level is where *Programming and build (data and analytics
engineering)* appears — trainees do not have it. It is worded as writing simple
programs or scripts under the direction of others.

Second, three of your eight skills are worded **identically** at trainee level:
data innovation, problem management, and testing. The framework does not raise
those between trainee and analytics engineer at all. If you are doing more than
the trainee wording describes on those — and most people at your level are — it is
worth recording, because a comparison of the two levels will not show it.

**The eight skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Communicating between the technical and non-technical
2. Data analysis and synthesis
3. Data innovation
4. Data modelling, cleansing and enrichment
5. Metadata management
6. Problem management
7. Programming and build (data and analytics engineering)
8. Testing

## Job skills

Eight skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the level expects,
what it looks like in practice, and what changes at senior analytics engineer.

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: communicate effectively
with technical and non-technical stakeholders; support and host discussions within
a multidisciplinary team, with potentially difficult dynamics; be an advocate for
the team externally; and manage differing stakeholder perspectives.

**What that looks like here.** Users ask for a field; what they need is an answer
to a question. Getting from one to the other, without either building the wrong
thing or making them feel interrogated, is most of the craft.

**The next step.** Identical wording at senior analytics engineer. This skill does
not change between your level and the one above.

### Skill: Data analysis and synthesis

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: undertake data profiling
and source system analysis; and present clear insights to colleagues to support the
end use of the data.

**What that looks like here.** Profiling means looking at what is actually in a
source before you model it — the distributions, the nulls, the values nobody
documented. It is where you discover that a field marked mandatory is empty for
everything before 2021.

### Skill: Data modelling, cleansing and enrichment

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: produce data models and
understand where to use different types; understand different tools and compare
different data models; reverse-engineer a data model from a live system; and
understand industry-recognised data modelling patterns and standards.

**What that looks like here.** Reverse-engineering a live system is the item worth
noticing — it means working out how something is structured from the thing itself,
usually because the documentation is missing or wrong. It is a common and
underrated part of the job.

**The next step.** At senior you build and review complex models, use integration
tools and advise teams on best practice, ensure data meets quality standards and is
interoperable, and work with other data professionals to improve patterns and
standards.

### Skill: Metadata management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: use metadata
repositories to complete complex tasks such as data and systems integration impact
analysis; and maintain a metadata repository to ensure information remains
accurate and up to date.

**What that looks like here.** Impact analysis means answering "if we change this
field, what breaks?" — which is only possible if the metadata is real. Maintaining
it is the unglamorous half, and it decays the moment nobody owns it.

**The next step.** At senior you design an appropriate repository, suggest
improvements, understand a range of tools, and advise less experienced colleagues.

### Skill: Problem management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: investigate problems in
systems, processes and services, with an understanding of the level of a problem —
strategic, tactical or operational; and contribute to the implementation of
remedies and preventative measures.

**Same wording as trainee analytics engineer.**

**What that looks like here.** Telling a one-off failure from a recurring pattern
from a design flaw. Contributing to preventative measures means fixing the cause,
not only the instance — and getting time to do that is usually the hard part.

**The next step.** At senior you initiate and monitor investigation into patterns
and trends, consult specialists, determine the resolution and assist with
implementation, and determine preventative measures.

### Skill: Programming and build (data and analytics engineering)

### Skill: Testing

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: correctly execute test
scripts under supervision; and understand the role of testing and how it works.

**Same wording as trainee analytics engineer.**

**What that looks like here.** Data tests — row counts, uniqueness, referential
checks, freshness. The framework's wording has you running them; in most teams an
analytics engineer at your level is writing them too, which is ahead of the
published expectation.

**The next step.** At senior you review requirements and specifications, define
test conditions, identify issues and risks, and analyse and report on test
activities.
