# Service designer - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a service designer in the UK Government Digital and Data
profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out what you do now,
where the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this level as a confident and competent designer who can develop designs
based on evidence of user needs and organisational outcomes. It expects you to be trusted to make
good decisions; recognise when to ask for further guidance and support; contribute to the
development of design concepts; and interpret evidence-based research and incorporate it into your
work.

The distinctive difficulty of service design at this level is that most of what needs to change is
not yours to change. The letter is owned by communications, the phone script by operations, the
eligibility rule by policy. Designing the whole service means persuading, not building.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Seven skills, each with an explanation, a rating and some
questions. Expect about 30 minutes.

## About this role level

**The role: service designer.** Service designers design the end-to-end journey of a service. This
helps a user complete their goal and government deliver a policy intent. Your work may involve
creating or changing transactions, products and content across both digital and offline channels,
provided by different parts of government.

**Your level: service designer.** You are a confident and competent designer who can develop designs
based on evidence of user needs and organisational outcomes. The framework expects that you:

- be trusted to make good decisions
- recognise when to ask for further guidance and support
- contribute to the development of design concepts
- interpret evidence-based research and incorporate this into your work

**Where this level sits.** This role has six levels:

1. Associate service designer
2. Junior service designer
3. **Service designer** — you are here
4. Senior service designer
5. Lead service designer
6. Head of service design

**Two things worth knowing about this role.**

First, four of your seven skills are worded **identically at junior service designer** — design
communication, designing for everyone, evidence-based design and iterative design. What changed on
promotion was designing strategically, designing together and leading design.

Second: **the framework publishes the same seven skills, word for word, for service designers,
interaction designers and graphic designers** at every level. Nothing in the published wording
reflects that your scope spans organisations, channels and policy while theirs does not.

**The seven skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Design communication
2. Designing for everyone
3. Designing strategically
4. Designing together
5. Evidence-based design
6. Iterative design
7. Leading design

## Job skills

Seven skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the level expects, what it looks like
in service design, and what changes at senior service designer.

### Skill: Design communication

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: clearly explain problems and design ideas
to others; and clearly explain design decisions to others using appropriate tools and methods.

**Same wording as junior service designer.**

**What that looks like here.** The most useful artefact is often a map of the service as it actually
is, with the failures marked. People defend a process they have never seen laid out; once they see
it, the conversation changes.

**The next step.** At senior you explain complex problems to stakeholders outside design, document
decisions with risks and unresolved issues, and build consensus by asking difficult questions and
challenging assumptions.

### Skill: Designing for everyone

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: work with others to design and deliver
inclusive, accessible and environmentally sustainable content or services that meet the needs of all
users; and ensure a design meets appropriate standards, for example accessibility regulations, **with
support**.

**Same wording as junior service designer.**

**What that looks like here.** Service-level exclusion is rarely about a web page. It is about
requiring photographic identity documents, or a bank account, or an address, or the ability to
attend on a weekday — none of which appear in an accessibility audit.

**The next step.** At senior you advise teams on inclusive and sustainable design, design ethically
considering the personal and social context of users, and ensure a design meets standards — without
the *with support* qualifier.

### Skill: Designing strategically

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: align your design work to both the goals
and vision of your team and the strategic objectives of your organisation; identify potential risks
and use design to mitigate them; identify opportunities and use design to take advantage of them; and
contribute to the development of design patterns and components.

**What that looks like here.** Service design risks are organisational: a step that generates
appeals, a handover that loses cases, a channel that quietly closes. Using design to mitigate those
means changing the process, not the interface.

**The next step.** At senior you help teams understand how user-centred design meets their goals,
use risks and constraints in technology, systems and policy to shape design, and identify and create
new patterns.

### Skill: Designing together

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: effectively plan and run a design session
with your team, users or stakeholders; identify and engage the right people throughout the design
process; and give and receive constructive design feedback.

**What that looks like here.** Running a session that spans operations, policy and digital is
genuinely hard — different vocabularies, different pressures, and often a history. Getting a shared
picture of the service into the room before anyone proposes anything is usually the way through.

**The next step.** At senior you advise others on running sessions, adapt sessions to achieve useful
outcomes, involve the right people throughout, and work across profession boundaries such as with
policy teams.

### Skill: Evidence-based design

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: analyse, synthesise and clearly explain
evidence relevant to users or a service, for example web analytics data; and work with researchers or
analysts to use evidence to inform, develop and test a design idea.

**Same wording as junior service designer.**

**What that looks like here.** The best service evidence is usually held by other people — complaint
teams, contact centres, casework systems. Getting access to it, and joining it to research, is the
work.

### Skill: Iterative design

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: apply iterative design principles and
agile methodologies to your work; iterate and improve a design based on successive rounds of
research; prototype your ideas at different fidelities to explore and test designs; use a range of
design tools and techniques; and use and iterate design patterns and components.

**Same wording as junior service designer.**

**What that looks like here.** Iterating a service is slower than iterating a page, because changes
need agreement from people who were not in the room. Prototyping the change first — as a role play,
a redrafted letter, a trial in one region — is how you get that agreement.

### Skill: Leading design

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: lead and co-ordinate design work in your
team, **with support**; communicate the value of user-centred design to your team; support other
designers; and work with digital and data leaders in your organisation, when needed.

**What that looks like here.** Service design work often needs a decision from someone senior in
another part of the organisation. Knowing who that is, and how to reach them without going through
four layers, is a large part of being effective.

**The next step.** Identical wording at senior service designer. This skill does not change between
your level and the one above.
