# Lead service designer - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a lead service designer in the UK Government Digital and Data
profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out what you do now,
where the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this level as an expert practitioner who influences and mentors others. It
expects you to work with service managers and programme directors to develop design concepts; set
direction and **assure the quality of design delivery across teams**; and lead multiple or highly
complex services.

Assuring service design quality across teams means noticing the failures that only show up at the
seams — where one team's output becomes another's input, where a channel hands over, where a case
waits. Individual teams almost never see those, because each part works.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Seven skills, each with an explanation, a rating and some
questions. Expect about 30 minutes.

## Advisory

## How to fill in this form

This is a static web page. There is nothing to type into and nothing to send from here — your answers
never touch this site.

Print it, copy the questions into a document, or paste them into whatever your organisation uses for
development conversations. Every question is numbered, so answers travel back on their own.

Worth knowing:

- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a finding.
- **"My designers do this" is not an answer to "what do you do".** Name your part.
- **"I haven't had the chance" is a real answer** at this level too.
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers, what they are used for, or how
  long they are kept, ask before you fill it in.

## Rating scale

Where a question asks for a rating, use this scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | You have not worked with this |
| 1 | Awareness | You know what it is and why it matters |
| 2 | Working | You do it with support |
| 3 | Practitioner | You do it unsupported, and handle the usual exceptions |
| 4 | Expert | You set the approach and develop others in it |

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not the remit on your job description.
- **A 4 is a claim about other people** — that someone else's practice changed because of you and
  would stay changed if you left. Mentoring and setting direction are written into your level, so
  several are reasonable.

A filled-in answer looks like this:

> *Evidence:* My teams work from evidence and I have got complaint data into planning conversations.
> Getting insight used by the wider organisation — let alone across government — has not happened.

## About this role level

**The role: service designer.** Service designers design the end-to-end journey of a service. This
helps a user complete their goal and government deliver a policy intent. Your work may involve
creating or changing transactions, products and content across both digital and offline channels,
provided by different parts of government.

**Your level: lead service designer.** You are an expert practitioner who influences and mentors
others. The framework expects that you:

- work with service managers and programme directors to develop design concepts
- set direction and assure the quality of design delivery across teams
- lead multiple or highly complex services

**Where this level sits.** This role has six levels:

1. Associate service designer
2. Junior service designer
3. Service designer
4. Senior service designer
5. **Lead service designer** — you are here
6. Head of service design

Your next step is head of service design: influencing both design and organisational strategy,
collaborating with counterparts across government, and focusing on the conditions and environment
designers need to work effectively.

**One thing worth knowing about this role.** Five of your seven skills are worded **identically at
head of service design** — design communication, designing for everyone, designing together,
evidence-based design and iterative design. Only designing strategically and leading design change,
and both change towards organisational reach.

**The seven skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Design communication
2. Designing for everyone
3. Designing strategically
4. Designing together
5. Evidence-based design
6. Iterative design
7. Leading design

## Job skills

Seven skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the level expects, what it looks like
in service design, and how it compares with head of service design.

### Skill: Design communication

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: gain support for design decisions and
design strategy from senior leaders; communicate design strategy across organisational boundaries;
coach others in how to effectively communicate design; and develop or introduce more effective
methods of communicating design and **working in the open**.

**Same wording at head of service design.**

**What that looks like here.** Communicating across organisational boundaries is not optional in
service design — it is the job. The most effective form is usually a shared picture of the service
that both organisations recognise, produced jointly rather than delivered.

### Skill: Designing for everyone

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: **measurably improve** the practice of

the organisation's convenience: office hours, one channel, documents that are easy for some people to

with leaders so design informs policy, technology and delivery decisions early enough to change them,

### Skill: Designing together

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: advise others how to effectively plan and
run design sessions with a team, users or stakeholders; adapt a design session to ensure you achieve
a useful outcome; effectively involve the right people throughout the design process; and work across
team or profession boundaries, for example with policy teams.

**What that looks like here.** At lead the useful move is making cross-boundary collaboration
routine rather than heroic — a standing forum, a shared measure, a regular walkthrough — so it does

### Skill: Evidence-based design

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: embed a practice of hypothesis-driven
design in a team; guide the organisation in how to use evidence to improve services; analyse,
synthesise and use evidence to improve the way the organisation works; and **ensure user insights are
shared with and used by the wider organisation and across government**.

**Same wording at head of service design.**

**What that looks like here.** Service evidence is the most persuasive material in government —
waiting times, failure demand, appeals upheld. Getting it in front of people who set policy is the
highest-leverage thing you can do with it.

### Skill: Iterative design

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: embed the practice of iterative design and
agile working in teams or the wider organisation; **lead on prototyping complex ideas at any
fidelity**; help teams adapt quickly to changes in requirements, priorities or user needs; and
iterate delivery processes or team structures to help teams work effectively.

**Same wording at head of service design.**

**What that looks like here.** Iterating team structures matters more in service design than
anywhere else — many service problems are org chart problems, and moving a responsibility fixes what
no amount of design can.

### Skill: Leading design

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: lead and co-ordinate design work in a
team; advocate for user-centred design with **leaders outside of digital and data**; create an
inclusive, productive environment for designers to work in; mentor and coach other designers; and
iterate and improve design processes within your team.

**What that looks like here.** The *with support* qualifier has finally gone. For service design,
advocating outside digital and data is not a stretch goal — operations and policy are where your
services actually live.

**The next step.** At head you set a vision for and lead design across multiple teams or a whole
organisation, work with senior leaders across the organisation or public sector, support a culture of
learning and experimentation, and measurably improve design maturity.
