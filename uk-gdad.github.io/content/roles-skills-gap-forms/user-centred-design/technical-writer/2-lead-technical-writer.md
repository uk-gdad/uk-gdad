# Lead technical writer - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a lead technical writer in the UK Government Digital and
Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out what you
do now, where the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this level as an expert practitioner who directs a team of technical
writers and assures the quality of technical writing across teams and its alignment to
strategy. It expects you to standardise tools, ways of communicating and processes; work
closely with service managers, technology leaders and senior stakeholders to resource teams
and resolve problems; **promote the discipline of technical writing**; engage with the
cross-government community; and keep up to date with industry changes.

Promoting the discipline is in the list because technical writing is chronically
under-resourced in government. Teams that would never ship untested code will happily ship
undocumented software, and someone has to keep making the argument.

Lead technical writer is the top of this role. Seven skills. Expect about 30 minutes.

## How to fill in this form

This is a static web page. There is nothing to type into and nothing to send from here —
your answers never touch this site.

Print it, copy the questions into a document, or paste them into whatever your organisation
uses for development conversations. Every question is numbered, so answers travel back on
their own.

Worth knowing:

- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a finding.
- **"My team does this" is not an answer to "what do you do".** Name your part.
- **"I haven't had the chance" is a real answer** at this level too.
- **Do not include anything sensitive from internal documentation.**
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers, what they are used
  for, or how long they are kept, ask before you fill it in.

| 3 | Practitioner | You do it unsupported, and handle the usual exceptions |
| 4 | Expert | You set the approach and develop others in it |

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not the remit on your job description.
- **A 4 is a claim about other people** — that someone else's practice changed because of
  you and would stay changed if you left. Directing a team and assuring quality are written
  into your level, so several are reasonable.

## About this role level

**The role: technical writer.** You take a user-centred approach to making complicated
technical concepts easier to understand for a specialist audience. You focus on specialist
content and software documentation, and write for technologists such as developers, technical
architects and technology leaders.

**Your level: lead technical writer.** You are an expert practitioner who directs a team of
technical writers and assures the quality of technical writing across teams and its alignment
to strategy. The framework expects that you:

- standardise tools and ways of communicating, along with processes
- work closely with service managers, technology leaders and senior stakeholders to resource
  teams and resolve problems
- promote the discipline of technical writing
- engage with the cross-government community
- keep up to date with industry changes

**There is no level above this one.** This role has two levels only, and this is the higher.
Where people go next is a question about other roles — content design leadership, or
leadership outside the profession — and the framework does not define it as a next step, so
this form does not invent one.

**Where this level sits:**

1. Technical writer
2. **Lead technical writer** — you are here

**One thing worth knowing about this role.** **Five of your seven skills are worded
identically at technical writer** — Agile working, prototyping, strategic thinking, technical
understanding, and user focus. Only stakeholder relationship management and user-centred
content design change. The published distinction between the two levels rests almost entirely
on those two, plus the accountabilities. If you are being assessed on the skills alone, very
little will separate you from the level below.

**The seven skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Agile working
2. Prototyping
3. Stakeholder relationship management
4. Strategic thinking
5. Technical understanding (user-centred design)
6. User focus
7. User-centred content design

## Job skills

### Skill: Agile working

**What the framework expects.** Three things: identify and compare the best processes or
delivery methods to use, including measuring and evaluating outcomes; help the team to decide
the best approach; and help teams to manage and visualise outcomes, prioritise work and adhere
to agreed minimum viable product, priorities and scope.

**Same wording as technical writer.**

**What that looks like here.** At lead the question is whether documentation is planned into
delivery across teams, or squeezed in wherever a writer can be borrowed. That is a resourcing
conversation more than a process one.

### Skill: Strategic thinking

lead organisational strategy identically, which is worth noticing if this skill is used to
compare the two levels.

### Skill: User-centred content design

**What the framework expects.** Six things: direct the approach to content life cycle
management to ensure content is regularly reviewed and evaluated by teams; oversee teams' work
to ensure the right content is being produced to meet the needs of users; demonstrate extensive
experience in creating, iterating, managing and overseeing content across multiple channels;
encourage teams to review and evaluate the effectiveness of processes and systems and support
them to iterate; **be accountable for the production of high quality, user-focused content**;
and identify gaps in content design skills and capability, and help teams grow and develop.

**This is the second of the two skills that distinguish your level from technical writer.**

**What that looks like here.** Being accountable for quality across teams means having a view
on documentation you did not write and cannot fully check. A review approach that runs without
you is the only version of this that scales.
