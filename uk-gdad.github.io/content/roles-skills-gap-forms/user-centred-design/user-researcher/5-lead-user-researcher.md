# Lead user researcher - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a lead user researcher in the UK Government Digital and Data
profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out what you do now,
where the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this level as an expert practitioner, leading and aligning user research
activities across several teams. It expects you to ensure that teams take a user-centred,
evidence-based approach to service design and delivery, and to develop and assure good user research
practice.

Two accountabilities, and both are about other people's work. The distinctive difficulty of this
level is that you are answerable for research you did not conduct, with participants you never met —
which only works if you have built standards, a review rhythm and a community that holds itself to
them.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Seven skills, each with an explanation, a rating and some
questions. Expect about 30 minutes.

  long they are kept, ask before you fill it in.

## Rating scale

Where a question asks for a rating, use this scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | You have not worked with this |
| 1 | Awareness | You know what it is and why it matters |
| 2 | Working | You do it with support |
| 3 | Practitioner | You do it unsupported, and handle the usual exceptions |
| 4 | Expert | You set the approach and develop others in it |

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not the remit on your job description.
- **A 4 is a claim about other people** — that someone else's practice changed because of you and
  would stay changed if you left. Assuring practice is written into your level, so several are
  reasonable.

A filled-in answer looks like this:

> **User research methods**
>
> - [x] 2 — Working: I do it with support
>
> *Evidence:* I run our community of practice and it has raised the standard of planning noticeably.
> Helping the organisation adopt methods it does not currently use — diary studies, ethnography — has
> not got past interest.

Named practice, and a clear line about where it stops.

## About this role level

**The role: user researcher.** User researchers plan, design and carry out research activities with
users that help teams get a deep understanding of the people who use government services. This
research informs policy, proposition, service, content and interaction design, so that services work
well for users and achieve policy intent.

**Your level: lead user researcher.** You are an expert practitioner, leading and aligning user
research activities across several teams. The framework expects that you:

- ensure that teams take a user-centred, evidence-based approach to service design and delivery
- develop and assure good user research practice

**Where this level sits.** This role has six levels:

1. Associate user researcher
2. Junior user researcher
3. User researcher
4. Senior user researcher
5. **Lead user researcher** — you are here
6. Head of user research

Your next step is head of user research: leading user researchers in an organisation, attracting and
building talent, defining and assuring best practice, influencing organisational strategy, and
collaborating across government.

**One thing worth knowing about this role.** Four of your seven skills are worded **identically at
head of user research** — analysis and synthesis, research management, stakeholder relationship
management, and user research methods. Three change: agile research practices (organisational
advocacy and setting standards for how insights are delivered), inclusive research (guiding
organisations rather than teams), and user-centred practice and advocacy (ensuring senior and
cross-organisation discussions consider user needs).

**The seven skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Agile research practices
2. Analysis and synthesis
3. Inclusive research
4. Research management, leadership and assurance
5. Stakeholder relationship management
6. User research methods
7. User-centred practice and advocacy

## Job skills

Seven skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the level expects, what it looks like in
practice, and what changes at head of user research.

### Skill: Agile research practices

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: advocate for agile working; adapt how you
design and conduct research to respond to the complexity of the product environment; understand the
strategic decisions the team needs to make so you can design appropriate research; and influence
decisions about priorities and agile processes in the team.

**Same wording as senior user researcher.**

**What that looks like here.** Across several teams, the useful question is whether research is
scheduled around delivery or delivery is informed by research. In most organisations it is the
former, and changing that is a matter of when research happens rather than how much.

**The next step.** At head you advocate for agile working at organisational level and with senior
stakeholders, help organisations adopt agile research practices, set standards for how insights are
delivered, and coach researchers in agile ways of working.

### Skill: Inclusive research

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: help teams understand the diversity of
users of government services; effectively include all kinds of users in appropriate research
activities; and advocate inclusive practices and help teams design and deliver accessible services that
work for all users.

**Same wording as senior user researcher.**

**What that looks like here.** Across teams the question becomes structural: is there a recruitment
route to people who are digitally excluded or in crisis, or does each team improvise? Improvised
recruitment reaches the same kinds of participants every time.

**The next step.** At head you help *organisations* understand diversity and guide them to adopt
inclusive practices and apply what they learn.

### Skill: User research methods

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: help an organisation adopt a wide range of
user research methods; and **lead a community of practice to help an organisation continually assure,
improve and innovate their user research**.

**Same wording at head of user research.**

**What that looks like here.** A community of practice is the mechanism that makes assurance
sustainable. Peer review of plans, shared session observation, and honest post-mortems raise the floor
faster than any amount of individual feedback from you.
