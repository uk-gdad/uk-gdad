# Head of user research - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you are a head of user research in a UK government organisation. Human
resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out what you do now, where the gaps are,
and what would close them.

The framework describes this role as leading user researchers in an organisation and **attracting and
building talent**. It expects you to be an expert practitioner who can define and assure best
practice; influence organisational strategy and priorities; and collaborate with colleagues across
government.

Attracting and building talent is named in the level description itself, which is unusual. User
research is a small profession, people move between departments, and whether your organisation is
somewhere researchers want to work is largely a consequence of decisions you make.

Head of user research is the top of this role. Seven skills. Expect about 30 minutes.

## Rating scale

Where a question asks for a rating, use this scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | You have not worked with this |
| 1 | Awareness | You know what it is and why it matters |
| 2 | Working | You do it with support |
| 3 | Practitioner | You do it unsupported, and handle the usual exceptions |
| 4 | Expert | You set the approach and develop others in it |

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not the remit on your job description.
- **A 4 is a claim about other people** — that someone else's practice changed because of you and
  would stay changed if you left. The honest test is whether research practice here would survive
  your departure.

> **Inclusive research**

>

> digital.

Named practice, and a clear line about where it stops.

## About this role level

**The role: user researcher.** User researchers plan, design and carry out research activities with
users that help teams get a deep understanding of the people who use government services. This
research informs policy, proposition, service, content and interaction design, so that services work
well for users and achieve policy intent.

**Your level: head of user research.** You lead user researchers in an organisation and attract and
build talent. The framework expects that you:

- be an expert practitioner who can define and assure best practice
- influence organisational strategy and priorities
- collaborate with colleagues across government

**There is no level above this one.** This role has six levels and this is the highest. Where people
go next is a question about other roles — user-centred design leadership, digital leadership, or
leadership outside the profession — and the framework does not define it as a next step, so this form
does not invent one.

**Where this level sits:**

1. Associate user researcher
2. Junior user researcher
3. User researcher
4. Senior user researcher
5. Lead user researcher
6. **Head of user research** — you are here

**One thing worth knowing about this role.** Four of your seven skills are worded **identically at
lead user researcher** — analysis and synthesis, research management, stakeholder relationship
management, and user research methods. Three are yours alone: agile research practices (organisational
advocacy and setting standards for how insights are delivered), inclusive research (guiding
organisations), and user-centred practice and advocacy (ensuring senior and cross-organisation
discussions consider user needs).

**The seven skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Agile research practices
2. Analysis and synthesis
3. Inclusive research
4. Research management, leadership and assurance
5. Stakeholder relationship management
6. User research methods
7. User-centred practice and advocacy

## Job skills

Seven skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the framework expects and what it looks
like in practice. There is no level above, so these say what the skill demands rather than what comes
next.

### Skill: Agile research practices

**What the framework expects.** Four things: advocate for agile working at the organisational level
and with senior stakeholders; help organisations adopt agile research practices; **set standards and
expectations of how research insights should be delivered to support delivery by agile teams**; and
coach and guide other user researchers to adopt agile ways of working.

**This is one of the three skills that distinguish your level from lead.**

**What that looks like here.** Setting expectations for how insight is delivered is a practical lever:
if the organisation expects findings in a form it can act on within days, research gets used; if it
expects long reports, research gets read late or not at all.

### Skill: Analysis and synthesis

**What the framework expects.** One thing: help an organisation to adopt a wide range of analysis and
synthesis techniques, and to continually assure, improve and innovate their practices to generate
clear and valuable findings.

**Same wording as lead user researcher.**

**What that looks like here.** At organisation scale the risk is findings that are confidently wrong —
produced under pressure, from too few sessions, and repeated until they become accepted fact. Assuring
against that is quiet work with no visible output.

### Skill: Inclusive research

**What the framework expects.** Two things: help **organisations** understand the diversity of users of
government services; and guide organisations to adopt inclusive practices and apply what they learn to
design and deliver accessible services that work for all users.

**This is the second of the three skills that distinguish your level from lead.**

**What that looks like here.** *Apply what they learn* is the harder half. Organisations can be shown
that a service excludes people and carry on, because acting would cost money or change a policy.
Turning inclusion findings into changes is where this skill actually bites.

### Skill: Research management, leadership and assurance

**What the framework expects.** Four things: establish and align user research programmes and teams to
meet organisational goals; synthesise findings from research programmes, producing strategic insights
to inform the organisation's roadmap; **set and assure good practice user research standards for the
organisation, including ethics and safeguarding**; and assure user research quality across the
organisation.

**Same wording as lead user researcher.**

**What that looks like here.** You are answerable for how the organisation treats research
participants. Many of them are in difficult circumstances, and the standards you set are the only
thing between good intentions and harm.

### Skill: User-centred practice and advocacy

**What the framework expects.** Three things: find ways to help an organisation adopt user-centred
practices and appreciate the value of understanding users; **ensure discussions at senior levels or
between organisations consider user needs and evolving user behaviour**; and help an organisation
understand the complete journey of the user and use this knowledge to improve their services.

**This is the third of the three skills that distinguish your level from lead.**

**What that looks like here.** Being the person who asks "what do we know about how this affects
people" in a room where nobody else will is the whole job compressed into one sentence. Doing it
without becoming the person people stop inviting is the skill.
