# User researcher - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a user researcher in the UK Government Digital and Data
profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out what you do now,
where the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this level as usually embedded in a multidisciplinary team and responsible
for planning and carrying out user research activities. At this level you are expected to **work
independently on a team**.

That is the whole level description — there is no separate accountability list. Working independently
on a team is the phrase that matters: you are the research capability for that team, which means the
quality of what they know about their users is largely down to you.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Seven skills, each with an explanation, a rating and some
questions. Expect about 30 minutes.

## Advisory

This skills gap form is created by AI and is work in progress for educational purposes. If you intend
to use it in any way, please consult with your organization's appropriate teammates, such as with
human resources staff, or people and organizational development staff, or legal compliance staff.

## How to fill in this form

You can fill this form in on screen: the boxes below take typing, the tick
boxes tick, and each skill has a rating to choose. Your answers are saved in
this browser, on this device, so you can close the page and come back to them.
Nothing is sent anywhere.

When you have finished, use Export TSV or Export as JSON at the top of the
page to take your answers with you, or print the page, or copy them into
whatever your organisation uses for development conversations. Every question
is numbered, so answers travel back on their own. Clear answers empties the
form and deletes the saved copy, which is worth doing on a computer other
people use.

Worth knowing:

- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a finding.
- **Answer about the job you do**, not the one on the job description.
- **"I haven't had the chance" is a real answer**, and more useful than a generous reading of a thin
  example.
- **Never include anything about a research participant** — no names, details or identifying quotes.
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers, what they are used for, or how
  long they are kept, ask before you fill it in.

## About this role level

**The role: user researcher.** User researchers plan, design and carry out research activities with
users that help teams get a deep understanding of the people who use government services. This
research informs policy, proposition, service, content and interaction design, so that services work
well for users and achieve policy intent.

**Your level: user researcher.** You are usually embedded in a multidisciplinary team and responsible
for planning and carrying out user research activities. At this level you are expected to work
independently on a team.

**Where this level sits.** This role has six levels:

1. Associate user researcher
2. Junior user researcher
3. **User researcher** — you are here
4. Senior user researcher
5. Lead user researcher
6. Head of user research

**Two things worth knowing about this role.**

First, five of your seven skills rose when you reached this level — agile research practices,
inclusive research, research management, stakeholder relationship management, and user-centred
practice and advocacy. Those five had been identical across the two levels below.

Second, and oddly: *Analysis and synthesis* and *User research methods* are worded **identically at
junior user researcher** — and the research methods wording still says **"plan and conduct a user
research project, with support"**, at a level whose description says you work independently. That is
how the framework publishes it. If you plan and run projects alone, you are beyond that wording.

**The seven skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Agile research practices
2. Analysis and synthesis
3. Inclusive research
4. Research management, leadership and assurance
5. Stakeholder relationship management
6. User research methods
7. User-centred practice and advocacy

## Job skills

Seven skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the level expects, what it looks like in
practice, and what changes at senior user researcher.

### Skill: Agile research practices

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: understand and explain the benefits and
limitations of agile working; **design and conduct your research so the findings can be embedded into
an agile workflow**; and understand and work with the different roles in a multidisciplinary digital
team to plan and do user research.

**What that looks like here.** Research that arrives after the decision has been made is expensive
decoration. Designing rounds so findings land while something can still change is the practical
version of this skill — and the *limitations* half matters too, because some questions cannot be
answered in a sprint.

**The next step.** Senior levels take this into shaping how research works across teams.

### Skill: Inclusive research

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: understand the diversity of users of
government services and the need to make services usable and accessible for everyone; and **work with
colleagues to include many kinds of users in appropriate research activities**.

**What that looks like here.** This is a real step up from the two levels below, which asked only for
awareness. Including many kinds of users takes budget, time and often a specialist recruiter — and
the people hardest to recruit are usually the ones the service serves worst.

**The next step.** Senior levels extend this into influencing how the organisation does inclusive
research.

### Skill: Research management, leadership and assurance

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: ensure research has a clearly defined scope
and purpose; get advice or support from someone more experienced when you need help to ensure research
is defined before it starts; build your understanding of good practice standards for user research;
and improve your practice through reflection and feedback.

**What that looks like here.** *Defined before it starts* is the discipline. Research that begins
because a round is due, rather than because there is a question, produces findings nobody uses.

**The next step.** At senior user researcher you evaluate the quality of research against accepted
professional standards, explain what good user research practice involves, and give constructive
feedback to other researchers so work meets those standards.

### Skill: Stakeholder relationship management

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: identify important stakeholders and
communicate with them clearly and regularly; tailor communication to stakeholders' needs and work with
them to build relationships while meeting user needs; build and reach consensus with stakeholders; and
work to improve stakeholder relationships using evidence to explain decisions.

**What that looks like here.** *While meeting user needs* is the tension. A stakeholder relationship
kept by softening findings is not a success, and knowing where that line sits is the judgement this
skill is really about.

**The next step.** At senior you work with the team to develop and maintain an understanding of
stakeholders and to implement communications strategies, identify and resolve issues, influence
stakeholders, and build long-term strategic relationships.

### Skill: User-centred practice and advocacy

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: describe and use a range of user-centred
practices; and advocate for the user by sharing research insights on user needs and changing user
behaviour with your team.

**What that looks like here.** Advocacy is now doing rather than explaining. The most effective form
is usually not argument but exposure — getting the team in front of users often enough that they stop
needing to be told.

**The next step.** At senior you evaluate and choose approaches for building your team's understanding
of the user, advocate for user research with sceptical colleagues, help inexperienced teams adopt
user-centred practices, identify the most important challenges and opportunities for your service, and
align research plans to team priorities.
