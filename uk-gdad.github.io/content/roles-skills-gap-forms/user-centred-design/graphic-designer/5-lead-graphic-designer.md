# Lead graphic designer - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a lead graphic designer in the UK Government Digital and Data
profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out what you do now,
where the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this level as an expert practitioner who influences and mentors others. It
expects you to work with service managers and programme directors to develop design concepts; set
direction and **assure the quality of design delivery across teams**; and lead multiple or highly
complex services.

Assuring quality across teams is the accountability that changes the shape of the week. You are
answerable for design work you did not do and may not have seen, which only works if you have
built something — a critique rhythm, a set of standards, a group of designers who hold each other
to them — rather than relying on your own eyes.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Seven skills, each with an explanation, a rating and
some questions. Expect about 30 minutes.

Where a question asks for a rating, use this scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | You have not worked with this |
| 1 | Awareness | You know what it is and why it matters |
| 2 | Working | You do it with support |
| 3 | Practitioner | You do it unsupported, and handle the usual exceptions |
| 4 | Expert | You set the approach and develop others in it |

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not the remit on your job description.
- **A 4 is a claim about other people** — that someone else's practice changed because of you and
  would stay changed if you left. Mentoring and embedding practice are written into your level, so
  several are reasonable.

## About this role level

**The role: graphic designer.** A graphic designer creates the graphic elements that underpin
interaction and service design. You use layout, spacing, colour, type and iconography to ensure
that content is legible and readable and that users see and understand interactions.

**Your level: lead graphic designer.** You are an expert practitioner who influences and mentors
others. The framework expects that you:

- work with service managers and programme directors to develop design concepts
- set direction and assure the quality of design delivery across teams
- lead multiple or highly complex services

**Where this level sits.** This role has six levels:

1. Associate graphic designer
2. Junior graphic designer
3. Graphic designer
4. Senior graphic designer
5. **Lead graphic designer** — you are here
6. Head of graphic design

Your next step is head of graphic design: influencing both design and organisational strategy and
priorities, collaborating with counterparts across government, and focusing on the conditions and
environment designers need to work effectively.

**One thing worth knowing about this role.** Four of your seven skills are worded **identically at
head of graphic design** — design communication, designing for everyone, evidence-based design and
iterative design. Three change: designing strategically (which becomes embedding design in
organisational strategy), designing together (running sessions with senior leaders and connecting
teams across boundaries), and leading design (setting a vision across a whole organisation and
improving design maturity).

If you want the head role, those three are the list — and all three are about reach beyond your
own teams rather than deeper craft.

**The seven skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Design communication
2. Designing for everyone
3. Designing strategically
4. Designing together
5. Evidence-based design
6. Iterative design
7. Leading design

## Job skills

Seven skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the level expects, what it looks
like in practice, and how it compares with head of graphic design.

### Skill: Design communication

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: gain support for design decisions and
design strategy from senior leaders; communicate design strategy across organisational boundaries;
coach others in how to effectively communicate design; and develop or introduce more effective
methods of communicating design and **working in the open**.

**Same wording at head of graphic design.**

**What that looks like here.** Working in the open — publishing what you are doing and why, in a
form other teams and departments can find — is named explicitly. It is the cheapest way to
influence beyond your own organisation and the first thing that stops when people are busy.

### Skill: Designing for everyone

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: **measurably improve** the practice of

before and after — an audit, a defect count, an assessment outcome — rather than an impression

### Skill: Designing strategically

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: help a team understand how
user-centred design helps it meet its goals; help teams align their work to the goals and vision
of their organisation; use risks, opportunities and constraints in technology, systems and policy
to shape design; and identify and create new design patterns and components.

**Same wording as senior graphic designer.** This skill does not change between those two levels.

**The next step.** At head this becomes ensuring design is part of developing and implementing
organisational strategy, working with leaders so design is included in policy, technology and
delivery processes early enough to inform decisions, and enabling use of patterns and components
across the organisation.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: advise others how to effectively plan

across organisational boundaries, coach designers in involving stakeholders at any level, and

### Skill: Leading design

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: lead and co-ordinate design work in a
team; advocate for user-centred design with **leaders outside of digital and data**; create an
inclusive, productive environment for designers to work in; mentor and coach other designers; and
iterate and improve design processes within your team.

**What that looks like here.** The *with support* qualifier that persisted through the two levels
below has finally gone. Advocating outside digital and data — to policy, operations, finance — is
where design either becomes part of how the organisation works or stays a delivery function.

**The next step.** At head you set a vision for and lead design across multiple teams or a whole
organisation, work with senior leaders across the organisation or wider public sector, support a
culture of learning and experimentation, and measurably improve design maturity.
