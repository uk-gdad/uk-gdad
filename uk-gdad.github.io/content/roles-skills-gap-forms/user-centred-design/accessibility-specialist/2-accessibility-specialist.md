# Accessibility specialist - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as an accessibility specialist in the UK Government
Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can
set out what you do now, where the gaps are, and what would close them.

The framework describes this level as being able to support teams in creating accessible
services. It expects you to coach and mentor more junior colleagues; support in providing
training materials and learning content; perform accessibility audits; and engage with and
contribute to the cross-government accessibility community.

Auditing is now yours. At the level below you assisted with testing under supervision; here
you produce the audit that a team will work from, which means your judgement about what
genuinely fails carries real weight.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Seven skills, each with an explanation, a rating
and some questions. Expect about 30 minutes.

## Advisory

This skills gap form is created by AI and is work in progress for educational purposes. If
you intend to use it in any way, please consult with your organization's appropriate

## How to fill in this form

This is a static web page. There is nothing to type into and nothing to send from here —
your answers never touch this site.

Print it, copy the questions into a document, or paste them into whatever your organisation
uses for development conversations. Every question is numbered, so answers travel back on
their own.

Worth knowing:

- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a finding.
- **Answer about the job you do**, not the one on the job description.
- **"I haven't had the chance" is a real answer**, and more useful than a generous reading
  of a thin example.
- **You do not have to disclose anything about yourself.** Nothing here asks about your own
  circumstances, and nothing here should be read as asking.
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers, what they are used
  for, or how long they are kept, ask before you fill it in.

## About this role level

**The role: accessibility specialist.** You provide support, advice and guidance to other
roles in the Government Digital and Data profession about how to create accessible digital
services. You use knowledge of accessibility guidelines, provide technical expertise, and
bring the voice of disabled users to ensure that product teams make digital services that
can be used by everyone.

**Your level: accessibility specialist.** You can support teams in creating accessible
services. The framework expects that you:

- coach and mentor more junior colleagues
- support in providing training materials and learning content
- perform accessibility audits
- engage with and contribute to the cross-government accessibility community

**Where this level sits.** This role has four levels:

1. Junior accessibility specialist
2. **Accessibility specialist** — you are here
3. Senior accessibility specialist
4. Head of accessibility

Your next step is senior accessibility specialist: working with people at a range of levels
to embed accessibility into what teams produce, inputting into accessibility strategy,
creating and delivering training, and possibly managing or leading individuals or
sub-teams.

**Two things worth knowing about this role.**

First, your level is where two skills appear for the first time — *Governance and assurance
(accessibility)* and *Leadership and guidance*. Junior specialists do not have them, so
this is new ground rather than something you should already be strong at.

Second, and more oddly: **your published wording for *Technical understanding
(accessibility)* is identical to the head of accessibility's**, the top level of this role.
Senior's is more demanding than both. That is how the framework publishes it, and it means
technical depth is not where the case for the top of this role is made.

**The seven skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Communicating information
2. Consultancy
3. Governance and assurance (accessibility)
4. Leadership and guidance
5. Technical understanding (accessibility)
6. Testing
7. User focus

### Skill: Communicating information

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: listen to the needs of design and
business stakeholders and interpret information; take part in discussions within a
multidisciplinary team; be an advocate for the team externally and manage differing
perspectives; and shape and share communications relevant to the audience.

**What that looks like here.** The same audit finding needs three framings — for a
developer, for a designer, and for a delivery manager deciding whether it fits in the
sprint. None of those framings should change what is true about the barrier.

**The next step.** At senior you work collaboratively and build relationships, identify
issues through health checks, manage stakeholder expectations, handle proactive and
reactive communication, and host or moderate difficult discussions with senior
stakeholders.

### Skill: Consultancy

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: provide advice, guidance and
recommendations based on your specialist knowledge and experience; propose methodologies to
follow and approaches to implementation; and frame problems so they can easily be
understood, and troubleshoot where required, to support the business in operating more
effectively.

**What that looks like here.** The advice is now yours rather than borrowed. Proposing an
approach means saying *how* to fix something in a way that fits how the team actually
works — a fix that requires a rewrite nobody will fund is not really a recommendation.

**The next step.** At senior you provide advice from significant specialist knowledge, and
lead the definition of guidance, informing how the organisation approaches delivery.

### Skill: Governance and assurance (accessibility)

**What the framework expects at this level.** One thing: analyse and provide feedback on
governance and project documents, and on governance boards, according to a predefined
framework, assessing them against accessibility standards.

**What that looks like here.** This skill is new at your level. It means accessibility gets
considered when decisions are made rather than checked after they are implemented — which is
where it is cheapest to fix and most often forgotten.

**The next step.** At senior you challenge teams, make full use of existing arrangements,
and build strong relationships to reduce or remove the risk of not meeting accessibility
standards.

### Skill: Leadership and guidance

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: contribute to best practice
guidelines; understand the sustainability and consequences of your decisions and make
decisions characterised by managed levels of risk and complexity; and resolve technical
disputes between wider peers and indirect stakeholders, taking into account all views and
opinions.

**What that looks like here.** Also new at your level. *Resolve technical disputes* is the
demanding item — two developers disagreeing about the right markup, with you as the person
whose answer settles it. That requires both technical grounding and the willingness to be
wrong in public occasionally.

**The next step.** At senior you make decisions with medium risk and complexity, build
consensus between services or independent stakeholders, fix problems in team dynamics,
give feedback well, and help form motivated teams.

### Skill: Technical understanding (accessibility)

**What the framework expects at this level.** Six things: demonstrate knowledge and
communicate the requirements of accessibility standards and legislation; advocate
appropriate technical solutions to a range of accessibility issues; perform detailed audits
of websites, services and documents, and document findings clearly for others to work from;
be an advocate for the people affected by accessibility barriers across the department;
provide introductory awareness and training about accessibility; and demonstrate confidence
using one or more assistive technologies, and knowledge of others.

**A note on the wording.** This exact text also appears at **head of accessibility**, the
top of this role, while senior's is more demanding — excellent knowledge including edge
cases, deep knowledge of technologies such as ARIA, understanding how usability influences
accessibility, and confidence with a *range* of assistive technologies. That is how the
framework publishes it.

**What that looks like here.** Confidence with at least one assistive technology is the
concrete item. Using a screen reader well takes months of practice, and doing it badly
produces misleading results.

### Skill: Testing

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: review requirements and
specifications, and define test conditions; identify issues and risks associated with work;
and analyse and report test activities and results.

**What that looks like here.** Defining the test conditions is the change from junior. You
decide what will be tested and against what, which means deciding what will not be tested —
and being explicit about that in the report.

**The next step.** At senior you manage testing activities within development or
integration work, manage risks and take preventative action, and manage customer relations.

### Skill: User focus

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: identify and engage with users
or stakeholders to collate user needs evidence; understand and define research that fits
user needs; and use quantitative and qualitative data about users to turn user focus into
outcomes.

**What that looks like here.** Your role description says you bring the voice of disabled
users to product teams. Evidence from actual disabled users carries far more weight than a
guideline reference, and getting that evidence into audits is what turns compliance work
into design improvement.

**The next step.** At senior you collaborate with user researchers and represent users
internally, explain the difference between user needs and desires, champion research that
covers all users, and guide others on understanding user stories.
