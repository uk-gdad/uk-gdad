# Principal DevOps engineer - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a principal DevOps engineer on the technical
track in the UK Government Digital and Data profession. It has come from human
resources or your manager, and it exists to establish where the gap sits between
what you do now and what the organisation needs from someone at this level.

Principal is the top of this role. The framework describes it as leading and
planning development across large or multiple teams and defining the strategic
vision for delivery, and the accountabilities are notably weighted towards
people and foresight: identifying, testing and championing emerging
technologies; ensuring security, stability and capacity are embedded rather than
inspected; developing capability by rewarding high performers; shaping career
paths and recruiting; and identifying skills gaps and important dependencies
within technical teams.

That last one is worth pausing on, because it is what a form like this exists
for. Finding out where the capability gaps are — and where the organisation
depends on one person who has not been replaced — is named as your job.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked, and no answer is compared with a
colleague's. Each of the ten skills gets an explanation, a note on how the
management role at this level differs, a rating and some questions. Expect 45
minutes to an hour.

## Advisory

This skills gap form is created by AI and is work in progress for educational

## About this role level

**The role: development operations (DevOps) engineer.** Commonly called DevOps
engineers, development operations engineers support the development and
operation of software through tools, environments and practices — managing tools
and testing environments, central code control, maintaining development
standards, and writing software that automates systems.

**Your level: principal DevOps engineer.** At this level you lead and plan
development across large or multiple teams, defining the strategic vision for
delivery. The framework expects that you:

- identify, test and champion the adoption of emerging technologies
- ensure that security, stability and capacity are embedded in the development
  and deployment of services
- develop a team's capability by rewarding high performers
- shape career paths and recruit the right talent
- identify skills gaps and important dependencies within technical teams

*Embedded* is the word that matters in the second one. Security, stability and
capacity being embedded means they are properties of how things get built, not
gates that catch problems afterwards.

**There is no level above this one.** The DevOps engineer role has nine levels
and this is the highest on the technical track. Where people go after principal
is a question about other roles and professions in the framework, or about
leadership outside it, and the framework does not define that as a next step for
this role — so this form does not invent one. What each skill block gives
instead is how the management role at this level is worded differently, which is
published and is a real choice.

**Where this level sits:**

1. Apprentice DevOps engineer
2. Junior DevOps engineer
3. DevOps engineer
4. Senior DevOps engineer
5. Senior DevOps engineer (management)
6. Lead DevOps engineer
7. Lead DevOps engineer (management)
8. **Principal DevOps engineer** — you are here
9. Principal DevOps engineer (management)

**The two roles at this level.** Four skills are worded differently:

| | Technical role (you) | Management role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Availability and capacity management | Ensure the correct implementation of standards and procedures; initiate remedial action | **Lead policy and strategy** for service and capacity management |
| Development process optimisation | Analyse processes; implement improvements; lead a team of experts; help set policy | **Set the strategy and manage resource allocation** for solution development programmes; work with client functions |
| Service support | Identify, locate and fix **complex** faults, and advise others on methodologies | Identify, locate and fix service faults |
| Systems integration | **Establish standards** across the service product life cycle and manage the resources for the integration function | Define the integration build; co-ordinate build activities |

The pattern holds all the way up this role: the management track owns strategy
and resourcing, while your track keeps the deepest technical practice — the hard
faults, and the governance of how integration is actually done. If your job is
closer to that description, use the
[principal DevOps engineer (management) form](9-principal-devops-engineer-management.md)
and say so in Q45.

## Job skills

Ten skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the framework

### Skill: Availability and capacity management

**What this means.** Whether services and the toolchain stay up and cope with
load — and, at this level, whether the standards and procedures for that are
genuinely implemented across the estate.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: ensure the correct
implementation of availability and capacity management standards and procedures;
identify capacity issues and implement the required changes; and initiate
remedial action.

**What that looks like here.** You know which services would not survive a
demand spike, and that knowledge came from evidence rather than from asking
people whether they felt confident. Remedial work gets started and funded
because you called for it.

**The management role differs.** It is worded around *leading policy and
strategy* for service and capacity management, and leading the identification
and implementation of required changes — setting what the standards should be
rather than seeing them applied.

### Skill: Development process optimisation

**What this means.** Improving how software gets built and released, as a
governed programme with people and policy behind it.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: analyse current
processes; identify and implement opportunities to optimise them; lead and
develop a team of experts to deliver service improvements; and help to evaluate
and establish requirements for the implementation of changes by setting policy
and standards.

**What that looks like here.** You know the delivery numbers across your area as
numbers. Improvement work has named people and a place in the plan. The policy
that governs how teams release is one you helped write, and you can say what it
prevents.

**The management role differs.** It goes further into strategy: setting the
strategy and managing resource allocation for solution development programmes,
and working with client functions to establish business requirements and then
initiate and lead those programmes.

### Skill: Information security

security practice your teams follow is one you set, and it holds when you are
not watching.

### Skill: Programming and build (software engineering)

**What this means.** Writing real software: reviewing specifications, then
designing, coding, testing, and documenting programs of genuine complexity.

**What the framework expects at this level.** That you collaborate with others
when necessary to review specifications, and use the agreed specifications to
design, code, test and document programs or scripts of medium-to-high
complexity. This is the senior wording — the framework does not raise it at any
level of this role, on either track.

**What that looks like here.** You can still read and reason about what your
teams build, and be corrected on detail. You occasionally build the hardest
thing yourself, which is how the reasoning stays honest.

**The management role differs.** It is worded identically. If deepening
engineering practice is what you want, no promotion inside this role supplies
it — that is worth naming rather than discovering.

### Skill: Service support

**What this means.** Keeping live services and the toolchain working, being the
person others bring their hardest faults to, and making sure that capability
does not depend on you.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: identify, locate and
fix complex service faults; and advise others on different methodologies and
types of service support.

**What that looks like here.** You are brought in on faults that have defeated
other people, and you are useful without becoming the centre of every incident.
What you have learned over years is written down where others can reach it. You
have shaped how support itself is done.

**The management role differs.** It carries the plain wording — identify, locate
and fix service faults — without the complexity or the advisory expectation.
Yours is the deeper one.

### Skill: Systems design

**What this means.** Deciding the shape of systems — components, boundaries,
technology — and the standards by which those decisions get made.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: design systems of
medium risk, impact and complexity; select appropriate design standards, methods
and tools and ensure they are applied effectively; and review other people's
designs for appropriate technology, efficient use of resources and integration
across systems.

**What that looks like here.** You have designed something substantial that
others built. Design practice across teams follows standards you selected. You
review designs and change their direction without overruling their authors.

**The management role differs.** It is worded identically. Note also that this
role's systems design expectation stays at *medium* risk even at principal —
unlike some other framework roles, it does not rise to high risk or enterprise
architecture. If designing at that level is what you want, it sits outside this
role.

### Skill: Systems integration

**What this means.** At this level, governing how integration is done: the
standards across the life cycle, whether practitioners follow them, and the
resources that make the function work.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things: establish standards and
procedures across a service product life cycle, including the development product
life cycle, and ensure practitioners adhere to them; and manage resources to
ensure the systems integration function works effectively.

**What that looks like here.** There is an integration standard, you established
it, and you can say how you know it is followed. You have argued for the people
or budget integration needs rather than letting it be absorbed into everyone's
spare time.

**The management role differs.** It stays at defining and co-ordinating the
integration build. The governing expectation is on your track, which is unusual
and worth knowing.

### Skill: User focus

**What this means.** Keeping the people who use the service at the centre of
technical decisions — the public using the services, and the engineers whose
working life your toolchain shapes.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: collaborate with user
researchers and represent users internally; explain the difference between user
needs and user desires; champion user research so it covers all users;
prioritise and define approaches to understanding the user story, guiding others
in doing so; and recommend the best tools and methods.

**What that looks like here.** You have established what your engineers need
rather than assumed it, and acted on it. You can connect infrastructure decisions
to what they do for the public. You represent users in rooms where no user has
been mentioned.

**The management role differs.** It is worded identically at this level.

## Legal compliance

Government services carry legal duties, and DevOps work sits close to several:
personal data lives in the environments you govern, access control is a security
matter, and logs are records. At principal you may be the last technical person
who could have caught a problem before it became public. These questions ask
about awareness, training and support — not about whether you can state the law
correctly, and not as a check on your conduct.
