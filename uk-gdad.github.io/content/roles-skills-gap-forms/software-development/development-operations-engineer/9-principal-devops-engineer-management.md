# Principal DevOps engineer (management) - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a principal DevOps engineer on the
management track in the UK Government Digital and Data profession. Human
resources or your manager has sent it so that you can set out what you do now,
where the gaps are, and what would close them.

Principal is the top of this role, and on your track the framework's emphasis is
strategy and resources. You lead policy and strategy for service and capacity
management. You set the strategy and manage resource allocation for solution
development programmes, working with client functions to establish what the
business actually needs and then proposing, initiating and leading the
programmes that meet it. Alongside that sit the accountabilities you share with
the technical role: championing emerging technology, embedding security,
stability and capacity, developing capability by rewarding high performers,
shaping career paths and recruiting, and identifying skills gaps and important
dependencies in technical teams.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked, and no answer is compared with a
colleague's. Each of the ten skills gets an explanation, a note on how the
technical role at this level differs, a rating and some questions. Expect 45
minutes to an hour.

## How to fill in this form

You can fill this form in on screen: the boxes below take typing, the tick
boxes tick, and each skill has a rating to choose. Your answers are saved in
this browser, on this device, so you can close the page and come back to them.
Nothing is sent anywhere.

When you have finished, use Export TSV or Export as JSON at the top of the
page to take your answers with you, or print the page, or copy them into
whatever your organisation uses for development conversations. Every question
is numbered, so answers travel back on their own. Clear answers empties the
form and deletes the saved copy, which is worth doing on a computer other
people use.

Worth knowing:

- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a finding.
- **Answer for yourself, not for your organisation.** Where a question is about
  programmes or people, it asks what you did.
- **"I haven't had the chance" is a real answer** at this level too.
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers, what they
  are used for, or how long they are kept, ask before you fill it in.

## About this role level

**The role: development operations (DevOps) engineer.** Commonly called DevOps
engineers, development operations engineers support the development and
operation of software through tools, environments and practices — managing tools
and testing environments, central code control, maintaining development
standards, and writing software that automates systems.

**Your level: principal DevOps engineer (management).** At this level you lead
and plan development across large or multiple teams, defining the strategic
vision for delivery. The framework expects that you:

- identify, test and champion the adoption of emerging technologies
- ensure that security, stability and capacity are embedded in the development
  and deployment of services
- develop a team's capability by rewarding high performers
- shape career paths and recruit the right talent
- identify skills gaps and important dependencies within technical teams

Identifying skills gaps and important dependencies is named as your work — which
is exactly what a form like this is for. It is worth applying the same method to
your own answers as you would to your teams'.

**There is no level above this one.** The DevOps engineer role has nine levels
and this is the highest on the management track. Where people go after principal
is a question about other roles and professions in the framework, or about
leadership outside it, and the framework does not define that as a next step for
this role — so this form does not invent one. What each skill block gives
instead is how the technical role at this level is worded differently, which is
published and is a real choice.

**The two roles at this level.** Four skills are worded differently:

| | Technical role | Management role (you) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Availability and capacity management | Ensure the correct implementation of standards and procedures | **Lead policy and strategy** for service and capacity management; lead the identification and implementation of required changes |
| Development process optimisation | Analyse processes; implement improvements; lead a team of experts; help set policy | **Set the strategy and manage resource allocation** for solution development programmes; work with client functions to establish requirements and lead programmes |
| Service support | Identify, locate and fix **complex** faults, and advise others on methodologies | Identify, locate and fix service faults |
| Systems integration | **Establish standards** across the service product life cycle and manage the resources for the integration function | Define the integration build; co-ordinate build activities; support integration testing |

Your track owns strategy and resourcing; the technical track keeps the deepest
technical practice — the hard faults and the governance of integration. If your
job is closer to that description, use the
[principal DevOps engineer form](8-principal-devops-engineer.md) and say so in
Q46.

### Skill: Availability and capacity management

**What this means.** Whether services and the toolchain stay up and cope with
load — and, on your track, what the policy and strategy for that should be in
the first place.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things, and this is the
furthest the framework takes this skill anywhere in the role: lead policy and
strategy for service and capacity management; and lead the identification and
implementation of required changes.

**What that looks like here.** The availability targets your organisation works
to are ones you set, and you can say why those numbers rather than tighter or
looser ones. Capacity planning has a rhythm and a budget rather than being an
annual panic.

**The technical role differs.** It is worded around ensuring the correct
implementation of standards and procedures and initiating remedial action — the
enforcement rather than the policy.

### Skill: Development process optimisation

**What this means.** Deciding which improvement programmes exist, what they are
for, and what resources they get.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things, and this is the skill
that defines your track: set the strategy and manage resource allocation for
solution development programmes; and work with client functions to establish
business requirements and identify, propose, initiate and lead those programmes.

**What that looks like here.** You have proposed a programme that did not exist
and got it funded. You allocate people and money across competing improvements
and can explain the reasoning. You talk to the parts of the organisation that
are not technology, in their terms, and come back with requirements rather than
complaints.

**The technical role differs.** It stops at analysing processes, implementing
improvements, leading a team of experts and helping set policy — delivering
improvement rather than deciding which improvements exist.

### Skill: Information security

### Skill: Programming and build (software engineering)

### Skill: Service support

**What this means.** Keeping live services and the toolchain working —
identifying, locating, and fixing faults.

**What the framework expects at this level.** That you identify, locate and fix
service faults. This is the plain wording; the technical role at your level

methodology.

becoming its centre. Incident actions get resourced rather than filed. You can
name failure classes your area no longer has.

**The technical role differs.** It goes deeper — complex faults, and advising

### Skill: Systems design

**What this means.** Deciding the shape of systems — components, boundaries,
technology — and the standards by which those decisions get made.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: design systems of
medium risk, impact and complexity; select appropriate design standards, methods
and tools and ensure they are applied effectively; and review other people's
designs.

**What that looks like here.** Design practice across your teams follows
standards you selected. You review designs and change their direction without
overruling their authors.

**The technical role differs.** It is worded identically. Note that this role's
systems design expectation stays at *medium* risk even at principal — it does not
rise to high risk or enterprise architecture anywhere in this role.

### Skill: Systems integration

**What this means.** Making separate systems work together — defining the build,
co-ordinating it across teams, and testing the seams.

**What the framework expects at this level.** That you define the integration
build, co-ordinate build activities across systems, and understand how to
undertake and support integration testing.

**What that looks like here.** You still understand how integration is done
across your programmes well enough to challenge it. Cross-organisation
dependencies are managed rather than hoped about.

**The technical role differs.** It goes further — establishing integration
standards across the service product life cycle and managing the resources that
make the function work. On this role, integration governance sits on the
technical track, which is worth knowing if that is what you want.

### Skill: User focus

**What this means.** Keeping the people who use the service at the centre of
decisions — the public using the services, and the engineers whose working life
your programmes shape.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: collaborate with user
researchers and represent users internally; explain the difference between user
needs and user desires; champion user research so it covers all users;
prioritise and define approaches to understanding the user story, guiding others
in doing so; and recommend the best tools and methods.

**What that looks like here.** You have established what your engineers need
rather than assumed it, and funded something because of it. You can connect
programme decisions to what they do for the public. You protect research capacity
in planning rounds.

**The technical role differs.** It is worded identically at this level.

## Legal compliance

Government services carry legal duties, and DevOps work sits close to several:
personal data lives in the environments your programmes create, access control
is a security matter, and logs are records. At this level your governance may be
what the organisation relies on. These questions ask about awareness, training
and support — not about whether you can state the law correctly, and not as a
check on your conduct.
