# Lead DevOps engineer - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a lead DevOps engineer on the technical
track in the UK Government Digital and Data profession. It has come from human
resources or your manager, and it exists to establish where the gap sits between
what you do now and what the organisation needs from someone at this level.

The framework describes a lead DevOps engineer as leading one or a small number
of related project teams and contributing to the strategic direction. Three
accountabilities carry the weight: acting as a *technical product owner* with
medium to long term strategies for product lines, providing technical leadership
through coaching and mentoring, and leading the sharing of knowledge and good
practice. Treating the toolchain as a product with a roadmap, users and a
lifecycle — rather than as a pile of scripts that grew — is the shift this level
is really about.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked, and no answer is compared with a
colleague's. Each of the ten skills gets an explanation, a note on what changes
at principal, a rating and some questions. Expect about 45 minutes.

## Advisory

This skills gap form is created by AI and is work in progress for educational
purposes. If you intend to use it in any way, please consult with your

You can fill this form in on screen: the boxes below take typing, the tick

When you have finished, use Export TSV or Export as JSON at the top of the

whatever your organisation uses for development conversations. Every question
is numbered, so answers travel back on their own. Clear answers empties the

Worth knowing:

- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a finding.
- **"The teams do this" is not an answer to "what do you do".** Name your part.
- **"I haven't had the chance" is a real answer**, and more useful than a
  generous reading of a thin example.
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers, what they
  are used for, or how long they are kept, ask before you fill it in.

## About this role level

**The role: development operations (DevOps) engineer.** Commonly called DevOps
engineers, development operations engineers support the development and
operation of software through tools, environments and practices — managing tools
and testing environments, central code control, maintaining development
standards, and writing software that automates systems.

**Your level: lead DevOps engineer.** At this level you lead one or a small
number of related project teams and contribute to the development of the
strategic direction. The framework expects that you:

- act as a technical product owner, developing medium to long term strategies
  for product lines
- provide technical leadership and guidance through coaching and mentoring
- lead the sharing of knowledge and good practice

*Technical product owner* is the phrase to sit with. It implies a roadmap, a set
of users whose needs you have actually established, decisions about what not to
build, and a view further out than the current quarter.

**The two roles at this level.** The framework defines a technical role and a
management role with identical accountabilities. This form is the technical one.
Four skills are worded differently:

| | Technical role (you) | Management role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Availability and capacity management | Manage service components to meet business needs and key performance indicators | Ensure the correct implementation of standards and procedures; initiate remedial action |
| Development process optimisation | Work under guidance to identify opportunities and contribute to implementation | Analyse processes; identify and implement opportunities; lead and develop a team of experts; help set policy and standards |
| Service support | Identify, locate and fix **complex** service faults, and advise others on methodologies | Identify, locate and fix service faults |
| Systems integration | **Establish standards and procedures** across the service product life cycle, ensure practitioners adhere to them, and manage resources so the integration function works | Define the integration build; co-ordinate build activities; support integration testing |

The split is unusual and worth understanding: your track owns the harder
fault-finding and the governance of integration, while the management track owns
capacity governance and process improvement as a programme. If your job is
closer to that description, use the
[lead DevOps engineer (management) form](7-lead-devops-engineer-management.md)
and say so in Q45.

**Where this level sits.** The DevOps engineer role has nine levels:

1. Apprentice DevOps engineer
2. Junior DevOps engineer
3. DevOps engineer
4. Senior DevOps engineer
5. Senior DevOps engineer (management)
6. **Lead DevOps engineer** — you are here
7. Lead DevOps engineer (management)
8. Principal DevOps engineer
9. Principal DevOps engineer (management)

Your step up on this track is principal DevOps engineer, at level 8. Four skills
change there, and the pattern is consistent: from doing it well to governing how
it is done. Availability and capacity management becomes ensuring standards are
implemented and initiating remedial action. Development process optimisation
becomes analysing processes, leading a team of experts and setting policy.
Information security adds review and quality assurance of other people's work.
Prototyping becomes coaching others and strategic service design end to end. The
principal accountabilities add the rest — championing emerging technology,
embedding security, stability and capacity across services, and developing
capability through career paths, recruitment, and skills gap analysis.

**The skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Availability and capacity management
2. Development process optimisation
3. Information security
4. Modern development standards
5. Programming and build (software engineering)
6. Prototyping
7. Service support
8. Systems design
9. Systems integration
10. User focus

## Job skills

Ten skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives the expectation at this
level, what it looks like in practice, and what changes at principal.

### Skill: Availability and capacity management

**What this means.** Whether services and the toolchain stay up and cope with the
load put on them.

**What the framework expects at this level.** That you manage service components
so they meet business needs and key performance indicators.

**What that looks like here.** The indicators your services are held to are ones
you helped choose. You make architectural decisions on capacity grounds rather
than configuration tweaks. You know which parts of the estate have never met
real load.

**The next step.** At principal this becomes governance: ensuring the correct
implementation of availability and capacity management standards and procedures,
identifying capacity issues and implementing the required changes, and
initiating remedial action.

### Skill: Development process optimisation

**What this means.** Improving how software gets built and released so that good
work reaches users faster and more safely.

**What the framework expects at this level.** That you work under guidance to
identify process optimisation opportunities, and contribute to implementing the
solutions. This is a narrower expectation than most lead DevOps jobs actually
carry, and narrower than the management track at your own level.

**What that looks like here.** You know the delivery numbers across your teams.
You have contributed to improvements someone else led. You can name what the
last change bought.

**The next step.** One of the largest changes at principal: analysing current
processes, identifying *and implementing* opportunities, leading and developing a
team of experts to deliver service improvements, and helping establish
requirements for change by setting policy and standards.

### Skill: Information security

**What this means.** Building systems that resist attack and contain damage, by

**What the framework expects at this level.** That you design solutions and

take alone.

solutions and services, and leading others in using appropriate information
security practices — assuring other people's security work rather than only your

### Skill: Programming and build (software engineering)

**What this means.** Writing real software: reviewing specifications, then
designing, coding, testing, and documenting programs of genuine complexity.

**What the framework expects at this level.** That you collaborate with others

detail to be corrected by the people you lead.

### Skill: Prototyping

**What this means.** Building rough versions to learn from, disposably, so
decisions rest on evidence rather than seniority.

**What the framework expects at this level.** That you approach prototyping as a
team activity, actively soliciting prototypes and testing them with others;
establish design patterns and iterate them; and use a variety of prototyping
methods, choosing the most appropriate.

**What that looks like here.** You ask for prototypes rather than opinions,
including from people senior to you. Patterns your teams share get revised. You
have used a prototype to change a decision above your level.

**The next step.** At principal it becomes sharing best practice and coaching
others in prototyping, and looking at strategic service design end to end —
prototyping how a whole delivery capability should work, not how a component
should.

### Skill: Service support

### Skill: Systems design

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: design systems of

### Skill: Systems integration

**What this means.** At this level, governing how integration is done rather
than doing it: the standards, the adherence, and the resources that make the
function work.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things, and this is the skill
where your track goes furthest: establish standards and procedures across a
service product life cycle, including the development product life cycle, and
ensure practitioners adhere to them; and manage resources to ensure the systems
integration function works effectively.

**What that looks like here.** There is an integration standard, you wrote it,
and you can say how you know it is followed. You have argued for the people or
budget that integration work needs, rather than absorbing it into everyone's
spare time.

**The next step.** Identical wording at principal DevOps engineer. Note that the
management track at your level does *not* carry this — it stays at defining and
co-ordinating the integration build.

### Skill: User focus

**What this means.** Keeping the people who use the service at the centre of
technical decisions — the public using the services, and the engineers using
your toolchain.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: collaborate with user
researchers and represent users internally; explain the difference between user
needs and user desires; champion user research so it covers all users;
prioritise and define approaches to understanding the user story, guiding others
in doing so; and recommend the best tools and methods.

**What that looks like here.** As technical product owner you have actually
established what your users need rather than assumed it. You treat engineers'
friction as evidence. You can connect toolchain decisions to what they do for
the public.

**The next step.** Identical wording at principal DevOps engineer.
