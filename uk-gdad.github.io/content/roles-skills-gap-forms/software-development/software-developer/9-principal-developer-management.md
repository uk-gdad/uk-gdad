# Principal developer (management) - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a principal developer on the management
track in the UK Government Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your
manager has sent it so that you can set out what you do now, where the gaps are,
and what would close them.

Principal is the top of this role, and on your track the framework's emphasis is
unmistakable. You set the strategy for solution development programmes and
manage the resources that deliver them. You work with client functions to
establish what the business actually needs, then propose, initiate and lead the
programmes that meet it. You govern availability and capacity standards, assure
other people's security work, and control design practice across an enterprise
architecture. Alongside that sit the accountabilities you share with the
technical role: deep expertise applied across multiple teams, capability built
through mobility, career paths and recruitment, and emerging technology
identified, tested, and championed.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked, and no answer is compared with a
colleague's. Each of your ten skills gets an explanation, a note on how the
technical role at this level differs, a rating and some questions. Expect 45
minutes to an hour.

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**. At this level the temptation is to
  rate the role rather than the practice; resist it.
- **A 4 is a claim about other people** — that someone else's practice changed
  because of you, and would stay changed if you moved on. A few are expected
  here. Ten would be worth interrogating.

## About this role level

**The role: software developer.** A software developer designs, runs and
improves software that meets user needs — writing clean, secure code following a
test-driven approach, and creating code that is open by default and easy for
others to reuse.

**Your level: principal developer (management).** A principal developer leads
and plans development across large or multiple teams. The framework expects that
you:

- are an expert in many technical areas, or a specialist with very deep
  knowledge in a particular technical area
- use that knowledge, and work with related disciplines, to keep multiple teams
  working effectively
- develop capability by facilitating internal mobility, shaping career paths and
  recruiting talent, and ensuring they collaborate
- identify, test and champion the adoption of emerging technologies

Internal mobility, career paths and recruitment are named as your work, not as
human resources' work you occasionally help with. Several questions below ask
about it directly.

**There is no level above this one.** Nine levels exist in the software
developer role, and this is the highest on the management track. Where people go
after principal is a question about other roles and professions in the framework,
or about leadership outside it, and the framework does not define that as a next
step for this role — so this form does not invent one. What each skill block
gives instead is how the technical role at this level is worded differently,
which is published and is a real choice.

**Where this level sits.** The software developer role has nine levels:

1. Apprentice developer
2. Junior developer
3. Developer
4. Senior developer
5. Senior developer (management)
6. Lead developer
7. Lead developer (management)
8. Principal developer
9. **Principal developer (management)** — you are here

**The two roles at this level.** Three skills are worded differently. Yours goes
further on availability and capacity governance and much further on development
process optimisation, where it becomes strategy and resource allocation for
programmes. The technical role goes further on programming and build, where it
carries technical responsibility across whole projects while yours stays at the
senior engineering wording. If your job is closer to that description, use the
[principal developer form](8-principal-developer.md) and say so in Q46.

**The skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

### Skill: Availability and capacity management

**What this means.** Whether services stay up and cope with load — and, on your
track, whether the standards and procedures for that are genuinely implemented
across the estate rather than merely published.

**What the framework expects at this level.** That you ensure the correct
implementation of availability and capacity management standards and procedures;
identify capacity issues and implement the required changes; and initiate
remedial action.

**What that looks like here.** You know which of your organisation's services
would not survive a demand spike, and that knowledge came from evidence rather
than from asking people whether they were confident. Remedial work gets
initiated and funded because you called for it.

**The technical role differs.** It is worded around managing service components
so they meet business needs and key performance indicators — the components
rather than the governance.

### Skill: Development process optimisation

**What this means.** Deciding which improvement programmes exist, what they are
for, and what resources they get.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Two things, and this is the skill
that defines your track: that you set the strategy and manage resource
allocation for solution development programmes; and that you work with client
functions to establish business requirements and identify, propose, initiate and
lead those programmes.

**What that looks like here.** You have proposed a programme that did not exist
and got it funded. You allocate people and money across competing improvements
and can explain the reasoning. You talk to the parts of the organisation that
are not technology, in their terms, and come back with requirements rather than
with complaints.

**The technical role differs.** It stops at analysing processes, implementing
improvements, leading a team of experts and helping to set policy and standards —
delivery of improvement rather than strategy and resourcing of it.

### Skill: Information security

**What this means.** Building services that resist attack and contain damage,

**What the framework expects at this level.** That you design, review and

information security practices.

what its authors could not see. You have stopped something going live. The
security practice your teams follow is one you set, and it holds when you are
not watching.

### Skill: Programming and build (software engineering)

**What this means.** The craft itself: reviewing specifications, then designing,
coding, testing, and documenting software of real complexity.

**What the framework expects at this level.** That you collaborate with others
when necessary to review specifications, and use the agreed specifications to
design, code, test and document programs or scripts of medium-to-high
complexity, using the right standards and tools. This is the senior wording, and
your track keeps it at every level above senior.

**What that looks like here.** You can still read and reason about the work your
teams produce, and you review specifications with judgement rather than with
deference.

**The technical role differs.** It advances: advising on how standards and
methods should be applied to ensure compliance, holding technical responsibility
for all stages of a project, and setting team standards for programming tools
and techniques. Being clear-eyed about which of these two descriptions fits your
practice — and which you want — is more useful at this level than a high rating.

### Skill: Systems design

**What this means.** Deciding the shape of systems at the highest risk band, and
governing how design is done across an organisation.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: design systems of
high risk, impact and business or technical complexity; control system design
practice within an enterprise or industry architecture; influence industry-based
models for the development of new technology applications; develop effective
implementation and procurement strategies consistent with business needs; and
ensure adherence to relevant technical strategies, policies, standards and
practices.

**What that looks like here.** Design practice across your teams follows an
architecture you help control. You have shaped how something gets bought, not
only how it gets built. You have influenced practice beyond your own
organisation — a cross-government group, a standard, a supplier's roadmap.

**The technical role differs.** It is worded identically at this level.

### Skill: User focus

**What this means.** Keeping the people who use the service at the centre of
decisions, including the ones taken far from them.

**What the framework expects at this level.** That you collaborate with user
researchers and can represent users internally; explain the difference between
user needs and user desires; champion user research so it covers all users;
prioritise and define approaches to understanding the user story and guide
others in doing so; and recommend the best tools and methods.

**What that looks like here.** You still watch research, at a level where nobody
would notice if you stopped. You represent users in rooms where no user has been
mentioned. When someone says "all users", you ask about the ones using assistive
technology, on a poor connection, or doing this once in their life under stress —
and you keep asking after it becomes tiresome.

**The technical role differs.** It is worded identically at this level.
