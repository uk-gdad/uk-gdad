# Senior quality assurance test analyst - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a senior quality assurance test analyst in
the UK Government Digital and Data profession. It has come from human resources
or your manager, and it exists to establish where the gap sits between what you
do now and what the organisation needs from someone at this level.

At senior the work turns outward. The framework has you influencing and guiding
others in test analysis, co-ordinating quality assurance activities across a
defined area, assessing the business impact of testing outcomes, and using what
you learn to improve how quality assurance is done. Much of your value now shows
up in other people's testing rather than your own — which is harder to evidence
and easier to lose track of.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked, and no answer is compared with a
colleague's. Each of the six skills gets an explanation, a note on what changes
at lead, a rating and some questions. Expect about 40 minutes.

- **A 4 is a claim about other people** — that someone else's practice changed

## About this role level

**The role: quality assurance test analyst.** A quality assurance test analyst
interprets testing needs, creating and executing appropriate tests to make sure
things work and match what was asked for — planning, designing and executing
tests, applying techniques and standards to learn about systems and reduce risk,
analysing artefacts and prototypes, recording defects, improving testing
practice, and recommending approaches.

**Your level: senior quality assurance test analyst.** At this level you
influence and guide others in test analysis, co-ordinating quality assurance
activities in a defined area, and you assess the business impact of testing
outcomes and use those insights to improve quality assurance processes. The
framework expects that you:

- use the most appropriate tools and techniques to analyse artefacts such as
  products, services, processes and change, to identify risks early
- work with others to support teams in developing approaches, plans and
  strategy, considering environments, data, tools and resources
- communicate chosen approaches, tools, techniques and outcomes to stakeholders
- guide others in quality assurance tools and techniques
- contribute to, and enable others to follow, quality assurance and testing
  standards

*Enable others to follow* is worth noticing. Standards that people cannot follow
because they lack the environment, the data or the time are not standards — they
are a reason to feel guilty. Part of this job is removing that gap.

**Where this level sits.** The quality assurance test analyst role has four
levels:

1. Associate quality assurance test analyst
2. Quality assurance test analyst
3. **Senior quality assurance test analyst** — you are here
4. Lead quality assurance test analyst

Your step up is lead, where every skill except test engineering moves from
guiding a defined area to setting standards across the organisation: setting test
design standards and making strategic decisions about techniques, leading and
coaching on defect processes, setting principles for test analysis across many
teams, and creating and managing multiple strategies.

**One thing worth knowing about this role.** The framework words *test
engineering* identically at all four levels — including at lead, where it still
says "with support". The deeper engineering expectation lives in the separate
test engineer role, not here.

**The skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Communicating between the technical and non-technical
2. Designing and executing tests
3. Managing, reporting and resolving defects
4. Test analysis
5. Test and quality planning
6. Test engineering

## Job skills

Six skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the level expects,
what it looks like in practice, and what changes at lead.

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What this means.** Interpreting what people need, managing what they expect,
and running the conversations that are difficult — including with people well
above your grade.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: listen to and
interpret the needs of technical and non-technical stakeholders and manage their
expectations; manage active and reactive communication; and support or host
difficult discussions within the team or with diverse senior stakeholders.

**What that looks like here.** You tell people about a quality risk before they
find it. You have hosted a conversation where a senior stakeholder wanted to ship
something you thought was not ready, and it stayed a professional discussion. You
adjust what you say for the audience without softening what is true.

**The next step.** At lead it becomes mediating and *strengthening* relationships
across all levels, moderating discussions about high risk and complex topics
under constrained timescales, and speaking for the testing community to large
audiences inside and outside the organisation.

### Skill: Designing and executing tests

**What this means.** Shaping how testing is done across your area — the types,
the techniques, the environments — and helping others do it well.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: set up suitable
environments; influence and guide the use of appropriate test types and
techniques to mitigate risk early; lead others in designing, building,
maintaining and executing tests aligned to user needs and requirements;
contribute to developing and implementing standards for designing and executing
tests; and improve test types and techniques through a structured process.

**What that looks like here.** Environments are yours to sort out, not to wait
for. You have changed how a team tests by guiding rather than instructing. When
you improve a technique, you do it deliberately and can say what improved.

**The next step.** At lead you *set* standards and influence organisational
decisions about test types and techniques, coach others in them, advocate for
continuous refinement, and make strategic decisions about new or improved
techniques in your area.

### Skill: Managing, reporting and resolving defects

**What this means.** Managing risk and defects across more than one team, and
learning from patterns rather than from individual failures.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: contribute to
developing standards for defect management processes; manage and escalate
dependencies, defects and risks across teams; contribute to mitigation and
contingency plans across teams; use defect patterns and trends to make
recommendations on testing and quality approaches, with support; and manage
stakeholder expectations and communications during defect resolution.

**What that looks like here.** You can see when two teams' defects are the same
underlying problem. You escalate with a recommendation rather than a complaint.
You have changed how something is tested because of what a trend showed.

**The next step.** At lead you *lead and coach* others in improving defect
processes, support others in assessing complex defects across the organisation,
coach in using patterns for tactical and strategic recommendations, and influence
improvements to quality processes.

### Skill: Test analysis

**What this means.** Finding the risk early, across teams, and building that
habit in other people.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: lead work with
stakeholders across teams to determine which functional and non-functional
quality characteristics add value; determine if an approach needs to change based
on effort and risk; ensure test needs are implemented early; use multiple
techniques to analyse complex information to identify risks; and coach others in
test analysis.

**What that looks like here.** You get the quality conversation happening before
the design is fixed — or you know exactly why you cannot. You change the approach
when the risk changes rather than following the plan. Your analysts analyse
better than they did a year ago.

**The next step.** At lead you lead and guide *multiple* teams, ensuring test
analysis happens early in the life cycle, advocate for risk-based analysis across
many teams, and set standards and principles for test analysis across the
organisation.

### Skill: Test and quality planning

**What this means.** Developing the approaches teams work to, feeding the
organisation's strategy, and making improvement something the data drives.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: work with teams to
develop and implement appropriate quality testing approaches, plans and
strategies; contribute to organisational quality testing strategies; implement
ways to capture data to drive continuous improvement; and advocate for full team
ownership of quality testing activities, encouraging early engagement.

**What that looks like here.** Teams have approaches they helped write rather
than approaches issued to them. You capture something — cycle time, escape rate,
where defects originate — and use it. You have argued, more than once, that
quality is the whole team's job.

**The next step.** At lead you create and manage *multiple* plans and strategies,
lead multiple teams in adopting them, advocate for early quality involvement in
organisational delivery processes, and guide teams across the organisation in
optimising their approaches using data.

### Skill: Test engineering

**What this means.** The processes, environments and tools that make testing
possible, including automated tests and the machinery that runs them.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: explain why testing
processes, environments and tools are important; follow test engineering
practices and standards, with support; and support the maintenance of automated
tests and tools required for testing.

**What that looks like here.** You keep to the engineering practices when you add
to the suite, and you can make the case for investment in environments and
tooling in terms leaders understand.

**The next step.** There is none within this role — the framework repeats this
wording at lead. The deeper expectation belongs to the test engineer role. If
you are effectively running the test engineering for your area, you are working
beyond the published expectation, and that is worth recording.

## Legal compliance

Testing sits close to two legal duties in particular: public sector services must
meet an accessibility standard in law, and testing is where that gets checked;
and test data is real data more often than people admit, including in defect
reports. At senior you may be the person others assume has checked. These
questions ask about awareness, training and support, not about whether you can
state the law correctly, and not as a check on your conduct.
