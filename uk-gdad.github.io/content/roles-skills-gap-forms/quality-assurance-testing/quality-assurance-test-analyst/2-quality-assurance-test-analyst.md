# Quality assurance test analyst - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a quality assurance test analyst in the UK
Government Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent
it so that you can set out what you do now, what you have not had the chance to
do, and what support would be most useful over the coming months.

This is the level where the supervision drops away. The framework expects you to
apply the most appropriate test approaches and plans *based on risk*, analyse
user stories, prototypes, processes and designs, decide what to test, make sure
coverage lines up with business risk, follow standards that catch problems early,
and execute, report and support resolution. The phrase that runs through all of
it is risk: at this level you are not testing everything, you are deciding what
matters.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked, and no answer is compared with a
colleague's. Each of the six skills gets an explanation, a note on what changes
at senior, a rating and some questions. Expect about 35 minutes.

## How to fill in this form

This is a static web page. There is nothing to type into and nothing to send
from here — your answers never touch this site.

Print it, copy the questions into a document, or paste them into whatever your
organisation uses for development conversations. Every question is numbered, so
answers travel back on their own.

Worth knowing:

- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a finding.
- **"I haven't had the chance" is a real answer**, and more useful than a
  generous reading of a thin example.
- **Name a real release, defect or piece of testing** rather than describing what
  you generally do.
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers, what they
  are used for, or how long they are kept, ask before you fill it in.

## About this role level

**The role: quality assurance test analyst.** A quality assurance test analyst
interprets testing needs, creating and executing appropriate tests to make sure
things work and match what was asked for — planning, designing and executing
tests, applying techniques and standards to learn about systems and reduce risk,
analysing artefacts and prototypes, recording defects, improving testing
practice, and recommending approaches.

**Your level: quality assurance test analyst.** At this level you design and
execute appropriate functional and non-functional tests, applying multiple
techniques to inform how risk is reduced. The framework expects that you:

- apply the most appropriate test approaches and plans based on risk
- analyse artefacts, such as user stories, prototypes, processes and designs
- determine what to test following an agreed approach
- ensure test coverage aligns with business risk
- follow appropriate standards to mitigate risk early
- execute tests, develop reports, record outcomes and support the resolution of
  defects

*Coverage aligns with business risk* is the sentence to hold on to. Coverage that
is even across a system is coverage that is wrong: it spends the same effort on
the thing nobody uses as on the thing that would make the evening news.

**Where this level sits.** The quality assurance test analyst role has four
levels:

1. Associate quality assurance test analyst
2. **Quality assurance test analyst** — you are here
3. Senior quality assurance test analyst
4. Lead quality assurance test analyst

Your step up is senior, where the work moves outward: influencing and guiding
how others test, leading test design across a team, coaching in test analysis,
managing defects and risks *across* teams, and contributing to organisational
quality strategy.

**One thing worth knowing about this role.** The framework words the *test
engineering* skill identically at all four levels here — including at lead, where
it still says "with support". The deeper engineering expectation lives in the
separate test engineer role. If tooling and automation are what you enjoy, that
is worth raising: this role will not grow that skill on paper however senior you
become.

**The skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Communicating between the technical and non-technical
2. Designing and executing tests
3. Managing, reporting and resolving defects
4. Test analysis
5. Test and quality planning
6. Test engineering

## Job skills

Six skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the level expects,
what it looks like in practice, and what changes at senior.

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What this means.** Making technical findings usable by people who are not
technical, and holding your ground when what you have found is unwelcome.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: communicate
effectively with technical and non-technical stakeholders; support and host
discussions within a multidisciplinary team, including ones with difficult
dynamics; be an advocate for the team externally; and manage differing
stakeholder perspectives.

**What that looks like here.** You can say "this is not ready" to someone who
wants it to be ready, and be heard. You can host a conversation between a
developer who thinks a defect is trivial and a product owner who thinks it is
fatal. You represent testing to people who see it as a delay.

**The next step.** At senior it becomes listening to and interpreting needs and
managing expectations, managing active and reactive communication, and hosting
difficult discussions with diverse *senior* stakeholders.

### Skill: Designing and executing tests

**What this means.** Choosing the right kinds of test, building them, running
them, and going looking for what the planned tests would not catch.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: set up suitable
environments with some support; select appropriate test types and techniques with
some support; design, build, maintain and execute tests that align to user needs
and requirements; conduct exploratory testing; and research and try new test
types and techniques.

**What that looks like here.** You pick techniques deliberately — boundary
values here, exploratory session there — and can say why. Your exploratory
testing finds things the scripted tests never would. You have tried a technique
that was new to you this year.

**The next step.** At senior you set up environments unaided, influence and guide
others' choice of test types, lead others in designing and executing, contribute
to standards, and improve techniques through a structured process.

### Skill: Managing, reporting and resolving defects

**What this means.** Making sure what is wrong is understood, prioritised and
resolved — and that people understand what it would mean to ship it.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: collaborate with
others to create a defect management process, with support; critically assess
dependencies, defects and risks, with support; contribute to mitigation and
contingency plans; and clearly communicate risks and the impact of defects to
stakeholders.

**What that looks like here.** You can say what a defect would cost in the real
world, not just what it does on screen. You have contributed to a plan for what
happens if a known problem bites. You spot when three separate defects are one
underlying cause.

**The next step.** At senior you contribute to standards for defect management,
manage and escalate dependencies and risks *across teams*, contribute to
cross-team mitigation plans, use defect patterns and trends to make
recommendations, and manage stakeholder expectations during resolution.

### Skill: Test analysis

**What this means.** Working out what matters about quality before testing
starts, and getting the conditions in place to test it.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: work with stakeholders
to determine which functional and non-functional quality characteristics add
value; determine what to test following an agreed approach; identify and advocate
for test needs such as data, access and environments, with support; and analyse
information to identify risks.

**What that looks like here.** You have asked a product owner which quality
characteristics actually matter and got a real answer. You ask for the test data
you need early enough for it to arrive. You read a design and can name where it
will go wrong.

**The next step.** At senior you lead this work with stakeholders *across teams*,
judge whether an approach needs to change based on effort and risk, ensure test
needs are implemented early, use multiple techniques on complex information, and
coach others in test analysis.

### Skill: Test and quality planning

**What this means.** Deciding the shape of the testing — what will be covered,
by whom, when — and improving that shape over time.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: create or adapt
quality testing approaches based on risk, with some support; follow a quality
testing strategy and contribute to its development; and contribute to continuous
improvement of approaches, plans and strategies.

**What that looks like here.** You have adapted the approach for a release whose
risk profile was different. You have suggested a change to the strategy and had
it taken. You can say what your team's testing does badly.

**The next step.** At senior you work with teams to develop and implement
approaches, contribute to *organisational* strategy, implement ways to capture
data driving continuous improvement, and advocate for the whole team owning
quality rather than the testers owning it.

### Skill: Test engineering

**What this means.** The processes, environments and tools that make testing
possible, including automated tests and the machinery that runs them.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: explain why testing
processes, environments and tools are important; follow test engineering
practices and standards, with support; and support the maintenance of automated
tests and tools required for testing.

**What that looks like here.** You keep to the team's practices when you add to
the automated suite. You can say what your environment problems cost in delivery
time. You help maintain the tests rather than only running them.

**The next step.** There is none within this role: the framework repeats this
exact wording at senior and at lead. The deeper expectation belongs to the test
engineer role. If this is the part you enjoy, that is a path conversation rather
than a promotion conversation.

## Legal compliance

Testing sits close to two legal duties in particular: public sector services must
meet an accessibility standard in law, and testing is usually where that gets
checked; and test data is real data far more often than people admit — including
in screenshots attached to defect reports. These questions ask about awareness,
training and support, not about whether you can state the law correctly, and not
as a check on your conduct.
