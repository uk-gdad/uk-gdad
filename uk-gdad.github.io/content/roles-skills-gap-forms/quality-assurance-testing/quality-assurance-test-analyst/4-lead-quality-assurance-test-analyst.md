# Lead quality assurance test analyst - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a lead quality assurance test analyst in the
UK Government Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has
sent it so that you can set out what you do now, where the gaps are, and what
would close them.

Lead is the top of this role. The framework has you setting the strategy for test
analysis and influencing test analysis practice across a broad area: developing,
monitoring and evaluating standards and methods, making strategic decisions that
improve test analysis in the organisation, leading and guiding teams, defining
and implementing test strategies, mentoring and coaching other analysts, and
negotiating and escalating major risks, issues and defects.

Almost all of that is exercised through other people. The useful answers below
are about what changed because of you, not what you are responsible for.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Each of the six skills gets an
explanation, a rating and some questions. Expect about 45 minutes, and expect the
uncomfortable questions to be the useful ones.

## About this role level

**The role: quality assurance test analyst.** A quality assurance test analyst
interprets testing needs, creating and executing appropriate tests to make sure
things work and match what was asked for.

**Your level: lead quality assurance test analyst.** At this level you set the
strategy for test analysis and influence test analysis practices across a broad
area, developing, monitoring and evaluating standards and methods and making
strategic decisions that improve test analysis in the organisation. The framework
expects that you:

- lead on setting standards and good practices across the test analysis
  community
- lead and guide teams across the organisation in test analysis
- define and implement test strategies
- mentor and coach other quality assurance test analysts
- negotiate and escalate major risks, issues and defects

*Negotiate* is the word doing the most work there. At this level, whether a
serious defect delays a release is not a technical judgement you announce; it is
an outcome you have to negotiate with people who have other pressures.

**There is no level above this one.** The quality assurance test analyst role has
four levels and this is the highest. Where people go next is a question about
other roles — test management, delivery, or leadership outside the profession —
and the framework does not define it as a next step for this role, so this form
does not invent one.

**Where this level sits:**

1. Associate quality assurance test analyst
2. Quality assurance test analyst
3. Senior quality assurance test analyst
4. **Lead quality assurance test analyst** — you are here

**One thing that does not change.** The framework words *test engineering*
identically at every level of this role, including yours: "follow test
engineering practices and standards, with support". A lead who sets
organisational standards for test analysis is still described as needing support
in test engineering. That is a genuine feature of the published framework, not an
oversight in this form — the deeper engineering expectation belongs to the
separate test engineer role. If you are in fact leading test engineering as well,
you are working well beyond the published expectation and should record it.

**The skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Communicating between the technical and non-technical
2. Designing and executing tests
3. Managing, reporting and resolving defects
4. Test analysis
5. Test and quality planning
6. Test engineering

## Job skills

Six skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the framework
expects, what it looks like in practice, and where it differs from senior.

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What this means.** Mediating, strengthening relationships, and speaking for a
professional community rather than for yourself.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: mediate between
people and strengthen relationships, adopting the appropriate communication
method with stakeholders at all levels; manage stakeholder expectations and
moderate difficult discussions about high risk and complex topics, even within
constrained timescales; and speak on behalf of and represent the community to
large audiences inside and outside the organisation.

**What that looks like here.** You have repaired a relationship between testing
and a delivery area that had gone sour. You moderate go-live arguments where the
risk is real and the deadline is fixed. You speak for testers where testers are
not in the room.

**Different from senior**, where the skill is interpreting needs and hosting
difficult discussions rather than mediating, strengthening relationships and
representing the community publicly.

### Skill: Designing and executing tests

**What this means.** Setting how testing is designed and executed across the
organisation, and coaching people in it.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: set standards and
influence organisational decisions for test types, techniques, design and
execution; coach others in them; advocate for continuous improvement and
refinement of test types and techniques; and make strategic decisions on new or
improved test types and techniques used in your area.

**What that looks like here.** The standards teams design to are ones you set,
and you can say how you know they are followed. You have retired a technique that
had outlived its usefulness — harder than adding one. You have decided to adopt
something new, and can explain the judgement.

**Different from senior**, which influences and guides use and contributes to
standards, rather than setting them and making strategic decisions.

### Skill: Managing, reporting and resolving defects

**What this means.** Improving how defects are handled across the organisation,
and using what defects reveal to change how things are built.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: lead and coach others
in improving test and defect management processes; support others in assessing
complex and challenging defects across the organisation; lead and coach others in
using defect patterns and trends to make tactical and strategic recommendations;
and influence improvements to quality processes informed by those patterns.

**What that looks like here.** Defect data is used to change how software is
built, not only to report on it. People bring you the defects nobody can
characterise. Your analysts read trends themselves rather than waiting for you
to.

**Different from senior**, which manages and escalates across teams and uses
patterns with support, rather than leading, coaching and influencing quality
processes.

### Skill: Test analysis

**What this means.** Setting the principles for how risk is found across the
organisation, and getting analysis to happen early enough to matter.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: lead and guide
multiple teams in test analysis, ensuring it is implemented early in the life
cycle; advocate for risk-based analysis to drive improvements across many teams;
and set standards and principles for test analysis across the organisation.

**What that looks like here.** Analysis happens before designs are fixed in more
places than it used to, because you changed something structural — a gate, a
ceremony, a definition of ready. Risk-based analysis is the default rather than
the exception. The principles are written and used.

**Different from senior**, which leads across a defined area and coaches
individuals, rather than setting organisational standards and principles.

### Skill: Test and quality planning

**What this means.** Creating and running the strategies that multiple teams
work to, and helping them optimise using evidence.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: create and manage
multiple quality testing plans, approaches and strategies; lead and guide
multiple teams in adopting quality testing strategy; advocate for early quality
testing involvement in organisational delivery processes; and guide teams across
the organisation in optimising their approaches using appropriate data.

**What that looks like here.** There is a test strategy, you wrote it, and people
outside testing can describe what it commits to. Teams adopt it because it helps
them rather than because it is mandated. You guide with data rather than
opinion.

**Different from senior**, which develops approaches with teams and contributes to
organisational strategy, rather than creating and managing multiple strategies.

### Skill: Test engineering

**What this means.** The processes, environments and tools that make testing
possible, including automated tests and the machinery that runs them.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things, and they are word for
word what the framework expects of an associate: explain why testing processes,
environments and tools are important; follow test engineering practices and
standards, with support; and support the maintenance of automated tests and tools
required for testing.

**What that looks like here.** You understand the engineering well enough to make
the investment case and to know when you are being told something convenient. The
building is done by test engineers.

**No change anywhere in this role.** If you are actually leading test engineering
across your area, you are working well beyond the published expectation — record
that, because nothing in the framework will otherwise show it.

## Legal compliance

Testing sits close to two legal duties in particular: public sector services must
meet an accessibility standard in law, and testing is where that gets checked;
and test data is real data more often than people admit. At this level your
assurance may be what the organisation relies on. These questions ask about
awareness, training and support, not about whether you can state the law
correctly, and not as a check on your conduct.
