# Test engineer - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a test engineer in the UK Government Digital
and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that you can
set out what you do now, what you have not had the chance to do, and what support
would be most useful over the coming months.

This is the level where you are responsible for writing, debugging and
refactoring test code. The framework expects you to work closely with software
developers to reach a common understanding of the code base and of coverage at
unit level, collaborate with analysts so the business scenarios are covered in
acceptance tests, work on both functional and non-functional areas, and coach and
mentor testers.

That first one is the distinctive part. Understanding unit-level coverage
alongside developers is what stops the automated suite duplicating what the unit
tests already prove, and lets it spend its time where the real risk is.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked, and no answer is compared with a
colleague's. Each of the six skills gets an explanation, a note on what changes
at senior, a rating and some questions. Expect about 35 minutes.

## About this role level

**The role: test engineer.** A test engineer undertakes test planning activity —
capturing what has been discovered, defining tests, and estimating the effort
testing will take — as part of a broader risk-based approach.

**Your level: test engineer.** At this level you are responsible for writing,
debugging and refactoring test code. The framework expects that you:

- work closely with software developers to reach a common understanding of the
  code base and test coverage at unit level
- collaborate with analysts to ensure the required business scenarios are
  covered in the acceptance test scripts
- work on both functional and non-functional areas of an application
- coach and mentor testers

*Refactoring* test code is named explicitly, which matters. Test code that is
never refactored becomes the reason teams stop trusting their suite.

**Where this level sits.** The test engineer role has four levels:

1. Associate test engineer
2. **Test engineer** — you are here
3. Senior test engineer
4. Lead test engineer

Your step up is senior test engineer, where you develop, standardise and extend
reusable frameworks rather than using them, coach others in building reliable
tests, research future testing needs, and maintain and adapt the pipelines
themselves.

**How this role relates to the test analyst role.** Five of your six skills —
everything except test engineering — are worded by the framework *exactly* as
they are for a quality assurance test analyst at the same level. Test engineering
is the difference, and in this role it grows at every level while in the analyst
role it does not grow at all. So the two roles are not senior and junior versions
of each other: they are the same quality craft with and without the engineering.

**The skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Communicating between the technical and non-technical
2. Designing and executing tests
3. Managing, reporting and resolving defects
4. Test analysis
5. Test and quality planning
6. Test engineering

## Job skills

Six skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the level expects,
what it looks like in practice, and what changes at senior.

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What this means.** Making technical findings usable by people who are not
technical, and working with developers as a peer rather than as an auditor.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: communicate
effectively with technical and non-technical stakeholders; support and host
discussions within a multidisciplinary team, including ones with difficult
dynamics; be an advocate for the team externally; and manage differing
stakeholder perspectives.

**What that looks like here.** You can tell a developer their design is hard to
test without it becoming a fight. You can explain to a product owner why the
suite cannot simply be made faster. You represent the testing view where it is
not popular.

**The next step.** At senior it becomes listening to and interpreting needs and
managing expectations, managing active and reactive communication, and hosting
difficult discussions with diverse *senior* stakeholders.

### Skill: Designing and executing tests

**What this means.** Choosing what kinds of test to build and where they belong,
building them, and going looking for what they will not catch.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: set up suitable
environments with some support; select appropriate test types and techniques with
some support; design, build, maintain and execute tests that align to user needs
and requirements; conduct exploratory testing; and research and try new test
types and techniques.

**What that looks like here.** You know which layer a check belongs in — unit,
integration, contract, end to end — and you argue about it. Your exploratory
testing finds what the suite never would. You have tried something new this year
rather than repeating what you know.

**The next step.** At senior you set up environments unaided, influence and guide
others' choices, lead others in designing and executing, contribute to standards,
and improve techniques through a structured process.

### Skill: Managing, reporting and resolving defects

**What this means.** Making sure what is wrong is understood and dealt with, and
that people know what shipping it would mean.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: collaborate with
others to create a defect management process, with support; critically assess
dependencies, defects and risks, with support; contribute to mitigation and
contingency plans; and clearly communicate risks and the impact of defects to
stakeholders.

**What that looks like here.** You can tell whether a failure is one defect or
three symptoms. You debug far enough to hand a developer something actionable.
You say what shipping a known defect would actually mean for users.

**The next step.** At senior you contribute to standards for defect management,
manage and escalate risks *across teams*, contribute to cross-team mitigation
plans, use patterns and trends to make recommendations, and manage stakeholder
communications during resolution.

### Skill: Test analysis

**What this means.** Working out what matters about quality before you build,
and getting the conditions in place to test it.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: work with stakeholders
to determine which functional and non-functional quality characteristics add
value; determine what to test following an agreed approach; identify and advocate
for test needs such as data, access and environments, with support; and analyse
information to identify risks.

**What that looks like here.** You ask what matters before you automate, rather
than automating what is easy to automate. You ask for the test data early enough
for it to arrive. You can read a design and say where it will be hard to verify.

**The next step.** At senior you lead this work with stakeholders across teams,
judge when an approach must change based on effort and risk, ensure test needs
are implemented early, use multiple techniques on complex information, and coach
others.

### Skill: Test and quality planning

**What this means.** Deciding the shape of the testing and improving it over
time.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: create or adapt
quality testing approaches based on risk, with some support; follow a quality
testing strategy and contribute to its development; and contribute to continuous
improvement of approaches, plans and strategies.

**What that looks like here.** You have changed the shape of the suite because
the risk changed — moved checks down a layer, deleted tests that proved nothing,
added a contract test where an integration kept breaking. You have proposed a
change to the strategy and had it taken.

**The next step.** At senior you work with teams to develop and implement
approaches, contribute to *organisational* strategy, implement ways to capture
improvement data, and advocate for whole-team ownership of quality.

### Skill: Test engineering

**What this means.** Building the machinery: frameworks, automated tests,
pipelines, and the coded solutions behind them. This is the skill that
distinguishes your role from the test analyst role, and the first level at which
the framework asks for real engineering.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: use test engineering
frameworks and tools to support testing activities; follow test engineering
practices and standards, such as source control and continuous integration and
deployment pipelines; integrate and execute tests to ensure early testing and
continuous feedback; create and maintain automated tests, with some support; and
write and review coded solutions, with some support.

**What that looks like here.** Your test code goes through review like
application code. Tests run early enough to give feedback while a change is still
being made rather than after it. You maintain what you build, including the parts
that have become tedious.

**The next step.** At senior this becomes developing, standardising and extending
*reusable* frameworks and tools, coaching others in building comprehensive and
reliable tests, researching future testing needs, and maintaining and adapting
the pipelines themselves.

## Legal compliance

Testing sits close to two legal duties in particular: public sector services must
meet an accessibility standard in law, and testing is where that gets checked;
and test data is real data more often than people admit — including in fixtures,
snapshots and recorded responses committed to a repository. These questions ask
about awareness, training and support, not about whether you can state the law
correctly, and not as a check on your conduct.

**Q34.** Which of these have you had training or a briefing on? Tick any that
