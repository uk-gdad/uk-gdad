# Senior test engineer - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a senior test engineer in the UK Government
Digital and Data profession. It has come from human resources or your manager,
and it exists to establish where the gap sits between what you do now and what
the organisation needs from someone at this level.

The framework describes a senior test engineer as doing what a test engineer
does, but at a more complex level and on a greater scale. What it adds is
specific: identifying and raising awareness of risks that come out of automation
results, identifying new and implementing existing test frameworks to improve
confidence in testing, being proficient in a range of coding languages — while
possibly being expert in only one — and mentoring and advising others in testing
practices.

That first one deserves attention. Automation produces results all day; turning
those results into a risk somebody acts on is a different skill from making the
automation work, and it is the one most often missing.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Each of the six skills gets an
explanation, a note on what changes at lead, a rating and some questions. Expect
about 40 minutes.

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not what you could pick up.
- **A 4 is a claim about other people** — that someone else's practice changed

## About this role level

**The role: test engineer.** A test engineer undertakes test planning activity —
capturing what has been discovered, defining tests, and estimating the effort
testing will take — as part of a broader risk-based approach.

**Your level: senior test engineer.** Similar responsibilities to a test
engineer, at a more complex level and on a greater scale. The framework expects
that you:

- identify and raise awareness of risks arising from the automation test results
- identify new and implement existing test frameworks to improve confidence in
  testing
- are proficient in a range of coding languages, and may be an expert in only one
- mentor and advise other team members in testing practices

The third is unusually candid for a framework, and worth taking at its word:
breadth across languages with depth in one is the expectation, not expertise in
everything.

**Where this level sits.** The test engineer role has four levels:

1. Associate test engineer
2. Test engineer
3. **Senior test engineer** — you are here
4. Lead test engineer

Your step up is lead test engineer, where test engineering becomes
organisational: establishing and leading practices, standards and behaviours,
influencing technology and tool choices across the organisation, and advocating
for testing solutions that align with organisational goals. The lead
accountabilities add promoting open source tools, proficiency in multiple
scripting languages, a presence in external test communities, and managing
communications between projects to control integration and dependencies.

**How this role relates to the test analyst role.** Five of your six skills are
worded by the framework exactly as they are for a senior quality assurance test
analyst. Test engineering is the difference — it grows at every level here and at
none there.

**The skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Communicating between the technical and non-technical
2. Designing and executing tests
3. Managing, reporting and resolving defects
4. Test analysis
5. Test and quality planning
6. Test engineering

## Job skills

Six skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the level expects,
what it looks like in practice, and what changes at lead.

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What this means.** Interpreting what people need, managing expectations, and
turning technical results into decisions other people can make.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: listen to and
interpret the needs of technical and non-technical stakeholders and manage their
expectations; manage active and reactive communication; and support or host
difficult discussions within the team or with diverse senior stakeholders.

**What that looks like here.** You can turn a pattern in test results into a
sentence a delivery manager acts on. You tell people about a growing problem —
suite runtime, flakiness, coverage rot — before it becomes an emergency. You hold
a difficult conversation about quality with people senior to you.

**The next step.** At lead it becomes mediating and strengthening relationships
at all levels, moderating discussions about high risk and complex topics under
constrained timescales, and speaking for the community to large audiences inside
and outside the organisation.

### Skill: Designing and executing tests

**What this means.** Shaping how testing is designed and run across your area,
and improving techniques deliberately.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: set up suitable
environments; influence and guide the use of appropriate test types and
techniques to mitigate risk early; lead others in designing, building,
maintaining and executing tests; contribute to developing and implementing
standards for designing and executing tests; and improve test types and
techniques through a structured process.

**What that looks like here.** Environments are yours to solve, not to wait for.
You have moved checks to the layer where they belong and can show what it bought.
When you improve a technique you measure it rather than asserting it.

**The next step.** At lead you *set* standards and influence organisational
decisions about test types and techniques, coach others in them, and make
strategic decisions about new or improved techniques in your area.

### Skill: Managing, reporting and resolving defects

**What this means.** Managing defects and risks across teams, and reading what
the pattern of failures says about how software is being built.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: contribute to
developing standards for defect management processes; manage and escalate
dependencies, defects and risks across teams; contribute to mitigation and
contingency plans across teams; use defect patterns and trends to make
recommendations on testing and quality approaches, with support; and manage
stakeholder expectations and communications during defect resolution.

**What that looks like here.** You can tell when two teams are hitting the same
underlying fault. You escalate with a recommendation and a cost. You have changed
what gets tested — or what gets built — because of what a trend showed.

**The next step.** At lead you lead and coach others in improving defect
processes, support others assessing complex defects across the organisation, and
influence quality processes informed by patterns.

### Skill: Test analysis

**What this means.** Finding the risk early across teams, and building that habit
in others.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Five things: lead work with
stakeholders across teams to determine which quality characteristics add value;
determine if an approach needs to change based on effort and risk; ensure test
needs are implemented early; use multiple techniques to analyse complex
information to identify risks; and coach others in test analysis.

**What that looks like here.** You get into the design conversation before the
architecture makes testing expensive. You can say when automating something is
not worth the effort. Your engineers think about risk before they think about
tools.

**The next step.** At lead you lead and guide multiple teams, ensure analysis
happens early in the life cycle, advocate for risk-based analysis across many
teams, and set standards and principles for test analysis across the
organisation.

### Skill: Test and quality planning

**What this means.** Developing the approaches teams work to, feeding
organisational strategy, and driving improvement with data.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: work with teams to
develop and implement appropriate quality testing approaches, plans and
strategies; contribute to organisational quality testing strategies; implement
ways to capture data to drive continuous improvement; and advocate for full team
ownership of quality testing activities, encouraging early engagement.

**What that looks like here.** You have instrumented something — flakiness rate,
suite duration, where defects originate, time to feedback — and used it to change
what the team does. Teams own quality rather than delegating it to you.

**The next step.** At lead you create and manage multiple plans and strategies,
lead multiple teams in adopting them, advocate for early quality involvement in
organisational delivery processes, and guide teams in optimising using data.

### Skill: Test engineering

**What this means.** Building the shared machinery: reusable frameworks, tools,
pipelines — and preparing for what testing will need next. This is the skill that
distinguishes this role from the test analyst role.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: develop, standardise
and extend reusable frameworks and tools to support a range of testing
activities; guide and coach others in creating and maintaining comprehensive and
reliable tests that meet standards; research and prepare for future testing
needs, including tools, methodologies and techniques; and maintain and adapt
continuous integration and deployment pipelines.

**What that looks like here.** Other teams use something you built. Reliability
is designed in rather than chased. The pipeline is yours to change, not
somebody else's to ask. And — the part that usually gets squeezed — you have
looked ahead at what testing will need in a year.

**The next step.** At lead this becomes establishing and leading test engineering
practices, standards and behaviours; influencing and guiding technology and tool
choices across the organisation; and advocating for testing solutions that align
with organisational goals and quality objectives.

## Legal compliance

Testing sits close to two legal duties in particular: public sector services must
meet an accessibility standard in law, and test data is real data more often than
people admit. At senior you are often the person others assume has checked. These
questions ask about awareness, training and support, not about whether you can
state the law correctly, and not as a check on your conduct.
