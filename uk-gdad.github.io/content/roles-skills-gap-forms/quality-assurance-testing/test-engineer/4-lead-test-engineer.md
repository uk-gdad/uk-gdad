# Lead test engineer - skills gap form

## Introduction

This form is for you if you work as a lead test engineer in the UK Government
Digital and Data profession. Human resources or your manager has sent it so that
you can set out what you do now, where the gaps are, and what would close them.

Lead is the top of this role. The framework describes it as writing new
automation test frameworks based on project requirements, and then adds a set of
accountabilities that reach well beyond your own keyboard: promoting the use of
open source tools, being proficient in multiple test scripting languages, having
a presence in external test communities and regularly sharing learning, working
with leads across the Government Digital and Data profession to champion quality,
guiding test team members to design test strategies for complex and larger-scale
delivery, and managing communications between projects to control integration and
dependencies.

Two of those are unusual and easy to let slide: an external presence, and
managing the communication between projects so that integration and dependencies
do not surprise anyone. Both are the first things to disappear when delivery
pressure rises.

Nothing here is scored, marked or ranked. Each of the six skills gets an
explanation, a rating and some questions. Expect about 45 minutes.

## How to fill in this form

This is a static web page. There is nothing to type into and nothing to send
from here — your answers never touch this site.

Print it, copy the questions into a document, or paste them into whatever your
organisation uses for development conversations. Every question is numbered, so
answers travel back on their own.

Worth knowing:

- **Skipping a question means nothing.** A blank is not a finding.
- **"The teams do this" is not an answer to "what do you do".** Name your part.
- **"I haven't had the chance" is a real answer** at this level too.
- **Ask who reads it.** If nobody has told you who sees your answers, what they
  are used for, or how long they are kept, ask before you fill it in.

Where a question asks for a rating, use this scale:

| 3 | Practitioner | You do it unsupported, and handle the usual exceptions |
| 4 | Expert | You set the approach and develop others in it |

Two things about the scale:

- It describes **what you do at work today**, not the remit on your job
  description.
- **A 4 is a claim about other people** — that someone else's practice changed
  because of you and would stay changed if you left. Several are reasonable
  here; six would be worth interrogating.

> **Test and quality planning**

## About this role level

**The role: test engineer.** A test engineer undertakes test planning activity —
capturing what has been discovered, defining tests, and estimating the effort
testing will take — as part of a broader risk-based approach.

**Your level: lead test engineer.** At this level you write new automation test
frameworks based on project requirements. The framework expects that you:

- promote the use of open source tools
- are proficient in multiple test scripting languages
- have a presence in external test communities and regularly share learning
- work closely with leads across the Government Digital and Data profession to
  champion quality
- guide test team members to design test strategies, which may involve more
  complex and larger scale delivery
- manage communications between projects to control integration and dependencies

**There is no level above this one.** The test engineer role has four levels and
this is the highest. Where people go next is a question about other roles — test
management, software engineering, or leadership outside the profession — and the
framework does not define it as a next step for this role, so this form does not
invent one.

**Where this level sits:**

1. Associate test engineer
2. Test engineer
3. Senior test engineer
4. **Lead test engineer** — you are here

**How this role relates to the test analyst role.** Five of your six skills are
worded by the framework exactly as they are for a lead quality assurance test
analyst. Test engineering is the whole published difference: yours reaches
organisational practices, standards and tool choices, while theirs stays at the
associate wording throughout. If you work alongside a lead analyst, that is the
line between you on paper — and it is worth agreeing where it sits in practice.

**The skills this form covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Communicating between the technical and non-technical
2. Designing and executing tests
3. Managing, reporting and resolving defects
4. Test analysis
5. Test and quality planning
6. Test engineering

## Job skills

Six skills, in the framework's order. Each block gives what the framework
expects, what it looks like in practice, and where it differs from senior.

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What this means.** Mediating, strengthening relationships, and speaking for a
professional community rather than for yourself.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: mediate between
people and strengthen relationships, adopting the appropriate communication
method with stakeholders at all levels; manage stakeholder expectations and
moderate difficult discussions about high risk and complex topics, even within
constrained timescales; and speak on behalf of and represent the community to
large audiences inside and outside the organisation.

**What that looks like here.** You have repaired a relationship between testing
and a delivery area. You moderate release arguments where the risk is real and
the date is fixed. You speak publicly about testing, which the framework treats
as part of the job rather than as a hobby.

**Different from senior**, which interprets needs and hosts difficult discussions
rather than mediating, strengthening relationships and representing publicly.

### Skill: Designing and executing tests

**What this means.** Setting how testing is designed and executed across the
organisation, and coaching people in it.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: set standards and
influence organisational decisions for test types, techniques, design and
execution; coach others in them; advocate for continuous improvement and
refinement; and make strategic decisions on new or improved test types and
techniques used in your area.

**What that looks like here.** The standards teams design to are ones you set,
and you can say how you know they are followed rather than filed. You have
retired an approach that had outlived its usefulness. When you adopt something
new, you can explain the judgement rather than the enthusiasm.

**Different from senior**, which influences and guides usage and contributes to
standards rather than setting them.

### Skill: Managing, reporting and resolving defects

**What this means.** Improving how defects are handled across the organisation,
and using what they reveal to change how software is built.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: lead and coach others
in improving test and defect management processes; support others in assessing
complex and challenging defects across the organisation; lead and coach others in
using defect patterns and trends to make tactical and strategic recommendations;
and influence improvements to quality processes informed by those patterns.

**What that looks like here.** Defect data changes how software is built rather
than only how it is reported. People bring you the failures nobody can
characterise. Your engineers read the trends themselves.

**Different from senior**, which manages and escalates across teams and uses
patterns with support.

### Skill: Test analysis

**What this means.** Setting how risk is found across the organisation, and
getting analysis to happen early enough to matter.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: lead and guide
multiple teams in test analysis, ensuring it is implemented early in the life
cycle; advocate for risk-based analysis to drive improvements across many teams;
and set standards and principles for test analysis across the organisation.

**What that looks like here.** Testability enters architecture conversations
because you changed something structural. Risk-based analysis is the default.
The principles exist in writing and people use them.

**Different from senior**, which leads across a defined area and coaches
individuals rather than setting organisational standards.

### Skill: Test and quality planning

**What this means.** Creating and running the strategies multiple teams work to,
and guiding them with evidence.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Four things: create and manage
multiple quality testing plans, approaches and strategies; lead and guide
multiple teams in adopting quality testing strategy; advocate for early quality
testing involvement in organisational delivery processes; and guide teams across
the organisation in optimising approaches using appropriate data.

**What that looks like here.** Teams adopt your strategy because it helps them,
not because it is mandated. You guide with numbers — feedback time, escape rate,
flakiness — rather than preference. Quality gets a place in delivery processes
because you argued for it.

**Different from senior**, which develops approaches with teams and contributes
to organisational strategy rather than creating and managing several.

### Skill: Test engineering

**What this means.** Setting how test engineering is done across the
organisation — the practices, the standards, the behaviours, and the technology
choices. This is the whole published difference between this role and the test
analyst role.

**What the framework expects at this level.** Three things: establish and lead
test engineering practices, standards and behaviours; influence and guide test
engineering technology and tool choices across the organisation; and advocate for
the adoption and use of appropriate testing solutions, ensuring alignment with
organisational goals and quality objectives.

**What that looks like here.** There are engineering practices for test code —
review, structure, reliability, ownership — and they exist because you
established them. Tool choices across the organisation go through you or are
influenced by you. When you advocate for a solution, you argue it in terms of
organisational goals rather than technical elegance.

**Different from senior**, which develops and standardises reusable frameworks
and maintains pipelines — yours is the organisational practice and the technology
direction.

## Legal compliance

Testing sits close to two legal duties in particular: public sector services must
meet an accessibility standard in law, and test data is real data more often than
people admit. At this level your assurance may be what the organisation relies
on, and your tool choices carry licensing consequences. These questions ask about
awareness, training and support, not about whether you can state the law
correctly, and not as a check on your conduct.
