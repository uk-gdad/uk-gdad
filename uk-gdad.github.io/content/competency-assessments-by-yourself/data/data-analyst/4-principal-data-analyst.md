# Principal Data Analyst - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This is for you: a chance to rate your own current strengths against the
baseline the UK GDAD Profession Capability Framework sets for this role
level, using the framework's own words for what it covers.

A data analyst collects, manages, explores and shares data to support organisational objectives and deliver business impact. A principal data analyst leads teams or significant areas of work in data analysis. They determine and implement appropriate standards, processes and structures to ensure data is prepared, managed and used effectively within the organisation.

Work through the competency matrix below, one skill at a time, then fill in
the overall determination at the end. Treat this as a mirror, not a test —
there's no assessor watching, so being honest with yourself only costs you a
slightly less flattering picture today in exchange for a far more useful one.
Read `## Advisory` before you begin.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating you
give yourself here must never be treated as equivalent to a formal
assessment for a hiring, promotion, pay or performance decision. Use it for
your own reflection, and talk to your manager, a mentor, or your
organisation's people development team about what you find.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you're rating
yourself against what this level actually expects rather than the level
above or below it. Then think back over recent, real examples of your work —
not how you'd like to have handled something, but what you actually did.

**Rate on evidence, not impression.** For every skill, try to recall a
specific moment that supports the rating you give yourself. If you can't
think of one, that's useful information too — it might mean the rating
should be lower, or just that you haven't had the chance to show this skill
yet, which is worth noting separately.

**Be consistent with yourself.** Try to apply the same standard across every
skill, rather than being generous where you feel confident and harsh where
you feel unsure. Coming back to this after a few days, with fresh examples in
mind, often sharpens a rating more than sitting with it in one go.

**Be kind, and be honest.** This only works if you can look at the result
without flinching. A low rating here is not a verdict on you — it's a
starting point. Everyone doing this role, at every level, has skills they are
still building.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix below is rated on the same five-point
scale. This is a **proficiency** scale — it rates how well someone does the
thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it or how they feel
about it.

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

**Worked example — Data management.** The framework expects a principal data
analyst to "champion and lead data management and governance processes within
your team and across your organisation" and to "define data management tools
and develop training requirements to implement data governance processes
effectively". Someone who advocates for good data management within their
own team, but has never defined tools or training for the wider organisation,
is showing evidence closer to **3 — Advanced** — strong practice, but not yet
this level's baseline. Someone who has led the definition of data
management tooling and training that other teams across the organisation now
use is showing **4 — Expert**, the baseline this level expects.

## About this role level

The Data profession's Data analyst role collects, manages, explores and shares
data to support organisational objectives and deliver business impact. Across
the role, a data analyst identifies, collects and moves data between a range
of systems; applies data analysis and data visualisation tools and
techniques; prepares, manages and models data, following appropriate
standards and practices; and communicates data, insights and conclusions in
appropriate formats.

The role has four levels, in order: Associate data analyst, Data analyst,
Senior data analyst, and Principal data analyst. This assessment is for the
fourth and most senior of them.

A **principal data analyst** leads teams or significant areas of work in data
analysis. They determine and implement appropriate standards, processes and
structures to ensure data is prepared, managed and used effectively within the
organisation. At this role level, they will influence organisational decision
making on data analysis and the use of data, oversee continuous improvement of
the organisation's data practices and management, and represent the data
analyst community inside and outside of the organisation.

The framework names nine skills for this role level: Applying statistical
and analytical tools and techniques, Communicating between the technical and
non-technical, Data ethics and privacy, Data management, Data preparation and
linkage, Data visualisation, Delivering business impact through data,
Developing code for analysis, and Managing a data project. The framework
lists Delivering business impact through data twice and Managing a data
project three times in the published summary, with identical wording each
time; this assessment treats each as the one skill it is.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Applying statistical and analytical tools and techniques

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal data analyst is expected to identify opportunities to develop
statistical insights and reports that support organisational objectives,
develop and guide others in a range of statistical and analytical techniques
and tools, oversee the quality assurance of statistical techniques and
analytical outputs while continually improving and innovating practices, and
use statistical and analytical outputs to influence stakeholders across the
organisation and beyond.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Overseeing (not just doing) quality assurance,
developing others' technique, and influencing stakeholders across and beyond
the organisation describes mastery used to teach and to shape organisational
decisions — the top of this skill's progression.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot identify an organisational opportunity for statistical
  insight or influence a decision with analytical output.
- 1 — Beginner: Applies technique competently themselves but does not
  influence organisational decisions with it.
- 2 — Intermediate: Contributes analytical insight to a decision, with
  support.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently leads insight development and quality assures a
  team's outputs.
- 4 — Expert: Identifies organisation-wide opportunities for statistical
  insight, develops and guides others in a range of technique, oversees
  quality assurance while innovating practice, and uses analytical output to
  influence stakeholders across the organisation and beyond it.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe a statistical or analytical output that
  changed a significant organisational decision, and your role in producing
  and presenting it.
- Ask how you oversee quality assurance across more analysts than you could
  personally review line by line.
- Ask about a stakeholder outside the organisation you have influenced with
  analytical evidence.

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal data analyst is expected to mediate between people and strengthen
relationships, adopting the appropriate communication method with
stakeholders at all levels, manage stakeholder expectations and moderate
difficult discussions about high risk and complex topics even within
constrained timescales, and speak on behalf of and represent the community to
large audiences inside and outside the organisation.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Speaking for the whole community to large audiences,
and moderating high-risk, time-constrained discussions, is the top of what
the framework describes for this skill — this level is where managing
differing stakeholder perspectives becomes representing and defending the
profession itself.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot represent the data analyst function to senior stakeholders
  or defend its position under pressure.
- 1 — Beginner: Communicates competently one to one but struggles in
  high-stakes group settings.
- 2 — Intermediate: Manages moderately difficult discussions with support.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently manages difficult discussions and represents
  the team externally.
- 4 — Expert: Is trusted to mediate the organisation's hardest, highest-risk
  discussions about data work, and represents the data analyst community
  credibly to large internal and external audiences.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe the most difficult, highest-stakes discussion
  you have led about data work, under time pressure, and what you did.
- Ask a referee about your standing with senior stakeholders and
  whether you have represented the function externally.

### Skill: Data ethics and privacy

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal data analyst is expected to work with stakeholders to identify and
address ethical and privacy concerns, demonstrate and communicate how data
ethical issues fit into the wider organisational context, research
developments in data ethics and privacy to improve compliance and processes,
and assess and constructively challenge proposed data ethics policies. The
framework's own wording for this skill is unchanged from the senior level
below.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Although this is the most senior level in the role's
ladder, the framework's own wording for this specific skill does not move
beyond the senior level, so the baseline is held at Advanced rather than
inflated to Expert on the strength of the job title alone — this level's
real step up in ethical influence shows through the organisation-wide scope
of the surrounding accountabilities, not through a change in this skill's own
wording.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot connect a data ethics issue in their own work to the
  wider organisational picture.
- 1 — Beginner: Applies ethical principles in routine work but does not yet
  research or challenge policy.
- 2 — Intermediate: Works with stakeholders on straightforward ethical or
  privacy concerns with support.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently works with stakeholders to identify and address
  ethical and privacy concerns, explains how they fit the wider organisational
  context, researches developments to improve compliance, and constructively
  challenges a proposed policy where it does not hold up.
- 4 — Expert: Sets and owns data ethics policy for the organisation.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe a proposed data ethics or privacy policy you
  constructively challenged, and what changed as a result.
- Ask how you keep informed of developments in data ethics and privacy
  practice and bring that back into the organisation.
- You don't need to disclose any personal data or case details as
  part of this evidence gathering.

### Skill: Data management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal data analyst is expected to champion and lead data management and
governance processes within their team and across the organisation, define
and lead on the implementation of data management responsibilities, define
data management tools and develop training requirements to implement data
governance processes effectively, and enable continuous improvement of data
management practices and compliance with data governance policies.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Defining tools and training requirements for the whole
organisation, and leading governance implementation, is organisation-wide
ownership — the top of this skill's progression, well beyond advocating for
policy within a team.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe how data management responsibilities are defined
  or implemented in the organisation.
- 1 — Beginner: Follows data management responsibilities but has not defined
  or led their implementation.
- 2 — Intermediate: Contributes to defining data management responsibilities
  for their own team.
- 3 — Advanced: Advocates for and monitors data management practice across a
  team.
- 4 — Expert: Champions and leads data management and governance across the
  organisation, defines tools and training requirements for it, and enables
  continuous improvement of practice and compliance.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe a data management tool or training programme
  you defined for organisation-wide use.
- Ask about a time you led the implementation of a data governance
  responsibility across more than one team.

  standards for the organisation or beyond, and mentors others in modelling.

### Skill: Data visualisation

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal data analyst is expected to translate business requirements into

  improvements.

  and advocates for organisation-wide standards, identifies capability gaps
  and implements training, and promotes visualisation adoption across the
  organisation.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe a visualisation standard or training

### Skill: Delivering business impact through data

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal data analyst is expected to champion the role of data across the

the organisation, lead others in delivering and identifying opportunities to

- 3 — Advanced: Leads others in delivering scalable data products aligned to
  organisational priorities.

  professions on when to pivot.

### Skill: Managing a data project

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal data analyst is expected to advise on problem statement
development ensuring standards are met, oversee and advise on the planning
and implementation of the project life cycle on varied data projects, share
good practice in adapting teams' ways of working to deliver project outcomes,
and lead and develop stakeholder relationships for their team's data
projects. The framework lists this skill three times in the published
summary, with identical wording each time; the bullets are merged here into
one block.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Advising across varied projects, and sharing good
practice for others to adopt, describes someone whose judgement is the
reference point for project management practice — the top of this skill's
progression, beyond independently managing one project.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot advise another team on problem statement development or
  project planning.
- 1 — Beginner: Manages their own project's problem statement but has not
  advised others.
- 2 — Intermediate: Shares practice with a close colleague within the same
  team.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently defines problem statements and plans project
  life cycle stages for their own work.
- 4 — Expert: Advises on problem statement development across varied
  projects, oversees planning and implementation of the project life cycle,
  shares good practice in adapting ways of working, and leads and develops
  stakeholder relationships for the team's data projects.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe a time you advised another team on how to
  develop or refine a problem statement for a data project.
- Ask about a stakeholder relationship you built and developed specifically
  to support your team's data projects.
