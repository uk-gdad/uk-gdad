# Data Scientist (Head of Data Science) - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This is for you: a chance to rate your own current strengths against the
baseline the UK GDAD Profession Capability Framework sets for this role
level, using the framework's own words for what it covers.

Data science is a broad and fast-moving field spanning maths, statistics, software engineering and communications. Data scientists will often work as part of a multidisciplinary team, using data and analytics to inform and achieve organisational goals. The head of data science has complete oversight of data science within their organisation.

Work through the competency matrix below, one skill at a time, then fill in
the overall determination at the end. Treat this as a mirror, not a test —
there's no assessor watching, so being honest with yourself only costs you a
slightly less flattering picture today in exchange for a far more useful one.
Read `## Advisory` before you begin.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating you
give yourself here must never be treated as equivalent to a formal
assessment for a hiring, promotion, pay or performance decision. Use it for
your own reflection, and talk to your manager, a mentor, or your
organisation's people development team about what you find.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you're rating
yourself against what this level actually expects rather than the level
above or below it. Then think back over recent, real examples of your work —
not how you'd like to have handled something, but what you actually did.

**Rate on evidence, not impression.** For every skill, try to recall a
specific moment that supports the rating you give yourself. If you can't
think of one, that's useful information too — it might mean the rating
should be lower, or just that you haven't had the chance to show this skill
yet, which is worth noting separately.

**Be consistent with yourself.** Try to apply the same standard across every
skill, rather than being generous where you feel confident and harsh where
you feel unsure. Coming back to this after a few days, with fresh examples in
mind, often sharpens a rating more than sitting with it in one go.

**Be kind, and be honest.** This only works if you can look at the result
without flinching. A low rating here is not a verdict on you — it's a
starting point. Everyone doing this role, at every level, has skills they are
still building.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

This is a **proficiency** scale, not a frequency scale: it measures how well
someone does the thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it
or how confident they feel about it.

**Worked example — Data ethics and privacy.** At 1 — Beginner, someone can
describe what data ethics means in general terms but has never owned
compliance for anything beyond their own work: for example, they can explain
why an algorithm might be biased, but cannot point to a framework or policy
they have written or enforced. At 2 — Intermediate, someone has applied
an existing ethical framework to their own team's work with some support from
others — for example, they ran an ethics review for one product using a
template someone else wrote, and escalated a genuine grey area rather than
deciding it alone. At 4 — Expert, someone has built or substantially
revised an ethical framework for an organisation, can describe a specific
instance where they changed how the organisation formulated or implemented a
policy because of a data ethics concern they raised, and can point to how they
have influenced other leaders — not just their own team — to act on it. An
assessor scoring this skill should look for that difference between knowing
about ethics, applying someone else's framework, and having built and spread
one across an organisation.

## About this role level

Data science is a broad, fast-moving field spanning maths, statistics,
software engineering and communications. Data scientists typically work in
multidisciplinary teams, using data and analytics to inform and achieve
organisational goals: exploring and visualising data, making recommendations
on complex problems, using data ethically, and finding patterns that turn into
organisational insight.

Head of data science is level 6 of 6 in the data scientist ladder — the top of
it — following Trainee data scientist, Associate data scientist, Data
scientist, Principal data scientist and Lead data scientist. Where a lead data
scientist leads and develops one or more teams, the head of data science has
**complete oversight of data science within their organisation**: the single
most senior data science accountability the framework defines for this role.

At this role level, the framework says someone will:

- set direction for data science across the organisation
- build capability, including the capability of other people, not only their
  own
- oversee resourcing, budgeting, professionalism, and outputs and products
- support and enable future IT developments
- understand and use a wide range of data science techniques, tools and
  technologies
- lead on ethics
- communicate and present data science and data ethics effectively to
  ministers and senior leaders
- champion the role of data science in supporting organisational priorities,
  and in collaborative working across professions
- represent the department on data science matters

The framework names eight skills for this level: Applied maths, statistics
and scientific practices; Data engineering; Data ethics and privacy; Data
science innovation; Delivering business impact; Developing data science
capability; Programming and build (data science); and Understanding product
delivery. Notably, the framework's own wording for Data engineering and for
Programming and build (data science) is markedly less hands-on-technical at
this level than it is for Lead data scientist below it — this level is worded
around working with specialists and providing direction, rather than owning
technical delivery directly. The competency matrix below reflects that
difference honestly rather than assuming this, the top level, must always
outscore the level beneath it on every skill.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Applied maths, statistics and scientific practices

**What the framework expects at this level**

The head of data science is expected to identify opportunities to develop
statistical insight, reports and models that support organisational
objectives, while collaborating effectively across the organisation to do so.
They critique statistical analyses, use a variety of data analytics
techniques — such as data mining, and prescriptive and predictive analytics —
for complex analysis through the whole data life cycle, and use model outputs
to produce evidence that helps design services and policies. They understand a
broad range of statistical tools, particularly those the organisation itself
uses, can use them appropriately, and can help other people use them too.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. The framework's own text asks for critique of other
people's statistical work, use of techniques across the whole data life
cycle, and helping other people use the organisation's statistical tools — all
teaching-and-guiding behaviours, and consistent with a head of function who
sets direction on how the organisation does statistical work rather than only
doing it themselves.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe how a statistical technique such as predictive
  analytics would apply to a real organisational question, and has no
  evidence of using one.
- 1 — Beginner: Can explain basic statistical concepts and has run a simple
  analysis, but needed close support to interpret or defend the result.
- 2 — Intermediate: Has produced a statistical model or report that informed a
  decision, using a tool the organisation already had in place, with some
  guidance from a more experienced statistician.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently designs and defends a statistical analysis
  through a full data life cycle, and can explain trade-offs between
  competing techniques for a given organisational question.
- 4 — Expert: Critiques other people's statistical analyses constructively,
  has helped colleagues elsewhere in the organisation use a statistical tool
  they did not previously know, and can point to a policy or service design
  decision their model outputs directly informed.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd talk through a statistical analysis you led that fed
  into a service or policy decision, including where you had to critique or
  correct someone else's earlier analysis.
- Ask a referee whether you have helped colleagues outside your
  immediate team adopt or use a statistical tool or technique.
- Look back at a report or model output you produced or directed, and try to explain the choice of technique and its limitations.

### Skill: Data engineering

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework's expectation here is narrower and more collaborative than at
Lead data scientist below it: the head of data science works with data
engineers and data scientists to design and provide products for the
organisation. It does not, at this level, ask someone to identify data
engineering requirements directly, cleanse and prepare data themselves, or use
specific architectures and tools hands-on — those are Lead data scientist's
own bullets, and this level's text steps back from them.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. The framework's own wording for this skill at this
level is deliberately about working effectively with data engineers to
design and provide products, not about owning the technical build — so the
baseline reflects strong, independent working competence at that
collaborative level, not the teach-others mastery of a hands-on data
engineering specialist.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has no experience working alongside data engineers, and cannot
  describe what a data engineering team would need from a data science
  product owner.
- 1 — Beginner: Can describe, in general terms, what data engineers do, but
  has needed close direction to work with one effectively on a real product.
- 2 — Intermediate: Has worked with data engineers on a product with support
  from someone more senior, but struggled to represent data science needs
  clearly when engineering constraints pushed back.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently works with data engineers and data scientists to
  design and provide a product, translating between the two disciplines
  without needing another leader to broker the conversation.
- 4 — Expert: Has set the working pattern between data science and data
  engineering teams for an organisation, and other leaders point to it as the
  model they now follow.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe a product where data engineering constraints
  changed the data science approach, and how you worked with the engineering
  team to resolve it.
- Ask a referee from a data engineering team how effectively you
  represented data science needs and understood engineering trade-offs.
- Look back at a case where you set expectations for how data science and
  data engineering teams would collaborate on a shared product.

### Skill: Data ethics and privacy

**What the framework expects at this level**

The head of data science oversees compliance with data ethics standards and
legislation across the whole organisation, not just their own team. They
develop, manage, and continuously improve an ethical framework within their
area, develop a data ethics culture and influence other people to act on it,
and ensure data ethics is applied appropriately when the organisation
formulates, implements, and evaluates its own policies and programmes.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. This matches the level's own top-line accountability to
"lead on ethics" for the whole organisation, and the skill's own text —
overseeing compliance organisation-wide, building a culture, and influencing
policy formulation — is teach-and-guide, organisation-wide work by definition.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe an ethical framework their organisation has used,
  and has no evidence of applying one.
- 1 — Beginner: Can describe data ethics principles in general terms but has
  never applied a framework to a real piece of work.
- 2 — Intermediate: Has applied an existing ethical framework to their own
  team's work, using a template or process someone else built, escalating
  genuine grey areas rather than deciding them alone.
- 3 — Advanced: Has independently run ethics reviews across more than one team
  or product, and can describe a case where they changed a design because of
  an ethics concern they identified themselves.
- 4 — Expert: Has built or substantially revised an ethical framework for an
  organisation, can point to a specific instance where they changed how a
  policy or programme was formulated or implemented because of a concern they
  raised, and has visibly influenced other leaders to act on data ethics.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe an ethical framework you built or
  substantially changed, and what evidence shows it changed practice beyond
  your own team.
- Ask for a specific example of formulating, implementing, or evaluating an
  organisational policy or programme where you applied a data ethics lens
  and it changed the outcome.
- Ask a referee, ideally someone outside your own team, whether
  you have influenced how you personally think about data ethics.

### Skill: Data science innovation

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework's text here is more modest than it is for Lead data scientist
below: the head of data science demonstrates practical knowledge of data
science tools and techniques, develops data science solutions that maximise
insight, and identifies opportunities for how data science can improve data
practices. It does not, at this level, repeat Lead's language about being "a
leader in the data science space," coaching others to be creative, or
challenging the status quo — the emphasis has shifted from hands-on technical
leadership towards practical, organisation-wide application.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. The skill's own text at this level asks for practical
knowledge and identifying opportunities rather than the in-depth, coaching-led
mastery the framework asks of Lead data scientist for the same skill name, so
the baseline sits a point below where a reflexive "top of the ladder, so
highest score" assumption would put it.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot name a data science technique that could plausibly improve
  how the organisation uses data, beyond generic buzzwords.
- 1 — Beginner: Can describe a data science tool or technique in the abstract
  but has not applied it to identify a real organisational opportunity.
- 2 — Intermediate: Has identified one opportunity to improve data practices
  using a technique someone else suggested or supported them in applying.
- 3 — Advanced: Has independently identified an opportunity to improve data
  practices, applied practical knowledge of the relevant tools and
  techniques, and developed a solution that measurably increased insight.
- 4 — Expert: Has repeatedly spotted and acted on innovation opportunities
  across multiple parts of the organisation, and other data science leaders
  seek their view before adopting a new technique or approach.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe an opportunity you spotted to improve data
  practices, what you did about it, and how you knew it had increased
  insight.
- Ask a referee whether you are someone other leaders come to for a
  view on adopting a new data science technique or tool.
- Look back at a business case, proposal, or product brief you wrote that
  argues for a specific data science innovation.

### Skill: Delivering business impact

**What the framework expects at this level**

The head of data science champions the role of data science within the
organisation, understands and champions user research, and can design and
manage processes to gather and establish user needs. They identify and create
opportunities to develop and deliver data science products that support
organisational objectives, collaborating across the organisation to fulfil
meaningful goals, and take responsibility for delivering scalable data science
products into the organisation and establishing their ongoing maintenance
support.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. This is one of the most senior-worded skill blocks in
the summary — "take responsibility for delivering scalable data science
products" and "champion the role of data science within the organisation" are
organisation-wide, accountable-for-outcomes language, matching the top-line
accountability to oversee outputs and products and to champion data science
across professions.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe a data science product they were accountable for
  taking into live use, or how it supported an organisational objective.
- 1 — Beginner: Has contributed to a data science product but was not
  responsible for whether it delivered value or reached users.
- 2 — Intermediate: Has been responsible for delivering one data science
  product into the organisation, with support from a more senior sponsor,
  including some basic engagement with user needs.
- 3 — Advanced: Has independently taken responsibility for delivering a
  scalable data science product into the organisation and establishing its
  maintenance, and can describe how user needs shaped the product.
- 4 — Expert: Has championed data science's role at an organisational level —
  for example, securing sponsorship or budget for a programme of products —
  and can point to more than one scalable product they made accountable for
  delivering and sustaining.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe a data science product you took
  responsibility for delivering at scale, including how you established
  ongoing maintenance rather than leaving it to decay after launch.
- Ask how you have championed data science's role to non-specialist senior
  colleagues, and what changed as a result.
- Ask a referee about a case where your product delivery
  materially supported an organisational objective.

### Skill: Developing data science capability

**What the framework expects at this level**

The head of data science acts as a leader or technical specialist, providing
detailed support and guidance across the organisation and helping colleagues
develop their skills. They set the direction of continuous professional
development within their team, keep up to date with new developments in data
science and match those to organisational opportunities, and talk confidently
about the benefits of data science approaches to existing and potential
customers. They demonstrate in-depth understanding of a wide range of data
science techniques — such as machine learning and natural language
processing — with detailed knowledge of at least one specialism, and use these
techniques to build data science solutions including reports, models and
dashboards.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. This directly matches the top-line accountability to
"build capability," and the skill's own text asks for setting CPD direction
and providing detailed guidance across the organisation — an explicitly
teach-and-guide-others expectation, the definition of Expert on this scale.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe having helped a colleague develop a data science
  skill, and has no view on where the organisation's data science
  capability needs to grow.
- 1 — Beginner: Has mentored one colleague informally but has not set CPD
  direction for a team or kept a structured view of organisational
  capability gaps.
- 2 — Intermediate: Has set CPD priorities for their own team with support
  from a more senior colleague, and has some depth in one data science
  specialism.
- 3 — Advanced: Has independently set the CPD direction for a team, provides
  detailed technical guidance to colleagues, and has demonstrable depth in at
  least one specialism such as machine learning or natural language
  processing.
- 4 — Expert: Has built data science capability across more than one team or
  the whole organisation, can describe how they match new developments in
  the field to organisational opportunity, and is sought out by other leaders
  for guidance on capability building.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe how you have built data science capability
  beyond your own immediate team, and what evidence shows it worked.
- Ask a referee about a specific example of you providing detailed
  technical guidance that visibly developed someone else's skill.
- Think about how you'd talk through your specialism in depth, including a
  solution — a report, model or dashboard — you built using it.

### Skill: Programming and build (data science)

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework's expectation at this level is markedly less technically
hands-on than at Lead data scientist below it. The head of data science
writes and tests scripts and creates basic models in one or more languages,
and collaborates on shared codebases using a variety of methodologies. It
does not, at this level, ask for writing complex programs, supervising junior
analysts on coding standards, understanding software architecture, or
performing user testing on products before launch — all of which are Lead
data scientist's own bullets for this skill, not this level's.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. The framework's own words — "basic models" and
"scripts," rather than complex programs or software architecture — describe
someone who retains working, hands-on fluency rather than deep technical
mastery. At the top of the ladder the emphasis has moved to direction-setting
and oversight, and this skill's own text reflects that step back honestly
rather than assuming this level must outscore the one below it.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot write or read a simple script or basic model in any
  language, and has no evidence of contributing to a shared codebase.
- 1 — Beginner: Can follow and lightly modify an existing script with close
  support, but has not independently written or tested one.
- 2 — Intermediate: Has independently written and tested a script or built a
  basic model in at least one language, and has made a contribution to a
  shared codebase, with some guidance on the team's chosen methodology.
- 3 — Advanced: Works independently across shared codebases using more than
  one methodology, and can explain trade-offs in how a basic model was built
  without needing another engineer to check the work.
- 4 — Expert: Sets or reviews coding conventions others follow and mentors
  colleagues on writing and testing scripts, going beyond what the level's
  own text asks for.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd walk through a script or basic model you wrote and
  tested recently, including how you verified it worked.
- Ask about your experience contributing to a shared codebase and which
  development methodology the team used.
- Where a work sample is practical, ask for a short script or basic model
  with an explanation of the approach, rather than a production-scale build.

### Skill: Understanding product delivery

**What the framework expects at this level**

The head of data science understands the differences between delivery
methods, such as Agile and waterfall, and can choose the most appropriate
method to deliver each product. They define the minimum viable product and
support decisions about priorities, and work with specialists in
multidisciplinary teams to smoothly deliver data science products into the
organisation.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. The skill's own text at this level is about choosing
delivery methods and supporting priority decisions rather than, as at Lead
data scientist, personally leading a team through every phase of delivery —
so the baseline reflects strong, independent judgement over delivery
approach and priorities, one step short of the hands-on team leadership Lead
data scientist's own text describes.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe the difference between two delivery methods, such
  as Agile and waterfall, or when one might suit a product better than the
  other.
- 1 — Beginner: Can describe delivery methods in general terms but has not
  chosen one for a real product themselves.
- 2 — Intermediate: Has helped choose a delivery method or define a minimum
  viable product for one product, with guidance from someone more senior.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently chooses the most appropriate delivery method
  for a given product, defines its minimum viable product, and supports
  priority decisions with a clear rationale.
- 4 — Expert: Has set how the organisation chooses delivery methods for data
  science products more broadly, and other product leaders defer to their
  judgement on priority trade-offs across more than one product.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe a product where you chose between delivery
  methods, and why you made that choice rather than a different one.
- Ask how you defined a minimum viable product for a real data science
  product, and what was deliberately left out of it.
- Ask a referee, ideally a product manager or delivery lead who worked with
  you, how you influenced priority decisions.
