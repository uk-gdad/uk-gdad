# Performance Analyst - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This is for you: a chance to rate your own current strengths against the
baseline the UK GDAD Profession Capability Framework sets for this role
level, using the framework's own words for what it covers.

A performance analyst conducts analysis. In this role, you will develop performance measurement frameworks - Key Performance Indicators (KPIs), goals, user needs and benefits - and analyse the performance of a service or product against these, adapting your approach and framework appropriately and in line with any changes. A performance analyst has a strong understanding of a wide range of sources and analytical tools needed for the project they are working on.

Work through the competency matrix below, one skill at a time, then fill in
the overall determination at the end. Treat this as a mirror, not a test —
there's no assessor watching, so being honest with yourself only costs you a
slightly less flattering picture today in exchange for a far more useful one.
Read `## Advisory` before you begin.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating you
give yourself here must never be treated as equivalent to a formal
assessment for a hiring, promotion, pay or performance decision. Use it for
your own reflection, and talk to your manager, a mentor, or your
organisation's people development team about what you find.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you're rating
yourself against what this level actually expects rather than the level
above or below it. Then think back over recent, real examples of your work —
not how you'd like to have handled something, but what you actually did.

**Rate on evidence, not impression.** For every skill, try to recall a
specific moment that supports the rating you give yourself. If you can't
think of one, that's useful information too — it might mean the rating
should be lower, or just that you haven't had the chance to show this skill
yet, which is worth noting separately.

**Be consistent with yourself.** Try to apply the same standard across every
skill, rather than being generous where you feel confident and harsh where
you feel unsure. Coming back to this after a few days, with fresh examples in
mind, often sharpens a rating more than sitting with it in one go.

**Be kind, and be honest.** This only works if you can look at the result
without flinching. A low rating here is not a verdict on you — it's a
starting point. Everyone doing this role, at every level, has skills they are
still building.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point proficiency scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

This rates how well someone does the thing, on the evidence available — not how often
they do it. A worked example, using this level's skill of Understanding analysis
across the product life cycle:

- **1 — Beginner**: they could describe, when asked, that a live service needs
  different measures from a service still in discovery.
- **2 — Intermediate**: they adjusted the measures a project team used as it moved from
  one service standard phase to the next, with some support.
- **3 — Advanced**: they independently ensured the team measured performance as
  appropriate for its current phase, and planned data requirements for the phase ahead.

A performance analyst's baseline for this skill, below, is 2.

## About this role level

**The role: performance analyst.** A performance analyst conducts analysis. In this
role, you develop performance measurement frameworks — key performance indicators
(KPIs), goals, user needs and benefits — and analyse the performance of a service or
product against these, adapting your approach and framework appropriately and in line
with any changes.

**The level: performance analyst.** The framework describes this level as having a
strong understanding of a wide range of sources and analytical tools needed for the
project being worked on. It is the second of five levels in this role: associate
performance analyst, performance analyst, senior performance analyst, lead performance
analyst, and head of performance analysis. At this level, you are responsible for the
performance analysis in your given area; help the project team measure and assess
outcomes (for example, what good looks like and how it will be measured); help plan and
ensure project delivery; find opportunities to share information with the project team,
community colleagues and others; work independently but know to seek guidance when
necessary; are adaptable in applying analysis techniques to new contexts, especially when
acting as the sole representative for performance analysts on a project; create a
performance measurement framework for a project with the wider team, including
identifying reporting requirements and advising on procurement, implementation and
validation of tools to deliver against these requirements; are responsible for the
accuracy and quality of data and analysis, and how they are used; and are engaged with,
and actively contribute to, the performance analysis community.

**The skills this level names**, in the summary's order: Analysis and insight;
Communicating analysis and insight; Performance measurement; Quality assurance of data
and analysis; Technical understanding (performance analyst); Understanding analysis
across the product life cycle; and User-centred analysis.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Analysis and insight

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a performance analyst to understand and help teams to apply a range
of techniques to analyse data and provide insight, be proactive and present compelling
findings that inform wider decisions, and apply innovative approaches to resolve
problems. This wording is also used unchanged at the senior performance analyst level
above.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Helping a whole team apply a range of techniques, and being
proactive rather than reactive with compelling findings, is a strong, independent
grasp — this text carries through to the senior level too, so both levels share this
baseline.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not applied more than one technique for analysis and insight
  independently.
- 1 — Beginner: has applied a single technique with close direction.
- 2 — Intermediate: has applied a range of techniques with some guidance and presented
  a finding a colleague acted on.
- 3 — Advanced: independently applies a range of techniques, proactively presents
  compelling findings that inform a wider decision, and has applied an innovative
  approach to resolve a problem.
- 4 — Expert: teaches a team to apply a range of analysis techniques themselves.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for a finding you proactively surfaced — not one you were asked to produce —
  that changed a decision.
- A structured interview question: "Tell me about a problem where the obvious
  analytical approach didn't work, and what you tried instead."
- Ask a referee about the range of techniques you have independently applied.

### Skill: Communicating analysis and insight

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework's wording here is identical to the associate performance analyst level
below: choose the appropriate medium to communicate findings, shape and share
communications relevant to the audience, present analysis and visualisations clearly
(with support), and give tactical recommendations. The published expectation for this
specific skill does not step up between these two levels, even though the level's wider
accountabilities clearly do.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. Because the text repeats the associate level's, the honest
baseline for this one skill stays the same as it was there, even though most of this
level's other skills sit higher.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not chosen a communication medium or shaped a message for an audience.
- 1 — Beginner: has helped format a findings report someone else designed.
- 2 — Intermediate: has chosen a format for findings, shaped the message for a named
  audience, and given a tactical recommendation, with some support presenting
  visualisations.
- 3 — Advanced: independently chooses medium, shapes communications and gives tactical
  recommendations, without needing support on visualisations.
- 4 — Expert: coaches others on communicating analysis clearly.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for a real report or chart you produced for a specific audience, and your
  choices in producing it.
- A structured interview question: "Tell me about tailoring the same findings for two
  different audiences."
- Look back at a written report or slide deck you produced, if available.

### Skill: Performance measurement

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a performance analyst to participate in discussions determining
which performance measurements are appropriate, with guidance from more experienced
colleagues, and to demonstrate experience in turning business needs and goals into
performance measures.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. "With guidance from more experienced colleagues" is a clear
match for "can perform tasks with some guidance" — a real step up from the associate
level's purely explanatory bullet, but not yet independent.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not turned a business need or goal into a performance measure.
- 1 — Beginner: has observed a discussion determining appropriate performance
  measurements without contributing.
- 2 — Intermediate: has participated in determining appropriate measurements with
  guidance, and turned a business need into a performance measure with support.
- 3 — Advanced: independently turns business needs and goals into performance
  measures for a project.
- 4 — Expert: leads the development of performance measurement frameworks across
  multiple projects.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Tell me about turning a business goal into a
  specific, measurable performance indicator."
- Ask for an example of participating in a discussion about which measures were
  appropriate, and what you contributed.
- Ask a referee how much guidance you typically needed choosing a performance
  measure.

### Skill: Quality assurance of data and analysis

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a performance analyst to identify appropriate ways to collect,
collate and prepare data, decide if data and analysis is accurate and fit for purpose,
perform data preparation and cleansing with limited guidance, undertake quality
assurance processes, and peer-review colleagues' outputs from data analysis to ensure
quality.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. "With limited guidance" and active ownership including
peer review, a clear step up from the associate level's purely explanatory wording,
matching "can perform tasks with some guidance."

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not prepared, cleansed or quality-checked a dataset.
- 1 — Beginner: has cleansed or prepared a small dataset only with close, step-by-step
  direction.
- 2 — Intermediate: prepares and cleanses data with limited guidance, decides whether a
  dataset is fit for purpose, and has peer-reviewed a colleague's analysis output.
- 3 — Advanced: independently sets up how a dataset should be collected, collated and
  prepared for a real project.
- 4 — Expert: designs and owns a quality assurance process for others to follow.

**Evidence to gather**

- A work sample: a dataset with a few realistic quality issues, asking you to prepare
  it and say whether it is fit for purpose.
- A structured interview question: "Tell me about a time you peer-reviewed someone
  else's analysis. What did you check, and what did you find?"
- Ask a referee how much guidance you typically needed to prepare data for
  analysis.

### Skill: Technical understanding (performance analyst)

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a performance analyst to show an understanding of the core technical
concepts related to the role, and to apply them.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. "Understand... and apply" core concepts, a step up from the
associate level's "show an awareness", but not yet the deep, landscape-level
understanding the senior level above asks for.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot apply a core technical concept related to the role.
- 1 — Beginner: knows the names of core technical concepts but has not applied one.
- 2 — Intermediate: understands and applies core technical concepts related to the
  role, such as how an analytics platform collects data.
- 3 — Advanced: independently applies a deep understanding of technical concepts to
  solve a non-obvious problem.
- 4 — Expert: keeps the wider team current on relevant technical concepts.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Explain, as if to a non-specialist, how the
  analytics tool you've used actually collects its data."
- Think about how you'd apply a core technical concept to a scenario — for example, explaining
  why a particular metric might be misleading.
- Ask a referee about your application of technical concepts in real work.

### Skill: Understanding analysis across the product life cycle

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a performance analyst to understand how the needs of the team and
product vary across the product life cycle, ensure the team is measuring performance as
appropriate for the relevant service standard phase, manage the delivery of services or
products at different phases, and plan and look at data requirements in future phases.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. Active ownership verbs ("ensure", "manage the delivery",
"plan") mark a clear step up from the associate level's single descriptive bullet,
though the range and independence of the senior level above is not yet expected.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not adjusted measurement to a life cycle phase.
- 1 — Beginner: can describe what phase a product is in and what measures might suit
  it, without having managed any of it.
- 2 — Intermediate: has ensured a team measured performance appropriately for its
  current phase, and looked ahead at data needs for the next phase, with guidance.
- 3 — Advanced: independently manages performance delivery across multiple life cycle
  phases and projects.
- 4 — Expert: advises other teams on measurement and data requirements across the life
  cycle.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Tell me about adjusting how you measured
  performance as a product moved from one phase to the next."
- Ask how you planned data requirements ahead of a phase change.
- Ask a referee about your role in ensuring a team measured the right things
  for its phase.

### Skill: User-centred analysis

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a performance analyst to use quantitative and qualitative data
about users to turn user focus into outcomes, deliver analysis and information that
addresses stakeholder needs and provides recommendations, effectively collaborate with
user researchers and other user-focused professions, and guide others on approaches to
understand user stories.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. "Deliver... recommendations", "effectively collaborate", and
notably "guide others" already appear at this level — a strong, independent grasp,
including an early guide-others expectation the associate level's single awareness
bullet does not have.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not turned user data into a performance outcome or recommendation.
- 1 — Beginner: has used a piece of user data with close direction.
- 2 — Intermediate: has used quantitative and qualitative user data to inform analysis,
  with some guidance.
- 3 — Advanced: independently turns user focus into outcomes, delivers recommendations
  addressing stakeholder needs, collaborates effectively with user researchers, and
  guides a colleague on understanding user stories.
- 4 — Expert: sets the strategic approach to user-centred analysis across projects.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Tell me about combining quantitative data with
  qualitative user research to make a recommendation."
- Ask for an example of guiding a colleague on how to understand or use a user story.
- Ask a referee about your collaboration with user researchers.
