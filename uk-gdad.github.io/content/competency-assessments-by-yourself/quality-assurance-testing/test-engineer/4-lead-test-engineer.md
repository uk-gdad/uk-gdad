# Lead Test Engineer - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This is for you: a chance to rate your own current strengths against the
baseline the UK GDAD Profession Capability Framework sets for this role
level, using the framework's own words for what it covers.

A test engineer undertakes test planning activity including discovery capture, test definition and the estimation of test effort, as part of a broader risk-based approach. A lead test engineer writes new automation test frameworks based on project requirements.

Work through the competency matrix below, one skill at a time, then fill in
the overall determination at the end. Treat this as a mirror, not a test —
there's no assessor watching, so being honest with yourself only costs you a
slightly less flattering picture today in exchange for a far more useful one.
Read `## Advisory` before you begin.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating you
give yourself here must never be treated as equivalent to a formal
assessment for a hiring, promotion, pay or performance decision. Use it for
your own reflection, and talk to your manager, a mentor, or your
organisation's people development team about what you find.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you're rating
yourself against what this level actually expects rather than the level
above or below it. Then think back over recent, real examples of your work —
not how you'd like to have handled something, but what you actually did.

**Rate on evidence, not impression.** For every skill, try to recall a
specific moment that supports the rating you give yourself. If you can't
think of one, that's useful information too — it might mean the rating
should be lower, or just that you haven't had the chance to show this skill
yet, which is worth noting separately.

**Be consistent with yourself.** Try to apply the same standard across every
skill, rather than being generous where you feel confident and harsh where
you feel unsure. Coming back to this after a few days, with fresh examples in
mind, often sharpens a rating more than sitting with it in one go.

**Be kind, and be honest.** This only works if you can look at the result
without flinching. A low rating here is not a verdict on you — it's a
starting point. Everyone doing this role, at every level, has skills they are
still building.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point scale.
This is a proficiency scale: it rates how well someone does the thing, on
the evidence available, not how often they do it or how they feel about it.

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

**Worked example: Test and quality planning.** At 3, someone works with
other teams to develop and implement a quality testing approach and
contributes to organisational strategy, but the strategy itself belongs to
someone else. At 4 — the baseline for this level — someone creates and
manages multiple quality testing plans, approaches and strategies, leads and
guides multiple teams in adopting them, and advocates for early quality
testing involvement in how the organisation delivers work generally, using
data to guide teams across the organisation in optimising their approach.
Look for the difference between "I contribute to our organisation's strategy
when asked" (3) and "several teams' quality testing strategy exists because
I created and lead it" (4).

## About this role level

**The role: test engineer.** A test engineer undertakes test planning
activity — capturing what has been discovered, defining tests, and estimating
the effort testing will take — as part of a broader, risk-based approach to
quality.

**This level: lead test engineer.** A lead test engineer writes new
automation test frameworks based on project requirements. At this level, the
framework expects someone to:

- promote the use of open source tools
- be proficient in multiple test scripting languages
- have a presence in external test communities and regularly share learning
- work closely with leads across the Government Digital and Data profession
  to champion quality
- guide test team members to design test strategies, which may involve more
  complex and larger-scale delivery
- manage communications between projects to control integration and
  dependencies

**Where this level sits.** The test engineer role has four levels, and this
is the fourth and most senior:

1. Associate test engineer
2. Test engineer
3. Senior test engineer
4. **Lead test engineer** — this level

Below this level, a senior test engineer works at a complex, cross-team
scale and mentors individual colleagues. A lead test engineer's remit is
organisation-wide: setting standards other teams follow, representing the
organisation's test community externally, and managing the communication
between projects that senior test engineers are not accountable for.

**The skills this assessment covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Communicating between the technical and non-technical
2. Designing and executing tests
3. Managing, reporting and resolving defects
4. Test analysis
5. Test and quality planning
6. Test engineering

The framework's wording at this level is consistently about setting
standards, leading multiple teams, and influencing beyond the organisation
itself — "set standards", "influence organisational decisions", "lead and
coach others", "represent... to large audiences inside and outside the
organisation". There is no remaining reference to support someone
receives anywhere in this level's text. Every skill here carries a baseline
of Expert: full, demonstrated mastery that others are actively taught or
led by, which is the highest point on the scale and the top of this role's
ladder.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead test engineer is expected to mediate between people and strengthen
relationships, adopting the appropriate communication method with
stakeholders at all levels, to manage stakeholder expectations and moderate
difficult discussions about high-risk and complex topics even within
constrained timescales, and to speak on behalf of, and represent, the
community to large audiences inside and outside the organisation.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. The accountability text explicitly names representing
the organisation to external audiences and mediating high-risk, complex
discussions under time pressure — full mastery exercised on behalf of
others, which is the definition of Expert on this scale.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Avoids representing testing to audiences outside their own
  immediate team.
- 1 — Beginner: Communicates within their team, but has not moderated a
  high-risk discussion or spoken to an external audience.
- 2 — Intermediate: Communicates across teams with some guidance, but has
  not yet mediated a complex, time-constrained discussion unsupported.
- 3 — Advanced: Manages difficult stakeholder discussions unsupported within
  the organisation, but has not yet represented the wider test community to
  a large audience.
- 4 — Expert: Mediates high-risk, complex discussions under real time
  pressure, and has spoken on behalf of the test community to a large
  audience inside or outside the organisation.

**Evidence to gather**

- Structured interview question: "Tell me about a high-risk, time-pressured
  discussion you moderated. What made it high-risk, and how did you handle
  the timescale?"
- Work sample or portfolio: a recording, slide deck, or write-up of a talk
  or presentation you have given to an external or large internal
  audience.
- Referee question: "How has you represented the organisation's
  test community externally?"

### Skill: Designing and executing tests

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead test engineer is expected to set standards and influence
organisational decisions for test types, techniques, design and execution,
to coach others in test types, techniques, design and execution, to
advocate for continuous improvement and refinement of test types and
techniques, and to make strategic decisions on new or improved test types
and techniques used in their area.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Standard-setting and strategic decision-making across
the organisation, plus coaching others, is the organisation-wide,
teach-others definition of Expert on this scale.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Uses whatever test types and techniques are already
  established, without shaping them.
- 1 — Beginner: Applies established test types and techniques to their own
  work only.
- 2 — Intermediate: Guides technique choices for their own team with some
  guidance from others.
- 3 — Advanced: Leads others in designing and executing tests within their
  own or a small number of teams, and contributes to a written standard.
- 4 — Expert: Sets the organisational standard for test types, techniques,
  design and execution, coaches others in them, and makes the strategic
  call on new or improved approaches in their area.

**Evidence to gather**

- Document review: an organisational standard, guide, or framework
  documentation you have written or owns.
- Structured interview question: "Describe a strategic decision you made
  about which test type or technique to adopt in your area. What was the
  alternative, and why did you choose what you chose?"
- Referee question: "How does you coach others in test design and
  execution, beyond your own immediate team?"

### Skill: Managing, reporting and resolving defects

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead test engineer is expected to lead and coach others in improving test
and defect management processes, to support others in assessing complex and
challenging defects across the organisation, to lead and coach others in
using defect patterns and trends to make tactical and strategic
recommendations, and to influence improvements to quality processes informed
by defect patterns and trends.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Every accountability here is about leading and
coaching others across the organisation, with no remaining reference to someone's own supervised practice — the clearest possible fit for full
mastery exercised through others.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Manages defects within their own remit only.
- 1 — Beginner: Escalates complex defects to someone more senior rather than
  assessing them.
- 2 — Intermediate: Assesses moderately complex defects with some guidance.
- 3 — Advanced: Manages and escalates complex defects across teams
  unsupported, and contributes to process improvements.
- 4 — Expert: Leads and coaches others in improving the organisation's
  defect management process, supports others across the organisation on
  complex or challenging defects, and uses defect patterns and trends to
  make strategic recommendations that change how quality is managed.

**Evidence to gather**

- Structured interview question: "Describe a strategic recommendation you
  made from defect patterns and trends. What changed as a result?"
- Referee question: "Has you supported another team with a
  complex defect that was outside your own remit? What happened?"
- Document review: a process improvement, retrospective write-up, or
  standard you authored or led.

### Skill: Test analysis

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead test engineer is expected to lead and guide multiple teams in test
analysis, ensuring it is implemented early in the life cycle, to advocate
for risk-based analysis to drive improvements across many teams, and to set
standards and principles for test analysis across the organisation.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "Set standards and principles... across the
organisation" is the framework's own words for full, organisation-wide
mastery — precisely what distinguishes this level's version of the skill
from the cross-team leadership named at senior test engineer.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Analyses only their own team's work.
- 1 — Beginner: Contributes analysis when asked, without shaping the
  approach.
- 2 — Intermediate: Analyses complex information for their own team
  unsupported.
- 3 — Advanced: Leads analysis work across more than one team and coaches
  individuals in it.
- 4 — Expert: Sets the standards and principles multiple teams use for test
  analysis, and drives risk-based improvements across many teams at once.

**Evidence to gather**

- Document review: an organisational standard or principle document for
  test analysis you authored.
- Structured interview question: "Tell me about a risk-based analysis
  improvement you drove across more than a handful of teams. How did you get
  it adopted?"
- Referee question: "How many teams look to you for test analysis
  standards, and how did that come about?"

### Skill: Test and quality planning

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead test engineer is expected to create and manage multiple quality
testing plans, approaches and strategies, to lead and guide multiple teams
in adopting quality testing strategy, to advocate for early quality testing
involvement in organisational delivery processes, and to guide teams across
an organisation in optimising their quality testing approaches, plans and
strategies using appropriate data.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Owning multiple strategies and guiding multiple teams
across the organisation is the full-mastery, teach-others end of the scale,
distinct from the "contribute to organisational strategy" wording at senior
test engineer.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Follows whatever quality testing plan already exists for their
  team.
- 1 — Beginner: Adapts a plan for their own team, with support.
- 2 — Intermediate: Develops a quality testing approach for their own team
  unsupported.
- 3 — Advanced: Works with other teams to develop shared approaches and
  contributes to organisational strategy set by someone else.
- 4 — Expert: Creates and manages multiple quality testing plans, approaches
  and strategies, leads multiple teams in adopting them, and uses data to
  guide organisation-wide optimisation.

**Evidence to gather**

- Document review: more than one quality testing strategy or plan you have created or owns.
- Structured interview question: "Describe how you have used data to guide
  more than one team's quality testing approach."
- Referee question: "How does you advocate for early quality
  testing involvement at an organisational level, not just within a

### Skill: Test engineering

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead test engineer is expected to establish and lead test engineering
practices, standards and behaviours, to influence and guide test engineering
technology and tool choices across the organisation, and to advocate for the
adoption and use of appropriate testing solutions, ensuring alignment with
organisational goals and quality objectives.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. This is a founder-and-leader description — establishing
practices and influencing technology choices organisation-wide — matched by
the level's own accountabilities to write new automation frameworks from
project requirements and be proficient in multiple scripting languages.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Uses the test engineering tools and practices already
  established by others without question.
- 1 — Beginner: Maintains existing frameworks and tools within their own
  team.
- 2 — Intermediate: Extends or standardises a framework for their own team
  unsupported.
- 3 — Advanced: Develops a reusable framework that more than one team uses
  and coaches colleagues on its standards.
- 4 — Expert: Establishes and leads the organisation's test engineering
  practices, standards and behaviours, and influences which technologies and
  tools the organisation adopts.

**Evidence to gather**

- Work sample or portfolio: an automation test framework you wrote
  from project requirements, and evidence of who else uses it.
- Structured interview question: "Describe a test engineering standard or
  practice you established that the organisation, not just your team, now
  follows."
- Referee question: "How has you influenced technology or tool
