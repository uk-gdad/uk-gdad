# Test Engineer - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This is for you: a chance to rate your own current strengths against the
baseline the UK GDAD Profession Capability Framework sets for this role
level, using the framework's own words for what it covers.

A test engineer undertakes test planning activity including discovery capture, test definition and the estimation of test effort, as part of a broader risk-based approach. A test engineer is responsible for writing, debugging and refactoring test code.

Work through the competency matrix below, one skill at a time, then fill in
the overall determination at the end. Treat this as a mirror, not a test —
there's no assessor watching, so being honest with yourself only costs you a
slightly less flattering picture today in exchange for a far more useful one.
Read `## Advisory` before you begin.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating you
give yourself here must never be treated as equivalent to a formal
assessment for a hiring, promotion, pay or performance decision. Use it for
your own reflection, and talk to your manager, a mentor, or your
organisation's people development team about what you find.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you're rating
yourself against what this level actually expects rather than the level
above or below it. Then think back over recent, real examples of your work —
not how you'd like to have handled something, but what you actually did.

**Rate on evidence, not impression.** For every skill, try to recall a
specific moment that supports the rating you give yourself. If you can't
think of one, that's useful information too — it might mean the rating
should be lower, or just that you haven't had the chance to show this skill
yet, which is worth noting separately.

**Be consistent with yourself.** Try to apply the same standard across every
skill, rather than being generous where you feel confident and harsh where
you feel unsure. Coming back to this after a few days, with fresh examples in
mind, often sharpens a rating more than sitting with it in one go.

**Be kind, and be honest.** This only works if you can look at the result
without flinching. A low rating here is not a verdict on you — it's a
starting point. Everyone doing this role, at every level, has skills they are
still building.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point scale.
This is a proficiency scale: it rates how well someone does the thing, on
the evidence available, not how often they do it or how they feel about it.

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

**Worked example: Test engineering.** At 1, someone can explain what a
continuous integration pipeline does and has fixed a small failing test only
with someone standing over the change. At 2 — the baseline for this level — someone uses the team's test engineering frameworks and tools to support
testing day to day, follows practices such as source control and continuous
integration and deployment, and creates or maintains automated tests and
coded solutions with some support on the harder cases, without needing that
support for routine ones. At 3, the same person would be creating and
maintaining automated tests unsupported across the board, and would diagnose
a flaky or failing test on their own rather than asking for a second pair of
eyes. Look for the difference between "I can do this, but I check the tricky
parts with someone" (2) and "I do this end to end without help, including
when it goes wrong" (3).

## About this role level

**The role: test engineer.** A test engineer undertakes test planning
activity — capturing what has been discovered, defining tests, and estimating
the effort testing will take — as part of a broader, risk-based approach to
quality.

**This level: test engineer.** A test engineer is responsible for writing,
debugging and refactoring test code. At this level, the framework expects
someone to:

- work closely with software developers to reach a common understanding of
  the code base and test coverage at unit level
- collaborate with analysts to ensure the required business scenarios are
  covered in the acceptance test scripts
- work on both functional and non-functional areas of an application
- coach and mentor testers

**Where this level sits.** The test engineer role has four levels, and this
is the second:

1. Associate test engineer
2. **Test engineer** — this level
3. Senior test engineer
4. Lead test engineer

Below this level, an associate test engineer works under supervision and
learns the basis of testing. Above it, a senior test engineer works at
greater scale and complexity, is proficient across a range of coding
languages, identifies risk from automation results, and mentors other team
members rather than coaching individual testers.

**The skills this assessment covers**, in the framework's order:

1. Communicating between the technical and non-technical
2. Designing and executing tests
3. Managing, reporting and resolving defects
4. Test analysis
5. Test and quality planning
6. Test engineering

The framework's wording at this level has largely dropped the "with support"
and "explain" language of the associate level. Most accountabilities here are
things someone now does, with a smaller amount of remaining support named
explicitly — "with some support" appears for the harder or newer parts of
several skills, but no longer for the routine parts. That is the shape of an
Intermediate baseline: knows the basics and performs with some guidance, not
close supervision throughout.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What the framework expects at this level**

A test engineer is expected to communicate effectively with both technical
and non-technical stakeholders, to support and host discussions within a
multidisciplinary team where the dynamics can be difficult, to be an
advocate for the team externally, and to manage differing stakeholder
perspectives.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. The framework has dropped the "explain" framing
of the associate level and now expects someone to run communication
themselves, but the scope named — team dynamics and external advocacy for
the team — is not yet the senior-stakeholder, high-risk communication a
senior test engineer is expected to manage.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Avoids explaining technical findings to non-technical
  colleagues, or leaves it for someone else to do.
- 1 — Beginner: Can explain a technical finding to a non-technical
  colleague when asked directly, but has not hosted or supported a
  discussion with mixed or difficult dynamics.
- 2 — Intermediate: Communicates effectively day to day with both technical
  and non-technical stakeholders, and has supported or hosted at least one
  team discussion with difficult dynamics.
- 3 — Advanced: Manages differing stakeholder perspectives unsupported, and
  is a reliable external advocate for the team without needing to check what
  to say first.
- 4 — Expert: Is sought out specifically to manage difficult communication
  involving senior or diverse stakeholders, and coaches others in how to do
  it.

**Evidence to gather**

- Structured interview question: "Tell me about a discussion within your
  team that had difficult dynamics. What was your part in it?"
- Referee question: "How does you handle a stakeholder who
  disagrees with a testing decision?"
- Observed scenario: sit in on a stand-up or a triage meeting you
  runs or contributes to, and note how you handle a disagreement.

### Skill: Designing and executing tests

**What the framework expects at this level**

A test engineer is expected to set up suitable test environments with some
support, to select appropriate test types and techniques with some support,
to design, build, maintain and execute tests that align to user needs and
requirements, to conduct exploratory testing, and to research and try new
test types and techniques.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. Two of the five accountabilities are explicitly
"with some support" — environment set-up and technique selection — while the
rest describe independent practice, which is the mixed picture Intermediate
is built to describe.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot design or build a test without a template, and has not
  attempted exploratory testing.
- 1 — Beginner: Builds and runs simple tests from a template, and needs
  close guidance to set up an environment or choose a technique.
- 2 — Intermediate: Designs, builds and executes tests independently for
  routine work, conducts exploratory testing, and checks with someone else
  on environment set-up or technique choice for less familiar work.
- 3 — Advanced: Sets up environments and selects techniques unsupported,
  including for unfamiliar work, and has tried a new test type or technique
  on their own initiative.
- 4 — Expert: Is someone others ask when a test environment or technique
  choice is genuinely difficult, and has introduced a new technique the team
  now uses.

**Evidence to gather**

- Work sample: ask you to design and set up a test for an
  unfamiliar feature, and note where you ask for help.
- Structured interview question: "Tell me about a piece of exploratory
  testing you did. What did you find that a scripted test would have
  missed?"
- Structured interview question: "Describe a new test type or technique you
  have tried. What made you try it, and did it work?"

### Skill: Managing, reporting and resolving defects

**What the framework expects at this level**

A test engineer is expected to collaborate with others to create a defect
management process, with support, to critically assess dependencies, defects
and risks with support, to contribute to mitigation and contingency plans,
and to clearly communicate risks and the impact of defects to stakeholders.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. Two accountabilities are explicitly "with
support" — creating the process and critically assessing risk — while
communicating impact and contributing to mitigation plans are treated as
things someone already does.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Reports defects but does not consider dependencies, risk, or the
  impact on stakeholders.
- 1 — Beginner: Reports and tracks defects reliably, but needs close
  guidance to assess how a defect affects other work or to help build a
  mitigation plan.
- 2 — Intermediate: Contributes to the team's defect management process and
  to mitigation plans, and clearly communicates the impact of a defect to
  stakeholders, checking harder risk assessments with someone else.
- 3 — Advanced: Critically assesses dependencies, defects and risks
  unsupported, and manages escalation across more than one team.
- 4 — Expert: Leads improvements to the defect management process itself and
  is trusted to make the final call on a difficult mitigation decision.

**Evidence to gather**

- Structured interview question: "Describe a defect that affected more than
  your own work. How did you work out what else it touched?"
- Work sample: ask you to draft a short stakeholder communication
  about the impact of a real or hypothetical defect.
- Referee question: "How does you contribute to a mitigation plan
  when a defect cannot be fixed immediately?"

### Skill: Test analysis

**What the framework expects at this level**

A test engineer is expected to work with stakeholders to determine which
functional and non-functional quality characteristics add value, to
determine what to test following an agreed approach, to identify and
advocate for test needs such as data, access and environments with support,
and to analyse information to identify risks.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. Advocating for test needs is explicitly "with
support"; determining what adds value and analysing for risk are treated as
independent, giving an overall Intermediate picture rather than a fully
independent one.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Tests whatever is asked without considering which quality
  characteristics matter for the work.
- 1 — Beginner: Can identify obvious quality characteristics for a piece of
  work, but needs support to work out what to test and why.
- 2 — Intermediate: Works with stakeholders to decide which quality
  characteristics matter, decides what to test following the team's agreed
  approach, and analyses information to spot risks, asking for support when
  advocating for test data, access or environments they do not have.
- 3 — Advanced: Advocates for the test needs they require unsupported, and
  identifies risks in complex or unfamiliar information without help.
- 4 — Expert: Coaches others in test analysis and is someone a team turns
  to when it is not clear what testing would actually add value.

**Evidence to gather**

- Structured interview question: "Describe a time you decided a piece of
  work needed non-functional testing, not just functional. How did you
  decide?"
- Work sample: give you a short requirement and try to say
  what you would test and why, and what data, access or environment you
  would need.
- Referee question: "Has you had to argue for test access or data
  you did not have? How did that go?"

### Skill: Test and quality planning

**What the framework expects at this level**

A test engineer is expected to create or adapt quality testing approaches
based on risk, with some support, to follow a quality testing strategy and
contribute to its development, and to contribute to continuous improvement
of quality testing approaches, plans and strategies.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. Creating or adapting an approach is explicitly
"with some support", while following and contributing to the strategy is
treated as independent — a mixed picture that sits at Intermediate rather
than Beginner or Advanced.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Follows a test plan without knowing why it says what it says.
- 1 — Beginner: Follows the team's test strategy, but needs close support to
  adapt an approach when the risk profile of the work changes.
- 2 — Intermediate: Follows and contributes to the team's strategy, and
  adapts an approach to a new piece of work based on risk with some support
  on the harder judgement calls.
- 3 — Advanced: Creates and adapts quality testing approaches unsupported,
  based on a clear read of the risk involved.
- 4 — Expert: Shapes organisational quality testing strategy and is
  consulted when other teams are unsure how to approach risk.

**Evidence to gather**

- Structured interview question: "Tell me about a time you adapted the
  standard test approach because the risk was different. What changed, and
  why?"
- Document review: ask to see a contribution you made to a test
  plan or strategy document.
- Structured interview question: "What is one improvement you have
  suggested to how your team plans testing?"

### Skill: Test engineering

**What the framework expects at this level**

A test engineer is expected to use test engineering frameworks and tools to
support testing activities, to follow test engineering practices and
standards such as source control and continuous integration and deployment
pipelines, to integrate and execute tests to ensure early testing and
continuous feedback, to create and maintain automated tests with some
support, and to write and review coded solutions with some support.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. The two most demanding accountabilities — writing
and maintaining automated tests, and writing and reviewing coded
solutions — are explicitly "with some support", which is the core of what
makes this role different from the associate level without yet reaching
independent mastery.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe how the team's continuous integration pipeline
  runs tests, or where automated tests live.
- 1 — Beginner: Runs and reads existing automated tests, but needs close
  support to write new ones or to review someone else's coded solution.
- 2 — Intermediate: Uses the team's frameworks and tools to support testing,
  follows source control and pipeline practices, integrates and executes
  tests for early feedback, and creates and maintains automated tests and
  reviews coded solutions with some support on the harder cases.
- 3 — Advanced: Creates and maintains automated tests and reviews coded
  solutions unsupported, and can be relied on to keep the pipeline healthy.
- 4 — Expert: Extends or improves the team's test engineering frameworks and
  tools themselves, and is proficient enough in coding languages to review
  others' work confidently.

**Evidence to gather**

- Work sample: ask you to write or review a small automated test,
  and to explain the choices you made in the code.
- Structured interview question: "Describe a coded test solution you wrote
  or reviewed recently. What would you change about it now?"
- Direct observation: watch you integrate a change through the
  pipeline and note how much support you need with the pipeline itself
  rather than the test content.
