# Problem Analyst - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This is for you: a chance to rate your own current strengths against the
baseline the UK GDAD Profession Capability Framework sets for this role
level, using the framework's own words for what it covers.

Problem managers aim to resolve and control the root causes of incidents caused by errors within the IT infrastructure. In this role, you will be expected to work to prevent the recurrence of these incidents. At this level, you will be responsible for maintaining software systems once they are up and running and, where necessary, support the problem manager in co-ordinating the resolution of any problems.

Work through the competency matrix below, one skill at a time, then fill in
the overall determination at the end. Treat this as a mirror, not a test —
there's no assessor watching, so being honest with yourself only costs you a
slightly less flattering picture today in exchange for a far more useful one.
Read `## Advisory` before you begin.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating you
give yourself here must never be treated as equivalent to a formal
assessment for a hiring, promotion, pay or performance decision. Use it for
your own reflection, and talk to your manager, a mentor, or your
organisation's people development team about what you find.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you're rating
yourself against what this level actually expects rather than the level
above or below it. Then think back over recent, real examples of your work —
not how you'd like to have handled something, but what you actually did.

**Rate on evidence, not impression.** For every skill, try to recall a
specific moment that supports the rating you give yourself. If you can't
think of one, that's useful information too — it might mean the rating
should be lower, or just that you haven't had the chance to show this skill
yet, which is worth noting separately.

**Be consistent with yourself.** Try to apply the same standard across every
skill, rather than being generous where you feel confident and harsh where
you feel unsure. Coming back to this after a few days, with fresh examples in
mind, often sharpens a rating more than sitting with it in one go.

**Be kind, and be honest.** This only works if you can look at the result
without flinching. A low rating here is not a verdict on you — it's a
starting point. Everyone doing this role, at every level, has skills they are
still building.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

This is a **proficiency** scale, not a frequency scale: it rates how well someone does the thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it.

**Worked example — Incident management.** At 1 — Beginner, someone might
describe logging an incident correctly but needing help working out its
likely cause. At 2 — Intermediate, someone for problem analyst should be
able to describe diagnosing and prioritising a straightforward incident
themselves, investigating its cause and finding a resolution, with a
colleague available to check their reasoning. That combination — independent
diagnosis on routine cases, with support still nearby for harder ones — is
what "Intermediate" evidence looks like at this level.

## About this role level

The IT operations profession's **problem manager** role covers people who aim
to resolve and control the root causes of incidents caused by errors within
the IT infrastructure, working to prevent those incidents recurring. It has
two levels:

1. **Problem analyst** — this level
2. Problem manager

The **problem analyst** is the entry level of the role. They are responsible
for maintaining software systems once they are up and running and, where
necessary, support the problem manager in co-ordinating the resolution of any
problems. The framework does not list a separate set of "at this role level"
accountabilities for this level beyond its skills — the skill bullets below
are its full description of what this level does.

This role level names eleven skills: Asset and configuration management,
Availability and capacity management, Change management, Community
collaboration, Continuity management, Incident management, Ownership and
initiative, Problem management, Service focus, Service management framework
knowledge, and User focus.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Asset and configuration management

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a problem analyst to track, log and correct information
to protect assets and components.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. This is a procedural, supporting task at this level —
recording data accurately — rather than owning configuration risk, so a
developing, closely-supervised grasp is expected.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot locate or correct an asset or configuration record without
  step-by-step help.
- 1 — Beginner: Logs and corrects straightforward records under supervision.
- 2 — Intermediate: Logs and corrects records unsupervised, and flags
  anomalies to a senior colleague.
- 3 — Advanced: Maintains accurate records across a varied caseload and links
  configuration issues to problems they are investigating.
- 4 — Expert: Spots systemic data quality issues and helps train others in
  logging discipline.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe how you would log and correct an inaccurate
  asset record you discovered while investigating a problem.
- Look back at a sample of records you have previously updated, if
  available.

### Skill: Availability and capacity management

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a problem analyst to manage service components to
ensure they meet business needs and key performance indicators (KPIs).

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. Managing service components against KPIs is a
meaningful responsibility, but at entry level it is expected with guidance
rather than full independent judgement about trade-offs.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot identify whether a service component is meeting its KPIs.
- 1 — Beginner: Checks a component against its KPIs when asked, with support
  interpreting the result.
- 2 — Intermediate: Monitors assigned components against KPIs unprompted, and
  raises concerns.
- 3 — Advanced: Investigates why a component is failing its KPIs and proposes
  a fix.
- 4 — Expert: Anticipates capacity or availability problems before they
  breach KPIs, across several components.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of noticing a service component was underperforming
  against a KPI, and what you did.
- Think about how you'd explain how you would interpret an availability report showing
  a dip in performance.

### Skill: Change management

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a problem analyst to implement change requests and
apply change control procedures under supervision.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. The framework says explicitly "under supervision",
which sets the baseline directly — this is a skill this level is expected to
be learning, not one it should already hold independently.

**Behavioural indicators**

  others on the procedure.

**Evidence to gather**

### Skill: Community collaboration

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a problem analyst to contribute to the work of others,
motivate and empower teams, create the right environment for teams to work in
and identify the best team makeup depending on the situation, and recognise
and deal with issues.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. The bullets describe leadership behaviours
("motivate and empower teams") that are unusually senior wording for an entry
level, but in context this level is expected to be developing these habits as
a contributor, not yet leading a team's makeup or environment.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Works in isolation, without contributing to others' work or
  noticing team issues.
- 1 — Beginner: Contributes to others' work when asked and notices obvious
  team issues.
- 2 — Intermediate: Proactively contributes to colleagues' investigations and
  raises team issues they notice.
- 3 — Advanced: Helps create a positive working environment for a small group
  and deals with minor issues directly.
- 4 — Expert: Is sought out by colleagues for how they contribute to and
  strengthen team working.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of contributing to a colleague's investigation or
  workload.
- Ask a referee how you have affected team dynamics, positively or

**Evidence to gather**

### Skill: Incident management

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a problem analyst to diagnose and prioritise incidents,
investigate their causes and find resolutions.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. This is a core, hands-on part of the role's daily
work — "maintaining software systems once they are up and running" — so a
working, largely independent grasp is expected even at entry level.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot diagnose the likely cause of a straightforward incident.
- 1 — Beginner: Diagnoses simple incidents with support, and needs help
  prioritising.
- 2 — Intermediate: Diagnoses and prioritises routine incidents
  independently, and investigates causes to a workable resolution.
- 3 — Advanced: Handles a varied caseload of incidents, including some
  complex ones, with minimal support.
- 4 — Expert: Diagnoses complex or unusual incidents quickly and is a
  reference point for colleagues doing the same.

**Evidence to gather**

- Give yourself a mock incident description and try to diagnose,
  prioritise and propose a resolution.
- Ask for an example of an incident you investigated and resolved
  yourself.

### Skill: Ownership and initiative

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a problem analyst to own an issue until a new owner has
been found or the problem has been mitigated or resolved.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. This is a foundational, unqualified expectation
of the level, matching its accountability for maintaining live systems, so a
working, largely reliable habit is expected.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Closes an issue once reassigned, without checking it has been
  picked up.
- 1 — Beginner: Follows up on reassigned issues, but only when prompted.
- 2 — Intermediate: Tracks their open issues and follows up on reassignments
  unprompted, most of the time.
- 3 — Advanced: Reliably owns every issue through to resolution or confirmed
  handover.
- 4 — Expert: Is known for never letting an issue drop, and models this for
  newer colleagues.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for a specific example of an issue you followed through after
  handing it to another team.
- Ask a referee how reliably your issues got resolved or properly
  handed over.

### Skill: Problem management

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a problem analyst to initiate and monitor actions to
investigate patterns and trends to resolve problems, effectively consult
specialists where required, determine the appropriate resolution and assist
with its implementation, and determine preventative measures.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. This is the skill the role exists for, described
here with real substance — initiating investigations and determining
preventative measures — so the baseline reflects a genuinely working level of
practice, even at entry.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Treats each incident as unconnected, without looking for
  patterns.
- 1 — Beginner: Notices a pattern when pointed to it, and needs support to
  investigate further.
- 2 — Intermediate: Initiates investigation of a pattern independently,
  consults specialists appropriately, and determines a workable resolution.
- 3 — Advanced: Runs problem investigations end to end, including
  preventative measures, for a varied caseload.
- 4 — Expert: Their preventative measures have measurably reduced repeat
  incidents, and they mentor others on problem investigation technique.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of spotting a pattern across several incidents and
  investigating its root cause.
- Ask how you decide when to consult a specialist rather than continue
  investigating yourself.

### Skill: Service management framework knowledge

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a problem analyst to show they have a Level 3 service
management framework qualification.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 1 — Beginner. This is a qualification threshold rather than a depth
of practice, so the baseline reflects holding, or being close to holding, the
qualification.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Holds no service management framework qualification.
- 1 — Beginner: Holds, or is working towards, a Level 3 qualification, and
  can describe its basic terms.
- 2 — Intermediate: Holds the qualification and applies its terminology
  correctly in day-to-day problem records.
- 3 — Advanced: Uses framework concepts to explain to a colleague why a
  process works the way it does.
- 4 — Expert: Helps induct new starters into the framework's terms and
  processes.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd confirm your qualification and describe what it
  covered.
- Think about how you'd explain the difference between an incident and a problem, in

### Skill: User focus

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a problem analyst to identify and engage with users or
stakeholders to collate user needs evidence, understand and define research
that fits user needs, and use quantitative and qualitative data about users
to turn user focus into outcomes.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. The bullets ask for active engagement with users
and use of data to improve outcomes, a working level of this skill even for
an entry-level analyst whose work directly affects what users experience.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Investigates problems purely technically, without reference to
  user impact.
- 1 — Beginner: Notes user impact when it is pointed out.
- 2 — Intermediate: Considers user impact unprompted when prioritising and
  investigating problems.
- 3 — Advanced: Uses evidence from several cases to argue for prioritising a
  problem because of its user impact.
- 4 — Expert: Their user-impact evidence has changed how the team prioritises
  problem investigation.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of prioritising a problem based on its impact on users
  rather than its technical severity alone.
- Ask how you would gather evidence of user impact for a problem you are
  investigating.
