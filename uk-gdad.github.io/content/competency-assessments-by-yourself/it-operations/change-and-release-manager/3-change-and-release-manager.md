# Change and Release Manager - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This is for you: a chance to rate your own current strengths against the
baseline the UK GDAD Profession Capability Framework sets for this role
level, using the framework's own words for what it covers.

Change and release managers lead the change advisory board, ensuring adequate risk assessment and scheduling of technical changes and releases. They own the configuration management database and the access, security and configuration of change activities and release procedures. A change and release manager ensures that technical changes to IT services are adequately assessed for impact, prioritised, scheduled, authorised and implemented. They ensure this is done in line with processes and using appropriate tools. They chair the Change Advisory Board (CAB).

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating you
give yourself here must never be treated as equivalent to a formal

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you're rating
yourself against what this level actually expects rather than the level
above or below it. Then think back over recent, real examples of your work —
not how you'd like to have handled something, but what you actually did.

**Rate on evidence, not impression.** For every skill, try to recall a
specific moment that supports the rating you give yourself. If you can't
think of one, that's useful information too — it might mean the rating
should be lower, or just that you haven't had the chance to show this skill
yet, which is worth noting separately.

**Be consistent with yourself.** Try to apply the same standard across every
skill, rather than being generous where you feel confident and harsh where
you feel unsure. Coming back to this after a few days, with fresh examples in
mind, often sharpens a rating more than sitting with it in one go.

**Be kind, and be honest.** This only works if you can look at the result
without flinching. A low rating here is not a verdict on you — it's a
starting point. Everyone doing this role, at every level, has skills they are
still building.

This is a proficiency scale, not a frequency scale — it rates how well someone does the thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it.

**Worked example — Asset and configuration management.** Someone who maintains accurate configuration records for their own area sits at 2. Someone who manages configuration management for the whole organisation, ensuring value for the business and adherence to company policy, and who advocates organisational commitment to asset control, sits at 4 — the framework names exactly this organisation-wide ownership and advocacy at this level, which is full mastery rather than independent working alone.

## About this role level

Change and release managers lead the change advisory board, ensuring adequate risk assessment and scheduling of technical changes and releases. They own the configuration management database and the access, security and configuration of change activities and release procedures. The role runs across three levels: configuration analyst, change and release analyst, and change and release manager.

The change and release manager level is the most senior of the three. The framework describes it as ensuring that technical changes to IT services are adequately assessed for impact, prioritised, scheduled, authorised and implemented, and that this is done in line with process and using appropriate tools. A change and release manager chairs the Change Advisory Board (CAB). At this level, you provide an escalation point, make decisions for all technical changes, and co-ordinate releases and interdependencies.

The framework names eleven skills at this level: asset and configuration management (managing configuration management for the organisation, ensuring value for the business and adherence to company policies, ensuring that changes to assets are recorded and controlled appropriately, and advocating organisational commitment to asset control), availability and capacity management (managing service components to meet business needs and KPIs), change management (implementing new and current change management processes, and supporting change management by actively improving and optimising processes), community collaboration (working collaboratively in a group and actively networking with others, adapting feedback to ensure it is effective and lasting, using their topic to identify and rectify problems in the team dynamic, and identifying issues through Agile "health checks" with the team), continuity management (explaining the basic principles of IT standard continuity management processes and procedures), incident management (diagnosing and prioritising incidents, investigating their causes and finding resolutions), ownership and initiative (owning an issue until a new owner has been found or the problem has been mitigated or resolved), problem management (initiating and monitoring actions to investigate patterns and trends, consulting specialists effectively, determining the appropriate resolution and preventative measures), service focus (taking inputs and establishing coherent frameworks that work), service management framework knowledge (holding a Level 3 service management framework qualification), and user focus (identifying and engaging with users or stakeholders to collate user needs evidence, understanding and defining research that fits user needs, and using quantitative and qualitative data about users to turn user focus into outcomes).

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Asset and configuration management

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a change and release manager to manage configuration management for the organisation, ensuring value for the business and adherence to company policies, to ensure that changes to assets are recorded and controlled appropriately, and to advocate organisational commitment to asset control.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "Manage configuration management for the organisation" and "advocate organisational commitment" are ownership and advocacy at an organisation-wide scale, which is full mastery.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe organisation-level configuration management policy.
- 1 — Beginner: Manages configuration accuracy for a single area with support.
- 2 — Intermediate: Manages configuration accuracy for a single area independently.
- 3 — Advanced: Ensures changes to assets are recorded and controlled across more than one area, catching gaps in policy adherence.
- 4 — Expert: Owns configuration management policy for the whole organisation and actively advocates for asset control commitment at a senior level.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe how you have secured organisational commitment to asset control, including a time it was resisted.
- Ask a referee whether you have owned configuration management policy at an organisational level.

### Skill: Availability and capacity management

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a change and release manager to manage service components so that they meet business needs and key performance indicators (KPIs).

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Managing components against a KPI, independently and reliably, is solid working practice; the wording here does not reach the organisation-wide ownership language used elsewhere in this level's skill set, so it stays at Advanced rather than Expert.

### Skill: Change management

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a change and release manager to implement new and current change management processes, and to actively support change management by improving and optimising processes.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. This is the defining skill of the role — chairing the CAB and owning process improvement — and the framework's own wording describes implementing and optimising the process itself, which is full mastery.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe how a change management process is designed or improved.
- 1 — Beginner: Follows an existing change process without contributing to its improvement.
- 2 — Intermediate: Suggests improvements to a change process with support implementing them.
- 3 — Advanced: Implements new change management processes independently and optimises existing ones.
- 4 — Expert: Chairs the CAB, is the final decision point on contested technical changes, and continuously improves the process the whole organisation follows.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe how you have chaired, or would chair, a Change Advisory Board, including how you handle a genuinely contested change.
- Ask for an example of a change management process you implemented or optimised, and what improved as a result.
- Ask a referee how you have handled being the escalation point for a difficult technical change decision.

### Skill: Community collaboration

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a change and release manager to work collaboratively in a group and actively network with others, to adapt feedback to ensure it is effective and lasting, to use their topic to identify and rectify problems in the team dynamic, and to identify issues through Agile "health checks" with the team.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Actively diagnosing and rectifying team dynamic issues is independent, applied leadership of collaboration, which matches Advanced.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 4 — Expert: Is asked to run or improve Agile health checks for other teams beyond their own.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe a team dynamic issue you identified through a health check and how you rectified it.
- Ask a referee how effectively you networks and collaborates across the organisation.
