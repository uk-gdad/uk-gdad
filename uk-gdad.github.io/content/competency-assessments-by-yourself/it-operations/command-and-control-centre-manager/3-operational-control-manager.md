## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating you
give yourself here must never be treated as equivalent to a formal
assessment for a hiring, promotion, pay or performance decision. Use it for

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you're rating
yourself against what this level actually expects rather than the level
above or below it. Then think back over recent, real examples of your work —
not how you'd like to have handled something, but what you actually did.

**Rate on evidence, not impression.** For every skill, try to recall a
specific moment that supports the rating you give yourself. If you can't
think of one, that's useful information too — it might mean the rating
should be lower, or just that you haven't had the chance to show this skill
yet, which is worth noting separately.

**Be consistent with yourself.** Try to apply the same standard across every
skill, rather than being generous where you feel confident and harsh where
you feel unsure. Coming back to this after a few days, with fresh examples in
mind, often sharpens a rating more than sitting with it in one go.

**Be kind, and be honest.** This only works if you can look at the result
without flinching. A low rating here is not a verdict on you — it's a
starting point. Everyone doing this role, at every level, has skills they are
still building.

## Rating scale

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

This is a proficiency scale, not a frequency scale — it rates how well someone does the thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it.

**Worked example — Incident management.** Someone who diagnoses and resolves routine incidents independently sits at 2 or 3. Someone who leads the investigation and resolution of incidents, acting as the named escalation point across IT operations, sits at 3 — this level's own baseline, since the framework describes this level as leading incident investigation and resolution. Someone who additionally is called on for the organisation's most difficult incidents and mentors others in leading them is showing evidence closer to level 4.

## About this role level

Command and control centre managers proactively monitor live services and performance trends to identify potential problems or areas for improvement that can then be investigated. The role runs across four levels: operations analyst, senior operations analyst, operational control manager, and head of command and control.

The operational control manager level sits third of four, above senior operations analyst and below head of command and control. The framework describes this level as accountable for day-to-day IT operations, acting as the escalation point for incidents and problems. At this level, you are responsible for effective change management processes that maintain the availability of services, using proactive and preventative measures; you are on call 24/7 or according to business requirements; and you are allowed to address issues across IT operations.

The framework names eleven skills at this level: asset and configuration management (maintaining secure configuration and accurate information, controlling IT assets in one or more areas, verifying the location and state of IT assets), availability and capacity management (managing service components to meet business needs and key performance indicators (KPIs)), change management (managing high impact, complex change requests, and ensuring that release policies, procedures and processes are applied), community collaboration (working collaboratively in a group and actively networking with others, adapting feedback to ensure it is effective and lasting, using their topic to identify and rectify problems in the team dynamic, and identifying issues through Agile "health checks" with the team), continuity management (managing the runbooks and the processes around service continuity, and testing the runbooks to ensure that service availability is maintained), incident management (leading the investigation and resolution of incidents), ownership and initiative (taking accountability for issues that occur and being proactive in searching for potential problems, and achieving excellent user outcomes), problem management (initiating and monitoring actions to investigate patterns and trends, consulting specialists effectively, determining the appropriate resolution and assisting with its implementation, and determining preventative measures), service focus (seeing the bigger picture by taking groups of services and investigating how to get the best of underlying services), service management framework knowledge (holding a Level 3 service management framework qualification and demonstrating knowledge of the life cycle or capability elements of ITIL), and user focus (collaborating with user researchers, representing users internally, explaining the difference between user needs and desires, championing user research, and guiding others in prioritising the user story).

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Asset and configuration management

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects an operational control manager to maintain secure configuration and accurate information, to control IT assets in one or more areas, and to verify the location and state of IT assets.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. The wording is unchanged from the level below, but an operational control manager is expected to carry out this work with full independence across day-to-day operations, matching Advanced.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe what a configuration record is for.
- 1 — Beginner: Updates configuration records accurately only with checking.
- 2 — Intermediate: Maintains accurate records for their own area with some guidance on edge cases.
- 3 — Advanced: Maintains secure, accurate configuration and asset information across the areas they are accountable for, entirely independently.
- 4 — Expert: Sets configuration and asset control standards other operational control managers follow.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe how you maintain configuration accuracy across day-to-day IT operations.
