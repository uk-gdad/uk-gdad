# Principal Application Operations Engineer - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This is for you: a chance to rate your own current strengths against the
baseline the UK GDAD Profession Capability Framework sets for this role
level, using the framework's own words for what it covers.

An application operations engineer supports, manages and maintains a single application or a suite of applications. A principal application operations engineer owns the application strategy and leads on resourcing, learning and development for their team.

Work through the competency matrix below, one skill at a time, then fill in
the overall determination at the end. Treat this as a mirror, not a test —
there's no assessor watching, so being honest with yourself only costs you a
slightly less flattering picture today in exchange for a far more useful one.
Read `## Advisory` before you begin.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating you
give yourself here must never be treated as equivalent to a formal
assessment for a hiring, promotion, pay or performance decision. Use it for
your own reflection, and talk to your manager, a mentor, or your
organisation's people development team about what you find.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you're rating
yourself against what this level actually expects rather than the level
above or below it. Then think back over recent, real examples of your work —
not how you'd like to have handled something, but what you actually did.

**Rate on evidence, not impression.** For every skill, try to recall a
specific moment that supports the rating you give yourself. If you can't
think of one, that's useful information too — it might mean the rating
should be lower, or just that you haven't had the chance to show this skill
yet, which is worth noting separately.

**Be consistent with yourself.** Try to apply the same standard across every
skill, rather than being generous where you feel confident and harsh where
you feel unsure. Coming back to this after a few days, with fresh examples in
mind, often sharpens a rating more than sitting with it in one go.

**Be kind, and be honest.** This only works if you can look at the result
without flinching. A low rating here is not a verdict on you — it's a
starting point. Everyone doing this role, at every level, has skills they are
still building.

## Rating scale

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

This is a proficiency scale, not a frequency scale — it rates how well someone does the thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it.

**Worked example — Service management framework knowledge.** Someone who holds only a Level 3 qualification, without the expert-level certificate the framework names at this level, sits at 2 or 3. Someone who holds the expert-level certificate but has not yet been asked to apply that depth for others sits at 3. Someone who holds the expert certificate and is regularly asked to settle disputes about how the framework should be applied, or trains others in it, sits at 4 — full mastery, demonstrated by teaching.

## About this role level

The application operations engineer supports, manages and maintains a single application or a suite of applications. The role runs across five levels: associate application operations engineer, application operations engineer, senior application operations engineer, lead application operations engineer, and principal application operations engineer.

The principal application operations engineer is the top of the ladder. The framework describes this level as owning the application strategy and leading on resourcing, learning and development for their team. At this level, you have expert technical understanding, are accountable for vendor and supplier management, and may still manage and maintain certain aspects of an application directly, depending on the complexity and size of a project and the likely impact on the business.

The framework names eleven skills at this level: asset and configuration management (managing configuration items and related information, and managing service compliance and risk), availability and capacity management (ensuring the correct implementation of availability and capacity management standards and procedures, identifying capacity issues and implementing required changes, and initiating remedial action), change management (managing high impact, complex change requests, and ensuring that release policies, procedures and processes are applied), incident management (leading the investigation and resolution of incidents), ownership and initiative (taking accountability for issues that occur, being proactive in searching for potential problems, and achieving excellent user outcomes), problem management (ensuring the right actions are taken to investigate, resolve and anticipate problems, and co-ordinating the team to investigate problems, implement solutions and take preventive measures), service focus (seeing the bigger picture by taking groups of services and investigating how to get the best of underlying services), service management framework knowledge (holding an expert certificate in the service management framework qualification), technical specialism (understanding the direction of future technologies and delivering a model to support and maintain those technologies and any databases that coexist in the current environment), testing (managing testing activities within development or integration activities, managing risks and taking preventative action when risks become unacceptable, and managing customer relations), and user focus (collaborating with user researchers, representing users internally, explaining the difference between user needs and desires, championing user research, and guiding others in prioritising the user story).

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Asset and configuration management

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a principal application operations engineer to manage configuration items and related information, and to manage service compliance and risk.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "Manage service compliance and risk" is organisation-level accountability, not day-to-day maintenance, and matches full mastery at the top of the role's ladder.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe what service compliance or risk means for configuration management.
- 1 — Beginner: Manages configuration items for a single area with support.
- 2 — Intermediate: Manages configuration items independently but has not yet owned compliance or risk at an estate level.
- 3 — Advanced: Manages configuration compliance and risk across the estate, catching issues before they become material.
- 4 — Expert: Owns configuration compliance and risk policy for the estate, and other leads escalate their hardest configuration risk decisions to this person.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe how you identify and manage configuration-related risk across an entire application estate, with a real example.
- Ask a referee whether you have owned a compliance issue that reached senior management attention, and how it went.
