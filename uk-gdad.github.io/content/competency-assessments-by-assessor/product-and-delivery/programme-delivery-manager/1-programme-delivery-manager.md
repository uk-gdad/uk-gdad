# Programme Delivery Manager - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This assessment is for an assessor — a line manager, a panel, or a reviewer —
judging whether a candidate or employee meets the baseline abilities expected
of a **programme delivery manager** in the product and delivery profession. It
measures demonstrated skill against the framework's own description of this
role level, for a hiring decision, a promotion into the role, or a periodic
capability check.

This is one input among several into that decision, never the whole of it. See
`## Advisory` below for why.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating on this
form must never be the sole basis for a hiring, promotion, pay, performance or
disciplinary decision. Before using it, consult your organisation's human
resources, people development, occupational psychology or legal compliance
colleagues.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` before you score anything.
This level is accountable for delivery of complex products and services
across multiple teams, or work carrying high technical or political risk, and
is the initial escalation point for the whole programme — the evidence you
need is about that breadth of accountability and about coaching other
delivery managers, not about running one team's delivery well.

**Score on evidence, not impression.** Every rating must trace to something
the person did, said, produced, or was observed doing. Ask for examples that
show programme-level accountability — a genuinely complex set of
dependencies the candidate managed across teams, a moment where they were the
first person escalated to when something went wrong, a delivery manager they
coached through a difficult situation. If you cannot point to evidence for a
rating, record it as unknown rather than guess.

**Be consistent.** Score every candidate for this role level the same way.
Where possible, have more than one assessor score independently before
comparing notes — this matters especially for a hiring or promotion decision.

**A note on sensitive content.** Some programme delivery manager posts carry
high political risk as well as technical risk. When gathering evidence about
how a candidate handled a politically sensitive escalation, record what
process and judgement they applied — not the substance of anything
confidential, market-sensitive or not yet public.

**Stay fair.** See [Fair and lawful use](../../../../uk-gdad-pcf-competency-assessments-by-assessor/spec/index.md#fair-and-lawful-use) below. Never let
a rating be influenced by anything other than demonstrated, job-related
evidence. This is an unvalidated, AI-drafted instrument: used inconsistently —
different questions for different candidates, one assessor's impression taken
as final — it can produce unlawful adverse impact. Consistent, evidence-based
administration, ideally by more than one assessor, materially reduces that
risk.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

This is a **proficiency** scale, not a frequency scale: it rates how well the
person does the thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it or
how they feel about it.

**Worked example — Maintaining delivery momentum.** At 2, a candidate tracks
and manages risks and dependencies within their own team, with some support.
At 3, a candidate independently facilitates their own team's delivery flow
and steps in when a dependency owned by another team threatens it. At 4 — the
baseline for this role level — a candidate optimises the delivery flow of
multiple teams at once, actively resolves the most complicated risks, issues
and dependencies including ones with no clear owner anywhere in the
organisation, and finds genuinely innovative ways to unblock them rather than
escalating and waiting. The distinction an assessor should look for is
whether the candidate takes ownership of a blocker that nobody else owns, or
waits for someone else to claim it.

## About this role level

The product and delivery profession's programme delivery manager role exists
to be accountable for delivering complex products and services where the
work is spread across multiple teams, or where the technical or political
risk is high. The framework does not describe a separate set of duties for
the role as distinct from the role level; the accountability described below
is both.

This role currently has one published level in the framework: Programme
delivery manager. There is no junior or senior variant published beneath or
above it — a person in this post is expected to arrive with substantial prior
delivery management experience rather than grow into the role from a lower
step in this ladder.

At this role level, the framework says a programme delivery manager will:

- manage dependencies of varying complexity, potentially planning and
  feeding into larger programmes and portfolios
- remove blockers and manage risks, commercials, budgets, and people
- balance objectives and redeploy people and resources as priorities change
- have an in-depth knowledge of Agile and other methodologies
- be responsible for understanding, managing and communicating to complex
  stakeholder groups, balancing priorities
- be the initial escalation point for the programme
- have an awareness of the bigger picture
- support the programme director by overseeing the delivery of their vision
  for the programme
- support and coach delivery managers

The framework names nine skills for this level: Agile and Lean practices,
Commercial management, Communicating between the technical and non-technical,
Community collaboration, Financial management, Life cycle management,
Maintaining delivery momentum, Making a process work, and Planning.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Agile and Lean practices

**What the framework expects at this level**

A programme delivery manager is expected to coach and lead teams in Agile and
Lean good practices, to create and tailor the right approach for a team,
challenging, evaluating and iterating the approach through the life cycle, to
experiment with new and innovative ways of working to improve delivery across
the organisation, and to act as a recognised expert and advocate for Agile
and Lean approaches.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "Coach and lead teams", "act as a recognised expert and
advocate" and improving delivery "across the organisation" describe mastery
exercised on behalf of other people and teams, which is the scale's own
definition of Expert.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has never coached another delivery manager or team in Agile or
  Lean practice.
- 1 — Beginner: Applies an Agile approach chosen and set up by someone else.
- 2 — Intermediate: Independently applies Agile principles within one team.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently compares and tailors an approach to a single
  team's context, and can explain the trade-offs of the choice.
- 4 — Expert: Coaches and leads more than one team in Agile and Lean good
  practice, tailors and iterates the approach across a whole life cycle, runs
  experiments in new ways of working that change how other teams deliver, and
  is recognised across the organisation as an expert advocate for the
  approach.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example where the candidate coached a delivery manager or team
  outside their own, and what changed as a result.
- Ask about an experiment with a new way of working they led that spread to
  teams they did not directly manage.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate is recognised across the organisation
  as an expert in Agile and Lean practice, not only within their own
  programme.

### Skill: Commercial management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A programme delivery manager is expected to act as the escalation point and
resolve large or high risk commercial management issues, and to coach others
in appropriate commercial management.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Acting as the escalation point for high-risk
commercial issues describes independent commercial judgement exercised with
real consequence; the framework describes coaching "others" rather than
setting commercial standards across the organisation, so the baseline stops
short of Expert.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe what makes a commercial management issue high
  risk or how it would be escalated.
- 1 — Beginner: Follows an existing commercial process under close guidance.
- 2 — Intermediate: Manages a straightforward commercial issue independently.
- 3 — Advanced: Resolves a large or high risk commercial management issue as
  the escalation point for it, and coaches at least one other person in
  handling a commercial issue well.
- 4 — Expert: Not distinctly expected at this role level by the framework's
  own wording for this skill.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of a large or high risk commercial issue the candidate
  was escalated to resolve, and how they resolved it.
- Ask how they have coached someone else through a commercial management
  problem.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate is trusted as the escalation point for
  commercial issues on their programme.

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What the framework expects at this level**

A programme delivery manager is expected to mediate between people and
strengthen relationships, adopting the appropriate communication method with
stakeholders at all levels, to manage stakeholder expectations and moderate
difficult discussions about high risk and complex topics even within
constrained timescales, and to speak on behalf of, and represent the
community to, large audiences inside and outside the organisation.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Representing "the community" to large audiences inside
and outside the organisation, and moderating high-risk discussions under time
pressure, describes mastery exercised on behalf of a whole discipline, not
just the candidate's own programme's stakeholders.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Struggles to explain a technical concept to a non-technical
  audience or vice versa.
- 1 — Beginner: Communicates simple, low-stakes information clearly.
- 2 — Intermediate: Supports a difficult discussion within a multidisciplinary
  team.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently moderates a difficult discussion involving
  senior stakeholders on their own programme's work.
- 4 — Expert: Mediates and strengthens relationships across stakeholders at
  every level, moderates high-risk, complex discussions under constrained
  timescales, and represents the delivery community to large audiences inside
  and outside the organisation.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example where the candidate represented the delivery community
  to an audience outside the organisation, or a large internal one.
- Ask about a high-risk, time-constrained discussion they moderated between
  stakeholders in tension.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate is trusted to speak on behalf of the
  programme to senior or external audiences.

### Skill: Community collaboration

**What the framework expects at this level**

A programme delivery manager is expected to solve and unblock issues between
teams or departments at the highest level, and to coach the organisation on
team dynamics and conflict resolution, while also building and growing the
community.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "At the highest level" and coaching "the organisation"
on team dynamics and conflict resolution are explicit organisation-wide
mastery, exercised to guide other people and teams rather than only the
candidate's own.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has never worked to resolve a conflict between two teams or
  departments.
- 1 — Beginner: Raises a cross-team issue for someone more senior to resolve.
- 2 — Intermediate: Resolves a straightforward disagreement between two teams
  independently.
- 3 — Advanced: Unblocks a genuinely difficult issue between departments and
  actively contributes to the wider delivery community.
- 4 — Expert: Solves and unblocks the highest-level issues between teams or
  departments, coaches the organisation on team dynamics and conflict
  resolution, and is visibly building and growing the delivery community
  beyond their own programme.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of a high-level conflict between departments the
  candidate resolved, and how they approached it.
- Ask what the candidate has done to build or grow a community of practice
  across the organisation.
- Ask a referee whether other delivery managers or teams have sought the
  candidate out for help with team dynamics or conflict.

### Skill: Financial management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A programme delivery manager is expected to influence or create complex
budgets across an organisation, programme or product view, and to manage the
budget they are given and make it work.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Influencing or creating a complex budget across a
programme, and making a given budget work, describe independent financial
judgement exercised with real weight; the framework does not describe this
skill in terms of teaching or guiding others, so the baseline stops short of
Expert.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe how a programme-level budget is put together or
  managed.
- 1 — Beginner: Monitors and reports on a budget set by someone else.
- 2 — Intermediate: Balances cost against value in day-to-day delivery
  decisions.
- 3 — Advanced: Influences or creates a complex budget spanning more than one
  product or team, and manages the budget given to them so that it actually
  delivers what was promised.
- 4 — Expert: Not distinctly expected at this role level by the framework's
  own wording for this skill.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of a complex budget the candidate influenced or created
  that spanned more than one team or product.
- Ask about a time a budget was under pressure and how they made it work
  without compromising delivery.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate's budgets have held up to scrutiny from
  finance colleagues.

### Skill: Life cycle management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A programme delivery manager is expected to lead and coach teams through
different phases of the life cycle, to ensure effective support for the
product or service and its users, to predict, prevent, mitigate and manage
potential risks at different phases of the life cycle, and to contribute to
the assessment of other teams, providing guidance and support.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "Lead and coach teams" through the life cycle, and
providing "guidance and support" to other teams' assessments, describe
mastery exercised on other teams' behalf, not only the candidate's own
programme.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe the phases of the product life cycle or what
  changes at each one.
- 1 — Beginner: Follows life cycle guidance set by someone else for their own
  team.
- 2 — Intermediate: Independently moves their own team through a life cycle
  phase, with some support.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently leads their own team through the life cycle,
  predicting and mitigating risks as phases change.
- 4 — Expert: Leads and coaches more than one team through different life
  cycle phases, ensures effective ongoing support for a service and its
  users, predicts and manages risk across phases, and provides guidance and
  support into other teams' assessments.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example where the candidate coached a team other than their own
  through a life cycle transition.
- Ask about a risk they predicted and mitigated before it became a problem.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate has contributed guidance to another
  team's service assessment.

### Skill: Maintaining delivery momentum

**What the framework expects at this level**

A programme delivery manager is expected to optimise the delivery flow of
teams, to actively address the most complicated risks, issues and
dependencies including where ownership exists outside the team or no clear
ownership exists, and to identify innovative ways to unblock issues.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "Optimise the delivery flow of teams" (plural) and
taking on risks with "no clear ownership" describe mastery exercised across
more than one team, on the hardest class of problem the framework names for
this skill.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe what affects delivery flow across more than one
  team.
- 1 — Beginner: Tracks a dependency or risk within their own team, with
  support.
- 2 — Intermediate: Tracks and manages risks and dependencies within their
  own team independently.
- 3 — Advanced: Facilitates their own team's delivery flow and steps in when
  a risk owned outside the team threatens it.
- 4 — Expert: Optimises delivery flow across multiple teams, actively
  resolves the most complicated risks and issues — including ones with no
  clear owner anywhere in the organisation — and finds genuinely innovative
  ways to unblock them.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of a risk or issue with no clear owner that the
  candidate took on and resolved.
- Ask how they optimised delivery flow across more than one team at the same
  time.
- Ask a referee for an example of an innovative unblock the candidate found
  that others had missed.

### Skill: Making a process work

**What the framework expects at this level**

A programme delivery manager is expected to identify and challenge
organisational processes of increasing complexity and those processes that
are unnecessarily complicated, to add value and coach the organisation to
inspect and adapt processes, and to guide teams through the implementation of
a new process.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Coaching "the organisation" to inspect and adapt its
own processes, rather than coaching a single team, describes mastery
exercised across the organisation, which is the scale's own definition of
Expert.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot identify a process that is getting in the way of a team's
  output.
- 1 — Beginner: Supports a team in following an established process.
- 2 — Intermediate: Helps a team adapt its own process, with some support.
- 3 — Advanced: Identifies and challenges an unnecessarily complicated
  process affecting their own programme, and guides one team through
  implementing a new one.
- 4 — Expert: Identifies and challenges organisational processes of
  increasing complexity, coaches the organisation itself to inspect and adapt
  how it works, and guides multiple teams through implementing a new process.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example where the candidate challenged an organisational process
  and coached others to inspect and adapt it, beyond their own programme.
- Ask how they guided more than one team through implementing a genuinely new
  process.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate's process challenges have led to
  organisation-wide change.

### Skill: Planning

**What the framework expects at this level**

A programme delivery manager is expected to lead a continual planning process
in a very complex environment, to plan beyond product delivery, to identify
dependencies in plans across services and co-ordinate delivery, and to coach
other teams as the central point of expertise.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "Lead... in a very complex environment", planning
"beyond product delivery", and being "the central point of expertise" for
coaching other teams describe mastery exercised well beyond a single team's
plan.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe a dependency that spans more than one service.
- 1 — Beginner: Contributes to a plan with support.
- 2 — Intermediate: Manages complex dependencies within their own team's
  plan.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently plans and co-ordinates delivery for their own
  team using data, removing blockers as they appear.
- 4 — Expert: Leads a continual planning process across a very complex
  environment, plans beyond a single product's delivery, co-ordinates
  dependencies across services, and coaches other teams as the organisation's
  central point of planning expertise.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example where the candidate led planning across a very complex
  environment spanning more than one product or service.
- Ask how they coached another team's planning as the point of expertise.
- Ask a referee whether other teams turn to the candidate for planning
  advice beyond their own programme.

or above the target shown for it above. Most skills at this level carry an
Expert baseline, reflecting how much of the framework's own wording for the
role — "recognised expert", "at the highest level", "the central point of
expertise", "coach the organisation" — describes teaching or guiding other
people and teams, not only delivering the candidate's own work well.
