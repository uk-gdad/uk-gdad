# Head of Business Analysis - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This assessment is for an assessor — a line manager, a panel, or a reviewer —
judging whether a candidate or employee meets the baseline abilities expected
of a **head of business analysis** in the product and delivery profession. It
measures demonstrated skill against the framework's own description of this
role level, for a hiring decision, a promotion from lead business analyst, or
a periodic capability check.

This is one input among several into that decision, never the whole of it. See
`## Advisory` below for why.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating on this
form must never be the sole basis for a hiring, promotion, pay, performance or
disciplinary decision. Before using it, consult your organisation's human
resources, people development, occupational psychology or legal compliance
colleagues.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` before you score anything.
This is the top of the business analyst ladder: leadership of the whole
business analysis function across the enterprise area, and building the
organisation's business analysis capability. The evidence you need is about
organisation-wide impact — capability building, workforce and standards
decisions, representing the function externally — not project delivery.

**Score on evidence, not impression.** Every rating must trace to something
the person did, said, produced, or was observed doing. At this level, ask for
evidence of organisational impact specifically: a capability strategy, a
standard adopted across a function, a case where the candidate represented
the discipline to senior leadership or externally. A confident description of
what good leadership looks like is not the same as having exercised it at
this scale. If you cannot point to evidence for a rating, record it as
unknown rather than guess.

**Be consistent.** Score every candidate for this role level the same way.
Where possible, have more than one assessor score independently before
comparing notes — this matters especially for a hiring or promotion decision.

**Stay fair.** See [Fair and lawful use](../../../../uk-gdad-pcf-competency-assessments-by-assessor/spec/index.md#fair-and-lawful-use) below. Never let
a rating be influenced by anything other than demonstrated, job-related
evidence.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

This is a **proficiency** scale, not a frequency scale: it rates how well the
person does the thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it or
how they feel about it.

**Worked example — Consultancy.** At 3, a candidate is sought out for advice
within their business area and proposes practical approaches to
implementation. At 4 — the baseline for this role level — a candidate
provides advice and recommendations to stakeholders based on significant
specialist knowledge and experience, and leads the definition of guidance
that informs how the whole organisation approaches delivery or uses digital
and data skills. The distinction from the lead business analyst level is
scope: a head of business analysis's guidance shapes the organisation's
approach, not just a business area's, and the evidence an assessor should
look for is organisation-wide adoption, not just a satisfied stakeholder.

## About this role level

The product and delivery profession's business analyst role exists to help
teams analyse and understand a business problem or opportunity: to research
how a business or business area works — its people, organisation, processes,
information, data and technology — identify areas for improvement, explore
feasible options, analyse the effects of change and define success measures.
Business analysts identify and elaborate user and business needs so that
services and business change can be effectively designed, developed and
tested; they make prioritisation and minimum viable product decisions using
analysis-led insights; they ensure new products and services meet business and
user needs and align with organisational goals; and they understand the
business and policy constraints a piece of work sits within, and assess the
implications.

The role has six levels: trainee business analyst, junior business analyst,
business analyst, senior business analyst, lead business analyst, and head of
business analysis. This assessment is for the sixth and most senior of those.

The **head of business analysis** leads the business analysis function across
the enterprise area and builds business analysis capability for the
organisation. Unlike the levels below it, the framework gives this level a
single, function-level statement rather than a bulleted list of
accountabilities — reflecting that at this point the job is defined by
outcome (a capable, well-led function) rather than by a set of discrete
duties. Where a lead business analyst leads within the enterprise arena and
owns the main stakeholder relationships, a head of business analysis owns the
function itself: its people, its standards, and its capability over time.

The framework names the same eleven skills for this level as for lead business
analyst: Adapting to delivery methodologies, Business modelling, Business
process improvement, Consultancy, Context, problem and option analysis,
Enterprise and business architecture (business analyst), Defining and
managing business needs, user needs and requirements, Stakeholder
relationship management, Digital and data systems analysis, Testing (business
analysis), and User experience analysis. The framework's wording for most of
these skills is identical to the lead business analyst level; where that is
so, this assessment says so rather than inventing a difference the framework
does not state, and grounds the baseline and indicators in this level's
distinct accountability — leading and building the whole function — rather
than in different skill wording.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Adapting to delivery methodologies

**What the framework expects at this level**

A head of business analysis is expected to set and iterate standards for
working within different delivery methodologies, to actively promote
collaboration and agreement to ensure the team delivers value at the right
time, to experiment with new and innovative ways of working to enhance
delivery outcomes, and to research new and evolving delivery methodologies
and identify how their role would work within them.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Unlike the lead business analyst level, the framework's
wording here has grown: "set and iterate standards" is function-wide
standard-setting, matching the scale's top point of mastery exercised to
guide others.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has never set a delivery methodology standard for others to
  follow.
- 1 — Beginner: Follows methodology standards set elsewhere.
- 2 — Intermediate: Adapts working practice to a chosen methodology for their
  own work.
- 3 — Advanced: Determines methodology approach and negotiates delivery
  priorities for a project or programme.
- 4 — Expert: Sets and iterates delivery methodology standards for the
  function, researches new and evolving methodologies to assess their fit,
  and promotes collaboration so the function delivers value at the right
  time.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of a delivery methodology standard the candidate set or
  changed across the function, not just a single project.
- Ask how they have researched an emerging delivery methodology and decided
  whether it was worth adopting.
- Ask a referee whether the function's approach to delivery has visibly
  evolved under the candidate's direction.

### Skill: Business modelling

**What the framework expects at this level**

A head of business analysis is expected to determine the scope and
methodology for the representation of complex programmes across an
organisation, and to document conceptual models to support strategic
planning and decision making.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. The framework's wording is unchanged from lead business
analyst; the distinguishing accountability at this level is that the
candidate does this in service of leading the whole function, and the
assessor should look for evidence at that scale.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has never scoped or designed a modelling approach for
  organisation-wide work.
- 1 — Beginner: Produces a model within a scope and methodology someone else
  has set.
- 2 — Intermediate: Leads modelling for a single project or programme.
- 3 — Advanced: Leads modelling across a project or programme independently
  and manages the impact of change.
- 4 — Expert: Determines scope and methodology for representing complex
  programmes across the organisation, and produces conceptual models that the
  organisation's senior leadership genuinely uses in strategic planning.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example where the candidate's modelling directly informed a
  decision at organisational, not just programme, level.
- Ask how they chose a modelling methodology appropriate to a genuinely
  complex, multi-programme piece of work.
- Ask a referee at senior leadership level whether they have used the
  candidate's models in planning.

### Skill: Business process improvement

**What the framework expects at this level**

A head of business analysis is expected to lead the identification of
large-scale opportunities to deliver business performance improvements, to
set the direction for analysis, design and evaluation of business process
improvements including methods, tools and standards, to set the direction for
design, execution and assessment of business process tests and usability
evaluations, and to coach others in designing, testing, implementing and
assessing business process improvements.

**Baseline for this role level**

  programme.

  discipline.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of a process improvement standard the candidate set that
  the whole function now follows.
- Ask how the candidate identified a large-scale opportunity that individual

### Skill: Consultancy

**What the framework expects at this level**

A head of business analysis is expected to provide advice and recommendations
to stakeholders based on their significant specialist knowledge and
experience, and to lead the definition of guidance and inform how the
organisation approaches delivery or uses digital and data skills more
effectively.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "Significant specialist knowledge" and leading the
definition of organisation-wide guidance describes the deepest form of
consultancy the framework names — advice that shapes how the organisation as
a whole operates, not just how one business area does.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Has never been asked to provide organisation-level advice on
  delivery or digital and data skills.
- 1 — Beginner: Offers an opinion on a familiar problem when directly asked.

  though not sought out proactively.
- 3 — Advanced: Is regularly sought out for advice within a business area,
  proposing practical approaches to implementation.
- 4 — Expert: Provides advice to senior stakeholders based on significant
  specialist knowledge, and leads the definition of guidance that changes how
  the organisation approaches delivery or uses digital and data skills.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of guidance the candidate defined that changed how the
  whole organisation, not one business area, approaches delivery.
- Ask which senior stakeholders seek the candidate's advice and on what kind
  of decision.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate's advice has shaped organisational
  policy or practice, and how they know.

### Skill: Context, problem and option analysis

**What the framework expects at this level**

A head of business analysis is expected to advise on approaches to options
analysis, feasibility and impact assessments to ensure best practice is
followed, to ensure all streams of work align to the organisation's strategic
context and goals, to advise on how the organisation can use business
analysis skills to add value, and to guide tactical decision making and
influence strategic plans in a changing environment.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. The framework's wording is unchanged from lead business
analyst; at this level it is exercised on behalf of the whole function's

**Behavioural indicators**

  recommendations.
- 4 — Expert: Advises across the organisation on options analysis best
  practice, ensures every stream of business analysis work aligns to
  organisational strategy, and influences strategic plans as circumstances
  change.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example where the candidate ensured multiple, otherwise
  disconnected pieces of work aligned to a shared strategic goal.
- Ask how they have influenced a strategic plan at organisational level as
  circumstances changed.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate's advice on best practice is followed

### Skill: Enterprise and business architecture (business analyst)

**What the framework expects at this level**

A head of business analysis is expected to work with limited direction to
translate business drivers, goals and constraints into business objectives,

to create operating models that meet business objectives.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. The framework's wording is unchanged from lead

A head of business analysis is expected to direct the stakeholder

### Skill: Digital and data systems analysis

**What the framework expects at this level**

A head of business analysis is expected to lead the identification and
analysis of IT system capabilities, developing models and system
requirements, to use systems analysis to support a deeper understanding of
how processes and systems work and to identify gaps, and to work with other
digital professionals to design and develop system and user interfaces,
identify gaps in functionality and assess the effect of proposed software
changes on users or the organisation.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. The framework's wording is unchanged from lead business
analyst; this level's own accountability — leading and building the function —
means the "lead" language here applies at the scale of the whole function's
systems analysis practice.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 2 — Intermediate: Independently analyses systems capabilities for a bounded
  piece of work.
- 3 — Advanced: Leads systems analysis across a project, working with other
  digital professionals on interface and functionality decisions.
- 4 — Expert: Sets the direction for systems analysis practice across the
  function, enables business analysts across the organisation to explore
  functionality gaps and software change effects, and is the point of common
  understanding between users, development teams and external suppliers at
  organisational level.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example where the candidate set systems analysis direction

- Ask how they have built a common understanding between users and technical

One or two skills below target is not automatically a fail. Weigh how central
the skill is to leading and building capability for the whole business
analysis function, consider whether the shortfall is genuinely coachable at
this seniority or whether it points to a mismatch with the role, and record
your reasoning in the evidence log below rather than relying on the numbers
alone. A shortfall in one of the three skills whose baseline sits at Advanced
rather than Expert (Enterprise and business architecture, Testing, or User
experience analysis) is less central to this level's own defining
accountability than a shortfall in one of the Expert-baseline skills would be.
