# Principal End User Computing Engineer - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This assessment is for an assessor — a line manager, an interview panel
member, or anyone else evaluating a candidate or an employee — who needs to
judge whether a person has the baseline abilities the IT operations
profession's principal end user computing engineer role level requires. It
measures demonstrated skill against the UK GDAD Profession Capability
Framework's own description of this level, for a hiring decision, a promotion
or progression board, onboarding a new starter, or a periodic capability
check. It is one input into that decision, never the whole of it — see
`## Advisory` before you begin.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating on this
form must never be the sole basis for a hiring, promotion, pay, performance or
disciplinary decision. Before using it, consult your organisation's human
resources, people development, occupational psychology or legal compliance
colleagues.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you know what the
framework actually expects before you look at any evidence. Then gather what
exists for this person: a CV or portfolio, a structured interview, a work
sample or task, direct observation of them at work, or references from people
who have worked with them. At this level the framework expects strategy
ownership, resourcing and development leadership, and a bigger-picture view of
business trends, so look for a track record over time rather than a single
task — evidence at this level takes longer to surface than at a working level.

**Score on evidence, not impression.** Every rating must trace to something
the person did, said, produced, or was observed doing. If you cannot point to
evidence for a rating, record it as unknown rather than guess — an honest gap
teaches you more than a guess dressed up as a score.

**Stay consistent.** Score every candidate for this role level the same way,
using the same evidence sources wherever you can. For a hiring or promotion
decision, have more than one assessor score independently, then compare notes,
rather than settling on a single view first.

**Fairness.** An unvalidated, AI-drafted instrument like this one, used
inconsistently, can produce unlawful adverse impact even when nobody intends
it. Consistent, evidence-based administration by more than one assessor
materially reduces that risk. Never let a rating be influenced by anything
other than demonstrated, job-related evidence.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point
proficiency scale — how well the person does the thing, on the evidence
available, not how often they do it.

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

**Worked example.** Take Skill: Service focus. At 2 — Intermediate, a
candidate organises their own work into a coherent routine from several
inputs. At 3 — Advanced, they improve a service framework so it works better
for the wider team. At 4 — Expert, the baseline for this level, they see the
bigger picture by taking groups of services together and investigating how to
get the best out of the underlying services that support them — the
framework's own wording for this level, and evidence of exactly the strategic,
cross-service view a principal is expected to hold. Calibrate against
evidence like this before you start scoring.

## About this role level

IT operations role: End user computing engineer. An end user computing
engineer is responsible for managing the product life cycle of all
service-raised incidents (incident control) and all service requests (request
control), which requires the use of knowledge management. In this role, you
are also responsible for informing customers on progress and advising on
workarounds where necessary, as well as the support and maintenance of end
user services.

The role has five levels, in order: associate end user computing engineer,
end user computing engineer, senior end user computing engineer, lead end user
computing engineer, and principal end user computing engineer. This assessment
covers principal end user computing engineer, the highest of those.

Role level: Principal end user computing engineer. A principal end user
computing engineer owns the strategy roadmap and leads on resourcing, learning
and development for their team. At this level, the framework expects someone
to look at the bigger picture and understand trends in the business. The
framework does not list separate day-to-day accountability bullets beyond this
description; its expectations are carried in the skills below.

The framework names ten skills for this level: Asset and configuration
management, Change management, IT service reporting, Incident management,
Ownership and initiative, Problem management, Service focus, Technical
specialism, Testing, and User focus. Note that the framework does not name
Service management framework knowledge at this level, though it does at the
three levels below — that is the framework's own choice, not an omission by
this assessment.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Asset and configuration management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal end user computing engineer manages configuration items and
related information, and manages service compliance and risk.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Managing service compliance and risk, rather than
controlling or verifying individual assets, is ownership of the whole
governance picture — full mastery by the scale's definition.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot describe what service compliance or risk means for asset
  and configuration management.
- 1 — Beginner: manages a single configuration item under a colleague's
  direction.
- 2 — Intermediate: manages configuration items and related information for
  their own area with some guidance.
- 3 — Advanced: manages configuration items and related information
  independently, and flags compliance or risk issues as they arise.
- 4 — Expert: owns service compliance and risk for asset and configuration
  management across the service, and sets how the wider team manages it.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe how they manage service compliance and risk
  for configuration items across a service, not a single item.
- Review a compliance or risk record they own.
- Ask a referee for an example of the candidate identifying and managing a
  configuration-related risk before it became a problem.

### Skill: Change management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal end user computing engineer manages high-impact, complex change
requests and ensures that release policies, procedures and processes are
applied.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. The framework's wording is unchanged from the lead
level, so the baseline holds at Expert — full ownership of the highest-impact
changes and release policy is expected at both the lead and the principal
level.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot describe what makes a change high-impact or complex.
- 1 — Beginner: contributes to part of a complex change under a colleague's
  direction.
- 2 — Intermediate: manages a moderately complex change with a senior
  colleague available to check progress.
- 3 — Advanced: manages a high-impact, complex change independently, and
  applies release policies correctly.
- 4 — Expert: manages the highest-impact, most complex changes in the
  service, and ensures the wider team applies release policy consistently.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a high-impact, complex change they managed
  end to end, including how they handled the risk.
- Review a change record or post-implementation review for a complex change
  they led.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate's management of complex changes has
  needed correction, and how release policy compliance was checked.

**Baseline for this role level**

### Skill: Incident management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal end user computing engineer leads the investigation and
resolution of incidents.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. The framework's wording is unchanged from the lead
level, so the baseline holds — leading incident investigation and resolution
is Expert-level work at both grades, and a principal is expected to still do
it well even while carrying a wider strategic remit.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot organise an incident response beyond their own actions.
- 1 — Beginner: follows another lead's direction during an incident.
- 2 — Intermediate: directs a straightforward incident response with support
  available if it escalates.
- 3 — Advanced: leads the investigation and resolution of most incidents
  independently, co-ordinating others involved.
- 4 — Expert: leads the response to the most serious or complex incidents,
  and is the person others learn incident leadership from.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe an incident they led the investigation and
  resolution of, including how they directed others involved.
- Ask a referee to describe the candidate's leadership during a difficult
  incident.
- Review an incident report or post-incident review the candidate authored or
  led.

### Skill: Ownership and initiative

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal end user computing engineer takes accountability for issues that
occur and is proactive in searching for potential problems, and achieves
excellent user outcomes.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. The framework's wording is unchanged from the lead
level; at principal level this proactive accountability is expected to extend
across the team's strategy, not just an individual's own work, which supports
holding the baseline at Expert rather than lowering it.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: only acts once an issue has already been raised.
- 1 — Beginner: owns an existing issue through to resolution when reminded to.
- 2 — Intermediate: owns issues through to resolution unprompted, but has not
  yet proactively surfaced a potential problem before it occurred.
- 3 — Advanced: takes accountability for issues that occur, proactively looks
  for potential problems, and consistently achieves a good outcome for users.
- 4 — Expert: builds a habit of proactive problem-searching and accountability
  into how the whole team works, and models it for other leads.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of a potential problem the candidate spotted and
  addressed before it became an incident, at a team or service level.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate takes accountability when something
  goes wrong, or shifts blame.
- Review evidence of a user outcome the candidate achieved that went beyond
  the minimum fix.

### Skill: Problem management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal end user computing engineer ensures that the right actions are
taken to investigate, resolve and anticipate problems, and co-ordinates the
team to investigate problems, implement solutions and take preventive
measures.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Ensuring the right actions are taken and co-ordinating
the whole team, rather than personally determining a resolution as at lead
level, is directing others through problem management — full mastery on this
scale.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot describe how problem management should be co-ordinated
  across a team.
- 1 — Beginner: contributes to a problem investigation someone else
  co-ordinates.
- 2 — Intermediate: co-ordinates a straightforward problem investigation with
  a colleague available to check the approach.
- 3 — Advanced: ensures the right actions are taken on most problems and
  co-ordinates the team's response independently.
- 4 — Expert: ensures the team consistently investigates, resolves and
  anticipates problems, and is the escalation point when co-ordination
  breaks down.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe how they co-ordinated a team through a
  significant problem investigation.
- Review a problem management process or escalation record they own.
- Ask a referee whether the team's problem management has improved under the
  candidate's co-ordination.

### Skill: Service focus

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal end user computing engineer sees the bigger picture by taking
groups of services and investigating how to get the best of the underlying
services that support them.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Taking groups of services together and investigating
how to get the best out of what underlies them is the strategic, big-picture
view the level description itself names, and matches Expert rather than
Advanced.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: only ever considers one service at a time.
- 1 — Beginner: helps analyse a group of services under a colleague's
  direction.
- 2 — Intermediate: analyses how two related services interact, with support.
- 3 — Advanced: improves how a single service's underlying components work
  together.
- 4 — Expert: takes a group of services together, investigates how the
  underlying services can best support them, and acts on what that reveals.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe an improvement they made that spanned more
  than one service.
- Review a strategy document or roadmap where the candidate analysed a group
  of services together.
- Ask a referee for an example of the candidate spotting an opportunity across
  services that a narrower view would have missed.

### Skill: Technical specialism

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal end user computing engineer understands the direction of future
technologies and delivers a model to support and maintain those future
technologies and any databases that coexist in the current environment.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Understanding where technology is heading and
delivering a support model for it, rather than setting today's standards as
at lead level, is forward-looking, strategic mastery — Expert on this scale.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has no view on how technology in this area is likely to change.
- 1 — Beginner: repeats a view on future technology direction that someone
  else has formed.
- 2 — Intermediate: forms a view on a specific future technology with a
  colleague checking the reasoning.
- 3 — Advanced: forms an independent, well-reasoned view on the direction of
  future technologies relevant to the service.
- 4 — Expert: delivers a model that supports and maintains future
  technologies alongside the databases and systems that coexist with them
  today.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a technology direction they identified and
  how they planned to support it alongside existing systems.
- Review a strategy roadmap or technical model the candidate produced.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate's view of technology direction has
  proved accurate.

### Skill: Testing

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal end user computing engineer manages testing activities within
development or integration activities, manages risks and takes preventative
action when risks become unacceptable, and manages customer relations.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Managing risk to the point of preventative action, and
managing customer relations around testing, is a wider, judgement-heavy remit

own description here.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot describe how testing risk connects to customer relations.
- 1 — Beginner: monitors a testing risk that someone else manages.

  unacceptable, and manages the customer relationship around that decision.

  unacceptable and took preventative action.

- Ask a referee how the candidate has managed a difficult customer

### Skill: User focus

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal end user computing engineer gives direction on which tools or
methods to use, demonstrates experience in meeting the needs of users across a
variety of channels, brings insight and expertise in how user needs have
changed over time to ensure they are met by the business, and applies
strategic thinking to provide the best service for the end user.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Bringing insight on how user needs have changed over
time and applying strategic thinking, on top of directing tools and methods,
is exactly the mastery-plus-guidance definition of Expert, and a clear step
beyond the lead level's championing role.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has no view on how user needs have changed over time.
- 1 — Beginner: follows direction on which tools or methods to use.
- 2 — Intermediate: meets user needs well through one channel with guidance.
- 3 — Advanced: champions user research and guides others through the user
  story.
- 4 — Expert: gives direction on tools and methods across a variety of
  channels, brings insight on how user needs have changed over time, and
  applies strategic thinking to keep the service meeting them.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe how user needs for this service have changed
  over time, and how they have responded strategically.
- Review a strategy or tooling recommendation the candidate directed.
- Ask a referee for an example of the candidate's strategic thinking on user
  focus changing the service's direction.
