# Principal Application Operations Engineer - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This assessment is for the person doing the assessing — a line manager, a hiring panel member, or a reviewer — who needs to judge whether a candidate or employee has the baseline abilities the principal application operations engineer role level requires. It covers the IT operations role of application operations engineer at its most senior level: someone who owns the application strategy, leads on resourcing, learning and development for their team, holds expert technical understanding, and is accountable for vendor and supplier management.

The competency matrix below turns the framework's own description of this role level into a rated, evidence-based instrument across the eleven skills it names. It is one input among several into a hiring, onboarding or development decision, never the whole of one — see `## Advisory` for why.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating on this form must never be the sole basis for a hiring, promotion, pay, performance or disciplinary decision. Before using it, consult your organisation's human resources, people development, occupational psychology or legal compliance colleagues.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` before you score anything. Gather whatever evidence exists ahead of time: a CV or portfolio, a structured interview, a work sample or task, direct observation, or references. At principal level, look for a sustained track record — strategy documents authored, vendor relationships managed over time, people developed — rather than a single project, since the framework's own accountabilities here are about owning strategy and building capability, not completing a task.

**Scoring on evidence, not impression.** Every rating must trace to something the person did, said, produced, or was observed doing. Seniority of job title alone is not evidence of a skill; a specific strategy the candidate wrote, and what changed because of it, is.

**Consistency.** Score every candidate for this role level the same way, and for a hiring or promotion decision have more than one assessor score independently before comparing notes — essential at principal level, where a single assessor's personal view of what "strategic" looks like can otherwise dominate the rating.

**Fairness.** An unvalidated, AI-drafted instrument like this one can produce unlawful adverse impact if it is used inconsistently or if ratings drift away from demonstrated, job-related evidence. Score against the behavioural indicators below, keep your evidence log, and use more than one assessor wherever you can — consistent, evidence-based administration is what materially reduces that risk. See [Fair and lawful use](../../../../uk-gdad-pcf-competency-assessments-by-assessor/spec/index.md#fair-and-lawful-use) for the full constraints this assessment is written to.

## Rating scale

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 1 | Awareness | You can describe skill fundamentals and show basic knowledge of related tools |
| 2 | Working | You can apply the skill with some support and use appropriate tools |
| 3 | Practitioner | You can apply the skill independently, determine the best tools, and share your experience |
| 4 | Expert | You can lead organisational best practice and teach advanced techniques |

This is a proficiency scale, not a frequency scale — it rates how well the person does the thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it.

**Worked example — Service management framework knowledge.** A candidate who holds only a Level 3 qualification, without the expert-level certificate the framework names at this level, sits at 2 or 3. A candidate who holds the expert-level certificate but has not yet been asked to apply that depth for others sits at 3. A candidate who holds the expert certificate and is regularly asked to settle disputes about how the framework should be applied, or trains others in it, sits at 4 — full mastery, demonstrated by teaching.

## About this role level

The application operations engineer supports, manages and maintains a single application or a suite of applications. The role runs across five levels: associate application operations engineer, application operations engineer, senior application operations engineer, lead application operations engineer, and principal application operations engineer.

The principal application operations engineer is the top of the ladder. The framework describes this level as owning the application strategy and leading on resourcing, learning and development for their team. At this level, you have expert technical understanding, are accountable for vendor and supplier management, and may still manage and maintain certain aspects of an application directly, depending on the complexity and size of a project and the likely impact on the business.

The framework names eleven skills at this level: asset and configuration management (managing configuration items and related information, and managing service compliance and risk), availability and capacity management (ensuring the correct implementation of availability and capacity management standards and procedures, identifying capacity issues and implementing required changes, and initiating remedial action), change management (managing high impact, complex change requests, and ensuring that release policies, procedures and processes are applied), incident management (leading the investigation and resolution of incidents), ownership and initiative (taking accountability for issues that occur, being proactive in searching for potential problems, and achieving excellent user outcomes), problem management (ensuring the right actions are taken to investigate, resolve and anticipate problems, and co-ordinating the team to investigate problems, implement solutions and take preventive measures), service focus (seeing the bigger picture by taking groups of services and investigating how to get the best of underlying services), service management framework knowledge (holding an expert certificate in the service management framework qualification), technical specialism (understanding the direction of future technologies and delivering a model to support and maintain those technologies and any databases that coexist in the current environment), testing (managing testing activities within development or integration activities, managing risks and taking preventative action when risks become unacceptable, and managing customer relations), and user focus (collaborating with user researchers, representing users internally, explaining the difference between user needs and desires, championing user research, and guiding others in prioritising the user story).

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Asset and configuration management

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a principal application operations engineer to manage configuration items and related information, and to manage service compliance and risk.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "Manage service compliance and risk" is organisation-level accountability, not day-to-day maintenance, and matches full mastery at the top of the role's ladder.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 1 — Awareness: Manages configuration items for a single area with support.
- 2 — Working: Manages configuration items independently but has not yet owned compliance or risk at an estate level.
- 3 — Practitioner: Manages configuration compliance and risk across the estate, catching issues before they become material.
- 4 — Expert: Owns configuration compliance and risk policy for the estate, and other leads escalate their hardest configuration risk decisions to this person.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe how they identify and manage configuration-related risk across an entire application estate, with a real example.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate has owned a compliance issue that reached senior management attention, and how it went.

### Skill: Availability and capacity management

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a principal application operations engineer to ensure the correct implementation of availability and capacity management standards and procedures, to identify capacity issues and implement the required changes, and to initiate remedial action.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "Ensure correct implementation of standards" across the estate, with authority to initiate remedial action, is ownership at the top of the discipline.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a time they identified a capacity standard was not being followed correctly, and what remedial action they initiated.

### Skill: Change management

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a principal application operations engineer to manage high impact, complex change requests, and to ensure that release policies, procedures and processes are applied.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Practitioner. The wording is identical to the lead level's, so the baseline stays at Practitioner rather than rising to Expert on wording alone — a principal candidate who also sets or redefines release policy for the estate should be rated 4, but that is not what the framework's own text names as the expectation.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 1 — Awareness: Handles a high impact change only with close direction.
- 2 — Working: Manages moderately complex changes with occasional guidance.
- 3 — Practitioner: Manages high impact, complex change requests independently, ensuring release policy is applied correctly throughout.
- 4 — Expert: Sets or substantially redefines the release policies and processes the whole team applies.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to walk through the highest impact change they have managed, including what could have gone wrong and how they prevented it.
- Ask a referee to describe how the candidate handled a change that started to go wrong.

**Evidence to gather**

### Skill: Problem management

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a principal application operations engineer to ensure the right actions are taken to investigate, resolve and anticipate problems, and to co-ordinate the team to investigate problems, implement solutions, and take preventive measures.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "Ensure the right actions are taken" and "co-ordinate the team" describe accountability for the whole team's problem management practice, not just personal investigation, which is mastery at the top of the discipline.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 1 — Awareness: Investigates individual problems personally, without co-ordinating others.
- 2 — Working: Co-ordinates a small piece of problem investigation involving another person.
- 3 — Practitioner: Co-ordinates the team's problem investigation for most situations, ensuring resolution and preventative measures follow.
- 4 — Expert: Owns and continuously improves how the whole team anticipates, investigates and resolves problems, including problems that span vendors.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe how they co-ordinate the team's response to a pattern of recurring problems, including how they anticipate problems before they occur.
- Ask a referee how the candidate has improved the team's problem management practice over time.

### Skill: Service focus

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a principal application operations engineer to see the bigger picture by taking groups of services and investigating how to get the best of underlying services.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "See the bigger picture" across groups of services is the broadest, most strategic reading of this skill anywhere in the role's ladder, and matches full mastery.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 4 — Expert: Is the person others rely on to see how the whole application estate's underlying services should best work together.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a time they improved outcomes by looking across a group of services rather than one application in isolation.
- Ask them what trade-off they would make between two underlying services competing for the same resource.

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Understanding where technology is heading and delivering a supporting model is forward-looking, strategic technical leadership, which is mastery at the top of the ladder.

### Skill: User focus

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a principal application operations engineer to collaborate with user researchers and represent users internally, to explain the difference between user needs and the desires of the user, to champion user research to focus on all users, to prioritise and define approaches to understand the user story while guiding others in doing so, and to offer recommendations on the best tools and methods to use.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. The wording matches the lead level exactly, but at the top of the ladder the expectation is that this guidance and these recommendations reach the whole team and beyond, which fits full mastery.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 1 — Awareness: Engages with user research when asked.
- 2 — Working: Represents users internally on straightforward matters.
- 3 — Practitioner: Champions user research and clearly distinguishes user needs from desires, guiding individual colleagues.
- 4 — Expert: Sets how the whole team approaches user focus, is the recognised source of tool and method recommendations, and actively coaches others, including other leads.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe how they have shaped the team's overall approach to user research, not just their own practice.
- Ask a referee who in the team goes to the candidate for advice on user-focused tools or methods, and how often.
