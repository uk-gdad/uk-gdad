# Senior Infrastructure Engineer - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This assessment is for an assessor — a line manager, an interview panel
member, or anyone else evaluating a candidate or an employee — who needs to
judge whether a person has the baseline abilities the IT operations
profession's senior infrastructure engineer role level requires. It measures
demonstrated skill against the UK GDAD Profession Capability Framework's own
description of this level, for a hiring decision, a promotion or progression
board, onboarding a new starter, or a periodic capability check. It is one
input into that decision, never the whole of it — see `## Advisory` before
you begin.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating on this
form must never be the sole basis for a hiring, promotion, pay, performance or
disciplinary decision. Before using it, consult your organisation's human
resources, people development, occupational psychology or legal compliance
colleagues.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you know what the
framework actually expects before you look at any evidence. Then gather what
exists for this person: a CV or portfolio, a structured interview, a work
sample or task, direct observation of them at work, or references from people
who have worked with them. At this level the framework expects work with
technical architects to translate designs into operations, input into
workforce, budget and roadmap management, and — on a managerial path —
leading and directing an infrastructure specialist team, so look for evidence
across both the technical and the wider organisational parts of the role.

**Score on evidence, not impression.** Every rating must trace to something
the person did, said, produced, or was observed doing. If you cannot point to
evidence for a rating, record it as unknown rather than guess — an honest gap
teaches you more than a guess dressed up as a score.

**Stay consistent.** Score every candidate for this role level the same way,
using the same evidence sources wherever you can. For a hiring or promotion
decision, have more than one assessor score independently, then compare notes,
rather than settling on a single view first.

**Fairness.** An unvalidated, AI-drafted instrument like this one, used
inconsistently, can produce unlawful adverse impact even when nobody intends
it. Consistent, evidence-based administration by more than one assessor
materially reduces that risk. Never let a rating be influenced by anything
other than demonstrated, job-related evidence.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point
proficiency scale — how well the person does the thing, on the evidence
available, not how often they do it.

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

**Worked example.** Take Skill: Systems design. At 2 — Intermediate, a
candidate produces a documented design for a system of managed risk, working
with well understood technology. At 3 — Advanced, the baseline for this
level, they design systems characterised by medium levels of risk, impact,
and business or technical complexity, select the appropriate design
standards, methods and tools, and review the systems designs of others — the
framework's own wording for this level. At 4 — Expert, they set the design
standards other reviewers work to. Calibrate against evidence like this
before you start scoring.

## About this role level

IT operations role: Infrastructure engineer. An infrastructure engineer
designs, builds, manages and supports the infrastructure services that
underpin all internal user services and services to the public.

The role has five levels, in order: associate infrastructure engineer,
infrastructure engineer, senior infrastructure engineer, lead infrastructure
engineer, and principal infrastructure engineer. This assessment covers
senior infrastructure engineer, the third of those.

Role level: Senior infrastructure engineer. Working in a team of
infrastructure specialists and engineers, a senior infrastructure engineer
builds, administers, supports and maintains solutions as directed and
according to departmental policy.

At this role level, the framework expects someone to:

- ensure services are integrated, delivered and operated as required
- work with and support third parties in providing infrastructure services
- work with technical architects to translate architectural designs into
  operations
- provide input into overall management — workforce, budget, technology
  roadmaps, projects and tasks
- lead and direct infrastructure specialist teams in building, managing,
  supporting and maintaining solutions according to departmental policy, if
  taking a managerial path

The framework names eleven skills for this level: Coding and scripting, IT
infrastructure, Information security, Modern development standards, Ownership
and initiative, Problem management, Service focus, Systems design, Systems
integration, Testing, and Troubleshooting and problem resolution.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Coding and scripting

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior infrastructure engineer can describe different scripting tools and
software that are available and currently in use.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. The framework's wording is unchanged from the
level below, but a senior engineer is expected to know this well enough to
choose between tools with confidence, without needing prompting — Intermediate
rather than Beginner.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot name a scripting tool used by the team.
- 1 — Beginner: describes the scripting tools and software currently in use
  when asked.
- 2 — Intermediate: explains, unprompted, which scripting tool suits a given
  task and why.
- 3 — Advanced: writes or amends scripts using the team's tools independently.
- 4 — Expert: recommends new scripting tools for the team to adopt.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe the scripting tools and software the team
  currently uses, and when each is the right choice.
- Ask for an example of a task where they chose between two scripting tools.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate's knowledge of the team's scripting
  tools is accurate and current.

### Skill: IT infrastructure

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior infrastructure engineer designs and implements infrastructure
solutions and services — for example, networking and physical infrastructure,
commercial-off-the-shelf (COTS) software, open source packages and solutions,
or virtual and cloud computing.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Designing infrastructure, not only building and
administering it as at the level below, is independent architectural work,
matching Advanced.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not designed an infrastructure solution.
- 1 — Beginner: has helped implement an infrastructure design someone else
  produced.
- 2 — Intermediate: designs straightforward infrastructure components with a
  senior colleague checking the approach.
- 3 — Advanced: independently designs and implements infrastructure solutions
  and services, and takes responsibility for how well they perform once live.
- 4 — Expert: sets infrastructure design standards for others to follow.

**Evidence to gather**

- Review an infrastructure design the candidate produced end to end, and ask
  them to justify the key decisions in it.
- Ask about a piece of infrastructure they designed and implemented, and what
  happened once it went live.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate's infrastructure designs have needed
  significant intervention after go-live.

### Skill: Information security

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior infrastructure engineer uses information security practices and
available security controls to contribute to protecting solutions and
services.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. Applying practices and controls to contribute to
protection, rather than only explaining them as at the levels below, is
applied, guided security work — a real step up, though not yet independent
security design.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot apply a security control to a real solution.
- 1 — Beginner: applies a security control someone else has selected, when
  told to.
- 2 — Intermediate: selects and applies information security practices and
  controls to contribute to protecting a solution, with some guidance.
- 3 — Advanced: independently ensures a solution's security controls are
  adequate before it goes live.
- 4 — Expert: sets security practice for infrastructure solutions across the
  team.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a security control they applied to a solution
  and why they chose it.
- Review a design or configuration record showing the security controls the
  candidate contributed.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate's security judgement has needed
  correction.

### Skill: Modern development standards

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior infrastructure engineer applies modern development standards and
supports others in applying them.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Applying standards independently and supporting
others, rather than applying them under guidance as at the level below, is
independent, strong-grasp work.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot apply a modern development standard without help.
- 1 — Beginner: applies a standard with a colleague checking the outcome.
- 2 — Intermediate: applies standards to their own work unassisted, but has
  not yet helped a colleague apply one.
- 3 — Advanced: applies modern development standards independently and
  supports a colleague in applying them correctly.
- 4 — Expert: leads how the team applies modern development standards.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe how they applied a modern development
  standard to a piece of work, unassisted.
- Ask for an example of them supporting a colleague to apply a standard.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate's standard of work is consistently
  compliant.

### Skill: Ownership and initiative

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior infrastructure engineer owns an issue until a new owner has been
found or the problem has been mitigated or resolved.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. The framework's wording is unchanged from the
level below; this level's growth is expressed through Systems design, IT
infrastructure and the wider management input rather than a broader Ownership
and initiative expectation.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: drops an issue once it is out of their hands.
- 1 — Beginner: keeps an issue open on their own list but needs reminding to
  chase it.
- 2 — Intermediate: tracks an issue through to a confirmed handover or
  resolution without being chased.
- 3 — Advanced: proactively checks that a handed-over issue actually landed
  with its new owner and was picked up.
- 4 — Expert: is the person others rely on to never let an issue slip, across
  a whole shift or team.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of an issue the candidate followed through to resolution
  or a confirmed handover.
- Ask a referee whether issues assigned to the candidate have gone missing or
  stalled.
- Review a ticket history showing the candidate's ownership of an issue over
  time.

### Skill: Problem management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior infrastructure engineer initiates and monitors actions to
investigate patterns and trends to resolve problems, effectively consults
specialists where required, determines the appropriate resolution and assists
with its implementation, and determines preventative measures.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Determining the resolution and preventative measures,
rather than only investigating and contributing as at the level below, is
independent, judgement-driven ownership of the whole problem.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot distinguish a problem from an incident.
- 1 — Beginner: investigates a problem when told exactly what to look at.
- 2 — Intermediate: investigates a problem and contributes ideas to a remedy,
  but relies on a colleague to decide the resolution.
- 3 — Advanced: initiates and monitors an investigation into a pattern or
  trend, consults specialists appropriately, determines the resolution and
  the preventative measure, and assists with implementing it.
- 4 — Expert: spots a pattern across problems that others have missed and
  changes how the team investigates as a result.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a problem investigation they initiated, who
  they consulted, and what resolution and preventative measure they
  determined.
- Review a problem record or root-cause report they own.
- Ask a referee for an example of a preventative measure the candidate
  determined that stopped a recurring issue.

### Skill: Service focus

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior infrastructure engineer takes inputs and establishes coherent
frameworks that work.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. The framework's wording is unchanged from the

  support work into a coherent routine.

  the wider team.

### Skill: Systems design

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior infrastructure engineer designs systems characterised by medium
levels of risk, impact, and business or technical complexity, selects
appropriate design standards, methods and tools and ensures they are applied
effectively, and reviews the systems designs of others to ensure the
selection of appropriate technology, efficient use of resources and
integration of multiple systems and technology.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Reviewing other people's designs, on top of producing
their own for medium-risk, medium-complexity systems, is the clearest sign in
this matrix that this level works independently and is trusted with judgement
calls the level below is not.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot judge whether a systems design is fit for purpose.
- 1 — Beginner: produces a systems design for review at every stage.
- 2 — Intermediate: designs a system of managed risk and complexity largely
  unassisted, but has not yet reviewed another person's design.
- 3 — Advanced: independently designs systems of medium risk, impact and
  complexity, selects the right standards and tools without being told which,
  and reviews colleagues' designs for technology choice, resource efficiency
  and integration.
- 4 — Expert: is the escalation point when a design review surfaces a
  disagreement, and shapes the design standards the review is measured
  against.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to walk through a design they produced and a design they
  reviewed, and what they changed in each.
- Review a design review comment thread or sign-off record the candidate
  produced.
- Ask a referee for an example of the candidate catching a poor technology
  choice or an inefficient design in someone else's work.

### Skill: Systems integration

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior infrastructure engineer defines the integration build, co-ordinates
build activities across systems, and understands how to undertake and support
integration testing activities.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Defining and co-ordinating a build across systems is
active ownership, a clear step up from building and testing simple interfaces
at the level below.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not taken responsibility for any part of an integration
  build.
- 1 — Beginner: contributes to one part of an integration build under
  direction.
- 2 — Intermediate: co-ordinates a small integration build with a senior
  colleague available to check progress.
- 3 — Advanced: defines an integration build and co-ordinates activities
  across systems independently, and supports the integration testing that
  follows.
- 4 — Expert: is trusted to define integration builds across complex,
  multi-system programmes.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe an integration build they defined and
  co-ordinated, including how they sequenced the work across systems.
- Review an integration plan or build schedule they produced.
- Ask a referee about the candidate's role in a recent integration testing
  effort — did they support it, or run it.

### Skill: Testing

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior infrastructure engineer reviews requirements and specifications and
defines test conditions, identifies issues and risks associated with work,
and analyses and reports test activities and results.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Defining test conditions from requirements and
analysing results, rather than only executing a script someone else wrote as
at the level below, is independent test-design work.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot say what a test condition is.
- 1 — Beginner: executes a test condition someone else has defined.
- 2 — Intermediate: defines a straightforward test condition from a
  requirement with a colleague checking it.
- 3 — Advanced: independently reviews requirements, defines test conditions,
  identifies risks in the work, and analyses and reports the results.
- 4 — Expert: sets the test approach for a wider piece of work and reviews
  others' test conditions.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to walk through test conditions they defined from a
  requirement or specification.
- Review a test report they produced, including how they analysed the
  results.
- Ask a referee for an example of a risk the candidate identified during

### Skill: Troubleshooting and problem resolution

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior infrastructure engineer breaks a problem down into its component
parts to identify and diagnose root causes, and troubleshoots and identifies
problems across different technology capabilities.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. The framework adds root-cause decomposition to the
level below's troubleshooting, and expects it applied across several
technology capabilities without direction — independent, strong-grasp work.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot describe a structured approach to diagnosing a fault.
- 1 — Beginner: diagnoses a familiar fault type when guided through it.
- 2 — Intermediate: diagnoses faults in a familiar technology area
  unassisted, but needs help breaking down a fault that spans several
  capabilities.
- 3 — Advanced: independently breaks a cross-capability problem into its
  component parts, diagnoses the root cause, and resolves it or hands it on
  with a clear diagnosis.
- 4 — Expert: is the escalation point for faults that have resisted diagnosis
  by others, and teaches root-cause analysis technique.

**Evidence to gather**

- Walk through a real or simulated multi-capability fault scenario and observe
  how the candidate decomposes it.
- Ask for an example of a root cause they identified that was not the first,
  more obvious, suspect.
- Ask a referee for an example of the candidate resolving a problem that
  spanned more than one technology area.
