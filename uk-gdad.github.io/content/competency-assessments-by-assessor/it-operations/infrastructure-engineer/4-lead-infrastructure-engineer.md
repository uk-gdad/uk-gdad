# Lead Infrastructure Engineer - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This assessment is for an assessor — a line manager, an interview panel
member, or anyone else evaluating a candidate or an employee — who needs to
judge whether a person has the baseline abilities the IT operations
profession's lead infrastructure engineer role level requires. It measures
demonstrated skill against the UK GDAD Profession Capability Framework's own
description of this level, for a hiring decision, a promotion or progression
board, onboarding a new starter, or a periodic capability check. It is one
input into that decision, never the whole of it — see `## Advisory` before
you begin.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating on this
form must never be the sole basis for a hiring, promotion, pay, performance or
disciplinary decision. Before using it, consult your organisation's human
resources, people development, occupational psychology or legal compliance
colleagues.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you know what the
framework actually expects before you look at any evidence. Then gather what
exists for this person: a CV or portfolio, a structured interview, a work
sample or task, direct observation of them at work, or references from people
who have worked with them. At this level the framework expects management of
third-party infrastructure provision, oversight of programmes and projects,
and expertise developing architectural solutions throughout the service
product life cycle, so look for evidence at that scale, not only individual
technical delivery.

**Score on evidence, not impression.** Every rating must trace to something
the person did, said, produced, or was observed doing. If you cannot point to
evidence for a rating, record it as unknown rather than guess — an honest gap
teaches you more than a guess dressed up as a score.

**Stay consistent.** Score every candidate for this role level the same way,
using the same evidence sources wherever you can. For a hiring or promotion
decision, have more than one assessor score independently, then compare notes,
rather than settling on a single view first.

**Fairness.** An unvalidated, AI-drafted instrument like this one, used
inconsistently, can produce unlawful adverse impact even when nobody intends
it. Consistent, evidence-based administration by more than one assessor
materially reduces that risk. Never let a rating be influenced by anything
other than demonstrated, job-related evidence.

**Handling sensitive material.** Infrastructure engineering at this level
routinely touches live infrastructure, third-party vendor arrangements and
security controls. When you record evidence in `## Assessor notes and
evidence log`, describe what the person did and how they reasoned about it —
not classified material, live vulnerability detail, or specific security
configuration.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point
proficiency scale — how well the person does the thing, on the evidence
available, not how often they do it.

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

**Worked example.** Take Skill: IT infrastructure. At 2 — Intermediate, a
candidate builds and administers infrastructure solutions as directed. At 3 —
Advanced, they independently design and implement infrastructure solutions
and take responsibility for how they perform once live. At 4 — Expert, the
baseline for this level, they lead teams and departments in the design,
implementation, administration and support of infrastructure solutions and
services — the framework's own wording for this level, and a clear step from
independent delivery into leading others at scale. Calibrate against evidence
like this before you start scoring.

## About this role level

IT operations role: Infrastructure engineer. An infrastructure engineer
designs, builds, manages and supports the infrastructure services that
underpin all internal user services and services to the public.

The role has five levels, in order: associate infrastructure engineer,
infrastructure engineer, senior infrastructure engineer, lead infrastructure
engineer, and principal infrastructure engineer. This assessment covers lead
infrastructure engineer, the fourth of those.

Role level: Lead infrastructure engineer. A lead infrastructure engineer
manages third-party provision of infrastructure services and the provision of
expertise to develop architectural solutions for infrastructure services,
throughout the service product life cycle.

At this role level, the framework expects someone to:

- oversee programmes and projects
- work with technical architects to translate the architectural designs into
  operations, and support technical architects in operationalising the
  designs
- lead and direct infrastructure specialist teams in building, managing,
  supporting and maintaining solutions according to departmental policy, if
  taking a managerial path

The framework names eleven skills for this level: Coding and scripting, IT
infrastructure, Information security, Modern development standards, Ownership
and initiative, Problem management, Service focus, Systems design, Systems
integration, Testing, and Troubleshooting and problem resolution.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Coding and scripting

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead infrastructure engineer interacts with, reads and writes code, and
carries out baseline repair activities.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Reading and writing code and carrying out baseline
repair is hands-on, independent technical work, a clear step up from
describing available scripting tools at the levels below.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot read or interpret a piece of code or a script.
- 1 — Beginner: reads code with help to understand what it does.
- 2 — Intermediate: reads and makes small, guided edits to existing code or
  scripts.
- 3 — Advanced: reads and writes code independently, and carries out baseline
  repair activities when something breaks.
- 4 — Expert: is the person colleagues bring the hardest code or scripting
  problems to.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to walk through a piece of code or a script they wrote or
  repaired.
- Observe or ask them to describe a baseline repair activity they carried
  out.
- Ask a referee about the quality and reliability of the candidate's code or
  scripts.

### Skill: IT infrastructure

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead infrastructure engineer leads teams and departments in the design,
implementation, administration and support of infrastructure solutions and
services.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Leading teams and departments through the whole
infrastructure life cycle, rather than personally designing and implementing

definition of Expert.

  administration and support of infrastructure solutions and services.

A lead infrastructure engineer designs solutions and services with security
controls included, specifically engineered to mitigate security threats.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Designing security controls into a solution from the

- Ask a referee whether the candidate's security designs have needed

### Skill: Ownership and initiative

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead infrastructure engineer takes accountability for issues that occur and
is proactive in searching for potential problems, and achieves excellent user
outcomes.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Proactively searching for problems before they occur,
rather than only owning an issue once it exists, is a genuine step up from
the levels below, matching independent, strong-grasp practice.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: only acts once an issue has already been raised.
- 1 — Beginner: owns an existing issue through to resolution when reminded to.
- 2 — Intermediate: owns issues through to resolution unprompted, but has not
  yet proactively surfaced a potential problem before it occurred.
- 3 — Advanced: takes accountability for issues that occur, proactively looks
  for potential problems, and consistently achieves a good outcome for users.
- 4 — Expert: builds a habit of proactive problem-searching into how the
  whole team works.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of a potential problem the candidate spotted and
  addressed before it became an incident.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate takes accountability when something
  goes wrong, or shifts blame.
- Review evidence of a user outcome the candidate achieved that went beyond
  the minimum fix.

### Skill: Problem management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead infrastructure engineer initiates and monitors actions to investigate
patterns and trends to resolve problems, effectively consults specialists
where required, determines the appropriate resolution and assists with its
implementation, and determines preventative measures.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. The framework's wording is unchanged from the senior
level, so the baseline holds; this level's added weight sits in IT
infrastructure leadership and third-party management rather than a wider
Problem management expectation.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot distinguish a problem from an incident.
- 1 — Beginner: investigates a problem when told exactly what to look at.
- 2 — Intermediate: investigates a problem and contributes ideas to a remedy,
  but relies on a colleague to decide the resolution.
- 3 — Advanced: initiates and monitors an investigation into a pattern or
  trend, consults specialists appropriately, determines the resolution and
  the preventative measure, and assists with implementing it.
- 4 — Expert: spots a pattern across problems that others have missed and
  changes how the team investigates as a result.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a problem investigation they initiated, who
  they consulted, and what resolution and preventative measure they
  determined.
- Review a problem record or root-cause report they own.
- Ask a referee for an example of a preventative measure the candidate
  determined that stopped a recurring issue.

### Skill: Service focus

A lead infrastructure engineer takes inputs and establishes coherent

### Skill: Systems design

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead infrastructure engineer designs systems characterised by medium
levels of risk, impact, and business or technical complexity, selects
appropriate design standards, methods and tools and ensures they are applied
effectively, and reviews the systems designs of others to ensure the
selection of appropriate technology, efficient use of resources and
integration of multiple systems and technology.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. The framework's wording for this skill is unchanged
from the senior level, so the baseline holds; this level's growth is expressed
through IT infrastructure leadership, third-party management and programme
oversight instead.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot judge whether a systems design is fit for purpose.
- 1 — Beginner: produces a systems design for review at every stage.
- 2 — Intermediate: designs a system of managed risk and complexity largely
  unassisted, but has not yet reviewed another person's design.
- 3 — Advanced: independently designs systems of medium risk, impact and
  complexity, selects the right standards and tools without being told which,
  and reviews colleagues' designs for technology choice, resource efficiency
  and integration.
- 4 — Expert: is the escalation point when a design review surfaces a
  disagreement, and shapes the design standards the review is measured
  against.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to walk through a design they produced and a design they
  reviewed, and what they changed in each.
- Review a design review comment thread or sign-off record the candidate
  produced.
- Ask a referee for an example of the candidate catching a poor technology
  choice or an inefficient design in someone else's work.

### Skill: Systems integration

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead infrastructure engineer establishes standards and procedures across a

ensure that the systems integration function works effectively.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Establishing standards across the whole product life

**Behavioural indicators**

- 1 — Beginner: follows an integration standard someone else has established.

  across systems.
- 4 — Expert: establishes the integration standards and procedures across the
  service product life cycle, ensures practitioners adhere to them, and
  manages the resources behind the integration function.

**Evidence to gather**

  established and how they ensured practitioners followed it.

### Skill: Troubleshooting and problem resolution

**What the framework expects at this level**

A lead infrastructure engineer uses lateral thinking to break a problem down
into its component parts to identify and diagnose root causes, and
troubleshoots and identifies problems across different technology
capabilities, including computing, storage, networking, physical
infrastructure, software, commercial-off-the-shelf software (COTS) and open
source software.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. The framework adds lateral thinking and names a wider
span of technology capabilities than the senior level's decomposition skill —
this level is the escalation point when a straightforward breakdown has not
found the cause, which is the definition of Expert on this scale.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot describe a structured approach to diagnosing a fault.
- 1 — Beginner: diagnoses a familiar fault type when guided through it.
- 2 — Intermediate: breaks down a fault within one technology capability
  unassisted.
- 3 — Advanced: independently breaks a cross-capability problem into its
  component parts and diagnoses the root cause.
- 4 — Expert: uses lateral thinking to diagnose a root cause across
  computing, storage, networking, physical infrastructure, software, COTS
  and open source software that has resisted a straightforward breakdown, and
  is the escalation point for others.

**Evidence to gather**

- Walk through a real or simulated multi-capability fault scenario and observe
  how the candidate uses lateral thinking to decompose it.
- Ask for an example of a root cause they found only by thinking laterally
  about the problem, beyond the obvious suspects.
- Ask a referee for an example of the candidate being the last resort when a
  fault had resisted diagnosis by others.
