# Senior Operations Analyst - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This assessment is for the person doing the assessing — a line manager, a hiring panel member, or a reviewer — who needs to judge whether a candidate or employee has the baseline abilities the senior operations analyst role level requires. It covers the IT operations role of command and control centre manager at its second level: someone who prioritises incoming messages for the team and ensures correct information flow, record keeping and data capture, within a function that proactively monitors live services and performance trends.

The competency matrix below turns the framework's own description of this role level into a rated, evidence-based instrument across the eleven skills it names. It is one input among several into a hiring, onboarding or development decision, never the whole of one — see `## Advisory` for why.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating on this form must never be the sole basis for a hiring, promotion, pay, performance, or disciplinary decision. Before using it, consult your organisation's human resources, people development, occupational psychology, or legal compliance colleagues.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` before you score anything. Gather whatever evidence exists ahead of time: a CV or portfolio, a structured interview, a work sample or task, direct observation, or references.

**Scoring on evidence, not impression.** Every rating must trace to something the person did, said, produced, or was observed doing. If you cannot point to evidence for a rating, record it as unknown rather than guess. This level includes an on-call, out-of-hours accountability — score only what the candidate actually did during an on-call shift, not their availability or personal circumstances around being on call, which are not part of this instrument.

**Consistency.** Score every candidate for this role level the same way, and for a hiring or promotion decision have more than one assessor score independently before comparing notes.

**Fairness.** An unvalidated, AI-drafted instrument like this one can produce unlawful adverse impact if it is used inconsistently or if ratings drift away from demonstrated, job-related evidence. Score against the behavioural indicators below, keep your evidence log, and use more than one assessor wherever you can — consistent, evidence-based administration is what materially reduces that risk. See [Fair and lawful use](../../../../uk-gdad-pcf-competency-assessments-by-assessor/spec/index.md#fair-and-lawful-use) for the full constraints this assessment is written to.

## Rating scale

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 1 | Awareness | You can describe skill fundamentals and show basic knowledge of related tools |
| 2 | Working | You can apply the skill with some support and use appropriate tools |
| 3 | Practitioner | You can apply the skill independently, determine the best tools, and share your experience |
| 4 | Expert | You can lead organisational best practice and teach advanced techniques |

This is a proficiency scale, not a frequency scale — it rates how well the person does the thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it.

**Worked example — Incident management.** A candidate who can log a straightforward incident but has never diagnosed its cause sits at 1 or below. A candidate who diagnoses and prioritises incidents, investigates their causes and finds resolutions sits at 2 or 3, depending on how consistently they do this and whether it includes incidents surfaced during on-call monitoring — this level's own baseline is 2, since the wording marks a step up from the entry level without yet reaching fully independent, complex-incident leadership. A candidate who diagnoses the organisation's hardest incidents unaided is showing evidence closer to the level above.

## About this role level

Command and control centre managers proactively monitor live services and performance trends to identify potential problems or areas for improvement that can then be investigated. The role runs across four levels: operations analyst, senior operations analyst, operational control manager, and head of command and control.

The senior operations analyst level sits second of four, above operations analyst and below operational control manager. The framework describes this level as prioritising incoming messages for the team and ensuring correct information flow, record keeping and data capture. At this level, you detect notifications and conduct monitor checks to ensure that systems are running correctly; in emergencies, you may be on call outside your working hours to check events that have been logged as not functioning; and in some organisations you oversee the virtual environment.

The framework names eleven skills at this level: asset and configuration management (maintaining secure configuration and accurate information, controlling IT assets in one or more areas, verifying the location and state of IT assets), availability and capacity management (managing service components to meet business needs and key performance indicators (KPIs)), change management (analysing and assessing the impact of change, documenting change requests, and actioning changes from change requests), community collaboration (contributing to the work of others, motivating and empowering teams, creating the right environment for teams to work in and identifying the best team makeup, and recognising and dealing with issues), continuity management (explaining the basic principles of IT standard continuity management processes and procedures), incident management (diagnosing and prioritising incidents, investigating their causes, and finding resolutions), ownership and initiative (owning an issue until a new owner has been found or the problem has been mitigated or resolved), problem management (initiating and monitoring actions to investigate patterns and trends, consulting specialists effectively, determining the appropriate resolution and assisting with its implementation, and determining preventative measures), service focus (taking inputs and establishing coherent frameworks that work), service management framework knowledge (holding a Level 3 service management framework qualification), and user focus (identifying and engaging with users or stakeholders to collate user needs evidence, understanding and defining research that fits user needs, and using quantitative and qualitative data about users to turn user focus into outcomes).

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Asset and configuration management

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a senior operations analyst to maintain secure configuration and accurate information, to control IT assets in one or more areas, and to verify the location and state of IT assets.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Working. This is a step up in wording from "track, log and correct" at the level below, describing active independent maintenance rather than supported administration, matching Working.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 1 — Awareness: Updates a configuration record accurately only with checking.
- 2 — Working: Maintains accurate configuration and asset records for their own area unprompted, and periodically verifies location and state.
- 3 — Practitioner: Proactively catches and corrects configuration discrepancies across more than one area.
- 4 — Expert: Owns configuration accuracy for the operations centre.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe how they maintain configuration accuracy for the systems they monitor.

### Skill: Availability and capacity management

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a senior operations analyst to manage service components so that they meet business needs and key performance indicators (KPIs).

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Working. Managing components against a KPI, including through monitor checks and on-call notification handling, is applied working-level practice at this level, matching Working.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 1 — Awareness: Reads KPI figures but needs help interpreting them.
- 2 — Working: Detects notifications and conducts monitor checks unprompted, taking routine action to keep KPIs within target.
- 3 — Practitioner: Spots capacity or availability trends before they breach KPIs and acts pre-emptively, including out of hours.
- 4 — Expert: Sets availability and capacity monitoring standards for the team.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe how they conduct a monitor check and what they do when a notification fires.
- Ask a referee about the candidate's record of catching an issue before it became a KPI breach.

### Skill: Change management

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a senior operations analyst to analyse and assess the impact of change, to document change requests, and to action changes from change requests.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Working. "Analyse and assess" independently, without the "under supervision" language used at the level below, marks a step up matching Working working practice.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 1 — Awareness: Documents a change request only with someone checking the impact assessment.
- 2 — Working: Assesses change impact and actions changes correctly, with occasional guidance on complex cases.
- 3 — Practitioner: Analyses and assesses change impact independently, including changes affecting monitored services.
- 4 — Expert: Sets the standard others follow for change impact assessment in the operations centre.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to walk through a change they analysed and actioned, including how they assessed its impact on monitoring.

**Rate this skill**

- [ ] 2 — Working

### Skill: Incident management

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a senior operations analyst to diagnose and prioritise incidents, to investigate their causes, and to find resolutions.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Working. "Diagnose and prioritise" and "find resolutions" is a clear step above logging and routing incidents at the level below, and fits Working for this stage of the role's ladder.

- 3 — Practitioner: Diagnoses and resolves incidents of varying complexity consistently.
- 4 — Expert: Is called on to diagnose the operations centre's hardest incidents.

**Evidence to gather**

### Skill: Problem management

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a senior operations analyst to initiate and monitor actions to investigate patterns and trends, to effectively consult specialists where required, to determine the appropriate resolution and assist with its implementation, and to determine preventative measures.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Working. "Initiate and monitor" independently is a step up from contributing to someone else's investigation at the level below, matching Working.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 1 — Awareness: Contributes a small piece of investigation when directed.
- 2 — Working: Initiates a problem investigation from a monitoring trend unprompted, consulting a specialist where needed and contributing to a resolution.
- 3 — Practitioner: Runs a problem investigation independently, from evidence to proposed remedy, for moderately complex problems.
- 4 — Expert: Leads problem investigations and judges escalation without checking.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a monitoring trend they investigated as a problem, who they consulted, and what preventative measure resulted.

**Rate this skill**

- [ ] 2 — Working
- [ ] 3 — Practitioner

### Skill: User focus

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a senior operations analyst to identify and engage with users or stakeholders to collate user needs evidence, to understand and define research that fits user needs, and to use quantitative and qualitative data about users to turn user focus into outcomes.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Working. This describes active engagement and use of data, matching Working working practice, unchanged from the level below.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 4 — Expert: Is sought out for how to turn user evidence into a concrete change.

**Evidence to gather**
