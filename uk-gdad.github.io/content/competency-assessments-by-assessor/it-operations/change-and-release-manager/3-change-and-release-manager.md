# Change and Release Manager - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This assessment is for the person doing the assessing — a line manager, a hiring panel member, or a reviewer — who needs to judge whether a candidate or employee has the baseline abilities the change and release manager role level requires. It covers the IT operations role of change and release manager at its most senior level: someone who ensures that technical changes to IT services are adequately assessed for impact, prioritised, scheduled, authorised and implemented, in line with process and using appropriate tools, and who chairs the Change Advisory Board (CAB).

The competency matrix below turns the framework's own description of this role level into a rated, evidence-based instrument across the eleven skills it names. It is one input among several into a hiring, onboarding or development decision, never the whole of one — see `## Advisory` for why.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating on this form must never be the sole basis for a hiring, promotion, pay, performance or disciplinary decision. Before using it, consult your organisation's human resources, people development, occupational psychology or legal compliance colleagues.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` before you score anything. Gather whatever evidence exists ahead of time: a CV or portfolio, a structured interview, a work sample or task, direct observation, or references. At this level, look for evidence of chairing or running a formal board or approval process, and of being the escalation point when a decision was genuinely contested, not just of following one.

**Scoring on evidence, not impression.** Every rating must trace to something the person did, said, produced, or was observed doing. Having sat on a change board is not the same as having chaired or decided in one — ask which.

**Consistency.** Score every candidate for this role level the same way, and for a hiring or promotion decision have more than one assessor score independently before comparing notes.

**Fairness.** An unvalidated, AI-drafted instrument like this one can produce unlawful adverse impact if it is used inconsistently or if ratings drift away from demonstrated, job-related evidence. Score against the behavioural indicators below, keep your evidence log, and use more than one assessor wherever you can — consistent, evidence-based administration is what materially reduces that risk. See [Fair and lawful use](../../../../uk-gdad-pcf-competency-assessments-by-assessor/spec/index.md#fair-and-lawful-use) for the full constraints this assessment is written to.

## Rating scale

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

This is a proficiency scale, not a frequency scale — it rates how well the person does the thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it.

**Worked example — Asset and configuration management.** A candidate who maintains accurate configuration records for their own area sits at 2. A candidate who manages configuration management for the whole organisation, ensuring value for the business and adherence to company policy, and who advocates organisational commitment to asset control, sits at 4 — the framework names exactly this organisation-wide ownership and advocacy at this level, which is full mastery rather than independent working alone.

## About this role level

Change and release managers lead the change advisory board, ensuring adequate risk assessment and scheduling of technical changes and releases. They own the configuration management database and the access, security and configuration of change activities and release procedures. The role runs across three levels: configuration analyst, change and release analyst, and change and release manager.

The change and release manager level is the most senior of the three. The framework describes it as ensuring that technical changes to IT services are adequately assessed for impact, prioritised, scheduled, authorised and implemented, and that this is done in line with process and using appropriate tools. A change and release manager chairs the Change Advisory Board (CAB). At this level, you provide an escalation point, make decisions for all technical changes, and co-ordinate releases and interdependencies.

The framework names eleven skills at this level: asset and configuration management (managing configuration management for the organisation, ensuring value for the business and adherence to company policies, ensuring that changes to assets are recorded and controlled appropriately, and advocating organisational commitment to asset control), availability and capacity management (managing service components to meet business needs and KPIs), change management (implementing new and current change management processes, and supporting change management by actively improving and optimising processes), community collaboration (working collaboratively in a group and actively networking with others, adapting feedback to ensure it is effective and lasting, using their topic to identify and rectify problems in the team dynamic, and identifying issues through Agile "health checks" with the team), continuity management (explaining the basic principles of IT standard continuity management processes and procedures), incident management (diagnosing and prioritising incidents, investigating their causes and finding resolutions), ownership and initiative (owning an issue until a new owner has been found or the problem has been mitigated or resolved), problem management (initiating and monitoring actions to investigate patterns and trends, consulting specialists effectively, determining the appropriate resolution and preventative measures), service focus (taking inputs and establishing coherent frameworks that work), service management framework knowledge (holding a Level 3 service management framework qualification), and user focus (identifying and engaging with users or stakeholders to collate user needs evidence, understanding and defining research that fits user needs, and using quantitative and qualitative data about users to turn user focus into outcomes).

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Asset and configuration management

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a change and release manager to manage configuration management for the organisation, ensuring value for the business and adherence to company policies, to ensure that changes to assets are recorded and controlled appropriately, and to advocate organisational commitment to asset control.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "Manage configuration management for the organisation" and "advocate organisational commitment" are ownership and advocacy at an organisation-wide scale, which is full mastery.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe organisation-level configuration management policy.
- 1 — Beginner: Manages configuration accuracy for a single area with support.
- 2 — Intermediate: Manages configuration accuracy for a single area independently.
- 3 — Advanced: Ensures changes to assets are recorded and controlled across more than one area, catching gaps in policy adherence.
- 4 — Expert: Owns configuration management policy for the whole organisation and actively advocates for asset control commitment at a senior level.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe how they have secured organisational commitment to asset control, including a time it was resisted.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate has owned configuration management policy at an organisational level.

### Skill: Availability and capacity management

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a change and release manager to manage service components so that they meet business needs and key performance indicators (KPIs).

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Managing components against a KPI, independently and reliably, is solid working practice; the wording here does not reach the organisation-wide ownership language used elsewhere in this level's skill set, so it stays at Advanced rather than Expert.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Does not know what availability or capacity KPIs apply to changes they schedule.
- 1 — Beginner: Reads KPI figures but needs help interpreting them.
- 2 — Intermediate: Manages routine capacity actions with some guidance.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently manages service components to meet KPIs, factoring capacity into change scheduling decisions.
- 4 — Expert: Sets or redefines the availability and capacity standards the CAB uses to judge change risk.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a time a capacity constraint changed how they scheduled a set of releases.

### Skill: Change management

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a change and release manager to implement new and current change management processes, and to actively support change management by improving and optimising processes.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. This is the defining skill of the role — chairing the CAB and owning process improvement — and the framework's own wording describes implementing and optimising the process itself, which is full mastery.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe how a change management process is designed or improved.
- 1 — Beginner: Follows an existing change process without contributing to its improvement.
- 2 — Intermediate: Suggests improvements to a change process with support implementing them.
- 3 — Advanced: Implements new change management processes independently and optimises existing ones.
- 4 — Expert: Chairs the CAB, is the final decision point on contested technical changes, and continuously improves the process the whole organisation follows.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe how they have chaired, or would chair, a Change Advisory Board, including how they handle a genuinely contested change.
- Ask for an example of a change management process they implemented or optimised, and what improved as a result.
- Ask a referee how the candidate has handled being the escalation point for a difficult technical change decision.

### Skill: Community collaboration

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a change and release manager to work collaboratively in a group and actively network with others, to adapt feedback to ensure it is effective and lasting, to use their topic to identify and rectify problems in the team dynamic, and to identify issues through Agile "health checks" with the team.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Actively diagnosing and rectifying team dynamic issues is independent, applied leadership of collaboration, which matches Advanced.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Works in isolation and does not engage in team health or collaboration practices.
- 1 — Beginner: Participates in a team health check without acting on what it surfaces.
- 2 — Intermediate: Networks and collaborates with support identifying underlying team dynamic issues.
- 3 — Advanced: Actively networks across the organisation, adapts feedback so it lands effectively, and uses insight from their own area to identify and rectify team dynamic problems.
- 4 — Expert: Is asked to run or improve Agile health checks for other teams beyond their own.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a team dynamic issue they identified through a health check and how they rectified it.
- Ask a referee how effectively the candidate networks and collaborates across the organisation.

### Skill: Problem management

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a change and release manager to initiate and monitor actions to investigate patterns and trends to resolve problems, to effectively consult specialists where required, to determine the appropriate resolution and assist with its implementation, and to determine preventative measures.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Initiating and monitoring investigation, and determining resolution independently, is a clear step above contributing to someone else's investigation, matching Advanced.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 2 — Intermediate: Initiates a problem investigation with support and determines a resolution with guidance.
- 3 — Advanced: Initiates and monitors problem investigations independently, consults specialists appropriately, and determines both the resolution and preventative measures.
- 4 — Expert: Sets the approach the organisation uses for problem investigation connected to change and release.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a pattern of recurring change-related problems they investigated, who they consulted, and what preventative measure resulted.
