# Principal Data Analyst - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This assessment is for an assessor — a line manager, a hiring panel member, or
a reviewer — judging whether a candidate or an employee meets the baseline
abilities required for the Data role of Data analyst, at the Principal data
analyst role level. It measures demonstrated skill against the UK GDAD
Profession Capability Framework's own description of this role level, for a
hiring decision, a promotion board, onboarding, or a capability check.

A principal data analyst leads teams or significant areas of work in data
analysis, determines and implements the standards, processes and structures
that ensure data is used effectively across the organisation, and represents
the data analyst community inside and outside it. This assessment looks for
organisation-wide influence and strategic judgement, on top of the strong
independent delivery expected below.

This assessment is one input among several into a decision, never the whole of
it. Read `## Advisory` before using it.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating on this
form must never be the sole basis for a hiring, promotion, pay, performance or
disciplinary decision. Before using it, consult your organisation's human
resources, people development, occupational psychology, or legal compliance
colleagues.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, then gather whatever
evidence exists: a CV or portfolio, a structured interview, a work sample or
task (for example, asking the candidate to describe how they would set data
analysis standards for an organisation, or to critique a proposed
organisational data product strategy), direct observation, or references —
particularly references that can speak to the candidate's standing with
senior stakeholders and their influence beyond their own immediate team.

**Score on evidence, not impression.** Every rating must trace to something
the person did, said, produced, or was observed doing. Evidence at this level
often takes longer to surface than a single task — a track record across
several years, rather than one example — so allow time for it to emerge, and
record a rating as unknown rather than guess if it cannot.

**Consistency.** Score every candidate for this role level the same way. For a
hiring or promotion decision, have more than one assessor score independently
before comparing notes.

**Fairness.** Every rating and every piece of evidence you gather must be
about demonstrated, job-related behaviour — see
[Fair and lawful use](../../../../uk-gdad-pcf-competency-assessments-by-assessor/spec/index.md#fair-and-lawful-use) in the project specification.
Never let a rating be coloured by anything other than what the person actually
did, said, or produced.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix below is rated on the same four-point
scale. This is a **proficiency** scale — it rates how well the person does the
thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it or how they feel
about it.

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 1 | Awareness | You can describe skill fundamentals and show basic knowledge of related tools |
| 2 | Working | You can apply the skill with some support and use appropriate tools |
| 3 | Practitioner | You can apply the skill independently, determine the best tools, and share your experience |
| 4 | Expert | You can lead organisational best practice and teach advanced techniques |

**Worked example — Data management.** The framework expects a principal data
analyst to "champion and lead data management and governance processes within
your team and across your organisation" and to "define data management tools
and develop training requirements to implement data governance processes
effectively". A candidate who advocates for good data management within their
own team, but has never defined tools or training for the wider organisation,
is showing evidence closer to **3 — Practitioner** — strong practice, but not yet
this level's baseline. A candidate who has led the definition of data
management tooling and training that other teams across the organisation now
use is showing **4 — Expert**, the baseline this level expects.

## About this role level

The Data profession's Data analyst role collects, manages, explores, and shares
data to support organisational objectives and deliver business impact. Across
the role, a data analyst identifies, collects, and moves data between a range
of systems; applies data analysis and data visualisation tools and
techniques; prepares, manages and models data, following appropriate
standards and practices; and communicates data, insights and conclusions in
appropriate formats.

The role has four levels, in order: Associate data analyst, Data analyst,
Senior data analyst, and Principal data analyst. This assessment is for the
fourth and most senior of them.

A **principal data analyst** leads teams or significant areas of work in data
analysis. They determine and implement appropriate standards, processes and
structures to ensure data is prepared, managed and used effectively within the
organisation. At this role level, they will influence organisational decision
making on data analysis and the use of data, oversee continuous improvement of
the organisation's data practices and management, and represent the data
analyst community inside and outside of the organisation.

The framework names nine skills for this role level: Applying statistical
and analytical tools and techniques, Communicating between the technical and
non-technical, Data ethics and privacy, Data management, Data preparation and
linkage, Data visualisation, Delivering business impact through data,
Developing code for analysis, and Managing a data project. The framework
lists Delivering business impact through data twice and Managing a data
project three times in the published summary, with identical wording each
time; this assessment treats each as the one skill it is.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Applying statistical and analytical tools and techniques

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal data analyst is expected to identify opportunities to develop
statistical insights and reports that support organisational objectives,
develop and guide others in a range of statistical and analytical techniques
and tools, oversee the quality assurance of statistical techniques and
analytical outputs while continually improving and innovating practices, and
use statistical and analytical outputs to influence stakeholders across the
organisation and beyond.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Overseeing (not just doing) quality assurance,
developing others' technique, and influencing stakeholders across and beyond
the organisation describes mastery used to teach and to shape organisational
decisions — the top of this skill's progression.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 1 — Awareness: Applies technique competently themselves but does not
  influence organisational decisions with it.
- 2 — Working: Contributes analytical insight to a decision, with
  support.
- 3 — Practitioner: Independently leads insight development and quality assures a
  team's outputs.
- 4 — Expert: Identifies organisation-wide opportunities for statistical
  insight, develops and guides others in a range of technique, oversees
  quality assurance while innovating practice, and uses analytical output to
  influence stakeholders across the organisation and beyond it.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a statistical or analytical output that
  changed a significant organisational decision, and their role in producing
  and presenting it.
- Ask how they oversee quality assurance across more analysts than they could
  personally review line by line.
- Ask about a stakeholder outside the organisation they have influenced with
  analytical evidence.

**Rate this skill**

- [ ] 1 — Awareness
- [ ] 2 — Working
- [ ] 3 — Practitioner
- [ ] 4 — Expert

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal data analyst is expected to mediate between people and strengthen
relationships, adopting the appropriate communication method with
stakeholders at all levels, manage stakeholder expectations, and moderate
difficult discussions about high risk and complex topics even within
constrained timescales, and speak on behalf of and represent the community to
large audiences inside and outside the organisation.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Speaking for the whole community to large audiences,
and moderating high-risk, time-constrained discussions, is the top of what
the framework describes for this skill — this level is where managing
differing stakeholder perspectives becomes representing and defending the
profession itself.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 1 — Awareness: Communicates competently one to one but struggles in
  high-stakes group settings.
- 2 — Working: Manages moderately difficult discussions with support.
- 3 — Practitioner: Independently manages difficult discussions and represents
  the team externally.
- 4 — Expert: Is trusted to mediate the organisation's hardest, highest-risk
  discussions about data work, and represents the data analyst community
  credibly to large internal and external audiences.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe the most difficult, highest-stakes discussion
  they have led about data work, under time pressure, and what they did.
- Ask a referee about the candidate's standing with senior stakeholders and
  whether they have represented the function externally.

**Rate this skill**

- [ ] 1 — Awareness
- [ ] 2 — Working
- [ ] 3 — Practitioner
- [ ] 4 — Expert

### Skill: Data ethics and privacy

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal data analyst is expected to work with stakeholders to identify and
address ethical and privacy concerns, demonstrate and communicate how data
ethical issues fit into the wider organisational context, research
developments in data ethics and privacy to improve compliance and processes,
and assess and constructively challenge proposed data ethics policies. The
framework's own wording for this skill is unchanged from the senior level
below.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Practitioner. Although this is the most senior level in the role's
ladder, the framework's own wording for this specific skill does not move
beyond the senior level, so the baseline is held at Practitioner rather than
inflated to Expert on the strength of the job title alone — this level's
real step up in ethical influence shows through the organisation-wide scope
of the surrounding accountabilities, not through a change in this skill's own
wording.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 1 — Awareness: Applies ethical principles in routine work but does not yet
  research or challenge policy.
- 2 — Working: Works with stakeholders on straightforward ethical or
  privacy concerns with support.
- 3 — Practitioner: Independently works with stakeholders to identify and address
  ethical and privacy concerns, explains how they fit the wider organisational
  context, researches developments to improve compliance, and constructively
  challenges a proposed policy where it does not hold up.
- 4 — Expert: Sets and owns data ethics policy for the organisation.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a proposed data ethics or privacy policy they
  constructively challenged, and what changed as a result.
- Ask how they keep informed of developments in data ethics and privacy
  practice and bring that back into the organisation.
- Do not ask the candidate to disclose any personal data or case details as
  part of this evidence gathering.

**Rate this skill**

- [ ] 1 — Awareness
- [ ] 2 — Working
- [ ] 3 — Practitioner
- [ ] 4 — Expert

### Skill: Data management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal data analyst is expected to champion and lead data management and
governance processes within their team and across the organisation, define
and lead on the implementation of data management responsibilities, define
data management tools and develop training requirements to implement data
governance processes effectively, and enable continuous improvement of data
management practices and compliance with data governance policies.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Defining tools and training requirements for the whole
organisation, and leading governance implementation, is organisation-wide
ownership — the top of this skill's progression, well beyond advocating for
policy within a team.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 1 — Awareness: Follows data management responsibilities but has not defined
  or led their implementation.
- 2 — Working: Contributes to defining data management responsibilities
  for their own team.
- 3 — Practitioner: Advocates for and monitors data management practice across a
  team.
- 4 — Expert: Champions and leads data management and governance across the
  organisation, defines tools and training requirements for it, and enables
  continuous improvement of practice and compliance.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a data management tool or training programme
  they defined for organisation-wide use.
- Ask about a time they led the implementation of a data governance
  responsibility across more than one team.

**Rate this skill**

- [ ] 1 — Awareness
- [ ] 2 — Working
- [ ] 3 — Practitioner
- [ ] 4 — Expert

### Skill: Data preparation and linkage

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal data analyst is expected to develop analytical data models using
a deep understanding of data sources, ensure data models are implemented using
tools and systems that align with modelling standards, set data quality
standards in the organisation or beyond, and mentor others in data quality
and modelling.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Setting data quality standards for the organisation "or
beyond", and mentoring others, is the top of this skill's progression — well
beyond planning a single data model's framework.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 1 — Awareness: Follows data quality standards but has not set them.
- 2 — Working: Contributes to data quality standards for their own team.
- 3 — Practitioner: Plans and implements analytical data model frameworks
  independently.
- 4 — Expert: Develops analytical data models using deep source knowledge,
  ensures implementation aligns with modelling standards, sets data quality
  standards for the organisation or beyond, and mentors others in modelling.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a data quality standard they set that other
  teams now follow.
- Ask about someone they have mentored in data modelling, and what changed
  for that person's practice.

**Rate this skill**

- [ ] 1 — Awareness
- [ ] 2 — Working
- [ ] 3 — Practitioner
- [ ] 4 — Expert

### Skill: Data visualisation

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal data analyst is expected to translate business requirements into
goals for data visualisations, set and advocate for new standards and
practices in inclusive, accessible data visualisation, identify data
visualisation capability gaps and implement appropriate training, promote the
value and adoption of data visualisations across the organisation, and
strategically support stakeholders in understanding and using a story.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Setting organisation-wide standards, identifying
capability gaps and implementing training is the top of this skill's
progression — well beyond simplifying a complex story, which is where the
level below is baselined.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 1 — Awareness: Produces good visualisations personally but has not set
  standards for others.
- 2 — Working: Suggests improvements to visualisation practice within
  their own team.
- 3 — Practitioner: Advises others on accessible design and suggests process
  improvements.
- 4 — Expert: Translates business requirements into visualisation goals, sets
  and advocates for organisation-wide standards, identifies capability gaps
  and implements training, and promotes visualisation adoption across the
  organisation.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a visualisation standard or training
  programme they set for the organisation.
- Ask about a capability gap in data visualisation they identified and how
  they closed it.

**Rate this skill**

### Skill: Delivering business impact through data

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal data analyst is expected to champion the role of data across the
organisation, strategically communicate the business impact of data across
the organisation, lead others in delivering and identifying opportunities to
deliver scalable data products that align to organisational objectives, and
partner with others, including other professions, to decide when to pivot to
deliver business impact. The framework lists this skill twice in the
published summary, with identical wording each time; the bullets are merged
here into one block.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Championing data's role across the organisation and
partnering with other professions on strategic pivots is organisation-wide
influence — the top of this skill's progression.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 1 — Awareness: Delivers a data product and can describe its own impact.
- 2 — Working: Identifies opportunities to deliver business impact
  within their own area.
- 3 — Practitioner: Leads others in delivering scalable data products aligned to
  organisational priorities.
- 4 — Expert: Champions the role of data across the organisation,
  strategically communicates its business impact, leads others in
  identifying and delivering scalable data products, and partners with other
  professions on when to pivot.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a time they championed the value of data to
  senior leadership, and what happened as a result.
- Ask about a strategic decision to pivot a piece of work that they helped
  make, in partnership with another profession.

**Rate this skill**

### Skill: Developing code for analysis

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal data analyst is expected to oversee and promote the
implementation of coding standards and practices on existing and new
analytical outcomes, products and services, contribute to coding standards
across the organisation, and ensure reproducibility of code and code
documentation across the organisation.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Overseeing coding standards and ensuring reproducibility
across the whole organisation is organisation-wide ownership — the top of
this skill's progression, well beyond designing and implementing one
analytical approach.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 1 — Awareness: Follows coding standards but has not shaped or promoted them.
- 2 — Working: Contributes to coding standards within their own team.
- 3 — Practitioner: Designs and implements analytical approaches to standard,
  including tests, and leads review of shared code.
- 4 — Expert: Oversees and promotes coding standards across existing and new
  outcomes, contributes to organisation-wide coding standards, and ensures
  reproducibility of code and documentation across the organisation.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a coding standard or reproducibility practice
  they promoted across more than one team.
- Ask about a time they found and fixed a widespread gap in code
  reproducibility across the organisation.

### Skill: Managing a data project

**What the framework expects at this level**

A principal data analyst is expected to advise on problem statement
development ensuring standards are met, oversee and advise on the planning
and implementation of the project life cycle on varied data projects, share
good practice in adapting teams' ways of working to deliver project outcomes,
and lead and develop stakeholder relationships for their team's data
projects. The framework lists this skill three times in the published
summary, with identical wording each time; the bullets are merged here into
one block.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Advising across varied projects, and sharing good
practice for others to adopt, describes someone whose judgement is the
reference point for project management practice — the top of this skill's
progression, beyond independently managing one project.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 1 — Awareness: Manages their own project's problem statement but has not
  advised others.
- 2 — Working: Shares practice with a close colleague within the same
  team.
- 3 — Practitioner: Independently defines problem statements and plans project
  life cycle stages for their own work.
- 4 — Expert: Advises on problem statement development across varied
  projects, oversees planning and implementation of the project life cycle,
  shares good practice in adapting ways of working, and leads and develops
  stakeholder relationships for the team's data projects.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a time they advised another team on how to
  develop or refine a problem statement for a data project.
- Ask about a stakeholder relationship they built and developed specifically
  to support their team's data projects.
