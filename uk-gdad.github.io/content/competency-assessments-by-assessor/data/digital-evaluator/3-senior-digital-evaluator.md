# Senior Digital Evaluator - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This assessment is for the person judging whether a candidate or an employee meets the
baseline a UK Government Digital and Data (GDAD) senior digital evaluator needs. It is
written for an assessor — a line manager, a panel member, or a reviewer — not for the
person being assessed.

A senior digital evaluator manages the evaluations of products or services, ensuring
they follow government guidance and meet standards. This assessment measures
demonstrated capability against the framework's own description of that level, across
the seven skills the framework names for it. It is one input among several into a
hiring, promotion or development decision, never the whole of one — see `## Advisory`
for why.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for educational
purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has not been piloted, normed
or checked for adverse impact — and a rating on this form must never be the sole basis
for a hiring, promotion, pay, performance or disciplinary decision. Before using it,
consult your organisation's human resources, people development, occupational
psychology or legal compliance colleagues.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` before you score anything. Gather
whatever evidence exists: a CV or portfolio, a structured interview, a work sample or
task, direct observation, or references. This is the level where the framework's
"with support" qualifiers for core delivery skills drop away — expect to see, and to
look for, genuinely independent work.

**Scoring on evidence, not impression.** Every rating must trace to something the
person did, said, produced, or was observed doing. If you cannot point to evidence for a
rating, record it as unknown rather than guess.

**Consistency.** Score every candidate for this role level the same way, using the same
kinds of evidence where possible. For a hiring or promotion decision, have more than one
assessor score independently before comparing notes.

**Fairness.** Never let a rating be influenced by anything other than demonstrated,
job-related evidence. See [Fair and lawful use](../../../../uk-gdad-pcf-competency-assessments-by-assessor/spec/index.md#fair-and-lawful-use)
in the project specification for what this rules out.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point proficiency scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

This rates how well the person does the thing, on the evidence available — not how often
they do it. A worked example, using this level's skill of Communicating analysis and
insight:

- **2 — Intermediate**: they presented findings clearly to a specialist audience, but
  needed help reworking the same material for a non-specialist board.
- **3 — Advanced**: they turned a complex, mixed-methods evaluation into a compelling
  report that a policy team acted on, unaided.
- **4 — Expert**: they coached two colleagues on how to turn complex data into a
  compelling story, and are now the person others send a difficult report to for review
  before it goes to a minister's office.

A senior digital evaluator's baseline for this skill, below, is 3.

## About this role level

**The role: digital evaluator.** A digital evaluator assesses the design, implementation
and outcomes of a digital product or service, and whether it delivers value for the
public and society. Some are also members of a government analytical profession where
evaluation is a core skill. In this role, you work closely with delivery and policy
teams; develop theories of change for products and services that explain the links
between activities, outputs, intended outcomes and unintended effects; follow
established monitoring and evaluation methods and government guidance; produce
evaluation plans; design quantitative and qualitative research; collect and analyse
data; and communicate findings, including by publishing reports.

**The level: senior digital evaluator.** The framework describes this level as managing
the evaluations of products or services, ensuring evaluations follow government
guidance and meet standards. It is the third of five levels in this role: junior
digital evaluator, digital evaluator, senior digital evaluator, lead digital evaluator,
and head of digital evaluation. At this level, you work in a multidisciplinary team;
develop theories of change and evaluation plans with stakeholders; manage relationships
with stakeholders, user researchers and performance analysts; collect and analyse
quantitative and qualitative data through research; assure the work of other
evaluators, including evaluation plans, data collection and analysis; and identify the
resources evaluation needs for evaluation activities.

**The skills this level names**, in the summary's order: Communicating analysis and
insight; Data ethics and privacy; Evaluation delivery; Evaluation planning and strategy;
Monitoring and evaluation across the product life cycle; Product and service monitoring;
and Quality assurance of data and analysis.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Communicating analysis and insight

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a senior digital evaluator to turn complex data into compelling,
clear and actionable stories; share data communication skills with the team and across
government; present analysis and visualisations clearly to communicate complex
messages; communicate both negative and positive information to stakeholders; and give
tactical and strategic recommendations.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Every bullet describes unsupported, high-stakes communication
work, including sharing the skill with others — a strong grasp working independently,
matching Advanced.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not communicated complex evaluation findings to a senior stakeholder.
- 1 — Beginner: has drafted part of a complex findings report with close direction.
- 2 — Intermediate: has presented complex findings to a specialist audience with some
  support adapting for a non-specialist one.
- 3 — Advanced: independently turns complex, mixed-methods findings into a compelling
  report or briefing, including delivering unwelcome findings clearly, and gives both
  tactical and strategic recommendations.
- 4 — Expert: is sought out to coach colleagues or across government on communicating
  analysis, and reviews others' high-stakes communications before they go out.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for a real report or briefing they produced that communicated a difficult or
  negative finding, and how the stakeholder responded.
- A structured interview question: "Tell me about a time your analysis wasn't what the
  stakeholder wanted to hear. How did you communicate it?"
- Ask a referee whether the candidate has coached others on communicating analysis.

### Skill: Data ethics and privacy

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a senior digital evaluator to work with stakeholders to identify and
address ethical and privacy concerns, demonstrate and communicate how data ethical
issues fit into the wider organisational context, research developments in data ethics
and privacy to improve compliance and processes, and assess and constructively
challenge proposed data ethics policies.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. "Assess and constructively challenge proposed... policies"
is independent, judgement-based work at organisational scale, well beyond applying a
principle under supervision — Advanced is the right fit.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not identified or addressed a data ethics or privacy concern
  independently.
- 1 — Beginner: has flagged a concern to someone more senior but not led the response.
- 2 — Intermediate: has worked with a stakeholder to address a specific ethics concern,
  with some guidance.
- 3 — Advanced: independently identifies and addresses ethics and privacy concerns,
  keeps up to date with developments in the field, and has constructively challenged a
  proposed data ethics policy.
- 4 — Expert: shapes the organisation's data ethics policy or framework, not just
  challenges it.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Tell me about a time you challenged a proposed
  policy or approach on data ethics grounds. What happened?"
- Ask how they keep their knowledge of data ethics developments current, and for a
  specific example of something they changed because of it.
- Ask a referee about a real ethics or privacy issue the candidate identified and
  resolved.

### Skill: Evaluation delivery

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a senior digital evaluator to evaluate digital services using some
appropriate techniques including process evaluation, impact evaluation, theory of
change, experimentation and economic evaluation; collect quantitative and qualitative
data; analyse and interpret data collected and data from other sources; and use a wide
range of analytical approaches, including descriptive and inferential analysis. This is
the level where the "with support" wording used at the two levels below drops away
entirely.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. No support qualifier remains, and "a wide range of analytical
approaches" (up from "some" at the levels below) signals a strong, independent grasp of
delivery.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: cannot independently run data collection or analysis for an evaluation.
- 1 — Beginner: still needs close direction to collect or analyse evaluation data.
- 2 — Intermediate: runs a straightforward evaluation with some guidance on technique
  choice.
- 3 — Advanced: independently selects and applies an appropriate technique — process,
  impact, theory of change, experimental or economic evaluation — and uses a wide range
  of analytical approaches on a real evaluation, unsupported.
- 4 — Expert: is trusted to deliver the most technically demanding evaluations in the
  team and to advise others on technique choice.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for a completed evaluation they led, and which technique or techniques they
  chose and why.
- A structured interview question: "Talk me through an evaluation where you had to
  choose between two different techniques. What made you pick the one you did?"
- Review a real analysis output, if available, for evidence of range and independence.

### Skill: Evaluation planning and strategy

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a senior digital evaluator to influence stakeholders to secure
resources for an evaluation project, identify evaluation and evidence needs, ensure
plans for monitoring and evaluation are appropriate and proportionate, consider wider
policy and practical constraints that affect evaluation strategy, and agree the
evaluation approach with diverse stakeholders.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Securing resources through influence and weighing policy
constraints against practical ones is judgement-heavy, unsupported work — a strong,
independent grasp, matching Advanced.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not secured resources or agreed an evaluation approach with a
  stakeholder unsupported.
- 1 — Beginner: has helped present evaluation options but not negotiated resources.
- 2 — Intermediate: has agreed an evaluation approach with a single stakeholder group,
  with support.
- 3 — Advanced: independently secures resources through influence, weighs policy and
  practical constraints, and agrees an approach across diverse stakeholders.
- 4 — Expert: sets evaluation strategy for a group of products or services, not a
  single project.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Tell me about a time you had to secure resource or
  buy-in for an evaluation from a reluctant stakeholder."
- Ask about a case where policy constraints and the ideal evaluation design conflicted,
  and how they resolved it.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate has agreed an evaluation approach across more
  than one stakeholder group.

### Skill: Monitoring and evaluation across the product life cycle

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework's wording here is identical to the digital evaluator level below: ensure
the team is measuring performance as appropriate for the relevant service standard
phase, manage the delivery of evaluation of services or products at different phases,
and plan and look at data requirements in future phases. The published expectation for
this specific skill does not step up between these two levels, even though the level's
wider accountabilities — managing relationships, assuring others' work — clearly do.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. Because the text is unchanged from the digital evaluator
level, the honest baseline for this one skill stays the same as it was there, even
though most of this level's other skills sit higher.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not managed evaluation delivery across life cycle phases.
- 1 — Beginner: can describe what phase a product is in without having managed
  evaluation delivery for it.
- 2 — Intermediate: has ensured a team measured performance appropriately for its phase
  and planned ahead for the next phase's data needs, with some guidance.
- 3 — Advanced: independently manages evaluation delivery across multiple life cycle
  phases for more than one product.
- 4 — Expert: advises other teams on evaluation and data requirements across the life
  cycle.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Tell me about managing evaluation for a service as
  it moved between phases."
- Ask how they planned data requirements for a future phase before that phase started.
- Ask a referee about the candidate's role in ensuring a team measured the right things
  for its current phase.

### Skill: Product and service monitoring

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a senior digital evaluator to develop a theory of change for
products or services through discussion with stakeholders, turn theory of change
indicators into measurement frameworks for a digital service, and work with
performance analysts to develop a performance dashboard reporting service analytics and
other identified indicators. The "with support" and "some" qualifiers used at the
digital evaluator level below are both gone here.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Unsupported theory of change development and cross-discipline
work with performance analysts to build a dashboard is independent, strong-grasp work.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not independently developed a theory of change.
- 1 — Beginner: has helped populate a theory of change someone else led.
- 2 — Intermediate: has developed a theory of change with some support, or turned some
  indicators into a measurement framework with guidance.
- 3 — Advanced: independently develops a theory of change through stakeholder
  discussion, turns it into a measurement framework, and works with performance
  analysts to build a dashboard.
- 4 — Expert: sets the methodology other evaluators use for theory of change and
  indicator development.

**Evidence to gather**

- A work sample: a short service description, asking them to sketch a theory of change
  and propose how it would become a dashboard.
- A structured interview question: "Tell me about a dashboard or measurement framework
  you built with a performance analyst. What was each of you responsible for?"
- Ask a referee about the candidate's independence in developing a theory of change.

### Skill: Quality assurance of data and analysis

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a senior digital evaluator to set up a system to get data ready for
use, specify how data should be cleansed and prepared, bring data together from
different sources, and communicate the limitations of data.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Setting up a system and specifying process, rather than
following one, is ownership-level work — a strong, independent grasp.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not designed a data preparation system or specified a cleansing
  approach.
- 1 — Beginner: has followed a data preparation process someone else designed.
- 2 — Intermediate: has prepared and combined data from more than one source with some
  guidance.
- 3 — Advanced: independently sets up a system for getting data ready for use,
  specifies how it should be cleansed, brings sources together, and communicates the
  limitations of the resulting data.
- 4 — Expert: designs quality assurance systems used across multiple teams.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Tell me about a data quality system or process you
  set up. How did you decide what it needed to check?"
- Ask about a time they had to communicate a data limitation to a stakeholder who
  wanted a cleaner answer than the data allowed.
- Review a documented data preparation or cleansing specification they produced, if
  available.
