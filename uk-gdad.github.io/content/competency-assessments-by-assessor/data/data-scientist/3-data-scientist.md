# Data Scientist - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This assessment is for an assessor — a line manager, a hiring panel member, or
a reviewer — judging whether a candidate or an employee meets the baseline for
the Data scientist role level in the Data profession. It measures demonstrated
skill against the UK GDAD Profession Capability Framework's own description of
this role level, across eight named skills, and produces a structured record
an assessor can use in a hiring decision, a promotion board, onboarding, or a
capability check.

It is one input among several, never the whole of a decision. Read
`## Advisory` before using it.

## Advisory

> This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
> educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
> not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating on this
> form must never be the sole basis for a hiring, promotion, pay, performance or
> disciplinary decision. Before using it, consult your organisation's human

> colleagues.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you know what this
level is accountable for and where it sits in the data scientist ladder before
you score anything. Then gather whatever evidence exists ahead of the
assessment: a CV or portfolio, a structured interview, a work sample or task,
direct observation of the person's work, or references. Do not start scoring
from memory or general impression.

**Scoring on evidence, not impression.** Every rating in the competency matrix
must trace to something the person did, said, produced, or was observed doing.
If you cannot point to evidence for a rating, record it as unknown rather than
guess — a guessed rating is worse than a gap, because it looks like knowledge
the assessor does not have.

**Consistency.** Score every candidate for this role level the same way, using
the same evidence types where possible. Where the decision matters — a hire or
a promotion — have more than one assessor score independently before comparing
notes and reconciling any difference.

**Fairness.** An unvalidated, AI-drafted instrument used inconsistently can
produce unlawful adverse impact even when nobody intends it. Consistent,
evidence-based administration by more than one assessor materially reduces
that risk. See [Fair and lawful use](../../../../uk-gdad-pcf-competency-assessments-by-assessor/spec/index.md#fair-and-lawful-use) considerations
built into this document — never let a rating be influenced by anything other
than demonstrated, job-related evidence.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same four-point scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 1 | Awareness | You can describe skill fundamentals and show basic knowledge of related tools |
| 2 | Working | You can apply the skill with some support and use appropriate tools |
| 3 | Practitioner | You can apply the skill independently, determine the best tools, and share your experience |
| 4 | Expert | You can lead organisational best practice and teach advanced techniques |

This is a **proficiency** scale, not a frequency scale: it rates how well the
person does the thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it.

**Worked example**, using Programming and build (data science):

- A rating of **1 — Awareness** looks like a candidate who can point to short
  scripts written under close guidance from a more senior data scientist, with
  no unsupervised testing or deployment experience — evidence might be a
  reference confirming they always paired on anything that reached production.
- A rating of **2 — Working** looks like a candidate who has written
  moderate-complexity programs on their own, but whose testing is inconsistent
  and who has deployed code only with someone else checking the release —
  evidence might be a work sample with partial test coverage, discussed openly
  in interview.
- A rating of **3 — Practitioner** looks like a candidate who can walk through a
  moderate to complex program they wrote unsupervised, explain how they tested
  it, and describe how they deployed it into a real environment without
  needing someone else to carry the risk — evidence might be a portfolio
  repository with a working test suite and a deployment history the candidate
  can talk through in detail.

## About this role level

A data scientist explores data, applies statistical and machine learning
techniques, and turns what is found into organisational insight. The framework
describes data science as a broad, fast-moving field spanning maths,
statistics, software engineering and communication, and expects a data
scientist to be inquisitive, to explore and visualise data, to make
recommendations that address complex problems and inform strategic and
operational decisions, to use data ethically, to be innovative and adaptable,
and to find patterns that become organisational insight.

This assessment covers **Data scientist**, level 3 of 6 in the Data scientist
ladder: Trainee data scientist → Associate data scientist → **Data scientist**
→ Principal data scientist → Lead data scientist → Head of data science. It is
the level at which a person is expected to work fully independently, rather
than under close supervision or with support to develop the basics — the
framework describes this level as someone who "will work independently and
have a good understanding of a range of topics, including data science
techniques, delivery methods and stages (such as minimal viable products),
tools and technologies."

At this role level, the framework says a person will:

- develop complex solutions using a range of data science techniques, whilst
  understanding any ethical considerations
- understand the role and benefits of data science within the organisation
- support capability building within the organisation
- collaborate with others to develop data science solutions and outputs
  supporting the organisation
- prepare and manipulate data, and perform complex analytics
- present and communicate effectively

The framework names eight skills at this level: Applied maths, statistics and
scientific practices; Data engineering; Data ethics and privacy; Data science
innovation; Delivering business impact; Developing data science capability;
Programming and build (data science); and Understanding product delivery.
Each is assessed in the competency matrix below.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Applied maths, statistics and scientific practices

**What the framework expects at this level**

At this level, a data scientist applies designated quantitative techniques —
such as time series analysis, optimisation and simulation — to create and
embed appropriate models for analysis and prediction. They provide guidance on
matching data sources to relevant applied mathematics and statistical
techniques to meet analysis goals, apply appropriate statistical techniques to
discover new relations in available data and offer insight into research
problems that helps improve organisational processes and decision making, and
they access and use the statistical tools available within the organisation.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Practitioner. The framework expects this level to create and embed
models independently and to guide others on matching techniques to data —
guidance-giving and independent model-building are advanced, not intermediate,
behaviours.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 1 — Awareness: Can name relevant techniques with supervision but needs
  step-by-step guidance to select and apply one to a real dataset.
- 2 — Working: Can apply a designated technique to a dataset when the
  technique has already been chosen for them, but struggles to justify why
  that technique fits the problem over an alternative.
- 3 — Practitioner: Independently selects and applies techniques such as time
  series analysis, optimisation or simulation to build a working model, and
  can explain the trade-offs of the choice made.
- 4 — Expert: Additionally advises others on matching data sources to the
  right technique, and has used the organisation's statistical tooling to
  uncover a relationship in the data that changed a decision or a process.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to walk through a model or analysis they built end to end:
  which technique they chose, why, and what alternative they rejected.
- A work sample: a notebook, script or report showing a statistical or
  mathematical technique applied to real or realistic data.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate has advised colleagues on which
  statistical approach fitted a problem.

**Rate this skill**

- [ ] 1 — Awareness
- [ ] 2 — Working
- [ ] 3 — Practitioner
- [ ] 4 — Expert

### Skill: Data engineering

**What the framework expects at this level**

At this level, a data scientist works with data engineers and other data
scientists to design and provide products for the organisation.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Working. The framework's own wording for this skill at this
level is collaborative — working with data engineers, not leading or designing
the pipeline alone — which sits below the independent, guidance-giving
baseline set for the more central data science skills.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 1 — Awareness: Can describe, in general terms, what a data engineer does, but
  has not contributed directly to designing a data product with one.
- 2 — Working: Has worked alongside data engineers and other data
  scientists on a real product, contributing requirements or feedback on data
  structure, format, or quality.
- 3 — Practitioner: Has actively shaped the design of a data product in
  collaboration with data engineers, for example by specifying what a
  pipeline needs to deliver for an analysis to work.
- 4 — Expert: Is sought out by data engineers for input on product design
  because their collaboration has repeatedly improved what gets delivered.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a data product they helped design with a data
  engineer, and what their specific contribution was.
- Ask a referee who has worked as a data engineer alongside the candidate how
  the collaboration worked.
- A structured interview question: "Tell me about a time a data pipeline did
  not give you what you needed. What did you do?"

**Rate this skill**

- [ ] 1 — Awareness
- [ ] 2 — Working
- [ ] 3 — Practitioner
- [ ] 4 — Expert

### Skill: Data ethics and privacy

**What the framework expects at this level**

At this level, a data scientist works with stakeholders to identify and
address ethical and privacy concerns, demonstrates and communicates how data
ethics issues fit into the wider organisational context, researches
developments in data ethics and privacy to improve compliance and processes,
and assesses and constructively challenges proposed data ethics policies.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Practitioner. The framework has this level actively challenging
proposed policy and researching developments to improve organisational
practice, not merely following rules set by others — that is independent,
judgement-based practice.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 1 — Awareness: Recognises an ethical or privacy concern when it is pointed
  out, but does not raise one unprompted.
- 2 — Working: Raises ethical or privacy concerns with stakeholders on
  their own work, but does not yet research wider developments or comment on
  organisational policy.
- 3 — Practitioner: Works with stakeholders to identify and address ethical and
  privacy concerns across a piece of work, and can explain how a specific
  issue connects to the organisation's wider context.
- 4 — Expert: Additionally researches developments in data ethics and privacy
  to improve the organisation's processes, and has constructively challenged a
  proposed data ethics policy with a reasoned alternative.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a real piece of work where they identified an
  ethical or privacy issue and what they did about it.
- A scenario to observe: present a short data science brief with an embedded
  ethical issue (for example, a proxy variable that could reveal a sensitive
  attribute) and see whether the candidate identifies and addresses it
  unprompted.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate has commented on or challenged a data
  ethics policy or process.

**Rate this skill**

- [ ] 1 — Awareness
- [ ] 2 — Working
- [ ] 3 — Practitioner
- [ ] 4 — Expert

### Skill: Data science innovation

**What the framework expects at this level**

At this level, a data scientist demonstrates practical knowledge of data
science tools and techniques, develops data science solutions that maximise
insight, and identifies opportunities for how data science can improve data
practices.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Practitioner. Developing solutions that maximise insight, and
proactively identifying opportunities to improve practice, are independent
contributions expected of a fully capable practitioner at this level.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 1 — Awareness: Has practical knowledge of a small set of standard data
  science tools, applied under guidance.
- 2 — Working: Applies a range of tools and techniques competently to
  build a solution, but rarely proposes a new way of working.
- 3 — Practitioner: Develops data science solutions that go beyond the immediate
  request to maximise the insight delivered, and has identified at least one
  real opportunity for data science to improve how the organisation works
  with data.
- 4 — Expert: Is known within the organisation as a source of new ideas for
  applying data science, and has taken an identified opportunity through to a
  working improvement in practice.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a time they went beyond the original brief to
  extract more insight from a dataset, and what the extra insight changed.
- A work sample or portfolio piece showing an innovative approach to a
  problem, with the candidate able to explain why it was chosen over a more
  conventional one.
- A structured interview question: "Describe an opportunity you spotted for
  data science to improve how your organisation used data, and what you did
  about it."

**Rate this skill**

- [ ] 1 — Awareness
- [ ] 2 — Working
- [ ] 3 — Practitioner
- [ ] 4 — Expert

### Skill: Delivering business impact

**What the framework expects at this level**

At this level, a data scientist shows an understanding of the organisation
and the benefits of data science, collaborates to help identify user needs
and develop and deliver data science products, and communicates effectively,
presenting analysis and visualisations tailored to their audience.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Practitioner. This level is expected to deliver products end to end
and tailor communication to different audiences unsupervised, which is
independent practice rather than intermediate, guided work.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 1 — Awareness: Can explain, when asked, how data science generally benefits
  the organisation, but has not identified user needs or delivered a product
  themselves.
- 2 — Working: Has contributed to identifying user needs and delivering a
  data science product as part of a team, with support shaping the final
  output.
- 3 — Practitioner: Independently collaborates with stakeholders to identify user
  needs, helps deliver the resulting product, and tailors a presentation of
  analysis or visualisations to suit a specific, named audience.
- 4 — Expert: Has repeatedly delivered data science products that
  demonstrably changed a business decision, and is sought out to present
  findings to senior or non-technical audiences because of how clearly they
  communicate.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to present a piece of analysis as if to a non-technical
  stakeholder, and observe how they adapt language and visuals.
- A work sample: a report, dashboard, or slide deck the candidate produced for
  a real audience, discussed for how it was tailored.
- Ask a referee for an example of a data science product the candidate
  delivered that changed a decision.

**Rate this skill**

- [ ] 1 — Awareness
- [ ] 2 — Working
- [ ] 3 — Practitioner

### Skill: Developing data science capability

**What the framework expects at this level**

At this level, a data scientist manages their own continuous professional
development and links their learning to objectives and organisational goals,
supports data science capability building across the team and wider
organisation, confidently talks about the benefits of data science approaches
to existing and potential customers, and demonstrates a good understanding of
a range of data science techniques — such as machine learning and natural
language processing — using them to build data science solutions, including
reports, models, and dashboards.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Working. The framework's own wording says this level
"supports" capability building rather than leads or owns it, which the
framework reserves for more senior levels in this ladder — that places the
baseline for the capability-building half of this skill at intermediate, even
though the technique-building half sits higher.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 1 — Awareness: Has a development plan but cannot yet build a data science
  solution — a report, model or dashboard — using a technique like machine
  learning or natural language processing without close support.
- 2 — Working: Manages their own CPD with a clear link to organisational
  goals, has helped a colleague with a specific data science technique when
  asked, and can build solutions using a range of techniques with occasional
  guidance.
- 3 — Practitioner: Actively supports capability building across the team — for
  example by running a short internal session or pairing regularly with less
  experienced colleagues — and builds data science solutions independently
  using a range of techniques.
- 4 — Expert: Is a recognised source of capability-building support across the
  wider organisation, and confidently and persuasively explains the benefits
  of data science approaches to internal or external customers.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe their current development plan and how it
  connects to their organisation's goals.
- Ask for an example of a report, model or dashboard they built using machine
  learning or natural language processing, and what problem it solved.
- Ask a referee whether the candidate has supported a colleague's or a team's
  data science capability, and how.

**Rate this skill**

- [ ] 1 — Awareness
- [ ] 2 — Working
- [ ] 3 — Practitioner
- [ ] 4 — Expert

### Skill: Programming and build (data science)

**What the framework expects at this level**

At this level, a data scientist writes moderate to complex programs and
scripts, and shows a good understanding of testing methodologies and how to
deploy code.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Practitioner. Writing moderate to complex programs unsupervised,
with a good understanding of testing and deployment, is independent
practitioner-level work rather than guided, intermediate work.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 1 — Awareness: Writes short, simple scripts under close supervision, with
  little or no independent testing.
- 2 — Working: Writes moderately complex programs with some guidance, and
  applies basic tests but relies on someone else to review before deployment.
- 3 — Practitioner: Independently writes moderate to complex programs and scripts,
  applies an appropriate testing approach, and understands how their code
  gets deployed into a working environment.
- 4 — Expert: Additionally reviews or improves others' code and testing
  practice, and has made a deployment decision or process better for the
  wider team.

**Evidence to gather**

- A work sample or take-home task: a moderately complex script or program,
  reviewed for structure, testing and readiness to deploy.
- Ask the candidate to walk through how they tested and deployed a piece of
  code they are proud of.
- A structured interview question: "Tell me about a bug that only showed up
  after deployment. How did you find it, and what did you change about how
  you test or deploy after that?"

**Rate this skill**

- [ ] 1 — Awareness
- [ ] 2 — Working
- [ ] 3 — Practitioner
- [ ] 4 — Expert

### Skill: Understanding product delivery

**What the framework expects at this level**

At this level, a data scientist understands the differences between delivery
methods, such as Agile and waterfall, and can choose the most appropriate
method to deliver each product, defines the minimum viable product (MVP) and
supports decisions about priorities, and works with specialists in
multidisciplinary teams to smoothly deliver data science products into the
organisation.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Working. The framework has this level choosing a delivery
method and supporting prioritisation decisions, which is solid working
knowledge applied with some organisational context, but stops short of owning
delivery strategy or leading multidisciplinary teams — that fuller ownership
sits at the levels above.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 1 — Awareness: Can describe delivery methods and the concept of an MVP in
  general terms, but has not chosen a method or defined an MVP for real work.
- 2 — Working: Has chosen an appropriate delivery method for a piece of
  data science work, defined an MVP for it, and worked with other specialists
  in a multidisciplinary team to deliver it.
- 3 — Practitioner: Regularly advises others on which delivery method fits a
  product, and has influenced prioritisation decisions with a clear
  rationale grounded in delivering value early.
- 4 — Expert: Is trusted to set delivery approach across multiple data science
  products, and has resolved a real disagreement about scope or priority
  between specialists in a multidisciplinary team.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask the candidate to describe a product where they chose between Agile and
  waterfall, or a similar choice, and why.
- Ask for an example of an MVP they defined, and what was deliberately left
  out of it.
- Ask a referee how the candidate worked with other specialists — data
  engineers, user researchers, product managers — to deliver a data science
  product.

**Rate this skill**

- [ ] 1 — Awareness
- [ ] 2 — Working
- [ ] 3 — Practitioner
- [ ] 4 — Expert
