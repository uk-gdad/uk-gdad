# Head of Performance Analysis - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This is for you: a chance to rate your own current strengths against the
baseline the UK GDAD Profession Capability Framework sets for this role
level, using the framework's own words for what it covers.

A performance analyst conducts analysis. In this role, you will develop performance measurement frameworks - Key Performance Indicators (KPIs), goals, user needs and benefits - and analyse the performance of a service or product against these, adapting your approach and framework appropriately and in line with any changes. The head of performance analysis leads the performance analysis community, acting as the point of contact between the community and the business.

Work through the competency matrix below, one skill at a time, then fill in
the overall determination at the end. Treat this as a mirror, not a test —
there's no assessor watching, so being honest with yourself only costs you a
slightly less flattering picture today in exchange for a far more useful one.
Read `## Advisory` before you begin.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating you
give yourself here must never be treated as equivalent to a formal
assessment for a hiring, promotion, pay or performance decision. Use it for
your own reflection, and talk to your manager, a mentor, or your
organisation's people development team about what you find.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you're rating
yourself against what this level actually expects rather than the level
above or below it. Then think back over recent, real examples of your work —
not how you'd like to have handled something, but what you actually did.

**Rate on evidence, not impression.** For every skill, try to recall a
specific moment that supports the rating you give yourself. If you can't
think of one, that's useful information too — it might mean the rating
should be lower, or just that you haven't had the chance to show this skill
yet, which is worth noting separately.

**Be consistent with yourself.** Try to apply the same standard across every
skill, rather than being generous where you feel confident and harsh where
you feel unsure. Coming back to this after a few days, with fresh examples in
mind, often sharpens a rating more than sitting with it in one go.

**Be kind, and be honest.** This only works if you can look at the result
without flinching. A low rating here is not a verdict on you — it's a
starting point. Everyone doing this role, at every level, has skills they are
still building.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point proficiency scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

This rates how well someone does the thing, on the evidence available — not how often
they do it. A worked example, using this level's skill of Performance measurement:

- **3 — Advanced**: they led the development of a KPI framework for a single, complex
  programme unsupported.
- **4 — Expert**: they oversee the development and consistency of performance
  measurement frameworks across every project in the organisation, and personally check
  each is aligned to business needs and strategy.

A head of performance analysis's baseline for this skill, below, is 4.

## About this role level

**The role: performance analyst.** A performance analyst conducts analysis. In this
role, you develop performance measurement frameworks — key performance indicators
(KPIs), goals, user needs and benefits — and analyse the performance of a service or
product against these, adapting your approach and framework appropriately and in line
with any changes.

**The level: head of performance analysis.** The framework describes this as the most
senior level of the role — leading the performance analysis community and acting as the
point of contact between the community and the business. It is the fifth of five levels
in this role: associate performance analyst, performance analyst, senior performance
analyst, lead performance analyst, and head of performance analysis. At this level, you
remove blockers and act as an escalation point for important issues; set the
performance analysis strategy with input from lead and senior performance analysts; are
responsible for defining a framework for quality assurance within performance analysis;
champion performance analysis and performance analysts, working with colleagues across
government and other Government Digital and Data communities and analytical
professions; and manage the organisation's performance analysis capability through
business planning, development and recruitment.

**The skills this level names**, in the summary's order: Analysis and insight;
Communicating analysis and insight; Performance measurement; Quality assurance of data
and analysis; Technical understanding (performance analyst); Understanding analysis
across the product life cycle; and User-centred analysis. Six of these — Communicating
analysis and insight, Performance measurement, Quality assurance of data and analysis,
Technical understanding (performance analyst), Understanding analysis across the
product life cycle, and User-centred analysis — carry text identical to the lead
performance analyst level below. Analysis and insight is the exception: its published
wording at this level matches the mid-career levels' "help teams apply a range of
techniques" framing rather than the lead level's "teach and supervise" language — noted
in that skill's own block below, and quoted as published rather than smoothed into a
tidy progression.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Analysis and insight

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a head of performance analysis to understand and help teams to
apply a range of techniques to analyse data and provide insight, be proactive and
present compelling findings that inform wider decisions, and apply innovative
approaches to resolve problems. This is the same wording used at the performance
analyst and senior performance analyst levels — it does not carry forward the lead
performance analyst level's "teach and supervise" language, even though this is the more
senior level. Quoted as published; treat it as a genuine anomaly rather than a sign the
role's expectations fell.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. The published wording for this one skill is textually less
demanding than the lead level's, so score strictly against what it actually says — but
weigh the level's overall seniority and its accountability to set strategy for the whole
community when judging whether a lower demonstrated score here is a genuine concern or
simply reflects the text's own limits. Most assessors will still expect Expert-level
practice from someone leading the community, even though this skill's own bullets would,
read alone, support a lower figure; note your reasoning either way.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not applied more than one technique for analysis and insight
  independently.
- 1 — Beginner: has applied a single technique with close direction.
- 2 — Intermediate: has applied a range of techniques with some guidance and presented
  a finding a colleague acted on.
- 3 — Advanced: independently applies a range of techniques, proactively presents
  compelling findings that inform a wider decision, and has applied an innovative
  approach to resolve a problem.
- 4 — Expert: is recognised across the organisation for applying and teaching a wide
  range of analysis techniques, and has helped the organisation learn a strategic
  insight that changed a programme's direction.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Tell me about a strategic insight your analysis
  work helped the organisation learn."
- Ask for an example of teaching or supervising others' use of an analysis technique,
  even though the framework's own text for this level does not name it explicitly.
- Ask a referee about your continued hands-on involvement in analysis and
  insight at this seniority.

### Skill: Communicating analysis and insight

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a head of performance analysis to communicate relevant, compelling
stories using the most appropriate medium, and to make complex recommendations at both
a tactical and a strategic level. This wording is identical to the lead performance
analyst level.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Choosing the medium and framing recommendations at the highest
altitude, for the most senior audiences the organisation has, is the essence of Expert.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not made a strategic recommendation from performance evidence.
- 1 — Beginner: has drafted part of a strategic communication with close direction.
- 2 — Intermediate: communicates tactical recommendations independently but needs
  support framing a strategic one.
- 3 — Advanced: independently communicates compelling stories and gives both tactical
  and strategic recommendations for a single area.
- 4 — Expert: is trusted to represent the whole performance analysis community's
  findings and recommendations to the organisation's and government's most senior
  stakeholders, and coaches others across the community to do the same.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of representing performance analysis findings to the
  organisation's most senior leadership or across government.
- A structured interview question: "Tell me about a time you had to advocate for
  performance analysis itself, not just present one project's findings."
- Ask a referee whether you have coached others across the community on
  communicating strategically.

### Skill: Performance measurement

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a head of performance analysis to oversee the development and
consistency of performance measurement frameworks across multiple projects, and to
ensure that these frameworks are aligned to business needs and strategy. This wording is
identical to the lead performance analyst level.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Overseeing consistency across multiple projects organisation-wide
is a mastery-level responsibility.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not overseen consistency across more than one measurement framework.
- 1 — Beginner: has contributed to one framework's development.
- 2 — Intermediate: has led one framework's development, with some support.
- 3 — Advanced: independently leads the development of a single framework and aligns
  it to business needs and strategy.
- 4 — Expert: oversees the development and consistency of performance measurement
  frameworks across every project in the organisation, ensuring each is aligned to
  business needs and strategy.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Tell me about ensuring consistency between
  performance measurement frameworks used across the organisation."
- Ask for an example of aligning a framework to organisational strategy, not just one
  project's goals.
- Ask a referee about your oversight role across all the organisation's
  frameworks.

### Skill: Quality assurance of data and analysis

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a head of performance analysis to set up tools and systems for
effectively assuring data and analysis across multiple teams, use appropriate
approaches for verifying and validating data and analysis, influence senior
stakeholders over data and analysis approaches, and coach and mentor others in assuring
data and analysis. This wording is identical to the lead performance analyst level, and
matches this level's own accountability to define the framework for quality assurance
within performance analysis.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Defining the organisation's quality assurance framework and
coaching and mentoring others is squarely the "teach or guide others" definition of
Expert.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not set up an assurance tool or system used by more than one team.
- 1 — Beginner: has followed an assurance system someone else set up.
- 2 — Intermediate: has contributed to an assurance system across teams with guidance.
- 3 — Advanced: independently verifies and validates data and analysis for their own
  team.
- 4 — Expert: defines the quality assurance framework the whole performance analysis
  community works to, has influenced senior stakeholders on data and analysis
  approaches, and coaches and mentors analysts at every level in assurance practice.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Tell me about the quality assurance framework you
  defined for performance analysis across the organisation."
- Ask for an example of influencing a senior stakeholder on a data or analysis
  approach.
- Ask a referee whether your assurance framework is used across the
  community.

### Skill: Technical understanding (performance analyst)

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a head of performance analysis to identify opportunities to use new
digital technologies to enhance analytical capability in alignment with business goals.
This wording is identical to the lead performance analyst level.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Identifying new-technology opportunities and aligning them to
business goals, at organisational scale, is forward-looking, expert-level judgement.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not identified an opportunity to adopt a new digital technology for
  analysis.
- 1 — Beginner: is aware of new digital technologies but has not proposed adopting one.
- 2 — Intermediate: has helped assess a proposed new technology with guidance.
- 3 — Advanced: independently identifies a new technology opportunity and proposes it
  for their own team.
- 4 — Expert: identifies and drives adoption of new digital technologies across the
  organisation's analytical capability, aligned to business goals, through business
  planning and investment decisions.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Tell me about a new digital technology or tool you
  identified and drove adoption of across the organisation, not just one team."
- Ask how you keep aware of emerging analytical technologies and assess you for
  organisational investment.
- Ask a referee about a technology adoption you drove at organisational

### Skill: Understanding analysis across the product life cycle

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a head of performance analysis to develop sustainable support
models, identify and deal with potential risks across or between all stages of the
product life cycle, coach others, contribute to the assessment of other teams
(providing guidance and support as they move through the product life cycle phases),
and lead and champion best practice while identifying improvements to current
approaches. This wording is identical to the lead performance analyst level.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Leading and championing best practice organisation-wide, and
coaching others through life cycle phases, is teach-and-guide-others work at the
broadest reach this skill covers.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not led an improvement to how the organisation approaches the product
  life cycle.
- 1 — Beginner: has flagged a life cycle risk to someone more senior.
- 2 — Intermediate: has raised a risk and suggested an improvement for one team, with
  support.
- 3 — Advanced: independently identifies and deals with risks and develops a support
  model for their own team.
- 4 — Expert: develops sustainable support models used across the organisation, deals
  with risks across all life cycle stages, coaches others, assesses other teams'
  progress through the life cycle, and leads best practice organisation-wide.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Tell me about a sustainable support model you
  developed that the whole organisation, not just one team, now uses."
- Ask for an example of assessing another team's progress through the product life
  cycle and providing guidance.
- Ask a referee about a best-practice improvement you led at organisational
  scale.

### Skill: User-centred analysis

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a head of performance analysis to give direction on which tools or
methods to use, bring insight and expertise to how user needs have changed over time
and ensure that they're met, apply strategic thinking to the problem of how to provide
the best service for the end user, and consider the political strategy and
implications of any analyses and insights. This wording is identical to the lead
performance analyst level.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Giving direction across the community, applying strategic
thinking, and weighing political implications is mastery-level, judgement-heavy work.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not given direction to others on user-centred analysis tools or
  methods.
- 1 — Beginner: has used a tool or method someone else directed.
- 2 — Intermediate: has recommended a tool or method to one colleague, with support.
- 3 — Advanced: independently applies strategic thinking to how a service best serves
  the end user.
- 4 — Expert: gives direction across the community on tools and methods, tracks how
  user needs have changed over time organisation-wide, and weighs political strategy
  and implications when advising the business on analysis and insight.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Tell me about a time you had to weigh the
  political implications of a piece of analysis alongside the technical answer, at
  organisational level."
- Ask for an example of giving direction across the community on which tools or methods
  to use.
- Ask a referee about your role tracking how user needs have changed over
  time across the organisation.
