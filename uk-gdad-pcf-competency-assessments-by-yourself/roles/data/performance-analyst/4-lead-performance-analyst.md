# Lead Performance Analyst - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This is for you: a chance to rate your own current strengths against the
baseline the UK GDAD Profession Capability Framework sets for this role
level, using the framework's own words for what it covers.

A performance analyst conducts analysis. In this role, you will develop performance measurement frameworks - Key Performance Indicators (KPIs), goals, user needs and benefits - and analyse the performance of a service or product against these, adapting your approach and framework appropriately and in line with any changes. A lead performance analyst manages junior performance analysts at any level, co-ordinating teams or groups of related analysts.

Work through the competency matrix below, one skill at a time, then fill in
the overall determination at the end. Treat this as a mirror, not a test —
there's no assessor watching, so being honest with yourself only costs you a
slightly less flattering picture today in exchange for a far more useful one.
Read `## Advisory` before you begin.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating you
give yourself here must never be treated as equivalent to a formal
assessment for a hiring, promotion, pay or performance decision. Use it for
your own reflection, and talk to your manager, a mentor, or your
organisation's people development team about what you find.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you're rating
yourself against what this level actually expects rather than the level
above or below it. Then think back over recent, real examples of your work —
not how you'd like to have handled something, but what you actually did.

**Rate on evidence, not impression.** For every skill, try to recall a
specific moment that supports the rating you give yourself. If you can't
think of one, that's useful information too — it might mean the rating
should be lower, or just that you haven't had the chance to show this skill
yet, which is worth noting separately.

**Be consistent with yourself.** Try to apply the same standard across every
skill, rather than being generous where you feel confident and harsh where
you feel unsure. Coming back to this after a few days, with fresh examples in
mind, often sharpens a rating more than sitting with it in one go.

**Be kind, and be honest.** This only works if you can look at the result
without flinching. A low rating here is not a verdict on you — it's a
starting point. Everyone doing this role, at every level, has skills they are
still building.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point proficiency scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

This rates how well someone does the thing, on the evidence available — not how often
they do it. A worked example, using this level's skill of Analysis and insight:

- **3 — Advanced**: they independently applied a range of techniques to a complex
  problem and presented a compelling finding that changed a decision.
- **4 — Expert**: they teach and supervise a wide range of analysis techniques across
  their team, and have helped the organisation learn a strategic insight from a piece of
  analysis that changed how it approached a whole programme.

A lead performance analyst's baseline for this skill, below, is 4.

## About this role level

**The role: performance analyst.** A performance analyst conducts analysis. In this
role, you develop performance measurement frameworks — key performance indicators
(KPIs), goals, user needs and benefits — and analyse the performance of a service or
product against these, adapting your approach and framework appropriately and in line
with any changes.

**The level: lead performance analyst.** The framework describes this level as
managing junior performance analysts at any level, co-ordinating teams or groups of
related analysts. It is the fourth of five levels in this role: associate performance
analyst, performance analyst, senior performance analyst, lead performance analyst, and
head of performance analysis. At this level, you assist, take forward and influence the
strategy determined by the head of performance analysis, including data implementation
and data architecture; remove blockers and act as an escalation point for important
issues; are an ambassador for analytics across communities, departments and government;
are responsible for the accuracy and quality of data and analysis, and of how data and
analysis are used; proactively share knowledge and promote a data-driven culture, as a
leader in the community; and coach and mentor senior performance analysts, performance
analysts and associate performance analysts.

**The skills this level names**, in the summary's order: Analysis and insight;
Communicating analysis and insight; Performance measurement; Quality assurance of data
and analysis; Technical understanding (performance analyst); Understanding analysis
across the product life cycle; and User-centred analysis. Five of these seven skill
blocks — Communicating analysis and insight, Performance measurement, Quality
assurance of data and analysis, Technical understanding (performance analyst), and
Understanding analysis across the product life cycle, plus User-centred analysis —
carry text identical to the head of performance analysis level directly above; what
genuinely distinguishes this level from head is the wording of Analysis and insight,
and the scope set out in each level's own accountability bullets.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Analysis and insight

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a lead performance analyst to understand, teach and supervise a
wide range of analysis techniques, help organisations create and learn from strategic
insights, and apply innovative approaches to resolve problems. This is different, more
senior wording than the levels below, which asked for helping a team apply techniques
rather than teaching and supervising them, and different again from the head of
performance analysis level above, whose own text for this skill reverts to the
mid-level wording rather than continuing this level's "teach and supervise" language —
quoted as published in each case, without smoothing the anomaly away.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. "Teach and supervise" is explicitly the teach-and-guide-others
definition of Expert, and "help organisations... learn from strategic insights" moves
beyond a single project's decisions.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not taught or supervised another analyst's use of a technique.
- 1 — Beginner: has demonstrated a technique to a colleague without supervising their
  use of it.
- 2 — Intermediate: has taught one technique to one colleague, with support.
- 3 — Advanced: independently teaches a range of analysis techniques and has helped
  produce a strategic, not just tactical, insight.
- 4 — Expert: teaches and supervises a wide range of analysis techniques across a team
  or community, and has helped the organisation learn a strategic insight from analysis
  that changed a programme's direction.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Tell me about teaching or supervising a colleague's
  use of an analysis technique. How did you know you'd learned it?"
- Ask for a strategic insight, not a tactical one, that your analysis helped the
  organisation learn.
- Ask a referee about your role teaching or supervising other analysts.

### Skill: Communicating analysis and insight

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a lead performance analyst to communicate relevant, compelling
stories using the most appropriate medium, and to make complex recommendations at both
a tactical and a strategic level. This wording is identical to the head of performance
analysis level.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Choosing the medium and framing recommendations at the highest
altitude, for the most senior audiences, is the essence of Expert.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not made a strategic recommendation from performance evidence.
- 1 — Beginner: has drafted part of a strategic communication with close direction.
- 2 — Intermediate: communicates tactical recommendations independently but needs
  support framing a strategic one.
- 3 — Advanced: independently communicates compelling stories and gives both tactical
  and strategic recommendations for their own area.
- 4 — Expert: is trusted to represent the performance analysis community's findings and
  recommendations to the organisation's most senior stakeholders, and coaches others to
  do the same.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of representing performance analysis findings to the
  organisation's most senior leadership.
- A structured interview question: "Tell me about a time you had to advocate for
  performance analysis itself, not just present one project's findings."
- Ask a referee whether you have coached others across the community on
  communicating strategically.

### Skill: Performance measurement

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a lead performance analyst to oversee the development and
consistency of performance measurement frameworks across multiple projects, and to
ensure that these frameworks are aligned to business needs and strategy. This wording is
identical to the head of performance analysis level.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Overseeing consistency across multiple projects, rather than
leading the development of one framework, is a mastery-level, organisation-wide
responsibility.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not overseen consistency across more than one measurement framework.
- 1 — Beginner: has contributed to one framework's development.
- 2 — Intermediate: has led one framework's development, with some support.
- 3 — Advanced: independently leads the development of a single framework and aligns
  it to business needs and strategy.
- 4 — Expert: oversees the development and consistency of performance measurement
  frameworks across multiple projects, ensuring each is aligned to business needs and
  strategy.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Tell me about ensuring consistency between
  performance measurement frameworks used on different projects."
- Ask for an example of aligning a framework to a stated business strategy, not just a
  project's own goals.
- Ask a referee about your oversight role across multiple frameworks.

### Skill: Quality assurance of data and analysis

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a lead performance analyst to set up tools and systems for
effectively assuring data and analysis across multiple teams, use appropriate
approaches for verifying and validating data and analysis, influence senior
stakeholders over data and analysis approaches, and coach and mentor others in assuring
data and analysis. This wording is identical to the head of performance analysis level.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Setting up assurance tools and systems across multiple teams, and
coaching and mentoring others in the practice, is squarely the "teach or guide others"
definition of Expert.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not set up an assurance tool or system used by more than one team.
- 1 — Beginner: has followed an assurance system someone else set up.
- 2 — Intermediate: has contributed to an assurance system across teams with guidance.
- 3 — Advanced: independently verifies and validates data and analysis for their own
  team.
- 4 — Expert: sets up tools and systems that assure data and analysis across multiple
  teams, has influenced a senior stakeholder to change an assurance approach, and
  coaches and mentors other analysts in assurance practice.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Tell me about a quality assurance system or tool
  you set up that more than one team used."
- Ask for an example of influencing a senior stakeholder to change a data or analysis
  approach.
- Ask a referee whether you have coached or mentored other analysts on
  assurance.

### Skill: Technical understanding (performance analyst)

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a lead performance analyst to identify opportunities to use new
digital technologies to enhance analytical capability in alignment with business goals.
This wording is identical to the head of performance analysis level.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Identifying new-technology opportunities and aligning them to
business goals, rather than applying known concepts, is forward-looking, expert-level
judgement.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not identified an opportunity to adopt a new digital technology for
  analysis.
- 1 — Beginner: is aware of new digital technologies but has not proposed adopting one.
- 2 — Intermediate: has helped assess a proposed new technology with guidance.
- 3 — Advanced: independently identifies a new technology opportunity and proposes it.
- 4 — Expert: identifies and drives adoption of new digital technologies that
  materially enhance analytical capability, aligned to business goals.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Tell me about a new digital technology or tool you
  identified and helped the team adopt. What business goal did it serve?"
- Ask how you keep aware of emerging analytical technologies.
- Ask a referee about a technology adoption you drove.

### Skill: Understanding analysis across the product life cycle

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a lead performance analyst to develop sustainable support models,
identify and deal with potential risks across or between all stages of the product life
cycle, coach others, contribute to the assessment of other teams (providing guidance and
support as they move through the product life cycle phases), and lead and champion best
practice while identifying improvements to current approaches. This wording is identical
to the head of performance analysis level.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Coaching others, assessing other teams, and leading and
championing best practice organisation-wide is teach-and-guide-others work at the
broadest reach this skill covers.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not led an improvement to how the organisation approaches the product
  life cycle.
- 1 — Beginner: has flagged a life cycle risk to someone more senior.
- 2 — Intermediate: has raised a risk and suggested an improvement for one team, with
  support.
- 3 — Advanced: independently identifies and deals with risks and develops a support
  model for their own team.
- 4 — Expert: develops sustainable support models used more broadly, deals with risks
  across all life cycle stages, coaches others, contributes to assessing other teams'
  progress through the life cycle, and leads best practice across the organisation.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Tell me about a sustainable support model you
  developed that other teams then used."
- Ask for an example of contributing to the assessment of another team's progress
  through the product life cycle.
- Ask a referee about a best-practice improvement you led.

### Skill: User-centred analysis

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework asks a lead performance analyst to give direction on which tools or
methods to use, bring insight and expertise to how user needs have changed over time
and ensure that they're met, apply strategic thinking to the problem of how to provide
the best service for the end user, and consider the political strategy and
implications of any analyses and insights. This wording is identical to the head of
performance analysis level.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 4 — Expert. Giving direction to others on tools and methods, applying
strategic thinking, and weighing political implications is mastery-level, judgement-
heavy work.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: has not given direction to others on user-centred analysis tools or
  methods.
- 1 — Beginner: has used a tool or method someone else directed.
- 2 — Intermediate: has recommended a tool or method to one colleague, with support.
- 3 — Advanced: independently applies strategic thinking to how a service best serves
  the end user.
- 4 — Expert: gives direction across a team or community on tools and methods, tracks
  how user needs have changed over time, and weighs political strategy and implications
  when advising on analysis and insight.

**Evidence to gather**

- A structured interview question: "Tell me about a time you had to weigh the
  political implications of a piece of analysis alongside the technical answer."
- Ask for an example of giving direction to others on which tools or methods to use.
- Ask a referee about your role tracking how user needs have changed over
  time.
