# Senior Data Analyst - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This is for you: a chance to rate your own current strengths against the
baseline the UK GDAD Profession Capability Framework sets for this role
level, using the framework's own words for what it covers.

A data analyst collects, manages, explores and shares data to support organisational objectives and deliver business impact. A senior data analyst leads data analysis in a team or across an area. They ensure data is appropriately prepared, managed and used, and that data analysis outcomes align with the organisation's goals.

Work through the competency matrix below, one skill at a time, then fill in
the overall determination at the end. Treat this as a mirror, not a test —
there's no assessor watching, so being honest with yourself only costs you a
slightly less flattering picture today in exchange for a far more useful one.
Read `## Advisory` before you begin.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating you
give yourself here must never be treated as equivalent to a formal
assessment for a hiring, promotion, pay or performance decision. Use it for
your own reflection, and talk to your manager, a mentor, or your
organisation's people development team about what you find.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you're rating
yourself against what this level actually expects rather than the level
above or below it. Then think back over recent, real examples of your work —
not how you'd like to have handled something, but what you actually did.

**Rate on evidence, not impression.** For every skill, try to recall a
specific moment that supports the rating you give yourself. If you can't
think of one, that's useful information too — it might mean the rating
should be lower, or just that you haven't had the chance to show this skill
yet, which is worth noting separately.

**Be consistent with yourself.** Try to apply the same standard across every
skill, rather than being generous where you feel confident and harsh where
you feel unsure. Coming back to this after a few days, with fresh examples in
mind, often sharpens a rating more than sitting with it in one go.

**Be kind, and be honest.** This only works if you can look at the result
without flinching. A low rating here is not a verdict on you — it's a
starting point. Everyone doing this role, at every level, has skills they are
still building.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix below is rated on the same five-point
scale. This is a **proficiency** scale — it rates how well someone does the
thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it or how they feel
about it.

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

**Worked example — Applying statistical and analytical tools and techniques.**
The framework expects a senior data analyst to "lead the development of
valuable statistical insights and reports" and to "quality assure statistical
techniques and analytical outputs across a team". Someone who applies
techniques well themselves but has never checked or improved someone else's
analytical work is showing **2 — Intermediate**, below this level's baseline.
Someone who has led the development of a genuinely useful set of insights
and who quality assures other analysts' outputs, catching errors before they
reach stakeholders, is showing **3 — Advanced** — the baseline this level
expects. Someone who has additionally built the team's whole approach to
quality assurance is showing evidence towards **4 — Expert**.

## About this role level

The Data profession's Data analyst role collects, manages, explores and shares
data to support organisational objectives and deliver business impact. Across
the role, a data analyst identifies, collects and moves data between a range
of systems; applies data analysis and data visualisation tools and
techniques; prepares, manages and models data, following appropriate
standards and practices; and communicates data, insights and conclusions in
appropriate formats.

The role has four levels, in order: Associate data analyst, Data analyst,
Senior data analyst, and Principal data analyst. This assessment is for the
third of them.

A **senior data analyst** leads data analysis in a team or across an area.
They ensure data is appropriately prepared, managed and used, and that data
analysis outcomes align with the organisation's goals. At this role level,
they will guide others in appropriate practices for preparing, managing and
using data, lead the application and improvement of processes and ways of
working with data, and lead communication of data and data projects with
stakeholders, adapting to their needs and requirements.

The framework names nine skills for this role level: Applying statistical
and analytical tools and techniques, Communicating between the technical and
non-technical, Data ethics and privacy, Data management, Data preparation and
linkage, Data visualisation, Delivering business impact through data,
Developing code for analysis, and Managing a data project. The framework
lists Delivering business impact through data and Managing a data project
twice each in the published summary, with identical wording each time; this
assessment treats each as the one skill it is.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Applying statistical and analytical tools and techniques

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior data analyst is expected to lead the development of valuable
statistical insights and reports, identify and apply a range of statistical
and analytical techniques and tools and support others in applying them,
quality assure statistical techniques and analytical outputs across a team,
and engage with stakeholders to share techniques and increase understanding
and trust.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Leading development, quality assuring a team's
outputs, and supporting others in applying technique describes strong,
independent practice that is also starting to shape others' work — a
significant step beyond applying technique under supervision.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot lead a piece of statistical analysis or judge whether
  someone else's analytical output is sound.
- 1 — Beginner: Applies a technique correctly themselves but cannot yet
  quality assure another's work.
- 2 — Intermediate: Quality assures straightforward analytical outputs with
  support.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently leads the development of valuable insights and
  reports, applies a range of techniques and supports others in applying
  them, quality assures outputs across a team, and builds stakeholder trust
  in the technique used.
- 4 — Expert: Develops and guides others in a wide range of statistical and
  analytical techniques, and sets the team's quality assurance approach.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe a time you quality assured another analyst's
  work, what you found, and how you handled it.
- Ask for an example of a statistical or analytical insight you led the
  development of, from question to finished report.
- Ask how you build stakeholder trust in an analytical technique that is
  unfamiliar to you.

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior data analyst is expected to listen to and interpret the needs of
technical and non-technical stakeholders and manage their expectations,
manage active and reactive communication, and support or host difficult
discussions within the team or with diverse senior stakeholders.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Managing expectations and hosting discussions with
diverse senior stakeholders describes confident, independent practice at a
wider scope than the earlier levels' team-level communication.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot interpret conflicting stakeholder needs or manage
  expectations proactively.
- 1 — Beginner: Communicates stakeholder needs upward but does not yet manage
  expectations independently.
- 2 — Intermediate: Manages routine stakeholder expectations but escalates
  discussions involving senior or diverse stakeholders.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently interprets and manages the needs of technical
  and non-technical stakeholders, including senior ones, and hosts difficult
  discussions without escalating.
- 4 — Expert: Mediates across the organisation and represents the community
  to large audiences.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe a time you managed the expectations of a
  senior stakeholder whose request was not realistic.
- Ask about a discussion you hosted involving people at very different
  levels of seniority or technical understanding.

### Skill: Data ethics and privacy

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior data analyst is expected to work with stakeholders to identify and
address ethical and privacy concerns, demonstrate and communicate how data
ethical issues fit into the wider organisational context, research
developments in data ethics and privacy to improve compliance and processes,
and assess and constructively challenge proposed data ethics policies.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Challenging proposed policy and connecting individual
issues to the wider organisational context describes someone shaping data
ethics practice, not just applying it — a clear step beyond applying
principles under supervision.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot connect a data ethics issue in their own work to the
  wider organisational picture.
- 1 — Beginner: Applies ethical principles in routine work but does not yet
  research or challenge policy.
- 2 — Intermediate: Works with stakeholders on straightforward ethical or
  privacy concerns with support.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently works with stakeholders to identify and address
  ethical and privacy concerns, explains how they fit the wider organisational
  context, and constructively challenges a proposed policy where it does not
  hold up.
- 4 — Expert: Champions and leads data ethics and privacy practice across the
  organisation.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe a time you raised a concern about a proposed
  data ethics or privacy approach, and what happened.
- Ask how you would explain to a junior colleague why a particular ethical
  concern matters beyond your own piece of work.
- You don't need to disclose any personal data or case details as
  part of this evidence gathering.

### Skill: Data management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior data analyst is expected to advocate for effective data governance
and data management policies and guidelines within their team and across the
organisation, monitor and improve data management practices in their team, and
help define, develop and support the use of data management tools, procedures
and methods in compliance with data governance policies.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Advocating for policy across the organisation and
improving a team's practices describes someone shaping data management, not
just following it.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Does not monitor or improve data management practice in their
  team.
- 1 — Beginner: Follows governance policy but does not yet advocate for it or
  improve practice.
- 2 — Intermediate: Monitors data management practices with support and
  suggests improvements.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently advocates for effective governance and
  management policy, monitors and improves practices in their team, and
  helps define tools, procedures and methods that comply with governance
  policy.
- 4 — Expert: Champions and leads data management and governance processes
  across the organisation.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe a data management practice you improved in
  your team, and what changed as a result.
- Ask about a time you advocated for a data governance approach beyond your own immediate team.

### Skill: Data preparation and linkage

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior data analyst is expected to plan the framework of an analytical
data model based on appropriate data sources, support the implementation of
data models using appropriate tools and systems, and ensure their team
understands the importance of data quality and modelling.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Planning a data model's framework, and ensuring a
team (not just themselves) understands data quality and modelling, is a step
beyond supporting the planning of a model, which is what the level below is
baselined against.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot plan the framework of an analytical data model.
- 1 — Beginner: Contributes to planning a data model but cannot lead it.
- 2 — Intermediate: Plans a straightforward data model's framework with
  support.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently plans the framework of an analytical data model
  from appropriate data sources, supports its implementation, and ensures
  their team understands why data quality and modelling matter.
- 4 — Expert: Develops analytical data models across the organisation using
  deep source knowledge and mentors others in modelling.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe a data model's framework you planned, and
  what data sources you chose and why.
- Ask about a time you helped a team member understand why a data quality or
  modelling issue mattered.

### Skill: Data visualisation

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior data analyst is expected to use a range of data visualisations to
simplify a complex story, adapt a story to the needs of multidisciplinary
stakeholders, advise and support others in developing inclusive, accessible
data visualisations, and suggest improvements to data visualisation
processes and standards.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Simplifying a genuinely complex story and advising
others on accessible design, plus suggesting process improvements, is a
significant step up from choosing the right visualisation for a focused
story, which is where the level below is baselined.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot simplify a complex story into a clear visualisation.
- 1 — Beginner: Produces correct, simple visualisations but struggles with
  genuinely complex stories.
- 2 — Intermediate: Simplifies a moderately complex story with support.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently uses a range of visualisations to simplify a
  complex story for different multidisciplinary audiences, advises others on
  accessible design, and suggests improvements to team visualisation
  standards.
- 4 — Expert: Sets visualisation standards for the organisation and promotes
  their adoption.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe a genuinely complex story you simplified
  into a visualisation, and how you adapted it for different audiences.
- Ask about a time you advised a colleague on making a visualisation more
  accessible.

### Skill: Delivering business impact through data

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior data analyst is expected to lead and support others in using data to
deliver business impact, define and communicate the scope of the business
impact they deliver so the value is clear, identify opportunities to develop
data products that deliver business impact, and develop and deliver scalable
data products that align to organisational priorities and enable decision
making. The framework lists this skill twice in the published summary, with
identical wording each time; the bullets are merged here into one block.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Leading others, and identifying opportunities for
scalable data products rather than only delivering ones already scoped, is a
clear step beyond developing and delivering a data product with support,
which is where the level below is baselined.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot articulate the business impact of a piece of work.
- 1 — Beginner: Delivers a data product but needs support articulating its
  business impact.
- 2 — Intermediate: Delivers a data product and explains its business impact
  with some support.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently leads and supports others in using data for
  business impact, defines and communicates the scope of that impact clearly,
  identifies new opportunities, and develops and delivers scalable data
  products aligned to organisational priorities.
- 4 — Expert: Champions the role of data across the organisation and
  partners with other professions on strategic pivots.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe a data product you identified the
  opportunity for and delivered, and how you communicated its value.
- Ask about a time you led or supported someone else in delivering business
  impact through data.

### Skill: Developing code for analysis

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior data analyst is expected to design and implement the analytical
approach, including code, using appropriate standards and tests, lead
collaboration to review and improve analytical approaches including shared
code, and guide others in producing analytical approach documentation,

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Designing and implementing to standards and tests,

- 3 — Advanced: Independently designs and implements analytical approaches

  review of shared code, and guides others in documentation.
- 4 — Expert: Oversees and promotes coding standards across the organisation.

**Evidence to gather**

### Skill: Managing a data project

**What the framework expects at this level**

A senior data analyst is expected to identify and define a problem statement,
apply and share project management methodologies including tools and
techniques, plan and implement different stages of the data project life
cycle, communicate progress of data projects with stakeholders, and adapt
teams' ways of working to deliver different project outcomes and
requirements. The framework lists this skill twice in the published summary,
with identical wording each time; the bullets are merged here into one block.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. Identifying and defining a problem statement
independently, and adapting a team's (not just their own) ways of working,
is a step beyond contributing to problem statements, which is where the level
below is baselined.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot define a problem statement or plan a project stage.
- 1 — Beginner: Contributes to a problem statement but cannot define one
  independently.
- 2 — Intermediate: Defines straightforward problem statements and plans
  simple project stages with support.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently identifies and defines a problem statement,
  applies and shares project management methodologies, plans and implements
  stages of the project life cycle, communicates progress to stakeholders, and
  adapts a team's ways of working to fit the project.
- 4 — Expert: Advises on problem statement development across varied
  projects and leads stakeholder relationships for a team's data projects.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe a data project problem statement you
  defined, and how you arrived at it.
- Ask about a time you adapted a team's way of working to fit a project's
  changing requirements.
