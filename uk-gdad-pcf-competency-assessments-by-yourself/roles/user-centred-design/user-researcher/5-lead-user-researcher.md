# Lead User Researcher - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This is for you: a chance to rate your own current strengths against the
baseline the UK GDAD Profession Capability Framework sets for this role
level, using the framework's own words for what it covers.

User researchers plan, design and carry out research activities with users that help teams get a deep understanding of the people that use government services. This research informs policy, proposition, service, content and interaction design so that services work well for users and achieve policy intent. A lead user researcher is an expert practitioner, leading and aligning user research activities across several teams.

Work through the competency matrix below, one skill at a time, then fill in
the overall determination at the end. Treat this as a mirror, not a test —
there's no assessor watching, so being honest with yourself only costs you a
slightly less flattering picture today in exchange for a far more useful one.
Read `## Advisory` before you begin.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating you
give yourself here must never be treated as equivalent to a formal
assessment for a hiring, promotion, pay or performance decision. Use it for
your own reflection, and talk to your manager, a mentor, or your
organisation's people development team about what you find.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you're rating
yourself against what this level actually expects rather than the level
above or below it. Then think back over recent, real examples of your work —
not how you'd like to have handled something, but what you actually did.

**Rate on evidence, not impression.** For every skill, try to recall a
specific moment that supports the rating you give yourself. If you can't
think of one, that's useful information too — it might mean the rating
should be lower, or just that you haven't had the chance to show this skill
yet, which is worth noting separately.

**Be consistent with yourself.** Try to apply the same standard across every
skill, rather than being generous where you feel confident and harsh where
you feel unsure. Coming back to this after a few days, with fresh examples in
mind, often sharpens a rating more than sitting with it in one go.

**Be kind, and be honest.** This only works if you can look at the result
without flinching. A low rating here is not a verdict on you — it's a
starting point. Everyone doing this role, at every level, has skills they are
still building.

## Rating scale

This is a proficiency scale, not a frequency scale — it measures how well
someone can do something, not how often they do it.

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

Worked example, using **Research management, leadership and assurance**:
someone at 2 (Intermediate) can help establish a research plan for one
product area, with guidance on how it should connect to wider goals. Someone
at 3 (Advanced) establishes and aligns a research programme independently,
producing insight that feeds into a roadmap. Someone at 4 (Expert) sets and
assures good practice user research standards, including ethics and
safeguarding, and is accountable for research quality across more than one
programme — not just their own. The baseline for Research management,
leadership and assurance at lead user researcher is 4 — this matches the
baseline set for it in `## Competency matrix` below.

## About this role level

**The role.** A user researcher plans, designs and carries out research
activities with users, to help teams get a deep understanding of the people
who use government services. This research informs policy, proposition,
service, content and interaction design, so that services work well for
users and achieve their policy intent.

**The level.** A lead user researcher is an expert practitioner, leading and
aligning user research activities across several teams. The role has six
levels in total, in order: associate user researcher, junior user researcher,
user researcher, senior user researcher, **lead user researcher** (this
level), and head of user research. At this level, you are expected to ensure
that teams take a user-centred, evidence-based approach to service design and
delivery, and to develop and assure good user research practice — accountable
for standards across several teams, not just your own work.

**The skills this level names**, in the order the summary lists them: Agile
research practices, Analysis and synthesis, Inclusive research, Research
management, leadership and assurance, Stakeholder relationship management,
User research methods, and User-centred practice and advocacy. Three of
these — Agile research practices, Inclusive research, and User-centred
practice and advocacy — are worded word-for-word identically to how the
framework describes them at senior user researcher, the level below. That
does not make them less important at lead level; it means the framework
expects the same standard from senior user researcher upward, and this
level's real step up shows in the other four skills, whose wording changes
markedly to describe organisation-wide programmes, standards and
stakeholder relationships rather than team-level work.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Agile research practices

**What the framework expects at this level.** A lead user researcher
advocates for agile ways of working and adapts how they design and conduct
research to match the complexity of the product environment. They understand
the strategic decisions the team needs to make, so that the research they
design actually answers those decisions, and they influence decisions about
priorities and agile processes within the team.

**Baseline for this role level:** Baseline: 3 — Advanced. This wording is
identical to senior user researcher, the level below, so the baseline stays
the same. The step up at lead level shows in this level's own distinct
accountability to align research across several teams, not in a change to
this skill's expected depth.

**Behavioural indicators**

| Value | Indicator |
| ---: | --- |
| 0 | Cannot explain how agile ways of working affect the sequencing or scope of research across more than one team. |
| 1 | Adapts their own research plan to one team's sprint cadence, but needs support to see how that should differ for a second team running at a different pace. |
| 2 | Coordinates research timing across two or three teams' agile cycles with guidance from a more senior colleague. |
| 3 | Advocates for agile working across the teams they support, adapting how research is designed and conducted to the complexity of each product environment, and influences priorities and agile processes across those teams. |
| 4 | Is someone other leads or senior researchers ask when a team's agile process is getting in the way of good research, and resolves the conflict without escalating it further. |

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask: "Tell me about a time two teams you supported were running agile
  processes that pulled research in different directions. How did you
  resolve it?"
- A plan or artefact showing how you coordinated research timing
  across more than one team's sprint cycle.
- Ask a referee whether you have been asked to sort out an agile
  process problem outside your own team.

### Skill: Analysis and synthesis

**What the framework expects at this level.** A lead user researcher helps
the organisation, not just their own team, adopt a wide range of analysis
and synthesis techniques, and works to continually assure, improve and
innovate those practices, so the organisation generates clear and valuable
findings.

**Baseline for this role level:** Baseline: 4 — Expert. The framework's
wording moves from team-level assurance at senior user researcher to
organisation-wide adoption, improvement and innovation at this level, which
is Expert-level mastery rather than independent Advanced practice.

**Behavioural indicators**

| Value | Indicator |
| ---: | --- |
| 0 | Has no experience helping more than their own team adopt an analysis or synthesis method. |
| 1 | Introduces a single new analysis technique to another team with close support from a more senior colleague. |
| 2 | Helps a second team adopt an analysis or synthesis method they already know well, with some guidance on how to introduce it. |
| 3 | Helps several teams or a community of practice adopt a range of analysis and synthesis techniques, and improves how the organisation applies them. |
| 4 | Continually assures, improves and innovates the organisation's analysis and synthesis practice, so that findings from different teams are comparable and demonstrably trustworthy. |

**Evidence to gather**

- Work sample: guidance or a standard you wrote to improve how
  more than one team analyses or synthesises research.
- Ask: "Tell me about a time you changed how the organisation, not just your
  own team, approached analysing research data."
- Ask a referee for an example of you spotting and fixing a
  systemic weakness in analysis practice.

### Skill: Inclusive research

**What the framework expects at this level.** A lead user researcher helps
teams understand the diversity of users of government services, effectively
includes all kinds of users in appropriate research activities, and
advocates inclusive practices so that teams design and deliver services that
work for everyone.

**Baseline for this role level:** Baseline: 3 — Advanced. This wording is
identical to senior user researcher, the level below, so the baseline stays
the same. At this level the same standard is expected to reach across
several teams rather than one, which is where the practical step up shows.

**Behavioural indicators**

| Value | Indicator |
| ---: | --- |
| 0 | Has not supported a team, beyond their own, to think about the diversity of the users it researches. |
| 1 | Shares their own team's inclusive research approach with another team when asked, with support from a more senior colleague. |
| 2 | Helps a second team adapt its recruitment or materials for a specific access need, with some guidance. |
| 3 | Helps teams across a programme understand the diversity of users of government services, and includes all kinds of users in appropriate research activities across more than one team. |
| 4 | Is someone teams across a programme turn to for advice on designing and delivering accessible services, and their advocacy has visibly changed how more than one team designs research. |

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask: "Tell me about a time you changed how a team, other than your own,
  approached including a group of users in your research."
- A piece of guidance or advice you gave to another team on
  inclusive recruitment or accessible research design.
- Ask a referee whether more than one team has sought your advice
  on inclusion.

### Skill: Research management, leadership and assurance

**What the framework expects at this level.** A lead user researcher
establishes and aligns user research programmes and teams to meet
organisational goals, synthesises findings from research programmes into
strategic insight that informs the organisation's roadmap, sets and assures
good practice user research standards for the organisation — including
ethics and safeguarding — and assures user research quality across the
organisation.

**Baseline for this role level:** Baseline: 4 — Expert. This is the clearest
statement of what distinguishes a lead: setting and assuring standards for
the whole organisation, not just evaluating a team's own work, which the
framework's wording places squarely at full mastery.

**Behavioural indicators**

| Value | Indicator |
| ---: | --- |
| 0 | Has not aligned a research programme or team to organisational goals. |
| 1 | Contributes to a research programme's plan when asked, with close support from a more senior lead. |
| 2 | Helps establish a research programme for one product area, with guidance on how it should align to organisational goals. |
| 3 | Establishes and aligns user research programmes and teams to meet organisational goals, and synthesises findings from more than one programme into insight that feeds the organisation's roadmap. |
| 4 | Sets and assures good practice user research standards for the organisation, including ethics and safeguarding, and is accountable for the quality of research across the organisation, not just their own programme. |

**Evidence to gather**

- Work sample: a research standard, ethics guidance, or safeguarding
  procedure you wrote or maintained for more than one team.
- Ask: "Tell me about a time you had to assure the quality of research you
  didn't carry out yourself. What did you check, and what did you do about
  what you found?"
- Ask a referee whether you have set a standard that other
  researchers are now measured against.

### Skill: Stakeholder relationship management

**What the framework expects at this level.** A lead user researcher directs
the stakeholder relationship strategy for the teams they lead, ensures
stakeholders' objectives are set and supports teams to meet them, and
influences and negotiates with senior stakeholders to resolve issues and
enable progress.

**Baseline for this role level:** Baseline: 4 — Expert. Directing a
stakeholder strategy and negotiating with senior stakeholders on behalf of
several teams is a step beyond the independent relationship-building expected
of a senior user researcher, and sits at full mastery.

**Behavioural indicators**

| Value | Indicator |
| ---: | --- |
| 0 | Has not directed a stakeholder strategy beyond their own project. |
| 1 | Follows a stakeholder strategy set by someone more senior, with support to apply it to their own team. |
| 2 | Sets stakeholder objectives for one team, with guidance from a more senior colleague. |
| 3 | Directs the stakeholder relationship strategy for the teams they lead, and ensures stakeholders' objectives are set and supports teams to meet them. |
| 4 | Influences and negotiates directly with senior stakeholders to resolve issues that are blocking more than one team's progress, and is trusted to represent research in rooms where the researcher's own team is not present. |

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask: "Tell me about a disagreement with a senior stakeholder that was
  blocking more than one team. How did you resolve it?"
- A stakeholder strategy document you directed, covering more than
  one team.
- Ask a referee whether you have represented research to senior
  stakeholders on behalf of teams other than your own.

### Skill: User research methods

**What the framework expects at this level.** A lead user researcher helps
an organisation adopt a wide range of user research methods, and leads a
community of practice to help the organisation continually assure, improve
and innovate its user research.

**Baseline for this role level:** Baseline: 4 — Expert. Leading a community
of practice for the whole organisation is a distinct, wider accountability
than advising colleagues within a team, and the framework's wording places
it at full mastery.

**Behavioural indicators**

| Value | Indicator |
| ---: | --- |
| 0 | Has not helped a team outside their own adopt a new research method. |
| 1 | Introduces one method they know well to another team, with close support. |
| 2 | Helps a second team adopt a method, with some guidance on how to introduce and support it. |
| 3 | Helps the organisation adopt a wide range of user research methods across several teams. |
| 4 | Leads a community of practice that continually assures, improves and innovates the organisation's user research methods, and other researchers cite their guidance as the reference point. |

**Evidence to gather**

  it change about how research methods were used across teams?"
- Materials you produced for a community of practice: guidance,

### Skill: User-centred practice and advocacy

**What the framework expects at this level.** A lead user researcher
evaluates and chooses approaches for building their team's understanding of
the user, advocates for user research with sceptical colleagues and
stakeholders, helps inexperienced teams adopt user-centred practices,
identifies the most important challenges and opportunities for their team's
service development, and aligns research plans to address team priorities.

**Baseline for this role level:** Baseline: 3 — Advanced. This wording is
identical to senior user researcher, the level below, so the baseline stays
the same. The step up at this level is that these accountabilities extend
across the several teams a lead is responsible for, not that the wording
asks for anything new.

**Behavioural indicators**

| Value | Indicator |
| ---: | --- |
| 0 | Has not advocated for user research to sceptical stakeholders outside their own team. |
| 1 | Repeats a case for user research made by someone more senior, with support to adapt it to a new audience. |
| 2 | Makes the case for user research to a moderately sceptical colleague, with some guidance. |
| 3 | Evaluates and chooses approaches for building understanding of the user across the teams they support, and helps inexperienced teams adopt user-centred practices. |
| 4 | Identifies the most important challenges and opportunities across several teams' service development, and aligns multiple teams' research plans to address them. |

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask: "Tell me about the most sceptical stakeholder you've had to convince
  of the value of research, across any team you've supported. What worked?"
- A plan showing how you aligned research priorities across more
  than one team.
- Ask a referee whether more than one team credits you with
  becoming genuinely user-centred.
