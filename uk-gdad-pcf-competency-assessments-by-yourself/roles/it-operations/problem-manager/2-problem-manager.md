# Problem Manager - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This is for you: a chance to rate your own current strengths against the
baseline the UK GDAD Profession Capability Framework sets for this role
level, using the framework's own words for what it covers.

Problem managers aim to resolve and control the root causes of incidents caused by errors within the IT infrastructure. In this role, you will be expected to work to prevent the recurrence of these incidents. A problem manager picks up feeds from numerous sources, such as the service desk, with the aim of putting in place continual improvements to stop incidents such as regular service outages recurring.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating you
give yourself here must never be treated as equivalent to a formal
assessment for a hiring, promotion, pay or performance decision. Use it for
your own reflection, and talk to your manager, a mentor, or your

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you're rating
yourself against what this level actually expects rather than the level
above or below it. Then think back over recent, real examples of your work —
not how you'd like to have handled something, but what you actually did.

**Rate on evidence, not impression.** For every skill, try to recall a
specific moment that supports the rating you give yourself. If you can't
think of one, that's useful information too — it might mean the rating
should be lower, or just that you haven't had the chance to show this skill
yet, which is worth noting separately.

**Be consistent with yourself.** Try to apply the same standard across every
skill, rather than being generous where you feel confident and harsh where
you feel unsure. Coming back to this after a few days, with fresh examples in
mind, often sharpens a rating more than sitting with it in one go.

**Be kind, and be honest.** This only works if you can look at the result
without flinching. A low rating here is not a verdict on you — it's a
starting point. Everyone doing this role, at every level, has skills they are
still building.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

This is a **proficiency** scale, not a frequency scale: it rates how well someone does the thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it.

**Worked example — Problem management.** At 2 — Intermediate, someone
might describe investigating a single recurring incident on request and
proposing a fix. At 3 — Advanced, someone for problem manager should be
able to describe co-ordinating a team to investigate several related problems
at once, ensuring the right actions were taken to resolve and anticipate
them, and leading preventive measures that stuck. That team co-ordination and
anticipation, not just individual investigation, is what "Advanced" evidence
looks like at this level.

## About this role level

The IT operations profession's **problem manager** role covers people who aim
to resolve and control the root causes of incidents caused by errors within
the IT infrastructure, working to prevent those incidents recurring. It has
two levels:

1. Problem analyst
2. **Problem manager** — this level

The **problem manager** sits above the problem analyst. They pick up feeds
from numerous sources, such as the service desk, with the aim of putting in
place continual improvements to stop incidents such as regular service
outages recurring.

At this role level, you will:

- resolve emerging and recurring problems
- perform root cause analysis to minimise the adverse impact of incidents
  caused by errors within the IT infrastructure
- work closely with major incident managers
- deal with problems and root cause analysis

This role level names eleven skills: Asset and configuration management,
Availability and capacity management, Change management, Community
collaboration, Continuity management, Incident management, Ownership and
initiative, Problem management, Service focus, Service management framework
knowledge, and User focus.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Asset and configuration management

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a problem manager to track, log and correct information
to protect assets and components.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. The wording is unchanged from the problem analyst
level, but a problem manager applies it across a wider caseload of emerging
and recurring problems, so a reliable, largely independent grasp is the
appropriate baseline.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Relies entirely on others to keep asset records accurate during
  an investigation.
- 1 — Beginner: Logs and corrects records with some supervision.
- 2 — Intermediate: Logs and corrects records unsupervised, across a varied
  caseload, and flags systemic anomalies.
- 3 — Advanced: Links configuration data quality issues to recurring problems
  they are managing.
- 4 — Expert: Improves how the team logs configuration data as part of
  reducing recurring problems.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of tracing a recurring problem back to a configuration
  data issue.
- Look back at a sample of asset records maintained under your oversight,
  if available.

### Skill: Availability and capacity management

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a problem manager to manage service components to
ensure they meet business needs and key performance indicators (KPIs).

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. The wording is unchanged from the problem
analyst level, but here it is applied to the manager's accountability for
minimising the adverse impact of recurring problems, so independent,
consistent practice is expected.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Does not connect capacity or availability data to recurring
  problems.
- 1 — Beginner: Reviews availability and capacity data when prompted.
- 2 — Intermediate: Regularly reviews components against KPIs and connects
  dips to problems they are managing.
- 3 — Advanced: Proactively identifies a capacity trend likely to cause a
  future recurring problem and acts on it.
- 4 — Expert: Their capacity analysis has prevented a significant recurring
  outage.

**Evidence to gather**

### Skill: Change management

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a problem manager to analyse and assess the impact of
change, document change requests, and action changes from change requests.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. Moving from "implement ... under supervision" at
the analyst level to "analyse and assess the impact of change" is a step up
into independent judgement, appropriate to a manager co-ordinating fixes for
recurring problems.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Actions changes without assessing their likely impact.
- 1 — Beginner: Assesses impact with support, and needs help documenting
  requests fully.
- 2 — Intermediate: Independently assesses impact, documents requests
  clearly, and actions changes correctly.
- 3 — Advanced: Anticipates second-order effects of a change before it is
  actioned.
- 4 — Expert: Is trusted to assess the impact of the most complex or
  high-risk changes arising from problem investigations.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of assessing the impact of a change before it was
  actioned, including anything that could have gone wrong.
- Ask to see, or describe, a change request document you authored.

### Skill: Community collaboration

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a problem manager to work collaboratively in a group,
actively networking with others, adapt feedback to ensure it is effective and
lasting, use their topic to identify problems or issues in the team dynamic
and rectify them, and identify issues through Agile "health checks" with the
team and help stimulate the right responses.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. This wording describes active facilitation of
team dynamics, a step up from the analyst level's contributory wording, but
this level's accountability is still to work closely with other managers
rather than to lead a whole function's culture.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Works alongside others without engaging with team dynamics.
- 1 — Beginner: Notices team issues but needs support addressing them.
- 2 — Intermediate: Actively networks across teams and helps address team
  dynamic issues when they arise.
- 3 — Advanced: Runs or contributes meaningfully to structured checks (such
  as Agile health checks) and acts on what they reveal.
- 4 — Expert: Is known across teams for improving how groups work together.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of identifying and helping resolve a team dynamic issue.
- Ask how you have used structured feedback (a retrospective, a
  health check) to improve how a team worked.

### Skill: Continuity management

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a problem manager to manage the runbooks and the
processes around service continuity, and test the runbooks to ensure that
service availability is maintained.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. Moving from "explain" at the analyst level to
"manage" and "test" runbooks is a step into active ownership, matching this
level's accountability for minimising the impact of infrastructure errors.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Is not involved in runbooks or continuity testing.
- 1 — Beginner: Follows an existing runbook with support.
- 2 — Intermediate: Manages and tests runbooks for their area, correctly and
  independently.
- 3 — Advanced: Improves a runbook based on what a test revealed.
- 4 — Expert: Their runbook testing has caught a gap that would otherwise
  have caused a real outage.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of testing a runbook, what it revealed, and what
  changed as a result.
- Ask how you decide when a runbook needs updating.

### Skill: Incident management

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a problem manager to lead the investigation and
resolution of incidents.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. "Lead the investigation and resolution" is a clear
step up from the analyst's "diagnose and prioritise", and fits this level's
explicit accountability to work closely with major incident managers.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Participates in incident investigations without taking a lead
  role.
- 1 — Beginner: Leads investigation of simple incidents with support.
- 2 — Intermediate: Leads investigation and resolution of routine incidents
  independently.
- 3 — Advanced: Leads investigation and resolution of complex or high-impact
  incidents, working closely with major incident managers.
- 4 — Expert: Is someone other managers want leading the investigation
  when an incident is serious and the cause is unclear.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of leading the investigation of a significant incident,
  including how you worked with a major incident manager.

### Skill: Ownership and initiative

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a problem manager to take accountability for issues
that occur and be proactive in searching for potential problems, and to
achieve excellent user outcomes.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. The shift from "own an issue" at the analyst level to
"proactive in searching for potential problems" marks active risk-seeking,
matching this level's accountability for stopping recurring incidents before
they recur again.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Only responds once a problem is raised by others.
- 1 — Beginner: Follows through reliably on raised problems, but rarely
  proactively searches for new ones.
- 2 — Intermediate: Occasionally identifies a potential problem before it is
  formally raised.
- 3 — Advanced: Regularly and systematically searches for potential problems
  across their feeds, and takes accountability for outcomes.
- 4 — Expert: Has a track record of catching serious recurring problems
  before they escalated, and models this for others.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of proactively identifying a problem before it was
  formally reported.
- Ask a referee about your reputation for proactive risk-spotting.

### Skill: Problem management

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a problem manager to ensure that the right actions are
taken to investigate, resolve and anticipate problems, and to co-ordinate the
team to investigate problems, implement solutions and take preventive
measures.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. "Co-ordinate the team" marks a genuine step up from
the analyst's individual-contributor wording — this is the core, defining
skill of the role at manager level, and the framework's own wording asks for
leadership of others' investigation work.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Investigates problems individually without co-ordinating others.
- 1 — Beginner: Co-ordinates a small investigation with close support.
- 2 — Intermediate: Co-ordinates straightforward team investigations
  independently.
- 3 — Advanced: Co-ordinates a team across multiple, sometimes competing,
  problem investigations, ensuring the right preventive measures are taken.
- 4 — Expert: Their co-ordination has visibly reduced repeat incidents across
  a service, and other managers ask them how they run investigations.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of co-ordinating a team's investigation of several
  problems at once, and how priorities were set.
- Ask a referee about your track record reducing recurring
  incidents.

### Skill: Service focus

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a problem manager to see the bigger picture by taking
groups of services and investigating how to get the best of underlying
services.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. This is a step up from the analyst's "take
inputs and establish coherent frameworks", but problem management at this
level is still primarily about resolving problems within services rather than
redesigning how groups of services work together, so the baseline sits in the
middle of the scale.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Investigates services in isolation from one another.
- 1 — Beginner: Notices when services are related but needs help
  investigating the connection.
- 2 — Intermediate: Investigates related services together and identifies
  some improvement opportunities.
- 3 — Advanced: Regularly improves outcomes by investigating grouped
  services rather than single ones.
- 4 — Expert: Has reshaped how a group of services is investigated for
  recurring problems.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of investigating a problem that turned out to involve
  more than one service.
- Ask how you decide when a problem needs to be looked at across services
  rather than within one.

### Skill: Service management framework knowledge

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a problem manager to show they have a Level 3 service
management framework qualification.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. The framework's wording is unchanged from the
analyst level, but a manager co-ordinating a team's investigations should
apply the qualification's concepts consistently, not just hold the
certificate.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Holds no service management framework qualification.
- 1 — Beginner: Holds the qualification but applies its terms imprecisely.
- 2 — Intermediate: Holds the qualification and applies its terminology
  correctly when co-ordinating investigations.
- 3 — Advanced: Uses framework concepts to explain to stakeholders why a
  process works the way it does.
- 4 — Expert: Is a reference point for how the framework should be applied
  locally.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd confirm your qualification and describe how you
  apply its concepts day to day.
- Think about how you'd explain a process decision in framework terms.

### Skill: User focus

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a problem manager to identify and engage with users or
stakeholders to collate user needs evidence, understand and define research
that fits user needs, and use quantitative and qualitative data about users to
turn user focus into outcomes.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. The wording is unchanged from the analyst level,
but at this level user needs evidence should inform how a manager prioritises
across a team's investigations, not just an individual case.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Prioritises problems by technical severity alone.
- 1 — Beginner: Considers user impact when prompted.
- 2 — Intermediate: Uses user needs evidence to help prioritise the team's
  investigations, unprompted.
- 3 — Advanced: Uses combined quantitative and qualitative evidence to make
  the case for prioritising a specific problem, and wins the argument.
- 4 — Expert: Their user-focused prioritisation has become how the wider
  team decides what to investigate first.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of using user needs evidence to change what the team
  prioritised.
- Ask how you balance user impact against technical severity when
  co-ordinating a team's work.
