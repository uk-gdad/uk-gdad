## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating you
give yourself here must never be treated as equivalent to a formal
assessment for a hiring, promotion, pay or performance decision. Use it for
your own reflection, and talk to your manager, a mentor, or your
organisation's people development team about what you find.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you're rating
yourself against what this level actually expects rather than the level
above or below it. Then think back over recent, real examples of your work —
not how you'd like to have handled something, but what you actually did.

**Rate on evidence, not impression.** For every skill, try to recall a
specific moment that supports the rating you give yourself. If you can't
think of one, that's useful information too — it might mean the rating
should be lower, or just that you haven't had the chance to show this skill
yet, which is worth noting separately.

**Be consistent with yourself.** Try to apply the same standard across every
skill, rather than being generous where you feel confident and harsh where
you feel unsure. Coming back to this after a few days, with fresh examples in
mind, often sharpens a rating more than sitting with it in one go.

**Be kind, and be honest.** This only works if you can look at the result
without flinching. A low rating here is not a verdict on you — it's a
starting point. Everyone doing this role, at every level, has skills they are
still building.

## Rating scale

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

This is a proficiency scale, not a frequency scale — it rates how well someone does the thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it.

**Worked example — Incident management.** Someone who can log a straightforward incident but has never diagnosed its cause sits at 1 or below. Someone who diagnoses and prioritises incidents, investigates their causes and finds resolutions sits at 2 or 3, depending on how consistently they do this and whether it includes incidents surfaced during on-call monitoring — this level's own baseline is 2, since the wording marks a step up from the entry level without yet reaching fully independent, complex-incident leadership. Someone who diagnoses the organisation's hardest incidents unaided is showing evidence closer to the level above.

## About this role level

Command and control centre managers proactively monitor live services and performance trends to identify potential problems or areas for improvement that can then be investigated. The role runs across four levels: operations analyst, senior operations analyst, operational control manager, and head of command and control.

The senior operations analyst level sits second of four, above operations analyst and below operational control manager. The framework describes this level as prioritising incoming messages for the team and ensuring correct information flow, record keeping and data capture. At this level, you detect notifications and conduct monitor checks to ensure that systems are running correctly; in emergencies, you may be on call outside your working hours to check events that have been logged as not functioning; and in some organisations you oversee the virtual environment.

The framework names eleven skills at this level: asset and configuration management (maintaining secure configuration and accurate information, controlling IT assets in one or more areas, verifying the location and state of IT assets), availability and capacity management (managing service components to meet business needs and key performance indicators (KPIs)), change management (analysing and assessing the impact of change, documenting change requests, and actioning changes from change requests), community collaboration (contributing to the work of others, motivating and empowering teams, creating the right environment for teams to work in and identifying the best team makeup, and recognising and dealing with issues), continuity management (explaining the basic principles of IT standard continuity management processes and procedures), incident management (diagnosing and prioritising incidents, investigating their causes, and finding resolutions), ownership and initiative (owning an issue until a new owner has been found or the problem has been mitigated or resolved), problem management (initiating and monitoring actions to investigate patterns and trends, consulting specialists effectively, determining the appropriate resolution and assisting with its implementation, and determining preventative measures), service focus (taking inputs and establishing coherent frameworks that work), service management framework knowledge (holding a Level 3 service management framework qualification), and user focus (identifying and engaging with users or stakeholders to collate user needs evidence, understanding and defining research that fits user needs, and using quantitative and qualitative data about users to turn user focus into outcomes).

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Asset and configuration management

**What the framework expects at this level**

The framework expects a senior operations analyst to maintain secure configuration and accurate information, to control IT assets in one or more areas, and to verify the location and state of IT assets.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. This is a step up in wording from "track, log and correct" at the level below, describing active independent maintenance rather than supported administration, matching Intermediate.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe what a configuration record is for.
- 1 — Beginner: Updates a configuration record accurately only with checking.
- 2 — Intermediate: Maintains accurate configuration and asset records for their own area unprompted, and periodically verifies location and state.
- 3 — Advanced: Proactively catches and corrects configuration discrepancies across more than one area.
- 4 — Expert: Owns configuration accuracy for the operations centre.

**Evidence to gather**

- Think about how you'd describe how you maintain configuration accuracy for the systems you monitor.
