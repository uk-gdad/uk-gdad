# Delivery Manager - Competency Assessment

## Introduction

This is for you: a chance to rate your own current strengths against the
baseline the UK GDAD Profession Capability Framework sets for this role
level, using the framework's own words for what it covers.

A delivery manager is accountable for the delivery of products and services. A delivery manager is accountable for the performance of the team.

Work through the competency matrix below, one skill at a time, then fill in
the overall determination at the end. Treat this as a mirror, not a test —
there's no assessor watching, so being honest with yourself only costs you a
slightly less flattering picture today in exchange for a far more useful one.
Read `## Advisory` before you begin.

## Advisory

This competency assessment is created by AI and is a work in progress for
educational purposes. It is not a validated assessment instrument — it has
not been piloted, normed or checked for adverse impact — and a rating you
give yourself here must never be treated as equivalent to a formal
assessment for a hiring, promotion, pay or performance decision. Use it for
your own reflection, and talk to your manager, a mentor, or your
organisation's people development team about what you find.

## How to use this assessment

**Preparing.** Read `## About this role level` first, so you're rating
yourself against what this level actually expects rather than the level
above or below it. Then think back over recent, real examples of your work —
not how you'd like to have handled something, but what you actually did.

**Rate on evidence, not impression.** For every skill, try to recall a
specific moment that supports the rating you give yourself. If you can't
think of one, that's useful information too — it might mean the rating
should be lower, or just that you haven't had the chance to show this skill
yet, which is worth noting separately.

**Be consistent with yourself.** Try to apply the same standard across every
skill, rather than being generous where you feel confident and harsh where
you feel unsure. Coming back to this after a few days, with fresh examples in
mind, often sharpens a rating more than sitting with it in one go.

**Be kind, and be honest.** This only works if you can look at the result
without flinching. A low rating here is not a verdict on you — it's a
starting point. Everyone doing this role, at every level, has skills they are
still building.

## Rating scale

Every skill in the competency matrix is rated on the same five-point scale:

| Value | Rating | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | None | No demonstrated experience of this skill |
| 1 | Beginner | Just starting to learn the skill; requires close supervision |
| 2 | Intermediate | Knows the basics and can perform tasks with some guidance |
| 3 | Advanced | Has a strong grasp of the skill and can work independently |
| 4 | Expert | Demonstrates full mastery and can teach or guide others in the skill |

This is a **proficiency** scale, not a frequency scale: it rates how well someone does the thing, on the evidence available, not how often they do it or
how they feel about it.

**Worked example — Team dynamics and collaboration.** At 2, someone has
supported a team in following its established norms but has not shaped them.
At 3 — the baseline for this role level — someone effectively brings
people together to form a motivated team, helps create the right environment
for the team to work in and empowers it to deliver, recognises and deals with
issues in the team's dynamics, and helps decide the best team makeup for a
given situation, all independently. At 4, by contrast, someone coaches
other delivery managers or whole organisations on team dynamics and conflict
resolution — evidence at that scale belongs to a senior delivery manager
assessment or above.

## About this role level

The product and delivery profession's delivery manager role exists to make a
team accountable for the delivery of products and services. A delivery
manager builds and maintains an effective team, removes obstacles, keeps the
team focused on what matters most, and helps the team improve how it works.

The role has four levels: associate delivery manager, delivery manager,
senior delivery manager, and head of (Agile) delivery management. This
assessment is for the second of those.

A **delivery manager** is accountable for the performance of the team. At
this role level, the framework says someone will build and maintain
teams, ensuring they are motivated, collaborating and working well; identify
obstacles and help the team overcome them; focus the team on what is most
important to the delivery of products and services; encourage and facilitate
continuous improvement of the delivery team; and coach and mentor both team
members and others to apply the most appropriate Agile and Lean tools and
techniques. The framework notes that the complexity or breadth of products or
teams will vary in this role, depending on context. This is a marked step up
from associate delivery manager: accountability for a team's overall
performance replaces learning under guidance, and coaching others appears for
the first time.

The framework names nine skills for this level: Agile and Lean practices,
Commercial management, Communicating between the technical and non-technical,
Financial management, Life cycle management, Maintaining delivery momentum,
Making a process work, Planning, and Team dynamics and collaboration.

## Competency matrix

### Skill: Agile and Lean practices

**What the framework expects at this level**

A delivery manager is expected to compare and select the most appropriate
delivery methods and processes, to recognise, reflect and adapt when
something does not work, encouraging experimentation, to use a blended
approach depending on the context, to help teams measure, evaluate and
visualise outcomes, and to encourage reflective practice to improve ways of
working.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. "Compare and select" and "use a blended approach
depending on the context" describe independent judgement about method, not
just applying a given approach — a strong, self-directed grasp of the skill.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot compare two delivery methods or say which would suit a
  given context.
- 1 — Beginner: Applies an Agile method someone else selected, with guidance.
- 2 — Intermediate: Applies Agile principles and tools independently within
  a single, familiar context.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently compares and selects the most appropriate
  delivery method for a team's context, recognises and adapts when something
  is not working, and encourages reflective practice to improve ways of
  working.
- 4 — Expert: Not expected at this level. Coaching and leading teams in Agile
  and Lean good practice, and acting as a recognised expert advocate, belongs
  to a senior delivery manager or above.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example where you chose one delivery approach over
  another for a specific team, and why.
- Ask how you noticed something was not working and what you changed.
- Ask a referee whether your team measurably improved how it
  worked under your leadership.

### Skill: Commercial management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A delivery manager is expected to act as the point of contact for contracted
suppliers, to understand appropriate internal contacts and processes within a
government department, and to understand how and when third parties should
be brought into digital, data and technology projects.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. The framework's wording for this skill is
unchanged from the associate delivery manager level; this level's growth in
accountability shows up in the team-facing skills rather than in commercial
management, which stays a supporting rather than a leading responsibility
here.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe why a project might need to work with a
  contracted supplier.
- 1 — Beginner: Can describe when a third party might be brought into a
  project.
- 2 — Intermediate: Acts as the day-to-day point of contact for a supplier,
  understands the internal processes involved, and knows how and when to
  involve a third party in a project.
- 3 — Advanced: Not expected at this level. Taking responsibility for complex
  supplier relationships and negotiating contracts belongs to a senior
  delivery manager or above.
- 4 — Expert: Not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example where you acted as the point of contact for a
  supplier and handled a query or issue.
- Ask how you decided a third party needed to be brought into a project.
- Ask a referee how confidently you manages day-to-day supplier
  contact.

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

**What the framework expects at this level**

A delivery manager is expected to listen to and interpret the needs of
technical and non-technical stakeholders and manage their expectations, to
manage active and reactive communication, and to support or host difficult
discussions within the team or with diverse senior stakeholders.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. "Manage" stakeholder expectations and "support or
host difficult discussions... with diverse senior stakeholders" describes
confident, largely independent handling of communication that carries real
stakes.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Struggles to explain a technical concept to a non-technical
  audience or vice versa.
- 1 — Beginner: Communicates simple, low-stakes information clearly.
- 2 — Intermediate: Supports discussions within a multidisciplinary team,
  with some tension in them.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently listens to and interprets stakeholder needs,
  manages their expectations, and hosts a difficult discussion involving
  senior stakeholders, keeping it constructive.
- 4 — Expert: Not expected at this level. Mediating and strengthening
  relationships across large or high-risk audiences belongs to a senior
  delivery manager or above.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example of a difficult discussion involving a senior stakeholder
  that you hosted or led.
- Ask how you manage a stakeholder's expectations when the answer is not
  what the stakeholder wants to hear.
- Ask a referee whether senior stakeholders trust you to represent
  the team's position accurately.

### Skill: Financial management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A delivery manager is expected to balance cost versus value, to consider the
impact of user needs, to report on financial delivery, to monitor cost and
budget, and to understand how and when to escalate issues.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. This is a step up from associate delivery
manager's pure understanding into real, active monitoring and reporting, but
short of setting or negotiating a budget, which belongs to a later level.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe how cost and value trade off against each other
  on a project.
- 1 — Beginner: Can explain the financial impact of the team's work but has
  not monitored or reported on a budget.
- 2 — Intermediate: Monitors cost and budget for a team, reports on financial
  delivery, and balances cost against value in a real decision.
- 3 — Advanced: Not expected at this level. Negotiating or setting budgets in
  complex environments belongs to a senior delivery manager or above.
- 4 — Expert: Not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example where you monitored a budget and reported on
  financial delivery.
- Ask about a decision where you balanced cost against value, and how you
  made the call.
- Ask a referee whether you escalates financial issues at the right
  time.

### Skill: Life cycle management

**What the framework expects at this level**

A delivery manager is expected to manage the delivery of products and
services that add value through different phases of the life cycle, with
some support, to identify if the product or service should move to the next
phase and, if it should, how to do this, to ensure the product or service is
developed in line with the appropriate service standards for a phase, and to
use new information to adapt approaches to life cycle management.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 2 — Intermediate. "With some support" signals real, hands-on
management of a product or service through a phase, though not yet the fully
independent, evidence-led judgement about whether a team should continue,
change direction, or stop that a later level describes.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot name the phases of a product or service life cycle or say
  what changes between them.
- 1 — Beginner: Can describe what changes between phases but has not managed
  delivery through one.
- 2 — Intermediate: Manages delivery through a phase with some support,
  assesses readiness to move to the next phase, and adapts the approach when
  new information emerges.
- 3 — Advanced: Not expected at this level. Deciding, with evidence, whether a
  team should continue, change direction or stop belongs to a senior delivery
  manager or above.
- 4 — Expert: Not expected at this level.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example where you assessed whether a product or
  service was ready to move to its next life cycle phase.
- Ask how you adapted an approach when new information came to light.
- Ask a referee whether your delivery has met the service
  standards expected for a phase.

### Skill: Maintaining delivery momentum

**What the framework expects at this level**

A delivery manager is expected to facilitate the delivery flow of a team,
managing the pace and tempo, and to actively address internal and external
risks, issues and dependencies, including where ownership exists outside the
team.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. "Facilitate... managing the pace and tempo" for a
whole team, and handling risks that reach outside the team's own boundary,
describes independent ownership of delivery flow, not the smaller, contained
scope described at the level below.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe what affects a team's delivery pace.
- 1 — Beginner: Tracks a dependency or risk when pointed at it.
- 2 — Intermediate: Tracks dependencies and flags risks within a small,
  supported scope.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently facilitates a team's delivery flow and pace,
  and actively addresses risks, issues and dependencies even where ownership
  sits outside the team.
- 4 — Expert: Not expected at this level. Optimising delivery flow across
  teams and finding innovative ways to unblock the most complicated issues
  belongs to a senior delivery manager or above.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example where you resolved a dependency owned by
  another team, outside your own.
- Ask how you have managed a team's pace to keep it sustainable under
  pressure.
- Ask a referee whether the team's delivery flow visibly improved under your leadership.

### Skill: Making a process work

**What the framework expects at this level**

A delivery manager is expected to identify and challenge organisational
processes of increasing complexity and those that are unnecessarily
complicated, to add value and coach the organisation to inspect and adapt
processes, and to guide teams through the implementation of a new process.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. "Identify and challenge" organisational, not just
team-level, processes and "coach the organisation" describe independent
judgement exercised beyond someone's own team.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot identify a process that is getting in the way of a team's
  output.
- 1 — Beginner: Supports a team in following an established process.
- 2 — Intermediate: Helps a team adapt its own process, with some support.
- 3 — Advanced: Identifies and challenges an unnecessarily complicated
  organisational process, coaches the organisation to inspect and adapt it,
  and guides a team through implementing a new process.
- 4 — Expert: Not distinctly expected at this level by the framework's own
  wording for this skill.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example where you challenged a process outside your own team and helped change it.
- Ask how you guided a team through adopting a genuinely new process.
- Ask a referee whether your process challenges have led to real
  change.

### Skill: Planning

**What the framework expects at this level**

A delivery manager is expected to understand the environment and prioritise
the most important or high value tasks, to use data to inform planning, to
manage complex internal and external dependencies, to provide delivery
confidence, to remove blockers or impediments that affect plans and develop a
plan for difficult situations, and to ensure teams plan appropriately for
their capacity.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. This is a substantial, active list — prioritising,
using data, managing complex dependencies, providing delivery confidence —
that describes independent ownership of a team's planning, a clear step up
from the awareness expected of an associate delivery manager.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot describe how a team's plan should account for its
  capacity.
- 1 — Beginner: Understands the importance of planning but has not produced
  one.
- 2 — Intermediate: Contributes to a plan with support, and can explain a
  dependency it depends on.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently prioritises high-value work using data, manages
  complex dependencies, removes blockers affecting the plan, and ensures the
  team plans to its actual capacity.
- 4 — Expert: Not expected at this level. Leading a continual planning
  process in a very complex environment and coaching other teams belongs to a
  senior delivery manager or above.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example where you used data to change a planning
  decision.
- Ask about a time you removed a blocker that was affecting the team's plan.
- Ask a referee whether your plans have proven realistic given the
  team's actual capacity.

### Skill: Team dynamics and collaboration

**What the framework expects at this level**

A delivery manager is expected to effectively bring people together to form a
motivated team, to help create the right environment for a team to work in
and empower it to deliver, to recognise and deal with issues, and to help
create the best team makeup depending on the situation.

**Baseline for this role level**

Baseline: 3 — Advanced. "Bring people together to form" a team and "empower
it to deliver" describes independent ownership of a team's dynamics, matching
the level description's own accountability for building and maintaining a
motivated, collaborating team.

**Behavioural indicators**

- 0 — None: Cannot explain what makes a team's dynamic work well or poorly.
- 1 — Beginner: Explains, in general terms, why team dynamics matter.
- 2 — Intermediate: Supports a team's established dynamic without shaping it.
- 3 — Advanced: Independently brings people together to form a motivated
  team, creates the right environment for it to deliver, recognises and deals
  with dynamics issues, and helps shape the team's makeup for a given
  situation.
- 4 — Expert: Not expected at this level. Coaching an organisation on team
  dynamics and conflict resolution belongs to a senior delivery manager or
  above.

**Evidence to gather**

- Ask for an example where you built or reshaped a team and
  improved how it worked together.
- Ask about a dynamics issue you recognised and dealt with directly.
- Ask a referee whether your team was visibly more motivated or
  collaborative under your leadership.
