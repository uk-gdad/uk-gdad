# Change and release manager - upskill resources

This page focuses on learning and growth resources for the work role of change and release manager.

- The resources include links to online courses, blogs, research articles, videos, and books.

- These help with job upskilling, work training, professional development, and career advancement.

- These are intended to be good starting points, not a comprehensive catalogue.

At this role level you ensure technical changes are assessed, prioritised, scheduled, authorised and implemented, you chair the change advisory board, you provide an escalation point, and you co-ordinate releases and their interdependencies. The resources below emphasise running change enablement that actually reduces failure, managing configuration for the organisation, and the team dynamics that make a board work.

We welcome constructive feedback to improve this page and these lists.

## Blog posts

- [Streamlining change approval, DORA](https://dora.dev/capabilities/streamlining-change-approval/): DORA's finding that external approval bodies have a negative effect on delivery performance with no evidence of lower change failure rates. Read this before defending your current process.

- [Change Advisory Boards Don't Work, Octopus](https://octopus.com/blog/change-advisory-boards-dont-work): The strongest case against the traditional board, worth engaging with seriously as the person who chairs one.

- [Change Advisory Board Best Practices: 15+ Industry Leaders Weigh In, InvGate](https://blog.invgate.com/do-we-still-need-the-change-advisory-board): A genuinely mixed set of practitioner views on whether and how the board should exist.

- [IT Change Management Checklist: The Complete ITIL 4 Guide, CheckFlow](https://checkflow.io/blog/it-change-management-checklist): Works through the practice, and clarifies that the board advises rather than authorises.

- [DORA change failure rate: what, why, and how, Swarmia](https://www.swarmia.com/blog/dora-change-failure-rate/): How the metric is defined and measured, and the benchmark ranges between elite and low performers.

- [When DORA metrics meet governance in banking, Team Topologies](https://teamtopologies.com/news-blogs-newsletters/when-dora-metrics-meet-governance-in-banking): How delivery metrics and regulatory governance can coexist, which is the argument you may have to make.

- [Release Engineering, Google SRE Book chapter 8](https://sre.google/sre-book/table-of-contents/): Release as an engineering discipline with standards of its own.

- [Embracing Risk, Google SRE Book chapter 3](https://sre.google/sre-book/embracing-risk/): Error budgets, which give you a defensible basis for saying yes to change rather than a defensible basis for saying no.

- [IT Change Management: ITIL Framework and Best Practices, Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/itsm/change-management): Reducing change-caused incidents while keeping the process agile.

- [Operate a reliable service, GOV.UK Service Standard point 14](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/service-standard/point-14-operate-a-reliable-service): The reliability standard your decisions are ultimately assessed against.

## Research articles

- [Categorization of Software Release Risks and Its Abatement Strategy](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/276499818_Categorization_of_Software_Release_Risks_and_Its_Abatement_Strategy): Release threats and the specific role change management plays in mitigating them.

- [A Reassessment of Risk Management in Software Projects](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/978-3-319-05915-0_20.pdf): What empirical research shows about risk management and performance, and the barriers to adoption in practice.

- [An empirical study on the implementation and evaluation of a goal-driven software development risk management model](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0950584913001328): A goal-driven risk model, evaluated rather than merely proposed.

- [Software release management challenges in industry: An exploratory study](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/267369294_Software_release_management_challenges_in_industry_An_exploratory_study): A 27-month study across 46 releases, placing the release manager at the centre of the process.

- [An empirical study on performance comparisons of different types of DevOps team formations](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/computer-science/articles/10.3389/fcomp.2025.1554299/full): Team structure against delivery performance, relevant to how change responsibility is distributed.

- [Implementation and impacts of IT Service Management in the IT function](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0268401223000099): A systematic review of empirical ITSM implementation research from 2012 to 2021.

- [Effectiveness of IT Service Management Capability: Value Co-Creation and Value Facilitation Mechanisms](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/333777833_Effectiveness_of_IT_Service_Management_Capability_Value_Co-Creation_and_Value_Facilitation_Mechanisms): Finds capability works through alignment, not through process weight.

- [Improved Root Cause Analysis Supporting Resilient Production Systems](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0278612522001273): Challenges and enablers in root cause analysis, including bias and data quality.

- [Continual Process Improvement for ITIL Service Operations: A Lean Perspective](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10580530.2019.1587576): A five-step framework for improving the process you own.

## Professional development tips

- Measure your own change failure rate and lead time, and publish both, because a board without data is a ritual
- Read the DORA evidence on approval processes honestly, and redesign rather than defend
- Chair the board to surface risk, not to distribute blame in advance
- Standardise the low-risk changes out of the board entirely, so it can spend attention where risk actually is
- Advocate for asset control across the organisation, since you cannot assess impact on records you do not trust
- Run agile health checks with the team and act on what they surface, rather than collecting them
- Co-ordinate interdependencies by making the dependency visible early, not by holding a schedule in your head
